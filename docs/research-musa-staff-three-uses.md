# Research Note — "What Is That in Your Right Hand": Musa's Three Uses and Their Fulfillment

**Status:** research only — no article drafted. Corpus-verified **and tafsir-verified**
(Ṭabarī / Jalālayn / Muyassar / Ibn Kathīr — raw report: `docs/research-musa-staff-tafsir-20-17-22.md`).
**Date:** 2026-07-23 (round 1), 2026-07-24 (round 2 — tafsir + second corpus pass)
**Seed:** Azam's intuition — the three things Musa names in 20:18 correspond to things the staff later *does*.

---

## The verdict on the seed

The intuition holds, and the corpus supports it more strongly than expected. But it needs one honest
correction to be defensible: **the correspondence is functional, not sequential.** Musa lists
lean → feed → other; the fulfillment arrives in the reverse-ish order other → lean → feed. Framing it
as a neat 1-2-3 checklist would be an overclaim. Framing it as *the shepherd's job description,
scaled to a nation* is fully warranted.

## The text

> قَالَ هِىَ عَصَاىَ أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ ﴿٢٠:١٨﴾

Three items:
1. **أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا** — I support myself on it.
2. **وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى** — I beat down [leaves] with it for my flock.
3. **وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ** — and I have in it other purposes.

---

## The mapping

### 1. "I support myself on it" → the sea becomes a road (26:63, 20:77)

- 26:63 — `ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ ٱلْبَحْرَ فَٱنفَلَقَ` — strike the sea with your staff, and it split.
- 20:77 — `فَٱضْرِبْ لَهُمْ طَرِيقًا فِى ٱلْبَحْرِ يَبَسًا` — strike **for them** a dry **path** in the sea.

The thing one man leaned on to walk becomes the ground an entire nation walks on. Note 20:77's
`لَهُمْ` — the benefit is transferred from Musa's own body to the people. A private walking aid
becomes a public road.

### 2. "I beat down leaves for my flock" → the rock gives drink to the tribes (2:60, 7:160)

This is the strongest leg. The **action-shape is identical**: strike an inanimate object with the
staff so that sustenance comes out of it, for the dependents in your charge.

- 20:18 — `أَهُشُّ **بِهَا**` (instrumental bāʾ + staff)
- 2:60 / 7:160 — `ٱضْرِب **بِّعَصَاكَ** ٱلْحَجَرَ` (same instrumental bāʾ + staff)

Different verbs (هشّ vs ضرب) — say so plainly; this is a shape echo, not a lexical one.

Supporting detail: the rock scene is narrated in **pastoral vocabulary**.
`قَدْ عَلِمَ كُلُّ أُنَاسٍ مَّشْرَبَهُمْ` — every group knew its **drinking-place** (مَشْرَب). That is a
shepherd's word for a watering point.

### 3. "Other purposes" → the serpent, and the swallowing of Pharaoh's craft

Musa names two uses and then **admits to a remainder he does not enumerate**. That open slot is the
one Allah fills — with the thing Musa could never have listed:

- 20:20 — `فَإِذَا هِىَ حَيَّةٌ تَسْعَىٰ`
- 20:69 / 26:45 / 7:117 — `تَلْقَفُ مَا يَأْفِكُونَ` — it swallows what they fabricate.

The article's turn: *the maʾārib ukhrā was prophethood.* He had it in his hand for years and could
only list two things it was good for.

## Corpus findings (all mechanically verified against `scripts/.corpus-cache/quranic-corpus.json`)

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **أَهُشُّ is a hapax legomenon** | Root هشش occurs **exactly once** in the Qur'an — 20:18. The verb for a shepherd feeding his flock exists nowhere else. |
| **مَـَٔارِب is near-hapax** | Root أرب occurs **twice** total: 20:18 (مَـَٔارِبُ) and 24:31 (أُولِى ٱلْإِرْبَةِ). |
| **أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ is the only "leaning in labor"** | Root وكأ occurs 11×. The other 10 are all the participle مُتَّكِئِين / مُتَّكَـًٔا — **reclining in ease**: 18:31, 36:56, 38:51, 52:20, 55:54, 55:76, 56:16, 76:13 (Jannah couches), 12:31 (Zulaykha's banquet), 43:34 (silver couches). Musa's is the single instance of anyone in the Qur'an leaning on something to *work*. |
| **The staff is plural only for the magicians** | عَصَا (sing.) for Musa throughout; عِصِيّ (pl.) only at 20:66 and 26:44 for the magicians. Many staffs against one. |
| **The right-hand echo (Ta-Ha internal)** | يمن appears at 20:17 (`وَمَا تِلْكَ بِيَمِينِكَ`) and 20:69 (`وَأَلْقِ مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ`). Ta-Ha alone refuses to *name* the staff at the showdown — 7:117 and 26:45 both say "your staff," Ta-Ha says "what is in your right hand." ⚠️ **Already covered** in `content/tadabbur/020-ta-ha/ayah-069.md` — do not re-mine. |
| **Madyan → the rock, same root, same construction** | 28:24 `فَسَقَىٰ **لَهُمَا**` (he watered **for the two**) → 2:60 `ٱسْتَسْقَىٰ مُوسَىٰ **لِقَوْمِهِ**` (he sought water **for his people**). Root سقي, identical lām-of-benefit, scaled from two women to a nation. |
| **The Madyan grievance is answered at the rock** | 28:23: the two women cannot water `حَتَّىٰ يُصْدِرَ ٱلرِّعَآءُ` — the weak wait until the shepherds withdraw. At the rock, **twelve** springs, `كُلُّ أُنَاسٍ مَّشْرَبَهُمْ` — every group assigned its own. The crowding problem Musa personally intervened against is structurally abolished. |

# ROUND 2 — Tafsir pass + second corpus sweep (2026-07-24)

## What the mufassirūn actually say

Raw report: `docs/research-musa-staff-tafsir-20-17-22.md`. Four findings that change the article.

### 1. Jalālayn gives the question a technical name — **istifhām li-t-taqrīr**

> ﴿وما تِلْكَ﴾ … الِاسْتِفْهام لِلتَّقْرِيرِ **لِيُرَتِّب عَلَيْهِ المُعْجِزَة فِيها** — *al-Jalālayn, 20:17*

"The interrogative is for *taqrīr* — so that the miracle may be **built upon** it." The question is not
information-seeking; it makes Musa affirm the ordinary fact so the extraordinary can be constructed on
top of his own admission. **We can now cite this.** The "why does Allah ask what He knows" theme is no
longer a bare structural reading — it has a named balāghah category behind it.

### 2. Jalālayn confirms Musa *over-answered* — in as many words

> ﴿أُخْرى﴾ … **زادَ فِي الجَواب بَيان حاجاته بِها** — *al-Jalālayn, 20:18*

"He **added** in the answer an explanation of his needs for it." The existing article's "he lingered,
he stretched the conversation" reading is classically warranted. Cite it.

### 3. ⚠️ **Correction to Round 1.** The "leave the blank blank" angle needs reframing.

I said the Qur'an's silence on *maʾārib ukhrā* should not be filled. But Jalālayn **does** fill it:

> ﴿مَآرِب﴾ جَمْع مَأْرُبَة … أيْ حَوائِج ﴿أُخْرى﴾ **كَحَمْلِ الزّاد والسِّقاء وطَرْد الهَوامّ**

— "like carrying provisions, and the waterskin, and driving off vermin."

This is *better* for the article, not worse. **Every single guess the tradition offers is another
shepherd-tool.** Provisions, a waterskin, chasing off pests. Nobody's list said *it will swallow an
empire.* So the honest, stronger framing is: the gap got filled — by more of the same. The
imagination of the man holding it, and of the scholars reading him, both topped out at luggage.

(This also removes the isrāʾīliyyāt risk from the third leg — Jalālayn's three items are mundane and
citable. Still avoid the lamp / glowing-branches material.)

### 4. Jalālayn reads Ṭuwā as **rehearsal for Pharaoh** — the article's thesis, in the tradition

> فَأَدْخَلَ يَده فِي فَمها فَعادَتْ عَصًا … وأُرِيَ ذَلِكَ السَّيِّد مُوسى **لِئَلّا يَجْزَع إذا انْقَلَبَتْ حَيَّة لَدى فِرْعَوْن** — *20:21*

"…and this was shown to Musa **so that he would not panic when it turned into a snake before Pharaoh**."

The tradition already reads the private scene as training for the public one. That is precisely the
logic of "what he says it's for at Ṭuwā is what it does later" — and it dissolves the order problem
flagged in Round 1. It is a rehearsal, not a checklist.

Also: Ṭabarī, narrating the transformation, **spontaneously recites both of Musa's uses back**:
`وكانت قبل ذلك خشبة يابسة، وعصا يتوكأ عليها ويهشّ بها على غنمه، فصارت حية بأمر الله` — "it was before
that a dry piece of wood, a staff he leaned on and beat leaves with for his sheep, and it became a
serpent by Allah's command." Ṭabarī instinctively frames the miracle *against the two stated uses*.

## New corpus findings (round 2)

### A. ⭐ The snake-word scales with the audience — clean, exceptionless

| Scene | Setting | Word |
|---|---|---|
| 20:20 Ṭā-Hā | private, Ṭuwā | `حَيَّةٌ تَسْعَىٰ` — a snake, moving |
| 27:10 Naml | private, Ṭuwā | `كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ` — as if a *jānn* (small, darting) |
| 28:31 Qaṣaṣ | private, Ṭuwā | `كَأَنَّهَا جَآنٌّ` |
| 7:107 Aʿrāf | **public, before Pharaoh** | `ثُعْبَانٌ مُّبِينٌ` — a **manifest** serpent |
| 26:32 Shuʿarāʾ | **public, before Pharaoh** | `ثُعْبَانٌ مُّبِينٌ` |

Same staff, same miracle — but every private telling uses a small or generic word, and every public
telling uses *thuʿbān* + **mubīn**. `مُّبِينٌ` appears in the throne-room scenes only: before Pharaoh it
must be undeniable. Jalālayn noticed the tension and harmonized it (20:20: it moved with the speed of a
*jānn* though it was a great serpent) — so the variance is a known crux, not a novelty claim.
**This is a standalone article.**

### B. ⭐ `أُخْرَىٰ` — Musa's word comes back from Allah's mouth, four ayahs later

- **20:18** — Musa: `وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ **أُخْرَىٰ**` — "I have *other* purposes in it."
- **20:22** — Allah: `ءَايَةً **أُخْرَىٰ**` — "*another* sign."

Between them sits **20:21** `سِيرَتَهَا **ٱلْأُولَىٰ**` — "its **first** state." So the dialogue runs
*other → first → other.* Musa gestures vaguely at a remainder he can't name; Allah returns the same
word and fills it — this time not with a use for the staff, but with the hand that was holding it.

Calibration: *ukhrā* is common (26× in the Qur'an, 4× in Ṭā-Hā). The strength is **adjacency inside one
dialogue**, not rarity. Say so.

⚠️ Prior coverage: `ayahs-022-024.md:159` reads *āyatan ukhrā* only as "second sign after the staff" —
it never links back to Musa's own *ukhrā*. **The link is unclaimed.**

### C. ⭐ Ṭā-Hā names Musa's staff exactly once, then takes the word away

Occurrences of عصو by surah — every other surah names it at every staff-moment:

| Surah | Musa's staff named |
|---|---|
| 7 Aʿrāf | 3× (107, 117, 160) |
| 26 Shuʿarāʾ | 3× (32, 45, 63) |
| 2, 27, 28 | 1× each |
| **20 Ṭā-Hā** | **1× — only 20:18, `عَصَاىَ`** (20:66 is the *magicians'* `عِصِيُّهُمْ`) |

After Musa attaches the possessive *yāʾ* — "**my** staff" — Ṭā-Hā never calls it that again:
- 20:69 → `مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ` ("what is in your right hand")
- 20:77 → `فَٱضْرِبْ لَهُمْ طَرِيقًا` (strike a path — **no instrument named at all**)

And `يمين` appears in **zero** staff scenes outside Ṭā-Hā (checked all 9). The right-hand designation is
this surah's alone. The surah of divine gentleness dispossesses him of the word, gently, once he's said it.
⚠️ The 20:17↔20:69 half of this is already in `ayah-069.md`; the **20:77 omission and the cross-surah
naming contrast are new.**

### D. ⭐ `أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟` is Form V — and the root splits perfectly on form

Root وكأ, 11 occurrences. **Ten are Form VIII** (`VF:8`) — every one of them reclining in ease:
18:31, 36:56, 38:51, 52:20, 55:54, 55:76, 56:16, 76:13 (Jannah couches), 12:31 (Zulaykhā's banquet),
43:34. **One is Form V** (`VF:5`): Musa, 20:18.

Sharper still — the root yields only **two finite verbs** in the whole Qur'an:

- **20:18** `أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟` — Musa, leaning on wood, working, in the presence of Allah.
- **43:34** `يَتَّكِـُٔونَ` — the deniers of ar-Raḥmān, reclining on silver couches, which 43:35 calls
  `مَتَٰعُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا`.

One man leans on a stick and is given prophethood; the others recline on silver and are told it is
worldly enjoyment. Verified against morphology `features` fields, not inferred.

### E. `سِيرَة` is a hapax

Root سير, 8 occurrences. Seven are the imperative `سِيرُوا` ("travel!"). **20:21 `سِيرَتَهَا` is the only
nominal form in the Qur'an** — the sole Qur'anic use of the word that later names the Prophet's *sīrah*.
Allah promises to return the staff to its "first *sīrah*." Handle lightly: the later technical sense is
post-Qur'anic, so this is resonance, not etymology-as-proof.

### F. `تَسْعَىٰ` — a three-beat sequence inside Ṭā-Hā

All three occurrences in the surah, in order:
- **20:15** `لِتُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا **تَسْعَىٰ**` — every soul repaid for what it *strives* for.
- **20:20** `حَيَّةٌ **تَسْعَىٰ**` — the staff really moves.
- **20:66** `**يُخَيَّلُ** إِلَيْهِ … أَنَّهَا **تَسْعَىٰ**` — the magicians' ropes only *appear* to move.

Real striving → real motion → counterfeit motion, with `يُخَيَّلُ` as the tell. Check `ayah-069.md`
before using; it may already own part of this.

### G. Weak — log but don't lead

Ṭabarī calls the pre-miracle staff `خشبة **يابسة**` (dry wood); 20:77 calls the sea-path `**يَبَسًا**`
(dry) — and the root يبس occurs only 4× in the Qur'an (6:59, 12:43, 12:46, 20:77), with `يَبَسًا` a
hapax form. Dry wood makes a dry road. **But** *yābisa* is Ṭabarī's word, not the Qur'an's, for the
staff — so this is a tafsir-to-text echo, not text-to-text. Do not present it as Qur'anic wordplay.

## Proposed article shape

**Working title:** *He Named Two Things and Left the Third Blank*

**Spine:** Allah asks a man what is in his hand. He gives a complete, honest, slightly rambling
inventory — and it is completely wrong, not because anything in it is false, but because it is a
shepherd's inventory of a prophet's instrument. Every single thing Musa says the staff is for, it
will do again at national scale. And the one item he could not fill in is the one that matters.

**Closing turn:** you can list what your life is for. Your list is accurate. It is also two items long,
and there is an *ukhrā* you have not been told yet.

**Closing turn (revised after round 2):** Musa listed two uses and gestured at a third he couldn't
name. The commentators tried to fill it in and came up with *luggage*. Not one of them — not Musa
holding it, not the scholars reading him — guessed the thing it was actually for. You can list what
your life is for. Your list is accurate. It is also two items long, and there is an *ukhrā* in it you
have not been told yet.
