# Course 3 — Ādam (ʿalayhi al-salām): *We Wronged Ourselves*

*Third in the AyahGuide paid-course line. Designed 2026-08-10. Companion to
[Iblīs — The Refusal That Had a Reason](iblis-course-design.md); the two are a pair the way
[Dāwūd](dawud-course-design.md) and [Sulaymān](sulayman-course-design.md) are a pair.*

## 0. Why this is two courses and not one

Ādam and Iblīs share a scene. They do not share a spine, and a combined course would have neither.

- **Ādam is a life.** A man is made, taught, warned, deceived, falls, and is *chosen afterwards*.
  It has a beginning and an end and a reversal in the middle. It is a story.
- **Iblīs is not a life.** He has one scene — 9 of the 11 verses that name him are the same
  prostration refusal — and then a *method* that runs to the end of time. There is no arc to
  narrate. That course is an anatomy, not a biography.

Two different spines, two different genres, two different adab loads. Combine them and the product
is a long-form version of a free article we have already published
(`/posts/adams-tawbah-and-iblis-refusal-the-fork-at-the-moment-of-wrong`), which is exactly the
thing nobody pays for. **Build both, bundle them, and let the bundle's pitch be the fork.**

**Order: Ādam first.** Its blocking work is smaller and precisely located (§7), its adab load is
lighter, and pedagogically you cannot teach the adversary before you have taught what he is
adversary *to*. The waswasa apparatus in Course 4 has its master template in the fall of Ādam —
so this course lays the ground the next one builds on.

## 1. The substrate

**Footprint — corpus-verified 2026-08-10.** Ādam is *named* in exactly **25 verses**, one occurrence
each:

2:31 · 2:33 · 2:34 · 2:35 · 2:37 · 3:33 · 3:59 · 5:27 · 7:11 · 7:19 · 7:26 · 7:27 · 7:31 · 7:35 ·
7:172 · 17:61 · 17:70 · 18:50 · 19:58 · 20:115 · 20:116 · 20:117 · 20:120 · 20:121 · 36:60

**The structural fact that shapes the whole course.** Unlike Dāwūd (scattered, no narrative) and
unlike Sulaymān (one sustained telling), Ādam's story is told **seven separate times**, in seven
sūrahs, each version keeping and dropping different things:

| Block | What only this telling gives you |
|---|---|
| **2:30–39** | The *khalīfah* announcement and the angels' objection; **the names taught** (2:31–33); and **2:37, the words he was taught to return with**. The only telling with the naming scene. |
| **7:11–27** | The longest and most complete. The fire/clay argument, the four-direction vow (7:16–17), the oath by which they were deceived (7:21), and **7:23 — the repentance, in their own words**. |
| **15:26–44** | The clay described (*ṣalṣāl*), the *jānn* made of fire, and **15:42 — the limit clause: you have no authority over My servants**. |
| **17:61–65** | The shortest and coldest. *La-aḥtanikanna dhurriyyatahu* (17:62), the cavalry and the shared wealth and children (17:64) — and **17:65, the limit again**. |
| **18:50** | One verse, and the only place the Quran states **he was of the jinn** — turned into a question aimed at the reader: *do you take him and his offspring as allies?* |
| **20:115–123** | The most human. **20:115 — *he forgot, and We found in him no firm resolve***. Then the waswasa (20:120), **20:121 — *and Ādam disobeyed his Lord and went astray*** — and **20:122, the answer**. |
| **38:71–85** | Made **with My two hands** (38:75), the question *did you grow arrogant?*, and **38:82 — Iblīs swears by God's might**. |

*That sevenfold retelling is itself a module.* It is also already flagged as the #1 open article
idea in `docs/knowledge-state.md` ("The Same Story Told Seven Times") — write that as free funnel

## 2. The vertical axis

> **Two beings are given one command. Both break it. One says *we wronged ourselves*; the other
> says *You made me stray*. Everything that happens to each of them afterwards follows from that
> one sentence — and the Quran encodes the whole difference in the grammar of a single root.**

**Corpus-verified spine (verified 2026-08-10).** The root **غ-و-ي** carries both sides of the fork,
in two different verb forms:

- **20:121 — *wa ʿaṣā Ādamu rabbahu **fa-ghawā*** ** — Form I. Ādam is the grammatical subject of
  his own straying. He did it.
- **7:16 and 15:39 — *bimā **aghwaytanī*** ** — Form IV, the causative, with **God** as the subject.
  *Because You made me stray.* Occurs in exactly two verses, both Iblīs.

Same root. Opposite grammar of responsibility. **This is a corpus fact, not a synthesis** — and as
far as the audit found, this lexical form of the fork is not yet published anywhere on the site.

And the Quran states the outcome of each. For Ādam:

- **20:122 — *thumma **jtabāhu** rabbuhu fa-tāba ʿalayhi wa hadā*** — "then his Lord **chose** him,
  and turned to him, and guided." **The election comes *after* the sin, and the verse says so with
  *thumma*.** Not restored to where he was. *Chosen.*

**The divine name enacted: al-Tawwāb.** And it is enacted, not asserted, in the most elegant way in
the Quran. At **2:37**, Ādam receives words *from* his Lord — *fa-talaqqā Ādamu min rabbihi
kalimātin* — and then **God turns**: *fa-tāba ʿalayhi innahu huwa al-Tawwāb al-Raḥīm.*

Three things there, and they are the whole course:

1. **He did not compose the repentance. He was given it.** The words came *from* God, and were used
   *on* God. The way back was supplied by the One being returned to.
2. **The same verb, both directions.** *Tāba* is what the servant does (turns back) and what God
   does (turns toward). The Quran uses one word for both halves of the transaction.
3. **The intensive form is God's, not ours.** *Al-Tawwāb* — the One who turns *again and again*.
   The grammar of maximum repetition is attached to the Forgiver, not the sinner. **God does more
   returning than we do.**

*(Compare Course 1: Dāwūd's spine was **al-Ghafūr** — the return is always received. This is the
prequel to that. Here we learn where the returning itself came from.)*

**The bridge to Course 4 (the bundle's one-line pitch).** At **14:22**, on the Day of Judgment,
Iblīs addresses his followers: *I had no authority over you except that I called and you answered.
**So do not blame me — blame yourselves.*** — *lūmū anfusakum.*

Which is, in substance, **the exact sentence Ādam said at 7:23**: *rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā* — we
wronged **ourselves**.

**The adversary knows the right answer. He has always known it. He says it out loud at the end of
the world. He simply never said it about himself.** That is the pair in one line, and it is
textually airtight.

## 3. Architecture — six parts, same spiral

- **M0 — Start Here: The Story.** Tell it straight through. Flag honestly that we are assembling a
  composite of seven tellings and that no single sūrah contains all of it. End on the course map.
- **M1 — Made, and Taught.** Before the fall: the *khalīfah* announcement, the angels' question
  (2:30), the names (2:31–33), *made with My two hands* (38:75), the breath (38:72). What kind of
  creature is this, and what was the honour actually for? Ends on: *and the first thing that happens
  to this creature is that somebody refuses to bow to it.*
- **M2 — The Machine.** The deception itself, run slowly: 7:19–22 and 20:120 as the Quran's own
  worked example. *Fa-waswasa* — what a whisper is and is not. The oath (7:21). The promise of what
  they already had. **And, welded on, the limit clauses (15:42, 17:65, 14:22) — never taught apart.**
  *(This module is the handshake with Course 4; keep it descriptive, not systematic — the full
  anatomy belongs there.)*
- **M3 — Two Sentences** *(flagship).* The fork. 7:23 against 7:16 and 15:39. The غ-و-ي grammar.
  Why *we wronged ourselves* is a harder sentence to say than it looks, and why the Quran treats it
  as the hinge on which a creature's entire future turns. Land 20:122 — **chosen afterwards** — and
  al-Tawwāb behind it.
- **M4 — What the Quran Won't Tell You.** The silences, and there are a lot of them: **the tree is
  never identified; the woman is never named; there is no serpent anywhere in the Quran; there is no
  inherited guilt.** Plus the sevenfold-retelling question — why tell one story seven times and drop
  something different each time? And the hard verse: **20:121, *and Ādam disobeyed his Lord***
  (see §4.4, this needs a position before a word is written).
- **M5 — The Words You Were Given.** Turned toward the learner. Three questions, then the close:
  the way back was never something you had to invent. It was taught. And the name on the other end
  of it is the one with the intensive form.

## 4. Adab guardrails (read before writing a word)

1. **The woman is never named in the Quran. Verified: *Ḥawwāʾ* has ZERO occurrences across all
   6,236 verses.** She is *zawjuka* — your wife — at 2:35, 7:19, 20:117. **Follow the published
   article's position exactly** (`/posts/quran-never-names-eve-quran`, guardrails at
   `scripts/article-backlog.md:329`): make the **negative claim only** — the Quran never singles her
   out, never blames her, never names her. **Do NOT argue that a singular verb somewhere exonerates
   her**; al-Ṭabarī reads the disobedience jointly, and that argument will not survive a read.
   Note instead the thing that *is* textual: **7:23's verb is dual — *qālā*. They repent together,
   in one sentence, in the plural.**
2. **The tree is never identified.** The Quran says *this tree* and stops. The tafsir substrate is
   saturated with identifications — wheat, grapevine, fig — and the Ḥawwāʾ/serpent/tree material sits
   at very high density in the reports a writer would be mining (57 *Ḥawwāʾ* tokens and 27 *serpent*
   tokens in `002-al-baqarah/tafsir-report-035-039.md` alone). **Do not name the tree.**
3. **There is no serpent in the Quran.** Not once. It is entirely absorbed material. Exclude it, and
   say plainly in M4 that it is not there — that absence is one of the course's best moments.
4. ⚠️ **20:121 — *wa ʿaṣā Ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā* — is the ʿiṣma problem, and the substrate has no
   answer prepared.** `020-ta-ha/ayahs-121-122.md` handles the two verbs substantively and contains
   **zero** discussion of prophetic infallibility. A course that teaches this verse in plain
   language, aloud, will hit the question in the first minute. **A position must be taken and
   grounded from named sources before M4 is drafted.** This is the single most likely place for the
   course to embarrass the project. Do not improvise it.
5. **No original sin, and no inherited guilt.** The Quran has the descent as *architecture*, not
   punishment — the position `/posts/the-first-descent-architecture-not-punishment` already
   established in print. The course must not drift back toward the Fall-as-catastrophe frame just
   because it makes a better story beat.
6. **Iblīs stays an exhibit here, not a character.** He is in this course only where he touches
   Ādam. His anatomy belongs to Course 4. Resist the pull.
7. **Every quotation** runs the full validator gate. Whole ayahs only (partial quotes fail
   `verify_arabic`). Author with `@@ARABIC@@` placeholders and fill via
   `scripts/fill-ayah-placeholders.mjs` — never type or paste an ayah.

## 5. Decisions carried over from Courses 1–2 (locked, do not re-litigate)

- **Plain language is the register.** Short sentences, every Arabic term glossed at first use, no
  grammar jargon. Target ~8th–9th grade.
- **Story before spiral.** M0 is chronological and the course map is stated on the page.
- **Vertical axis must be a divine name**, demonstrated rather than asserted.
- **Text-first is script-first.** Modules are shooting scripts for the video course. Write to be
  *said aloud*.
- **HELD.** Nothing published free; free facet articles are the funnel and stay separate.
- **Name the ikhtilāf with names attached.** Course 2's Module 4 established the standard: where the
  classical scholars split, both camps get named holders, and a reading is never taught without its
  guard clause. (See `docs/courses/sulayman-module-4-what-the-quran-wont-tell-you.html`.)
