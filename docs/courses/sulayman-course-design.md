*The companion course to [Dāwūd — The Return Inside the Crown](dawud-course-design.md). Same
machine, second run. Where the father is tested by a mistake, the son is tested by abundance.*

## 1. The substrate

**Footprint — corpus-verified.** Sulaymān is *named* in **16 verses across 7 sūrahs**:
2:102 · 4:163 · 6:84 · 21:78, 79, 81 · 27:15, 16, 17, 18, 30, 36, 44 · 34:12 · 38:30, 34.

**But the real unit is bigger, and this is the key structural fact.** Unlike Dāwūd — who is
scattered across nine sūrahs with no continuous narrative anywhere — Sulaymān is given **one
sustained story: An-Naml 27:15–44, a continuous ~30-verse block** with a beginning, a middle and an
ending. The Quran hands him the thing it withheld from his father.

*That contrast is a whole module.* Dāwūd's Module 4 asked "why is he scattered?" Sulaymān's asks the
inverse: why does this one get the sustained telling?

| Facet | Verses | One line |
|---|---|---|
| **The inheritance** | 27:15–16 | Given knowledge; inherits from Dāwūd; "we have been taught the speech of birds and given of everything." |
| **The ant** | 27:17–19 | An army of jinn, men and birds halted by one ant — and his response is a prayer for *gratitude*. |
| **The hoopoe** | 27:20–31 | A missing bird returns with news, and corrects the king. The letter to Sabaʾ. |
| **Bilqīs and the throne** | 27:32–44 | The queen's counsel, the throne fetched in a blink, the glass floor, her submission. |

## 2. The vertical axis

> **Sulaymān is given more command over the created world than any human in the Quran — wind,
> spirits, the speech of animals — and at the summit of it he asks one question about himself:
> *am I grateful or ungrateful?* His father was tested by a failure. He is tested by everything
> going right.**

**Corpus-verified spine.** The root ش-ك-ر (gratitude) lands at exactly his two extremes:

- **27:19 — the ant.** The smallest creature in the story stops his army, and he smiles and prays:
  *my Lord, grant me that I be grateful for Your favour.*
- **27:40 — the throne.** The largest display of power in his story, and the root fires **three
  times in one verse**: *"This is from my Lord's favour, **to test me whether I am grateful or
  ungrateful**. And whoever is grateful is grateful only for himself; and whoever is ungrateful —
  then my Lord is Free of need, Generous."*
- **34:13 — the command.** *Work, O family of Dāwūd, in gratitude — and few of My servants are
  grateful.*

And the block *opens* in the same key: 27:15's first quoted words from father and son are
*al-ḥamdu lillāh* — praise before any test arrives. Gratitude bookends the smallest moment and the
largest. And 27:40 names the test explicitly, in his
own voice, which is rare: most Quranic figures are told they are being tested. He works it out
himself, mid-triumph.

**The divine name enacted (Samir Mahmoud principle 1).** For Dāwūd it was **al-Ghafūr** — the return
is always received. For Sulaymān it is **al-Ghaniyy al-Karīm**, and it comes from his own mouth in
the same verse: *whoever is ungrateful, my Lord is Free of need, Generous.* God gives everything and
needs nothing back. Gratitude adds nothing to Him; ingratitude takes nothing from Him. Which is
precisely why the test is real — the whole transaction is for the receiver's sake.

*Tafsir-grounded (verified 2026-07-24):* al-Muyassar on 27:40 reads the test exactly this way —
*shall I be grateful in acknowledgement of His favour, or ungrateful by abandoning gratitude?* —
and then makes the axis explicit: whoever is grateful, **the benefit returns to himself**; whoever
denies it, *my Lord is free of need of his gratitude, Generous — His goodness reaches, in this
world, both the grateful and the ungrateful alike.* That last clause is the whole course in one
line: the giving is not conditional on the thanks, which is what makes the thanks a real test.

**The bridge to Course 1 (the single best structural find).** In Sūrat Ṣād, ten verses apart:

- **38:24** — Dāwūd is tested, *fa-staghfara rabbahu wa kharra rākiʿan wa **anāb*** — and returned.
- **38:34** — Sulaymān is tested, *wa-alqaynā ʿalā kursiyyihi jasadan thumma **anāb*** — then returned.

**Same verb, same form, same surah, same position in the story.** Corpus-verified: the perfect
*anāba* occurs in only four verses Quran-wide (13:27, 31:15, 38:24, 38:34) — and two of them are
this father and this son, being tested. **And in both cases the Quran withholds the content of the
test.** The return is the family inheritance. Gratitude is what Sulaymān does with power.

## 3. Architecture — six parts, same spiral

- **M0 — Start Here: The Story.** Easier than Dāwūd's: An-Naml supplies a real chronology. Tell it
  straight through, then the course map. *(Still flag the cross-sūrah joins as assembled.)*
- **M1 — Everything and Everyone.** The inheritance and the scale of the gift: knowledge, the speech
  of birds, wind, jinn, molten copper — "we have been given of everything" (27:16). Ends on the
  question the course hangs on: *what does a person like this have left to be tested on?*
- **M2 — The Ant and the Hoopoe.** Two scenes of power listening downward. An ant halts the army and
  he smiles; a missing bird returns and corrects him and he checks its claim rather than punishing
  it. The smallest voices are the ones the Quran gives lines to.
- **M3 — The Queen and the Question** *(flagship — the full Bilqīs sequence, 27:22–44).* The
  letter and her counsel; her one-line political wisdom ("kings ruin cities," 27:34); the gift she
  sends and he refuses (27:36–37 — the first gratitude test: *does more mean anything to him?*);
  the throne arriving in a blink; and at the peak, the spine lands — 27:40, *am I grateful or
  ungrateful*, with al-Ghaniyy al-Karīm behind it. Close on her ending (27:44): a monarch who lets
  go of everything she *has* for what she could *be* — the thesis performed by the other protagonist.
- **M4 — What the Quran Won't Tell You.** The Ṣād passage (38:30–40) as one unit — the two tests —
  plus the other silences, and the inverse-distribution question:
  1. **The horses (38:31–33)** — he says abundance pulled him from remembrance; then the famous
     ambiguity: the verse says he *wiped* their legs and necks; commentators differ sharply on what
     that means (see §4). What the Quran withholds here is the *content of the correction*.
  2. **The body on the throne (38:34)** — the test named, the content withheld entirely; then
     *anāba* — the father's verb. The 38:35 prayer ("a kingdom for no one after me") is taught here,
     honestly, with its opening *rabbi ghfir lī* and closing *al-Wahhāb*.
  3. **The sorcery charge (2:102)** — what the Quran *does* say, and the rare direct exoneration.
  4. **The death nobody noticed (34:14)** — he dies standing; the jinn keep working.
  5. **Why he gets the one sustained narrative** while his father is scattered across nine sūrahs.
- **M5 — Your Own Kingdom.** Three questions, then the close: gratitude is the one response with a
  promise of *increase* attached (14:7), and the Quran calls the grateful **few** (34:13) — a rare
  category anyone may join. Hand off to the contemplation product.

## 4. Adab guardrails (heavier than Dāwūd — read before writing a word)

1. **38:31–33, the horses.** The verse says only: the fine horses were shown to him; he said *"I
   loved the love of good things over the remembrance of my Lord"* until it/they disappeared from
   view; he called them back and *set to wiping (masḥan) their legs and necks*. Al-Muyassar and
   al-Jalālayn read the *masḥ* as putting them to the sword (and Muyassar reads the missed *dhikr*
   as ʿaṣr prayer — note the verse itself never says *ṣalāh*); other readings take the wiping at
   face value, as care for the horses. **Teach the ikhtilāf honestly and keep the verse's own
   restraint** — the existing article (`/posts/` horses-and-sun piece) and tadabbur
   (`038-sad/ayahs-030-033.md`) are the substrate. The scene is load-bearing for the thesis: it is
   his only recorded statement that abundance cost him remembrance.
2. **38:34, the *jasad*.** The Quran says only: *We tested Sulaymān, and cast upon his throne a body
   — then he returned.* The classical commentators give **three mutually incompatible accounts**:
   al-Ṭabarī (a shayṭān in human form, and they disagree even on its name — Ṣakhr / Āṣaf / Āṣir /
   Ḥabqīq); al-Muyassar (the half-formed child, after the oath sworn without *in shāʾ Allāh*);
   al-Jalālayn (the lost ring, a jinn impersonating him, his kingdom taken).
   **Position: keep strictly to the Quranic text** — the site already set this precedent in
   `/posts/ayat-al-kursi-throne-twin-quran`. Name that the commentators differ, teach the withholding
   as deliberate (exactly as with Dāwūd's test), and **do not narrate the ring story**, which has no
   Quranic basis and cuts against prophetic *ʿiṣma*. If the oath reading is mentioned, it must carry
   a properly sourced and graded hadith citation — **flagged as needing sourcing before use.**
3. **2:102 must be taught, not skipped.** The Quran explicitly clears him: *Sulaymān did not
   disbelieve — it was the devils who disbelieved.* This is one of the few direct exonerations of a
   prophet in the Quran, answering a slander that was in circulation. Handle the *siḥr* material
   soberly; the verse condemns the practice unambiguously.
4. **Bilqīs.** The Quran ends her story at 27:44 with her own words of submission. It never says
   Sulaymān married her. **Do not import it.** She is a monarch who reasons her way to belief — that
   is the Quranic portrait, and it is better than the folklore.
5. **The jinn.** Teach 34:12's frame — they worked *by his Lord's permission*, and were punished
   for deviating. Avoid all folklore; avoid anything resembling occult instruction.
6. **34:14, his death.** This one *is* Quranic — the staff, the creature of the earth, the jinn
   working on unaware. Teach it as given; resist embellishment.
7. **38:35's prayer** — *a kingdom that will not belong to anyone after me* — reads strangely to
   modern ears. Present the classical readings honestly rather than psychologising it, and note it
   opens with *rabbi ghfir lī* (forgive me) and closes on *al-Wahhāb* (the Bestower). **Placed: M4.**
8. **The *shukr* spine is a synthesis** grounded in the repeated word at 27:19 / 27:40 / 34:13 —
   present it as a reading the text invites, not a novel claim.
9. **Every quotation** runs the full three-validator gate. Whole ayahs only (partial quotes fail
   `verify_arabic`). Morphology tags use the corpus **segment** index, `pos` = coarse tag (`V`/`N`).

## 5. Decisions carried over from Course 1 (locked, do not re-litigate)

- **Plain language is the register.** Short sentences, every Arabic term glossed at first use, no
  grammar jargon. Target ~8th–9th grade. This was the single biggest persona finding.
- **Story before spiral.** M0 is chronological and the course map is stated on the page.
- **Vertical axis must be a divine name**, demonstrated rather than asserted.
- **Text-first is script-first.** These modules are the shooting scripts for the video course, not
  the final product. Write them to be *said aloud*.
- **HELD.** Nothing published free; free facet articles are the funnel and stay separate.

## 6. Open questions — ANSWERED (Fable review, 2026-07-24)

1. **Sequencing: separate courses, bundled offer.** The *anāba* bridge (38:24/38:34) is the
   bundle's one-line pitch.
2. **The oath narration: INCLUDE, carefully.** Unlike the Dāwūd backstory this is NOT israʼīliyyāt —
   it is in **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Muslim** (the seventy-wives / "he did not say in shāʾ Allāh"
   hadith). Teach it in M4 with citation + grading; teach its **connection to 38:34 as a
   commentarial position** (the hadith itself never mentions the throne or the jasad); keep the
   verse's silence as the primary teaching. Never narrate the ring story.
3. **Funnel articles, in priority order:** (a) **2:102 exoneration** first — "did Sulaymān do
   magic" is a live contested-verse search pattern, the Amina entry door; (b) the hoopoe's
   correction (27:20–28); (c) the death nobody noticed (34:14).
