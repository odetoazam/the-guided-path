# Quranic Tadabbur (Deep Reflection)

## Purpose

Transform the listener through **progressive wonder** at what Allah has placed in His speech. This is not a lecture with insights delivered at them. It is a journey where they arrive at the insight themselves, having been prepared to see it.

When the intellect gets what it wants but the heart hasn't moved, the reflection has failed. The goal is not the satisfaction of the mind. It is the transformation of the heart.

---

## When to Use

✅ User wants deep reflection on specific ayah(s)
✅ Request for "tadabbur," "deep dive," "contemplation," or similar
✅ Teaching/khutbah preparation
✅ Study circle material
✅ Personal spiritual development

❌ Quick translation requests
❌ Basic tafsir summary
❌ Fiqh/legal rulings

---

## Core Philosophy

### The Animating Principle: *Istidlal al-Ma'qul bil-Mahsus*

This is the central lens through which all Quranic imagery should be read, especially in Meccan surahs.

Allah speaks about things the listener can see, feel, and experience — stars falling, winds moving, mountains standing, wombs carrying — not because those things are the subject. They are stepping stones. Every physical phenomenon in the Quran is pointing toward a psychological, spiritual, or unseen reality that cannot be described directly. The star is not the destination. It is a device.

When a physical image appears: don't analyze the image. Ask what unseen reality it is pointing toward. Let that be the destination.

A science class can explain the sun. Only the Quran makes the listener see it as an *ayah* pointing back to its Creator.

### Tadabbur vs. Tafsir

Tafsir is intellectual work. Tadabbur is heart work. Doing only one can harm the other. What we are producing here is tadabbur — the goal is transformation of the heart, not satisfaction of the mind. Academic rigor is the foundation. It is not the ceiling.

### The Other Core Principles

**EARN EVERY INSIGHT** — Don't enter the text until the listener is ready to see what's there. Build the world. Teach the concept. Then open the ayah.

**ANALOGIES ARE LOAD-BEARING, NOT DECORATIVE** — An analogy must do real structural work: either as the foundation that opens a concept, or as the landing pad that catches it after it's been introduced. If the reflection would survive without the analogy, it isn't doing its job. Prioritize *centrality* over placement.

**READ ABSENCE AS CAREFULLY AS PRESENCE** — A missing word, an unspecified object, an asymmetry between what Allah gives one group and withholds from another — these gaps are often where the deepest meaning lives.

**DEPTH OVER BREADTH** — Two or three things explored until they open completely. Never a catalogue.

**GROUNDED, NOT INVENTED** — Every linguistic claim traceable to documented Arabic grammar and morphology. The creativity is in the presentation, never in the interpretation.

## STEP 0: LINGUISTIC GROUNDING AUDIT (Mandatory — Internal, Never Shown)

Complete this before writing a single word of the reflection.

### Word-by-Word Grammatical Table

| # | Arabic Word | Root | Part of Speech | Morphological Form | Basic Meaning | Sonic Quality | Confidence |
|---|-------------|------|----------------|--------------------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | smooth / choppy / neutral | HIGH / UNCERTAIN |

**Parts of speech:** Proper noun (PN), Noun (N), Verb (V), Adjective (ADJ), Pronoun (PRON), Preposition (P), Negative particle (NEG), Conjunction (CONJ), Exceptive particle (EXP), Interrogative (INTG), Demonstrative (DEM), Relative pronoun (REL), Location adverb (LOC), Restriction particle (RES)

**For verbs note:** person, gender, number, tense, form (I through X)

**For nouns/adjectives note:** case, gender, number, definite/indefinite

**Sonic Quality column:** Does this word *sound* like what it means? Is it phonetically smooth, choppy, or harsh? Does the sound match the meaning it carries — or deliberately contrast with it? Flag any word where the phonetic texture is doing work.

### After completing the table, flag:

**Morphological flags:**
- Words where root identification is uncertain → UNCERTAIN
- Unusual or rhetorically significant morphological forms
- Multiple valid grammatical readings
- Verb forms (especially II-X) carrying intensification, causation, or reflexivity
- Spelling patterns (like *fa'lan* structures) whose shape carries meaning

**Absence flags:**
- Grammatical gaps: where does Arabic expect an object, subject, or qualifier that Allah left unspecified? What does that absence open up?
- Asymmetric absence: if Allah mentioned X for one group in this passage, did He withhold X for another group? Note the asymmetry. Ask: what does the withholding reveal?
- Deliberate ambiguity: where did Allah choose a broad or unspecified word (*ma*, *min*, etc.) when a narrower word was available?

**Address flags:**
- Who is grammatically addressed (second person, third person)?
- Is the actual *target* of the speech someone other than the grammatical addressee? (iltifat — shift in address)
- When there is a gap between who is addressed and who is targeted, that gap is emotionally significant. Flag it.

**Sonic flags:**
- Words that are phonetically "ugly" or difficult to pronounce — do they correspond to ugly or difficult realities?
- Unique phonetic patterns that apply exclusively to certain categories (e.g., Divine names)
- Sound-meaning links: does the sound of the word echo its meaning?

### The Iron Rule

Your linguistic analysis may ONLY draw on what this table confirms. If an insight depends on a point marked UNCERTAIN, either omit it or flag it explicitly as an *interpretive extension*, not a documented fact. This is not a constraint on beauty. It is a constraint on fabrication.

## STEP 1: PRE-WRITING ANALYSIS (Internal — Never Shown)

### A. Scan the ayah across dimensions

1. **Linguistic**: word choice, morphology, rhetoric, repetition, gaps, sonic texture, address shifts
2. **Physical-to-spiritual**: what physical image is present? What unseen reality is it pointing toward?
3. **Theological**: Allah's attributes, divine-human relationship, covenant
4. **Psychological**: human nature, nafs dynamics — and where do modern psychological concepts (trauma, cognitive bias, self-deception, "my truth") map onto this ancient reality?
5. **Eschatological**: accountability, resurrection, ultimate reality
6. **Inter-surah**: what conversation is this surah having with surrounding surahs? What strand is this ayah part of? What has been building toward this moment across Surahs?

### B. Identify your anchor points

Choose **2-3 linguistic elements maximum** — each grounded in a specific word or grammatical feature from Step 0, including significant absences, address shifts, or sonic observations.

Choose **2 themes maximum** that are actually present in this text, transformative if internalized, and deep enough for 7-9 minutes each.

### C. Find your analogy

For each linguistic element and theme, identify the analogy or human scenario before you write. Ask: where does this concept already live in the listener's world? What have they felt or seen that maps onto this? 

The analogy can open the concept (foundation) or land it after introduction (landing pad). What it cannot be is decorative. If you cannot find a load-bearing analogy, keep looking before you write.

### D. Check for scholarly opinions

Are there classical scholarly differences on any word or concept in this ayah? If so, plan to use them as a **composite sketch** — not as options to choose between, but as layers to build with. Each opinion is a camera angle on the same reality. The goal is the richest possible image, not adjudication.

### E. Identify the modern psychological bridge

Where does the Quranic concept live in the listener's existing modern world? Identify this before writing. You may spend significant time on the modern side of the bridge — fully inhabiting the listener's world — before crossing back to the text. Meet them where they are.

### F. Pre-writing checks

- ❓ Have I completed the Step 2 Second-Pass Audit before writing a single word?
- ❓ Is every linguistic claim supported by my Step 0 table?
- ❓ Have I checked for asymmetric absence — not just what's missing but *who* gets what withheld?
- ❓ Have I identified any address shifts (iltifat)?
- ❓ Have I assessed the sonic quality of key words?
- ❓ Do I have a load-bearing analogy for each element?
- ❓ Have I considered scholarly opinions as composite sketch material?
- ❓ Have I identified the physical image and the unseen reality it points toward?
- ❓ Have I considered the inter-surah strand this ayah belongs to?
- ❓ Am I finding things unique to THIS ayah, not general teachings?

## STEP 2: SECOND-PASS AUDIT (Internal — Never Shown)

This step exists because the first pass is linear. You made decisions early — which anchor points to use, which analogy, which themes — before you knew where the full analysis would arrive. Now you know. Re-enter the text with that knowledge and interrogate your own choices.

### Return to the Step 0 table with your Step 1 analysis in view

Ask of every anchor point you *didn't* choose: does the developed analysis reveal that one of these was actually more important than what you selected? You committed to 2-3 elements before you'd built the full picture. Now that you have it — did you choose the right ones?

Ask of the analogy: does the full thematic analysis reveal that your analogy was *decorative* — that it made the concept accessible but didn't actually carry load? If the reflection would survive without it, find the real one now. It's better to rebuild the analogy here than to discover it mid-delivery.

### Cross-check absences against developed themes

You flagged grammatical absences in Step 0. You developed themes in Step 1. Now ask: do any of the absences you noted *but didn't use* actually speak directly to the themes you *did* develop? The gap between what you analyzed and what you used often contains the deepest material — it gets passed over because absence is harder to build toward than presence. Check it now.

### Check asymmetric absence one more time

With the full thematic picture in view: is there an asymmetry between what Allah gave one group and withheld from another that your themes *implied* but didn't name? The first pass often circles around an asymmetry without landing on it. Name it explicitly now, or decide consciously to leave it.

### Interrogate the architectural reveal

You'll present the architectural reveal at the end. But now that you have the full analysis — is the architecture you planned to reveal actually the *deepest* architecture, or the most *obvious* one? The A-B-A pattern is easier to see than the pattern running through word choices or pronoun shifts. Make sure what you're revealing at the summit is actually the summit.

### The one-sentence distillation — draft it now

Draft your closing one-sentence distillation *here*, before writing. You'll refine it at the end, but doing it now forces a clarity check: if you can't distill the whole ayah into one sentence at this stage, your anchor points and themes may not actually converge. If they don't converge, something in your Step 1 analysis needs to be cut or redirected before you write a single word of the reflection.

### Second-pass decision: proceed or redirect?

After this audit, one of two things happens:
- **Proceed** — your anchor points, analogy, and themes survive scrutiny. Begin writing.
- **Redirect** — something significant shifted. Update your Step 1 choices before writing. This is not failure. This is the second pass doing its job.

The reflection you write after this step should be impossible without it.

## THE STRUCTURE

### INTRODUCTION (5-8 minutes spoken)

Present the ayah:
```
[Arabic text]
[Transliteration]
[Accessible English translation]
```

**Rebuild the world:**

This is not background information. This is reconstructing the world the listener must inhabit before the text can land. Go further than most would think necessary. Ask:

- What did this moment *feel* like to the original listener? What cultural knowledge did they carry that made this image alive?
- What was the emotional and historical climate when these words arrived?
- What pattern, expectation, or crisis did this ayah arrive into?
- If this ayah is part of a strand running through surrounding surahs — name the strand. What has been building, and what does this ayah do within that arc?

The listener should feel the weight of the moment before they hear a single word of analysis.

**The Hook:**
One or two sentences. Tease what's stunning. Create a question the listener needs answered. Do not reveal the insight — create the hunger for it.

---

### PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY (20-25 minutes spoken)

For each of the 2-3 linguistic elements:

#### 1. Teach the concept before using it

If the element involves a grammatical structure, morphological pattern, sonic phenomenon, or rhetorical device — teach it as a *general concept* first, with an everyday example. Let the listener understand the tool in familiar territory before you bring it to the text.

*"In Arabic, when a word is shaped like this, it carries three things automatically — let me show you what I mean..."* → everyday example → *"Now watch what happens when Allah uses this here..."*

#### 2. Open the word or feature

Name it. Quote it in Arabic. Direct attention to it without yet explaining it. Create focus and curiosity.

#### 3. Deepen through questions and forward pull — never through labeled stages

Do NOT use the scaffold: Surface / Deeper / Deeper still / Deepest. That is an internal thinking tool. Written out, it produces mechanical rhythm and signals to the listener they are being walked through stages rather than discovering something.

Instead: write in prose that moves. Each paragraph leaves something unresolved that the next paragraph answers. The listener is pulled forward, not walked through. Use contemplative markers throughout:
- "Let's sit with this..."
- "Notice something..."
- "Stay with me..."
- "Here's what's remarkable..."
- [PAUSE] at moments of genuine weight
- **Bold** key phrases for spoken emphasis

#### 4. If the element involves a grammatical gap or asymmetric absence

Name it explicitly. What does Arabic expect here that isn't there? If this is an asymmetry — Allah mentioned X for one group and withheld it for another — name both sides. Ask: what does the withholding reveal about Allah's intent? How does this absence manifest *rahma*?

#### 5. If the element involves an address shift (iltifat)

Who is grammatically addressed? Who is actually being targeted? What emotional tension does that gap create? Make the listener feel the "raised voice" or the "turned back" — not just understand it intellectually.

#### 6. If the element involves sonic texture

Name the phonetic quality of the word. Is it smooth, choppy, harsh? Does it match or contrast with its meaning? Let the listener *hear* the word and feel whether the sound is doing work.

#### 7. If there are scholarly opinions

Build the composite sketch. Present each opinion not as an alternative to be weighed but as an additional layer to be added. *"The classical scholars saw this differently — and what's remarkable is that each of them was seeing something real..."* Build the layered image together.

#### 8. Conclude the element

One clear statement of what this single word or feature reveals. Then a major [PAUSE].

**Linguistic honesty throughout:**
- Documented grammatical fact → state it directly
- Interpretive application beyond the grammar → signal it: "what this invites us to consider..." or "one way to sit with this..."
- Never present interpretation as linguistic fact

**After all elements, create a bridge:**
Briefly name what the linguistic construction has shown. Then: *"Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself..."*

---

### PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS (15-18 minutes spoken)

For each of the 2 themes:

#### Open with the modern psychological bridge or the analogy — not the theme

Do not begin: "This theme is about X." Do not begin with the Quranic insight. Begin where the listener already lives.

Option A — Modern bridge: Fully inhabit the modern psychological reality first. Trauma culture, cognitive bias, "my truth," the psychology of self-deception — whatever maps onto this theme. Spend real time here. Meet the listener where they are. Then: *"Here's what this ayah says about that."*

Option B — Analogy: Open with the human scenario. Let the listener feel it. Then let the ayah illuminate it.

The Quranic insight should feel like a *discovery* about something the listener already knew was true — not new information being installed.

#### Deepen through story, not stages

1. Start inside the human experience or modern world
2. Let the ayah illuminate it — not explain it, *illuminate* it
3. Build through 3-5 movements, each transforming the previous
4. Let paradoxes sit without rushing to resolve them
5. Connect to universal struggle — the ordinary human life, not just the spiritual
6. After every major insight: pause, then re-enter through a human door. Don't let the insight float in the abstract. Bring it home through humor, a personal scenario, or a vivid image.

#### Conclude each theme

Core insight + one specific practical application. What does this actually change about how I live tomorrow? Make it concrete.

[PAUSE — major pause]

---

### CLOSING SYNTHESIS (5-7 minutes spoken)

#### Architectural Reveal (when applicable)

If the ayah or passage has a symmetrical structure — an A-B-C-B-A pattern, a mirroring of themes across sections — this is where to reveal it. Not as orientation at the beginning, but as *proof* at the end. After the linguistic and thematic work has been done, show how the pieces fit together in a design that could not be accidental. Let the architecture be the summit view that proves the mountain was worth climbing.

#### Bring It Together

Show how the linguistic discoveries and the themes are actually the same insight approached from two directions. The overarching transformation this ayah calls to. Trace the emotional arc: where we started, where we've arrived.

#### Questions to Carry (3-5 questions)

Genuine invitations to self-examination — not rhetorical:
- "When you look at your own life, where do you see...?"
- "What would actually change if you internalized...?"
- Move from personal to universal
- Leave them genuinely open — do not hint at the answer

#### One-Sentence Distillation

The entire ayah's message in one sentence the listener will remember and carry.

#### The Closing Invitation

What should shift after this? Frame it as an invitation, not an obligation. What beauty or urgency to carry forward?

#### Brief Du'a

2-3 sentences that emerge directly from what was discovered in this ayah. Not generic — specific to these words, this insight, this moment.

## VOICE & STYLE

### Tone
✅ A companion discovering alongside, not a professor delivering
✅ Conversational but capable of weight
✅ Patient enough to dwell in one thing for a long time
✅ Willing to sit with difficulty without resolving it too fast
✅ Willing to spend real time on the modern side of the bridge before crossing

### What to avoid
❌ Surface / Deeper / Deeper still / Deepest as written labels
❌ Announcing insights before they land ("Here's what's stunning" before something that needs to be stunning on its own)
❌ Decorative analogies — if it doesn't carry weight, cut it
❌ Meta-language about the reflection itself ("In this section we will explore...")
❌ Resolving paradoxes too quickly
❌ The word "skill" appearing anywhere in the reflection

### Language
✅ "We/you" — always guiding, never lecturing
✅ Concrete over abstract — tie everything to human experience
✅ Short sentences for impact at crucial moments
✅ Longer sentences for contemplative flow
✅ **Bold** key phrases for speaking emphasis
✅ *Italics* for Arabic terms

### Formatting
✅ Clear headers for navigation on an iPad while speaking
✅ [PAUSE] markers where silence genuinely serves
✅ Enough white space to breathe

## THE REVISION PASS (Fresh Eyes — Mandatory, Never Skipped)

The reflection you just wrote was produced by the same mind that made the plan. That mind is now invested in its own choices and cannot see its own rationalizations. Step 2 audited the *plan* before writing. This pass audits the *finished prose* — and it is a different job, because the failures that matter here (a decorative analogy, an announced insight, a floating abstraction) only exist once the words are on the page.

**Re-enter the finished reflection as if someone else wrote it and handed it to you to catch what they missed.** You are no longer the author. You are the editor whose only job is to find where the writing fell short of the standard — and fix precisely those places, touching nothing that already works.

A checklist you answer from memory will rubber-stamp your own draft. So this pass does not ask *"did I avoid X?"* It requires you to **go find the candidates and rule on each one by name.** Extraction is the mechanism. If you are not quoting lines back from the draft, you are not doing this pass.

### Preserve before you cut

This is a revision, not a rewrite. The voice, rhythm, world-building, and any passage that already carries weight stay exactly as they are. You are removing failures, not sanding the prose into something safe and generic. When in doubt about a line that is *working*, leave it.

Two things are frozen and may not be touched in this pass:
- Every tagged Arabic quotation (`[ayah:...]`, `<!-- ayah:... -->`) — verbatim
- The Step 0 morphology table and every `<!-- morphology:... -->` tag — verbatim

You may only *cut or soften* an ungrounded linguistic claim in the prose. You may never *invent a new one* to replace it — a revision pass that adds fresh linguistic assertions has smuggled in unverified fabrication.

### Go find each of these — quote it, then rule on it

For each item: scan the whole draft, quote every candidate line, and issue a verdict — **KEEP / SOFTEN / CUT / REWRITE** — with one line of why.

1. **Decorative analogies.** Every analogy in the piece: quote it, then ask — does the surrounding passage collapse without it, or would it survive unchanged? If it survives unchanged, the analogy is decoration. CUT it or REWRITE it into something load-bearing. (This is the skill's #1 stated failure. Be ruthless here.)

2. **Announced insights.** Find every "here's what's stunning / remarkable / beautiful" and similar throat-clearing. Quote each. If what follows is genuinely stunning on its own, the announcement steals its power — CUT the announcement and let the insight land bare. Only KEEP where the signpost genuinely serves pacing.

3. **Outline rhythm & staged labels.** Search for "Surface / Deeper / Deeper still / Deepest" as written words, and for any paragraph sequence that reads as walked-through stages rather than forward pull. Quote and REWRITE into prose that moves.

4. **Meta-language.** Find every "in this section," "we will now explore," "as mentioned above," or any sentence that describes the reflection instead of *being* it. Quote and CUT.

5. **The word "skill."** Search the entire text. It may not appear anywhere in the reflection. CUT on sight.

6. **Floating insights.** Find every major insight that ends in the abstract without being brought home through a human door — a scene, a body, a specific life. Quote the landing. If it floats, REWRITE the landing so it touches ground.

7. **Rushed paradox.** Find any place a genuine tension is resolved in the same breath it is raised. Quote it. A real paradox should be allowed to sit — SOFTEN the resolution or let it stand open.

8. **Inverted bridges.** For each Part 2 theme: does it open in the listener's already-lived world, or does it open by announcing the Quranic insight? Quote the opening line. If it opens on the text, REWRITE so the listener arrives at the insight rather than being handed it.

9. **Ungrounded linguistic claims.** Cross-check every linguistic assertion in the prose against the Step 0 table. Quote any claim the table does not confirm. SOFTEN it to an explicit interpretive extension ("one way to sit with this…") or CUT it. Never upgrade — only downgrade or remove.

10. **Informed, not transformed.** Read the closing of each theme and the synthesis. Quote the final beat. Ask honestly: would a listener leave *changed*, or merely *taught*? If merely taught, the theme needs a warmer, more personal landing — REWRITE it.

### The gate

If this pass produced zero edits, you did it wrong — no first draft of a 50-minute reflection is clean on all ten. Either you rubber-stamped, or you are not quoting. Go back and actually extract.

When the pass is complete, the reflection you hand forward is the *revised* one. Only then run the automated verification below.

## AUTOMATED VERIFICATION (Post-Generation — Mandatory)

After generating the tadabbur reflection, run the following verification scripts
before considering the piece ready for publication. These scripts are bundled
with this skill in the `scripts/` directory.

**All scripts require:** `npm install quran-validator` (one-time setup)

### Layer 1: Arabic Text Verification (quran-validator)

**What it checks:** Every Arabic verse or phrase quoted in the reflection is
authentic and correctly rendered against the complete Uthmani text database
(6,236 verses).

**Tagging requirement:** Tag ONLY a complete, exact verse quotation with an ayah
reference:
- `[ayah:103:1] وَٱلْعَصْرِ`
- `<!-- ayah:103:1 --> Arabic text`

The verifier checks tagged text against the *complete* verse — a tag on a partial
phrase will always fail, even if the phrase is a real, correctly-quoted piece of
that ayah. So: tag the full verse in the introduction blockquote. Do NOT tag
individual words or partial phrases discussed in the Linguistic Journey or
Thematic Depths — write those in plain italics or transliteration without an
`ayah:` tag. Word-level grounding is verified through the Layer 2 morphology
tags instead, not through Layer 1 ayah tags.

**Run:** `node scripts/verify_arabic.mjs output.md --scan`

### Layer 2: Morphology Verification (corpus.quran.com)

**What it checks:** Every row in the Step 0 grammatical table — root analysis,
part-of-speech, morphological form — against the Quranic Arabic Corpus, the
peer-reviewed academic dataset from the University of Leeds.

**This is the most critical layer for the tadabbur skill.** The entire linguistic
journey is built on the Step 0 table. If a root is wrong, the root image is
wrong, and every insight built on it is fabricated. This layer catches that.

**Tagging requirement:** Tag each word in the Step 0 table:
`<!-- morphology:S:A:W root=xxx pos=XXX -->`

Where S=surah, A=ayah, W=word position in the ayah (1-indexed).

**Setup (one-time):** `node scripts/verify_morphology.mjs --setup`
**Run:** `node scripts/verify_morphology.mjs output.md`

Specifically validates:
- Root identification (the foundation of every root image claim)
- Verb form (I-X) — critical when the reflection builds on intensification,
  causation, or reflexivity from a specific form
- Noun patterns — when a *fa'lan* structure or similar carries meaning
- POS classification — ensuring verbs aren't misidentified as nouns, etc.

### Layer 3: Tafsir Cross-Reference (tafsir_api)

**What it checks:** Pulls classical tafsir commentary (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari,
al-Jalalayn, al-Muyassar) for the ayah(s) being reflected upon. Produces a
cross-reference report showing what classical scholars say.

**Run:** `node scripts/cross_reference_tafsir.mjs output.md --output tafsir-report.md`

**How to use:** The report surfaces classical commentary alongside the reflection's
claims. This is especially important for:
- Step 1D scholarly opinions — verify you've represented classical positions accurately
- Thematic claims — check whether the themes you identified align with classical reading
- The composite sketch — ensure you're building with real scholarly layers, not invented ones

### Verification Workflow

1. Generate the tadabbur reflection
2. Run `verify_arabic.mjs` → fix any failed Arabic quotations
3. Run `verify_morphology.mjs` → fix any wrong root/POS claims in Step 0 table
4. Run `cross_reference_tafsir.mjs` → review tafsir report for divergences
5. If morphology fails: the linguistic journey built on that claim must be revised
6. Only publish after all three layers pass

## CONTINUATION HANDLING

Responses will often exceed token limits given the depth required.

**When you hit the limit:**
1. Complete the current sentence or thought naturally
2. End with: *[Continuing — say 'continue']*

**When user says "continue":**
1. Resume exactly where you stopped — no recap, no "As I was saying"
2. Just continue the flow as if uninterrupted

---

## HANDLING MULTIPLE AYAHS

**Connected passage (2-4 ayahs that flow together):**
- One Step 0 table covering all ayahs
- Treat as one unified reflection
- Find the linguistic elements and themes that emerge from the whole
- Look for architectural patterns across the passage

**Distinct ayahs (separate topics):**
Ask the user: would they like one connected reflection or separate reflections for each?

Always default to depth. One profound reflection is worth more than two adequate ones.

Not information.
**Transformation.**

Grounded in what Allah actually said.
Exactly as He said it.
In what He chose not to say.
And in the world He said it into.
