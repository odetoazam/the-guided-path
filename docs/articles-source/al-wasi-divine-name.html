Al-Wasi: The Divine Name That Answers the Fear There Will Not Be Enough

Al-Wasi (ٱلْوَٰسِع) is usually translated "the All-Encompassing" or "the Vast." Both are fair renderings of the root, and both are vague enough to be useless. The classical commentaries are not vague. Al-Jalalayn glosses the name with a single word almost every time it appears: faḍluhu — His bounty. Not vast in general. Vast in what He gives out.

And once you know that, the nine occurrences stop looking like a scattered set of verses about divine greatness. They start looking like a list of specific worries, each one answered in the same closing breath.

Nine occurrences, and every one is answering an arithmetic problem

The word occurs nine times in this form, and all nine name God. Seven of them close their verse as wāsiʿun ʿalīm — vast, knowing. One reads wāsiʿan ḥakīmā, vast, wise (4:130). One is different in a way we will come back to (53:32).

Here is what each of the nine has just finished talking about:

  • 2:247 — a king who has no money.
  • 2:261 — whether giving away wealth depletes it.
  • 2:268 — a voice promising you poverty if you give.
  • 24:32 — people too poor to marry.
  • 4:130 — a divorce that leaves two households where there was one.
  • 3:73 — whether God's favour is a closed franchise held by one community.
  • 5:54 — what happens to the religion if people leave it.
  • 2:115 — which direction you are allowed to pray in.
  • 53:32 — whether forgiveness runs out.

Money, marriage, membership, direction, mercy. Different subjects, one shape: someone has done a sum and concluded there is not enough to go around.

The verse that makes the point with its own vocabulary

The clearest case is the objection raised against Talut.

وَقَالَ لَهُمْ نَبِيُّهُمْ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ قَدْ بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا ۚ قَالُوٓا۟ أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لَهُ ٱلْمُلْكُ عَلَيْنَا وَنَحْنُ أَحَقُّ بِٱلْمُلْكِ مِنْهُ وَلَمْ يُؤْتَ سَعَةً مِّنَ ٱلْمَالِ ۚ قَالَ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصْطَفَىٰهُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَزَادَهُۥ بَسْطَةً فِى ٱلْعِلْمِ وَٱلْجِسْمِ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ يُؤْتِى مُلْكَهُۥ مَن يَشَآءُ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

"And their prophet said to them: God has raised up Talut as a king for you. They said: how can he have the kingship over us, when we have more right to kingship than he does, and he has not been given ample wealth? He said: God has chosen him over you and increased him in breadth of knowledge and body. And God gives His kingship to whom He wills — and God is Vast, Knowing."

Al-Baqarah (2:247)

The complaint is a budget. He does not have saʿatan mina'l-māl — breadth of wealth. Al-Jalalayn spells out what they meant: enough to fund the running of a kingdom.

Now look at the last three words of the verse. Wa'llāhu wāsiʿun ʿalīm.

Saʿah and wāsiʿ are the same root. The objection's own word comes back as God's name. They said the man has no breadth; the verse answers that breadth is not a thing the man was supposed to be carrying. Al-Jalalayn's gloss on the closing name is, again, one word: faḍluhu — His bounty is the wide thing.

The Quran does this twice. In An-Nisa, on divorce:

وَإِن يَتَفَرَّقَا يُغْنِ ٱللَّهُ كُلًّا مِّن سَعَتِهِۦ ۚ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعًا حَكِيمًا

"And if the two separate, God will enrich each of them from His breadth. And God has always been Vast, Wise."

An-Nisa (4:130)

Two words from the root w-s-ʿ inside one short verse: saʿatihi, His breadth, and wāsiʿan, the name. Al-Jalalayn refuses to leave "enrich" abstract and says what it means in practice — that He provides her another husband and provides him another wife. The fear inside a separation is that there is one life here and it is now being cut in half. The verse answers with a supply question, not a sentiment.

The voice that does the arithmetic for you

Al-Baqarah names the source of the fear outright.

ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ يَعِدُكُمُ ٱلْفَقْرَ وَيَأْمُرُكُم بِٱلْفَحْشَآءِ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ يَعِدُكُم مَّغْفِرَةً مِّنْهُ وَفَضْلًا ۗ وَٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

"Shaytan promises you poverty and commands you to indecency, while God promises you forgiveness from Him and abundance. And God is Vast, Knowing."

Al-Baqarah (2:268)

Al-Jalalayn reads the whole verse as a scene about giving: Shaytan frightens you with poverty if you give charity, so you hold on; and what he calls "indecency" here is glossed as the miserliness itself and the withholding of zakah. Against that, God's promise is forgiveness and faḍl — which al-Jalalayn renders as provision replacing what you spent.

Two promises about the same money, made to the same person, at the same moment. One of them is a threat and it is the one that sounds like prudence. The verse's last word on the matter is the name for breadth.

Three verses earlier, the same name closes the parable of the grain that grows seven ears with a hundred grains in each. Al-Jalalayn on that one: His bounty — and ʿalīm, knowing who deserves the multiplication. The vastness is not indiscriminate. That is what the second name is doing there.

"If they are poor, God will enrich them"

An-Nur applies it to a decision people still make on a spreadsheet.

وَأَنكِحُوا۟ ٱلْأَيَـٰمَىٰ مِنكُمْ وَٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَ مِنْ عِبَادِكُمْ وَإِمَآئِكُمْ ۚ إِن يَكُونُوا۟ فُقَرَآءَ يُغْنِهِمُ ٱللَّهُ مِن فَضْلِهِۦ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

"And marry off those among you who are single, and the righteous among your male and female servants. If they are poor, God will enrich them from His bounty. And God is Vast, Knowing."

An-Nur (24:32)

The verse anticipates the objection before anyone can raise it. Al-Jalalayn's gloss on yughnihimu'llāh is short and slightly startling: by the marriage itself. Al-Muyassar expands the closing name — vast, abundant in good, immense in favour, knowing the states of His servants.

This is not a promise that poverty is imaginary. It is a statement about which direction provision moves in, aimed at people who have decided the sum does not work.

Where the vastness is finally specified

Eight of the nine leave wāsiʿ standing alone as a description. The ninth attaches it to a noun, and it is the only place in the Quran where the Quran tells you what the breadth is of.

ٱلَّذِينَ يَجْتَنِبُونَ كَبَـٰٓئِرَ ٱلْإِثْمِ وَٱلْفَوَٰحِشَ إِلَّا ٱللَّمَمَ ۚ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ وَٰسِعُ ٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ ۚ هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِكُمْ إِذْ أَنشَأَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ وَإِذْ أَنتُمْ أَجِنَّةٌ فِى بُطُونِ أُمَّهَـٰتِكُمْ ۖ فَلَا تُزَكُّوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ ۖ هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَنِ ٱتَّقَىٰٓ

"Those who avoid the major sins and the indecencies, except for small lapses — indeed your Lord is vast in forgiveness. He knew you best when He produced you from the earth, and when you were hidden in your mothers' wombs. So do not claim purity for yourselves; He knows best who has been mindful of Him."

An-Najm (53:32)

Wāsiʿu'l-maghfirah — vast in forgiveness. Al-Jalalayn glosses it in two parts: vast in forgiveness through that, and in the accepting of repentance. Al-Muyassar reads al-lamam as the small sins a person does not persist in, or stumbles into rarely, and says that alongside the obligations kept and the forbidden things left, God forgives them and covers them over.

And then the verse turns and prohibits something. Do not declare yourselves pure. Al-Jalalayn reports the occasion — it came down about people who were saying our prayers, our fasting, our pilgrimage. The same sentence that makes the forgiveness wide makes self-congratulation out of bounds.

Which is the pattern in miniature. The name is not for people who have concluded they are fine. It is for people running out.

Reading the name

Al-Wasi appears nine times and never once as a standalone meditation on divine greatness. It is always the last thing said after somebody's calculation — a kingdom with no treasury, a charity that felt unaffordable, a marriage nobody could fund, a household splitting in two, a sin count that seemed to have passed the limit.

Seven of the nine attach al-ʿAlīm to it, and that pairing is doing real work. Vastness on its own would be a shrug. Vastness beside complete knowledge is a claim that the supply is wide and that the distribution is aimed.

The Quran already has a name for provision as a job — Ar-Razzaq, the One who provides. Al-Wasi is the answer to a different question. Not who will provide, but is there enough.
