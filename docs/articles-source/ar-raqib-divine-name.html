Ar-Raqib: The Divine Name Isa Hands Back on the Day of Judgement

Ar-Raqib (ٱلرَّقِيب) appears five times in the Quran. In one of them a prophet uses it to describe a duty he held and then gave up. Questioned about what his followers made of him, Isa answers that he watched over them while he was among them — and that when he was taken, the watching became God's. The name arrives at the moment a man stops being responsible for what people do with his message.

The root

The root is r-q-b (ر ق ب). Its sense is watching with attention — keeping eyes on something because something depends on it. The same root gives raqabah, a neck, and the connection is physical: the neck is what turns to keep a thing in view. It also gives irtiqāb, waiting and watching for an outcome, and murāqabah, the term Islamic spiritual writing uses for a person keeping watch over their own state because God is watching.

The word describes surveillance with a stake in it. A guard watches; a lookout watches; a person waiting for news watches. None of them is a passive observer.

The verse where a prophet holds it

Surah Al-Ma'idah closes with a scene set on the Day of Judgement. Isa is asked whether he told people to take him and his mother as gods. His answer runs several verses, and the pivot is this.

مَا قُلْتُ لَهُمْ إِلَّا مَآ أَمَرْتَنِى بِهِۦٓ أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ رَبِّى وَرَبَّكُمْ ۚ وَكُنتُ عَلَيْهِمْ شَهِيدًا مَّا دُمْتُ فِيهِمْ ۖ فَلَمَّا تَوَفَّيْتَنِى كُنتَ أَنتَ ٱلرَّقِيبَ عَلَيْهِمْ ۚ وَأَنتَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

"I said to them only what You commanded me: worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord. And I was a witness over them as long as I was among them. But when You took me, You were the Raqib over them. And You are, over all things, a Witness."

Al-Ma'idah (5:117)

Al-Jalalayn glosses Isa's word for himself, shahīdan, with the very word the verse gives to God a clause later: raqīban — and adds what the watching consisted of, amnaʿuhum mimmā yaqūlūn, restraining them from what they say. Then kunta anta'r-raqība ʿalayhim is glossed al-ḥafīẓ li-aʿmālihim, the keeper of their deeds.

Al-Muyassar renders the second half as God being the one who sees into their innermost, with nothing hidden from Him in earth or heaven.

The sentence has a hinge in the middle: mā dumtu fīhim, as long as I was among them. Isa marks the exact boundary of his own accountability. Inside it he was watching and restraining. Outside it the watch belongs to Someone else, and so does the answer for what happened.

The word given to an angel

One of the five occurrences applies the word to a created watcher.

مَّا يَلْفِظُ مِن قَوْلٍ إِلَّا لَدَيْهِ رَقِيبٌ عَتِيدٌ

"He utters no word except that there is with him a watcher, ready."

Qaf (50:18)

Here raqīb is the recording angel, paired with ʿatīd — present, on station, prepared. The Quran uses the same noun for the attribute of God and for the created being assigned the same task on a smaller scale, exactly as it does with ḥafīẓ.

Reading the two verses together sharpens what the divine name adds. The angel at 50:18 watches one person's speech. The Raqib of 5:117 watched a whole community across the centuries after their prophet left.

Where the name opens a surah

The remaining occurrences place the word where a person is most likely to think nobody is looking. Surah An-Nisa opens with it, at the head of the surah that will go on to legislate inheritance, orphans' property and marriage.

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَٰحِدَةٍ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًا كَثِيرًا وَنِسَآءً ۚ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ٱلَّذِى تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَيْكُمْ رَقِيبًا

"O people, be mindful of your Lord, who created you from one soul and created from it its mate and spread from the two of them many men and women. And be mindful of Allah, through whom you ask one another, and of the wombs. Indeed Allah is ever, over you, a Watcher."

An-Nisa (4:1)

The placement is deliberate. Family property, guardianship of orphans, what happens inside a marriage — these are the transactions with the fewest witnesses. The surah puts the name for watching in its first verse and then spends the rest of its length on matters conducted behind closed doors.

Surah Hud gives the word to Shu'ayb closing his argument with Madyan, and Surah Al-Ahzab uses it at the end of a verse regulating the Prophet's ﷺ own household. In both, the subject is conduct that would otherwise be unverifiable.

Reading the name

English translations give Ar-Raqib as "the Watchful," "the Watcher," "the All-Observant." The word behind them describes attention with responsibility attached rather than mere observation.

What the five occurrences share is placement. The name arrives where accountability would otherwise lapse — a prophet gone from his people, a word spoken with no one around, an estate divided among relatives, a household nobody outside can see into.

And in the one verse where a human being held the office, he held it for a stated period and then said plainly that it had passed to God. The Quran records him drawing that line himself, in front of everyone, on the day it matters.
