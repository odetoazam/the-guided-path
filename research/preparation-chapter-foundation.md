# The Preparation Chapter — Foundation

*Research foundation for Chapter 7, "The Preparation": the states that open a person to
receiving guidance, and the practice of contemplation. Reservoir-rich, research-thin — so
this note supplies the verse-anchors and the through-line that the existing tadabbur/article
reservoir (book-backbone-map §4-5, ch. 7) then populates.*

**Date:** 2026-07-22. **Method:** corpus census + four-mufassir tafsir. Keystone state-verses
(35:15, 14:7, 57:16), the practice-verse (47:24), the ikhlāṣ anchor (98:5), the awe/khushūʿ-
in-ṣalāh anchor (23:1-2), the ṣabr anchor (2:153), and the tadabbur-purpose verse (38:29) are

the ablution tradition rather than a reservoir file (gap noted in §2). Owes the living-scholar

## 0. The framing: states are qualities of soil, not checkboxes

The preparation states are not prerequisites you satisfy so guidance is dispensed — they are
*conditions of the receiving ground*. The same seed (revelation, sign) is offered to all;
what grows depends on the soil. This is the Same Rain thesis (ch. 5) turned toward practice:
the chapter is about *tending the ground* so the constant offer can land. The corpus supplies
five states and a practice, and — the load-bearing finding — **they share one axis: dhikr.**

---

## 1. The master through-line: every state is a mode of the heart oriented by dhikr

Before the five states individually, the finding that unifies them. Three of the heart's
receptive conditions are keyed, in the corpus, to *the same variable* — remembrance:

- **Awe (khushūʿ) — 57:16:** *a-lam yaʾni lilladhīna āmanū an **takhshaʿa qulūbuhum li-dhikri
  llāh*** — "has the time not come for believers' hearts to be humbled **to the remembrance
  of God**?" al-Ṭabarī: *an talīna qulūbuhum li-dhikri llāhi fa-takhḍaʿa* — "that their hearts
  **soften** to the remembrance of God, and become humble." And the verse names the opposite,
  as a warning: those before them, *ṭāla ʿalayhimu l-amadu **fa-qasat qulūbuhum*** — "the term
  grew long, so their hearts **hardened**." *(al-Jalālayn's occasion: revealed about the
  Companions when they had grown too casual/jesting — the softening is a summons back.)*
- **Tranquility (ṭumaʾnīna) — 89:27 / 13:28:** the muṭmaʾinna is the soul at rest *ilā dhikri
  llāh* (al-Muyassar), and 13:28 states it as law: *a-lā bi-dhikri llāhi taṭmaʾinnu l-qulūb*.
- **Softening (līn) — 39:23:** hearts *talīnu… ilā dhikri llāh* — soften to the remembrance of
  God (already tafsir-verified, dossier A.1).

**So the whole Preparation chapter runs on the Hinge chapter's variable.** The states are not
a miscellany of virtues; they are the postures of a heart *turned toward remembrance*, and
their common enemy is *qaswa* — the hardening that is the heart's **default** when dhikr
lapses and time passes (57:16). This is why preparation is *continuous*, not once-achieved
(the Fatiha 17× point, ch. 1; the feedback loop, ch. 2): left alone, the ground hardens.

## 2. The five states, anchored

### Need (faqr) — the constitutive condition, not a mood
**35:15** — *yā ayyuhā l-nāsu **antumu l-fuqarāʾu ilā llāhi** wa-llāhu huwa l-ghaniyyu
l-ḥamīd* — "O mankind, **you are the poor toward God**; and God is the Rich." al-Muyassar:
*antumu l-muḥtājūna ilā llāhi fī kulli shayʾin, **lā tastaghnūna ʿanhu ṭarfata ʿayn*** —
"needy of God **in everything, not independent of Him for the blink of an eye**." al-Jalālayn:
*bi-kulli ḥāl* (in every state). So faqr is not situational neediness — it is the *ontological
structure of the creature*, always and totally dependent. The Fatiha's *ihdinā* ("guide
**us**") presupposes exactly this: the ask is only honest from one who knows he cannot produce
the light himself. Need is the doorway state because it is simply *accuracy about what one is*.
*(Reservoir: 35:15 has a dedicated file — `035-fatir/ayah-015.md`; plus 6:40-41 & 16:53, the
fiṭrī collapse into pure duʿāʾ under duress, where need strips away every rival object of
hope.)*

### Gratitude (shukr) — the feedback loop with God
**14:7** — *la-in **shakartum la-azīdannakum*** — "if you are grateful, I will surely increase
you." al-Jalālayn: *la-in shakartum niʿmatī bi-l-tawḥīdi wa-l-ṭāʿati la-azīdannakum* — thanks
rendered as tawḥīd + obedience is answered with increase. Gratitude *restructures the inner
world around what is given* rather than what is missing (the determinate-world reorganized,
ch. 2) — and God answers the restructuring with more to be grateful for. It is a loop that
runs upward, the bright mirror of the diseased heart that compounds (2:10). *(Reservoir: 55:18
the *fa-bi-ayyi* refrain as an itemizing gratitude-machine; 36:71-73 re-seeing the taken-for-
granted; the counter to hedonic adaptation, 29:67.)*

### Awe (khushūʿ / khashya) — stilled by the weight of what is before you
**57:16** (§1) is the anchor: dhikr-keyed heart-softening, its opposite the qaswa of the
distracted. **23:1-2** binds it to practice: *qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn, alladhīna hum fī
ṣalātihim khāshiʿūn* — the flourishing believers are *khāshiʿūn* in their prayer. ✓
**Tafsir-verified:** al-Muyassar glosses khushūʿ as *tafrughu lahā qulūbuhum wa-taskunu
jawāriḥuhum* — "their **hearts are emptied** for it and their **limbs are stilled**"; al-
Jalālayn, *mutawāḍiʿūn* (humbled). This is the mechanism exactly: awe is the moment the ego's
narration ("how does this affect me?") goes quiet — the heart *emptied* — and the sign can
finally register. And the *falāḥ* (flourishing) is predicated on it. *(khushūʿ 17/16 +
khashya 48/40.)*

### Patience (ṣabr) — the largest state, the ground under all the others
Census: **ṣabr 103 tok / 93 verses** — by far the most-attested of the five, which the chapter
should reflect. Patience is the endurance that holds the ground steady long enough for the
slow work of the other states to take. ✓ **Anchor tafsir-verified — 2:153** (*istaʿīnū
bi-l-ṣabri wa-l-ṣalāh*): al-Muyassar gives the classical threefold ṣabr — *ʿalā l-nawāʾibi
wa-l-maṣāʾib* (over calamity), *ʿalā tarki l-maʿāṣī* (in abstaining from sin), *ʿalā
l-ṭāʿāt* (upon obedience) — and glosses its companion ṣalāh as *the prayer **through which
the self finds rest (taṭmaʾinnu bihā l-nafs)*** — **the dhikr/rest through-line again, even
under patience**. *(Reservoir: 20:129-130, ṣabr structured by scheduled dhikr; 73:10, hajr
jamīl; 38:41-44, Ayyūb naming his pain as adab.)*

### Purity (ikhlāṣ / ṭahāra) — clearing the channel
**98:5** — *wa-mā umirū illā li-yaʿbudū llāha **mukhliṣīna lahu l-dīn** ḥunafāʾ* — commanded
only to worship God *making the religion purely His*. ✓ **Tafsir-verified:** al-Ṭabarī —
*mufridīna lahu l-ṭāʿata, lā yakhliṭūna ṭāʿatahum… bi-shirk* ("singling out obedience to Him,
not **mixing** their obedience with any shirk"); al-Muyassar — *qāṣidīna bi-ʿibādatihim
wajhah* (intending His face). Ikhlāṣ is *unmixed, unmediated* address — nothing enthroned
between the self and God (**the exact inverse of hawā-as-god, ch. 2**); and *dīn al-qayyima*
= the upright religion (ties to *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm*, ch. 1). **2:222** (*inna llāha… yuḥibbu
l-mutaṭahhirīn*) and the ablution tradition make outward purity a *signal to the self* that
what one approaches is not ordinary — aligning body with intention. *(Reservoir:
39:3 al-dīn al-khāliṣ — richest ikhlāṣ file; 92:17-18 tazkiyah by giving. Census: ikhlāṣ
31/30, ṭahāra 31/26.)* **Gap flag (from mining):** ṭahāra as a *distinct guidance-opener* is
thin at the tag layer — the chapter leans on ikhlāṣ; source 2:222 + the ablution ḥadīth
directly rather than expecting a reservoir file.

## 3. The practice: tafakkur / tadabbur / tadhakkur — and it presupposes the prepared organ

The states ready the ground; the practice is what one then *does* on it. The Quran's
contemplation-vocabulary (census): **tafakkur 18/18, tadabbur 44/44, tadhakkur/dhikr
292/264** — three distinct acts:
- **tafakkur** — sustained deliberate thinking-into a thing (the *afalā tatafakkarūn*
  invitations; 3:191, reflection on creation).
- **tadabbur** — reflection that asks *so what? / what does this demand of me?* — the site's
  own method. **38:29** names it the Quran's *purpose*: *kitābun anzalnāhu ilayka mubārakun
  **li-yaddabbarū āyātihi wa-li-yatadhakkara ulū l-albāb*** — revealed *so that* they ponder
  its signs and the discerning take heed. ✓ **Tafsir-verified:** al-Jalālayn — *yanẓurū fī
  maʿānīhā **fa-yuʾminū*** (look into their meanings and so believe); al-Muyassar — *li-
  yatafakkarū fī āyātihi **wa-yaʿmalū bi-hidāyātihi wa-dalālātih*** (reflect on its signs and
  **act on its guidances**). So tadabbur is purpose-built to issue in belief and action —
  tadabbur → hidāya → ʿamal, the site's method named as the Book's own reason for descending.
- **tadhakkur** — remembrance; recalling what the soul already knows but let slip (the Quran
  calls itself *dhikr*). The return to what was always known.

**The load-bearing link (47:24):** *afalā **yatadabbarūna** l-qurʾāna **am ʿalā qulūbin
aqfāluhā*** — "do they not ponder the Quran, or are there **locks on hearts**?" al-Muyassar:
*bal hādhihi l-qulūbu **mughlaqatun** lā yaṣilu ilayhā shayʾun min maʿānī hādhā l-qurʾān* —
"these hearts are **locked**; nothing of the Quran's meanings reaches them." So the practice
is not a technique that works on any heart — **it presupposes an unlocked one**. This welds
Preparation to the Instrument (ch. 2): the states *unlock the organ*; the practice *reads
through it*. A locked heart can perform the motions of tadabbur and receive nothing — which is
precisely why the states come first. *(Ibn al-Qayyim's four movements of contemplation — from
the ebook — belong here as the practice's structure; verify the locus per dossier D.4.7.)*

## 4. Chapter shape this licenses (for the eventual draft)

1. **The soil, not the checklist** (§0) — states as ground-conditions; the seed is offered to
   all.
2. **The five states** (§2) — Need (the honest starting point) → Gratitude (re-seeing) → Awe
   (going quiet) → Patience (holding steady) → Purity (clearing the channel).
3. **The one axis under all five** (§1) — dhikr; and the default is hardening (57:16 qaswa),
   which is *why* the ground must be tended continuously.
4. **The practice** (§3) — tafakkur / tadabbur / tadhakkur; named as the Quran's purpose
   (38:29); blocked by a locked heart (47:24) — so the states must precede the practice.
5. **Close** — hand off to the practice proper: begin with one verse, one sign, in the
   prepared state. (The ebook's §7-8 already model the voice for this.)

**Voice note:** lead each state with the lived version (the neediness you feel only in a
crisis; the blessing you stopped seeing; the silence that lets something land), then the
verse, then the mechanism. Keep census counts and the *afalā*-formula grammar in endnotes.

## 5. Interlock summary

- **→ Hinge (5):** the whole chapter runs on dhikr (§1); Preparation is the Hinge applied to
  the states.
- **→ Instrument (2):** the states unlock the organ (47:24 aqfāl); the default hardening
  (57:16) is the determinate world's downward entropy that preparation reverses.
- **→ Signs (6):** the practice (tadabbur/tafakkur) is the sign-reading method; awe is the
  silence in which a sign registers (12:105's muʿriḍ is the un-prepared reader).
- **→ Opening (1) & Return (8):** Need (faqr, 35:15) is the state behind the Fatiha's *ihdinā*;
  the states are the soil that makes return (tawba) take root.
