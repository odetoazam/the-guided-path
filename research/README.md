## The thesis (one paragraph)

Guidance is not primarily information or moral instruction. It operates by reshaping the
**determinate world** — the heart's inner configuration of what is enthroned, which filters
all perception. The same sign, verse, or event lands or doesn't depending on that inner
condition. The book makes the *mechanism* of this explicit — in the way Ibn al-Qayyim made
the mechanism of the whisper explicit — woven into accessible narrative, with every step
receipted from the Quran and its classical commentators. **It stands in the tradition's
lineage (Ibn Qayyim, Ghazālī, Izutsu); it claims craft + receipts, never novelty of idea.**

## Cross-cutting findings (the genuine discoveries — these are the book's spine)

These emerged *across* the foundations, verified independently in multiple chapters. They are
the strongest intellectual results of the research:

1. **Dhikr is the master variable of the entire book.** Remembrance is what the whole system
   turns on, verified in chapter after chapter: it keys the soul-at-rest (89:27 = 13:28, same
   root), awe (57:16), heart-softening (39:23); it moves the self *up* the nafs-spectrum
   (ch. 2); it is the axis under all five preparation states (ch. 7); its *absence* is the
   heart's default hardening (57:16 *qaswa*); and it is the hinge on which both machines turn
   (the waswasa doc's dhikr-oscillator, 43:36 ↔ 7:201). **The book is not eight themes — it is
   one mechanism (the determinate world reshaped by remembrance) seen from eight angles.**
2. **The perceiving organ is the heart, not the eye** (22:46, al-Muyassar: blindness is of
   the *baṣīra*). The determinate world = the heart's configuration; qadar is *whose hand
   reshapes it*, the two machines are *how* (toward light / toward darkness), the signs are
   *what it filters*, the Instrument is *the organ itself*.
3. **The book closes its own circle in the Quran's vocabulary.** It opens asking to be guided
   on the path (Fātiḥa, *ihdinā l-ṣirāṭ*) and closes with the return that *culminates in
   guidance* (20:82, *thumma htadā*) and *produces light racing ahead on that same path*
   (66:8). The threads *hidāya + nūr + ṣirāṭ* bind chapter 1 to chapter 8.
4. **"Divine act keyed to a human cause"** is one grammar running through the hardening-verses
   (veiling ← hawā 6:25; ruʿb ← shirk 3:151; swerve ← zaygh 61:5) — the same khalq/kasb
   structure as the qadar page. The machines and the qadar chapter run one grammar.
5. **The determinate-world thesis is stated by the Quran about itself** — the "Same Rain"
   family (2:26, 9:124-125, 17:82, 41:44, 74:31, 22:52-54, 6:122): identical revealed content,
   opposite outcomes, keyed to the receiver. Plus nūr (43×, never plural) vs ẓulumāt (23×,
   never singular): truth is one, error is many.
