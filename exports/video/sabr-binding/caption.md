We translate صَبْر (sabr) as "patience" — as if it means waiting quietly for something to pass.

But the root ص-ب-ر is far more active. In the classical lexicons (Lisān al-ʿArab, al-Mufradāt) it means: to bind. To hold firm. To restrain yourself.

Sabr isn't passive endurance. It's the grip that holds you together when everything is pulling you apart — the force of staying on the path when every part of you wants off it.

Which reframes the promise:

"Indeed, Allah is with the ṣābirīn." (2:153)

Not with the ones who merely wait. With the ones who *hold*.

So if you're white-knuckling your way through something right now — that grip itself is the worship. And you are not gripping alone.
