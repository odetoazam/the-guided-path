# AyahGuide · Rabb Is Not Lord — Design

## Concept
Format 2: Translation Betrayal. The Arabic word **Rabb** (رَبّ) is almost universally translated as "Lord."
But the root r-b-b means: to grow, to nurture, to raise, to develop.
The English "Lord" carries feudal/hierarchical baggage that the Arabic root doesn't.
A more faithful image: the gardener who grows you from seed, tends you at every stage.

## Validation
- Lane's Lexicon (Vol. III, p. 1003): r-b-b — "to bring up, foster, nourish, rear, educate"
- Mu'jam al-Mufradat (Raghib al-Isfahani): "Rabb in its origin signifies the rearing of a thing… nourishing it stage by stage to the point of completion"
- This is classical, not a modern reinterpretation.

## Style — INVERTED PALETTE
This reel breaks the navy default. Cream/parchment background — like an old Arabic manuscript page.
Navy serif text. Gold accents. The look of a scholar's annotation.

## Colors
- `#f5f1e8` — Cream. Background — manuscript parchment.
- `#0f172a` — Navy. Primary text — ink on paper.
- `rgba(201, 168, 76, 0.85)` — Gold. Arabic, dividers, accents.
- `rgba(15, 23, 42, 0.55)` — Navy muted. Secondary text.
- `rgba(201, 168, 76, 0.18)` — Gold dim. Background ornament.

## Typography
- **Amiri** — Arabic word رَبّ. Hero scale.
- **Playfair Display** — Display headings, italic for the takeaway.
- **Inter** — UI labels, the "what English says" line.

## Motion Vocabulary
- Cross-out and replace mechanic — "Lord" gets struck through, "Gardener" rises.
- The Arabic word holds steady — the English meaning shifts beneath it.
- Gentle entrances — sine.out, power2.out. No drama, just clarity.
- Final scene reveals the pivot line.

## What NOT to Do
- No emphatic punctuation.
- No fast/bouncy transitions on the manuscript palette — calm only.
- No criticizing the translation as wrong — it's a *partial* translation. Frame as "more than."
- No Arabic letter splits — keep words intact for legibility on the inverted background.
