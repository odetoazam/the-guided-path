One sentence in the Quran. Two verbs. One is finished. One has no end.

The Arabic of Surah Adh-Dhariyat 51:47 does something most translations quietly smooth over.

بَنَيْنَٰهَا (banaynā) — "We built it." Past tense, completive. Sealed. Done. The architecture of the heavens is finished.

لَمُوسِعُونَ (la-mūsiʿūn) — not a verb. An active participle. Not "We expanded" or even "We will expand." The participle says: this is what He is, right now, without interruption, with no endpoint in sight.

But here is what the translation hides: the object is unspecified in the Arabic. "The sky" is a choice made by translators. The text leaves it open — grammatically, cosmically open. The expansion could be the heavens. It could be provision. It could be you.

The building is complete. The expansion never stops.

There is an architecture that is done, and there is a generosity that is ongoing — and you are somewhere inside both.

وَٱلسَّمَآءَ بَنَيْنَٰهَا بِأَيْي۟دٍ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ
Surah Adh-Dhariyat, 51:47
