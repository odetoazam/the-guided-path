Someone asked the Prophet ﷺ what God is made of. The Quran answered by stripping everything human away.

Not by describing. By dismantling the question.

There are two Arabic words for "one." وَاحِدٌ (wahid) means numerical one — first in a sequence. You can count to it. You can count past it.

أَحَدٌ (ahad) is different. It doesn't sit on the number line at all. It describes something categorically alone — not the first of many, but the only of its kind.

Al-Ikhlas opens with أَحَدٌ. It closes with أَحَدٌ. The same word. The surah forms a ring — the first theological statement and the last word are identical. Not coincidence. Architecture.

The question was about lineage: who came before? who came after? Every verse is a cut:

ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ — no hollow, no need. The word al-Ṣamad closes the mouth at every consonant. It sounds sealed.

لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ — no offspring, no origin. Same root, opposite voices. The family tree has nowhere to attach.

وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ — no peer, no equal. And there it is again: ahad.

They asked an ontological question disguised as a genealogy question. The surah saw through the disguise.

قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
Surah Al-Ikhlas, 112:1-4
