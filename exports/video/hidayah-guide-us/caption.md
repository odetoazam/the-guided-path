You say it in every rakʿah — at least 17 times a day:

ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَاطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ
"Guide us to the straight path." (1:6)

But here's the strange part. You're already Muslim. You're already on the path. The Prophet ﷺ recited this too. So why keep asking for guidance you already have?

Because hidāyah was never a one-time event — a switch that flips from "lost" to "guided" and stays flipped.

*Ihdinā* doesn't mean "make me guided," once and done. It means: **keep guiding me** — this step, and the next, and the next.

Guidance isn't a place you arrive at. It's a path you walk, and every step needs fresh direction. The scholar of fifty years asks for it in the exact same words as the brand-new convert.

That's why the asking never ends — and why this is the one du'a Allah built into every single prayer.
