# Caption — fatiha-grammar-reveals

You say Al-Fatiha 1:7 seventeen times a day. But the grammar is doing something most translations completely hide.

One verse. Three groups. Three different grammatical voices — and each one reveals something about divine character.

**Group One — أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ**
Active voice. 2nd person. YOU blessed them.
Allah is the named actor. He takes direct credit for the good He gives.

**Group Two — ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ**
Passive voice. Wrath was enacted upon them — but no actor is named.
He does not name Himself as the source of punishment. That restraint is intentional. The classical scholars called it divine *adab* — a kind of courtesy even in judgment.

**Group Three — ٱلضَّآلِّينَ**
Active participle. *They* went astray. Not led astray. Not pushed. The form attributes the wandering to the people themselves.

Three grammatical voices. Three different relationships with consequence.

Before you understand the translation, the grammar has already told you everything about how Allah holds blessing, punishment, and human agency differently in His hands.

Say this verse again in your next prayer. You'll hear it differently.
