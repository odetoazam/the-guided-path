The Arabic word for "heart" is قَلْب — qalb.

And it is named for the one thing it always does.

The root ق-ل-ب means: to turn over. To flip. To overturn. (Lisān al-ʿArab, al-Mufradāt.)

The heart is called *qalb* because it never stops turning — shifting, changing, reversing.

And here is the proof, hidden in the Quran's own wording:

"…a Day on which hearts and eyes will turn about." (24:37)

The word for their turning — *tataqallabu* — is built from qalb's own root. The heart was always the organ that turns.

So when yours keeps changing — wavering in faith, in mood, in certainty — that isn't a defect. It's the meaning of its name.

Which is exactly why the duʿā is made to the One who turns the hearts: keep mine firm.

Save this for an unsteady day 🤍
