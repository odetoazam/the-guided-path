We translate شُكْر (shukr) as "gratitude" — and treat it as a quiet feeling. A polite thank-you.

But the root ش-ك-ر is about showing, not just feeling. In the classical lexicons it means: to recognize a favour — and let its effect show on you.

It's the exact opposite of kufr (ك-ف-ر), which *covers* a blessing until you stop seeing it. Shukr uncovers it.

And that reframes the most-quoted promise about gratitude:

لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ
"If you are grateful, I will surely increase you." (14:7)

Notice the order. Gratitude isn't the reaction you have *after* you get more. It's the cause of it. You don't wait to feel grateful — you uncover what's already there, and He multiplies it.

So name three uncovered blessings right now. Out loud. Watch what it does.
