For four verses, Al-Fatiha describes Allah in the third person.

رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ — Lord of the Worlds.
الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ — The Merciful.
مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ — Master of the Day.

You're describing Him. Like reading a nameplate from a distance.

Then at 1:5, the grammar pivots — without warning, without transition.

إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ

Object before verb in Arabic isn't poetry. It's restriction — ḥaṣr. Not "we worship you." *You alone* we worship.

You are no longer talking about Him. You are talking to Him.

This shift — from third person to second person — is called *iltifāt* in Arabic rhetoric. It's one of the most studied moments in all of Quranic grammar. And you recite it seventeen times a day.

Every salah, it happens again. The turn.
