Every translation you've read says: "fear of God."

That isn't what the root says.

The root is و-ق-ي — and the classical lexicons (Lisān al-ʿArab, al-Mufradāt) define it as: to shield, to guard, to place a barrier between yourself and harm.

The command form اتَّقُوا (*ittaqū*) appears throughout the Quran. It was never "feel afraid." It means: **build your shield.**

The early Muslims had an image for it: gathering your robe carefully when walking through thorns. Not frozen. Moving — but with full awareness of what can hurt you.

Then look at 65:3:

وَمَن يَتَّقِ اللَّهَ يَجْعَل لَّهُ مَخْرَجًا

"Whoever *shields himself* with Allah — He opens a way out for him."

The يَتَّقِ in that verse? Same root. Same shield.

Fear paralyzes. Taqwa moves you — carefully, toward Him.
