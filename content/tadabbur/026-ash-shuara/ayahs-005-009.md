# The Turning Away That Always Returns

## INTRODUCTION

### The Ayahs

> [ayah:26:5] وَمَا يَأْتِيهِم مِّن ذِكْرٍ مِّنَ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ مُحْدَثٍ إِلَّا كَانُوا۟ عَنْهُ مُعْرِضِينَ
> [ayah:26:6] فَقَدْ كَذَّبُوا۟ فَسَيَأْتِيهِمْ أَنۢبَـٰٓؤُا۟ مَا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يَسْتَهْزِءُونَ
> [ayah:26:7] أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ كَمْ أَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍ كَرِيمٍ
> [ayah:26:8] إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَةً ۖ وَمَا كَانَ أَكْثَرُهُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ
> [ayah:26:9] وَإِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

*Wa mā ya'tīhim min dhikrin min ar-Raḥmāni muḥdathin illā kānū 'anhu mu'riḍīn. Fa-qad kadhdhabū fa-saya'tīhim anbā'u mā kānū bihi yastahzi'ūn. Awalam yaraw ilā al-arḍi kam anbatnā fīhā min kulli zawjin karīm. Inna fī dhālika la-āyatan, wa mā kāna aktharuhum mu'minīn. Wa inna Rabbaka la-huwa al-'Azīzu ar-Raḥīm.*

"And no fresh reminder comes to them from the Most Merciful except that they turn away from it. They have indeed denied, so the news of what they used to ridicule will come to them. Have they not looked at the earth — how much of every noble pair We have caused to grow in it? Indeed in that is a sign, yet most of them are not believers. And indeed your Lord — He is the Mighty, the Merciful."

### Rebuilding the World

You have to imagine the man who first heard these words.

A Messenger ﷺ who has spent years now — *years* — calling his own people, his own tribe, the children of his uncles and the friends of his youth. He has watched them mock him in the streets. He has watched them throw refuse at his door. He has watched the small group of believers he has gathered tortured in the sand. And the surah opens — just before our ayahs — with one of the most heartbreaking lines in the Quran:

*"Perhaps you would kill yourself in grief that they will not be believers."*

[PAUSE]

The Prophet ﷺ is grieving so intensely that Allah names it. Not "you are sad." Not "you are tired." *You are about to destroy yourself.*

This is the emotional climate. A Messenger crushed by the wall of his people's refusal. A man who genuinely cannot understand how a soul can hear what he is hearing and turn away.

And into that grief, Allah sends these five ayahs.

These are, in their primary grammar, ayahs about the disbelievers — the classical commentators are unanimous on this point. Al-Tabari anchors them plainly in the mushrikeen who deny the Prophet and reject what he has brought them from his Lord. They are the grammatical subject; they are the actual subject. And yet: these ayahs are also spoken *to him*. To the broken-hearted Messenger. Both are simultaneously true — the grammar points outward at *them*, and the address lands inward in *his* chest. The consolation works *through* the description of the disbelievers, not instead of it. By naming their pattern precisely, Allah gives His Messenger something to hold.

These are ayahs of consolation — and of something stranger and deeper than consolation. They are ayahs that reframe rejection itself.

This whole surah, Ash-Shu'arā', will then go on to tell the story of seven prophets — Mūsā, Ibrāhīm, Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ, Shu'ayb — and after each one, the same refrain: *"Indeed in that is a sign, yet most of them are not believers. And indeed your Lord — He is the Mighty, the Merciful."* The exact final line of our passage. Repeated eight times in this surah. A drumbeat. A medicine.

So our ayahs are the first beat of that medicine.

### The Hook

There is something hidden in these five ayahs that, once you see it, you cannot unsee. Allah, in the very moment of describing how His reminder is rejected, *names Himself by the name of mercy*. Twice. He bookends the rejection with mercy. He calls the rejected reminder a reminder *from Ar-Raḥmān*. He closes the passage with *Ar-Raḥīm*.

Why?

Why, when describing those who turn away from Him — would Allah insist on being introduced as the Most Merciful?

What is He showing the Prophet ﷺ — and us — about the relationship between mercy and rejection? About what is actually happening in the soul of someone who turns away? About what the earth itself is saying every spring, every harvest, while we walk over it without looking?

Stay with these questions. The text is going to answer them in a way that will reorganize how you understand every act of denial — your own and others'.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Muḥdath* — The Freshness That Was Refused

Before we open the word, let me ask you something.

Have you ever been given a gift — a real, careful, considered gift — by someone you love, and watched the recipient barely look at it before setting it aside? Watched their eyes glance off it the way eyes glance off a flyer pushed into your hand on the street?

There is a particular pain in that. The pain is not that the gift was rejected — outright rejection at least sees the gift. The pain is that the gift was treated as *not new*. As *already known*. As *not requiring attention*.

Hold that. We're going to come back to it.

Now look at the first ayah:

*"And no fresh reminder comes to them from the Most Merciful — *muḥdath* — except that they turn away from it."*

The word is **مُحْدَث** — *muḥdath*. From the root *ḥ-d-th*, which means: to occur newly, to be brought into being just now, to be fresh.

In Arabic this word doesn't just mean "new" the way English uses "new." It means *just-now-arrived*. *Hot*. *Still steaming*. The word a classical Arab would use for news that broke this morning. For a child just born. For a wound still bleeding.

[PAUSE]

So Allah is not just saying "a reminder comes to them." He is saying: *a reminder comes to them that is fresh — that has not yet been heard before — that is arriving newly in the world, this morning, from Ar-Raḥmān Himself.*

Notice what this does. It dignifies the Quran. Each ayah, when it descended on the Prophet ﷺ, was *muḥdath* — fresh from the source. Not a recycled message. Not a copy. The first soul on earth to hear those words was hearing them as a brand-new event in creation.

Al-Muyassar adds a layer the translation cannot fully hold: the word *muḥdath* here is specifically tied to the *incremental pattern* of revelation — piece by piece, each new descent its own arrival, command after command. Al-Tabari uses the very verbal root of the word to describe the mechanism: what Allah *newly produces* and *reveals* to the Prophet, so that he reminds the people with it. The freshness is not merely novelty to the world; it is an ongoing act of divine origination, renewed at each moment of descent. Wave after fresh wave of address — and wave after wave refused.

And the description Allah gives of those who reject? They treat what is *muḥdath* as if it were *qadīm* — as if it were old. Already-known. Already-dismissed.

Stay with me. This is where the word starts to do its real work.

The classical scholars noted something about the pairing of *muḥdath* with *mu'riḍīn* — turning away. Because to turn away from something you must have already decided you know what it is. *I'rāḍ* — turning away — is not the response of a soul still in question. It is the response of a soul that has *closed the file*.

So the tragedy in this single word: Allah is sending freshness. They are receiving it as staleness. Allah is delivering this morning's message. They are filing it under "already heard."

What this invites us to consider — and here we cross from grammar into the soul — is that the great enemy of guidance is not hostility. It is *familiarity*. The disbeliever who turns away in this ayah is not someone who hasn't heard. He is someone who has heard *just enough* to think he knows what he's hearing.

[PAUSE]

And now — the question that should make every one of us still our breath:

What in our own lives is *muḥdath* that we are treating as old?

When you open the Quran tomorrow morning — is it *muḥdath* in your hands, or have you filed it as something you already know?

When the call to prayer rises five times a day — is it *muḥdath*, fresh from the One who never repeats Himself in His creation, or has it become the background hum of your day?

The very accusation Allah levels at the disbelievers — *they treated the fresh as if it were stale* — is the accusation that hovers over every Muslim who has memorized without being moved. Allah is naming a spiritual disease, not a tribal one.

What this single word reveals: the message is always fresh. The deafness is in the receiver.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Mu'riḍīn* — The Architecture of Turning

Now look at the next word, which is the response to that freshness:

*"...except that they were *mu'riḍīn* — turning away from it."*

The word is **مُعْرِضِينَ** — from the root *'-r-ḍ*. Before we go to its meaning, let me teach you a small thing about Arabic grammar that will open this word.

In Arabic, when you want to say someone *did* something, you use a verb — *kafara*, he disbelieved. When you want to say someone *is* something — when you want to make their action into an *identity* — you use a participle, an *ism fā'il*. *Mu'riḍ*, plural *mu'riḍīn*, is that participle form. It does not say "they turned away." It says: *they were turners-away.*

Notice the difference. "He stole" is an event. "He is a thief" is a character.

Allah uses the participle. He is naming this not as an act, but as a *condition*. They are *those-who-turn-away*. Turning-away has become *who they are*.

Now let me show you what the root means. *'-r-ḍ* in classical Arabic means: the *side* of something. The *width* of a thing. To present one's *side*. The *ʿarḍ* of a body is its breadth, the side that doesn't face you.

So *i'rāḍ* — turning away — is not running. It is not fleeing. It is something subtler and more permanent: it is *presenting your side*. Standing close enough to hear, but with your shoulder toward the speaker. Your face elsewhere.

[PAUSE]

Picture this. You are at a dinner. Someone is telling you something important — something that, if you were to actually hear it, would require a response. And you, without leaving the table, simply turn your face. You do not contradict. You do not argue. You just... orient elsewhere.

That is *i'rāḍ*.

It is more devastating than disagreement, because disagreement at least *engaged*. It is more devastating than walking out, because walking out at least *acknowledged the weight* of what was said. *I'rāḍ* says: *I have heard enough that I do not need to hear more, but not enough that I will leave.*

It is the posture of a soul that wants to remain in proximity to the truth without being touched by it.

Al-Tabari adds a precise detail to this picture. What the mushrikeen abandoned in this turning was not merely the act of listening — it was *the use of thought itself*. The Arabic he uses is pointed: they abandoned the practice of *turning the words over in the mind, of reflecting upon them* — the practice of *tadabbur*, which is the very word for what this kind of encounter with the Quran is meant to be. In al-Tabari's reading, *i'rāḍ* and *tadabbur* are structural opposites. One presents the side to the message; the other turns the mind into it. What the disbelievers refused was not just to hear — they refused to *think about what they had heard*.

And here is what this construction reveals to anyone willing to see it. The Quran is naming a behavior we *all* recognize. The friend who changes the subject when you bring up something serious. The relative who deflects every time death is mentioned. The version of yourself that suddenly remembers a phone notification when an ayah hits too close. *I'rāḍ.* Side-presentation.

Allah is not describing a tribe of monstrous deniers in seventh-century Mecca. He is describing a *posture of the human soul* that every soul knows.

But there is one more layer in this word, and it is the one that turns the whole passage.

The classical lexicographers note that *i'rāḍ* often implies that the person turning away *has already received enough light to be responsible*. You don't *i'rāḍ* from what you don't know exists. You *i'rāḍ* from something you have seen and chosen not to look at again.

[PAUSE]

Which means: every act of turning away is, structurally, an act of *recognition that has been buried*. The turner-away is not innocent. He is not a man who didn't see. He is a man who saw, and turned his side.

What this single word reveals: rejection is rarely a verdict. Most often, it is a posture — a soul that has chosen not to face what it has already seen.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: The Asymmetry That Changes Everything

Now let me show you something that almost everyone reads past.

In ayah 7, Allah pivots. The disbelievers have been described, condemned, warned. And then suddenly:

*"Have they not *looked* at the earth — how much of every *noble pair* We have caused to grow in it?"*

The word for "looked" is *yaraw* — from *ra'ā*, to see. But notice the phrase: *yaraw ilā al-arḍ*. *Looked TO the earth.* The preposition *ilā* — "toward" — is added. In Arabic this preposition shifts the meaning. *Ra'ā* alone means *saw*. *Ra'ā ilā* means *looked deliberately at, gazed toward, directed the eyes upon*.

This is not "did they not see the earth?" This is *"did they not turn their faces toward the earth?"*

[PAUSE]

Stay with me. Do you see what just happened?

In ayah 5, they turned their *side* away from the reminder — *mu'riḍīn*, side-presenters. And in ayah 7, Allah is asking: *did they not turn their face toward the earth?*

The same body. The same posture-language. But with a devastating asymmetry.

The earth is *right there*. Beneath their feet. The signs are not hidden behind a veil — they grow up out of the ground in colors and shapes that no soul can ignore. Allah is essentially saying: *you turned your side to My speech — but did you not even turn your face toward My creation?*

If they had — even just looked *at the earth* the way a child looks at the earth — they would have seen the answer.

Now the word *zawj karīm* — *noble pair*.

**زَوْجٍ كَرِيمٍ**. *Zawj* in Arabic does not just mean "kind" or "type." It means *pair* — that which is partnered. Every plant in the earth, Allah says, is grown in *pairs*. Modern botany would not articulate the dual nature of plants — male and female reproductive structures, paired chromosomes — for many centuries after this ayah. But more than the botanical fact, notice the *theology* in the word.

A *zawj* requires two. A *zawj* is not an isolated thing — it is something whose existence implies its complement. Allah is pointing out that the earth itself is structured to testify. And al-Tabari identifies precisely who is being addressed in this rhetorical question: *those who deny resurrection and the gathering*. The pivot to the earth is not a change of subject — it is a proof aimed at the deepest layer of their rejection. An earth that was *dead*, with no vegetation anywhere in it, and was then made to bring forth noble, paired growth in abundance from that same dead soil — that earth is constructing an argument. The One who raises living things from dead ground can raise souls from dead graves. The sign is not ornamental. It is prosecutorial.
