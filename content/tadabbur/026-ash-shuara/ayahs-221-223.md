# On Whom the Devils Descend
## The Anatomy of a Counterfeit Revelation — Ash-Shu'ara 26:221-223

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The Ayah

> [ayah:26:221] هَلْ أُنَبِّئُكُمْ عَلَىٰ مَن تَنَزَّلُ ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينُ
> [ayah:26:222] تَنَزَّلُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ أَفَّاكٍ أَثِيمٍ
> [ayah:26:223] يُلْقُونَ ٱلسَّمْعَ وَأَكْثَرُهُمْ كَـٰذِبُونَ

*Hal unabbi'ukum 'alā man tanazzalu ash-shayāṭīn?*
*Tanazzalu 'alā kulli affākin athīm.*
*Yulqūna as-sam'a wa aktharuhum kādhibūn.*

**"Shall I inform you upon whom the devils descend?**
**They descend upon every sinful liar.**
**They cast what they hear, and most of them are liars."**

---

### Rebuilding the World

To hear these three ayahs the way the first listener heard them, you have to step into a Mecca where the air itself was thick with claims about unseen voices.

The Quraysh did not live in a disenchanted world. They lived in a world full of soothsayers — *kuhhān* — men and women who would sit in the dark, fall into rhythmic, half-sung speech, and emerge with cryptic predictions. People paid them. People consulted them before marriages, before raids, before journeys. Everyone in Mecca knew at least one. Everyone had heard the rhymed prose, the *saj'*, that came out of their mouths.

And the *kuhhān* themselves had a story about where their words came from. They claimed that jinn — invisible spirits — would eavesdrop at the edges of the heavens, snatch fragments of conversation among the angels, and pass those fragments down to their human partners. The soothsayer's words were said to be jinn-words: parts true, parts mangled, parts invented to fill the gaps.

Now imagine a man stands in this same Mecca and recites speech of a different order. Speech that does not chain into the clipped, forced cadences of the *kuhhān*'s *saj'* — and yet arrests the ear more completely than any soothsayer's verse. Speech that does not predict the next raid but cuts open the human heart. Speech that is consistent across years, that does not mangle, that does not contradict itself.

[PAUSE]

The Quraysh have a problem. They have to explain him. And they reach for the only category they have. They say: *He is a kāhin. The jinn whisper to him. The shayāṭīn descend upon him.*

This is the accusation Surah Ash-Shu'ara has been circling for two hundred ayahs. And now, at the very end, Allah does something audacious. He does not deny that devils descend. He does not say "no such thing happens." He says: *yes — devils descend. Let me tell you exactly upon whom.*

### The Hook

What is about to happen in three short ayahs is a perfect inversion. The Quraysh point at the Prophet ﷺ and say: *the devils descend on him.* Allah responds: *do you want to know upon whom devils actually descend? Look in the mirror.*

But the inversion is not just rhetorical. Buried inside these ayahs is an entire theory of how counterfeit revelation works — the precise psychological and moral profile of a vessel that devils can occupy. And once you see that profile clearly, you understand something terrifying about your own heart.

[PAUSE]

Stay with me. We are going to spend time in three places: the strange double-form of the verb *tanazzalu*, the asymmetry between *affāk* and *athīm*, and the eavesdropping image in *yulqūna as-sam'a*. Each one opens the same insight from a different angle.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Tanazzalu* — The Verb of Stages

Let's start with the very first verb. *Tanazzalu*. "They descend."

Before we look at it in the ayah, let's teach the concept.

In Arabic, verbs have forms — patterns the root is poured into. The simple form is Form I. *Nazala* — he descended. One motion, one act. He came down.

But there is another form, Form V — *tafa''ala* — and one of its core functions is to express **gradualness, repetition, or descent in stages**. *Tanazzala* is the Form V of the same root. It does not mean "descended" in one drop. It means **descended bit by bit, in installments, in successive waves**.

Think of the difference in English between "he fell" and "he was falling, falling, falling." One is an event. The other is a process you can watch unfold.

[PAUSE]

Now look at our ayah:

> هَلْ أُنَبِّئُكُمْ عَلَىٰ مَن **تَنَزَّلُ** ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينُ
> **تَنَزَّلُ** عَلَىٰ كُلِّ أَفَّاكٍ أَثِيمٍ

Allah uses *tanazzalu* — the gradual, drip-by-drip form — not once but **twice in two consecutive ayahs**. He could have used *tanzilu* (Form I, the simple descent). He didn't.

Notice something. This same root, in this same surah, just a few ayahs earlier, was used for the descent of the Qur'an itself:

> وَإِنَّهُۥ لَتَنزِيلُ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ
> "And indeed, it is the *tanzīl* of the Lord of the Worlds." (26:192)

*Tanzīl*. The verbal noun of Form II — *nazzala* — which also carries the meaning of bringing down **in stages, in measured portions**. The Qur'an itself, we are reminded throughout the Book, was sent down piece by piece over twenty-three years.

[PAUSE]

So here is the discomfort. Allah uses a "gradual descent" form for the Qur'an. And He uses a "gradual descent" form for the devils' whispers to liars.

Why would He do that?

Stay with me. Because this is where the mirror starts to gleam.

The Quraysh are saying: *what comes to Muhammad and what comes to our soothsayers is the same kind of thing.* Allah's response is to acknowledge a structural similarity — yes, both are forms of *tanazzul*, gradual coming-down — and then to draw a line down the middle of that similarity that the Quraysh cannot cross.

Both are gradual. Both come in waves. **The form of the channel looks similar. The source could not be more different.**

This is a stunningly honest move. Allah does not pretend the surface phenomena are unrelated. He concedes the surface and then exposes the depth. *Of course there are voices that descend onto human beings in stages. The question is not whether the descent happens. The question is upon whom — and from where.*

[PAUSE]

What this single verb-form reveals: **Allah is not afraid of the comparison the disbelievers are making. He invites it. Because the moment you actually compare carefully, the categories collapse — and the only thing left standing is the truth of who is receiving what.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Two: *Affāk* and *Athīm* — The Two-Word Anatomy of the Vessel

Now look at the second ayah.

> تَنَزَّلُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ أَفَّاكٍ أَثِيمٍ

"They descend upon every *affāk* — *athīm*."

Two words. Allah is going to give us the entire profile of who can host a devil's transmission in two words. Watch how surgical this is.

#### *Affāk*

The root is *ʾ-f-k*. *Ifk* in Arabic is not just "lie." *Ifk* is the kind of falsehood that **inverts reality** — that takes something and turns it on its head. It comes from a root meaning to overturn, to flip. The word is used elsewhere in the Qur'an for the great cities of Lūṭ that were "overturned" — *al-mu'tafikāt*. Same root. The cities were physically inverted. *Ifk* is verbal inversion.

So *kadhib* (lying) is to say what is not true. But *ifk* is something more specific — it is to **take what is upright and present it upside-down**. To take the true and call it false, to take the false and call it true.

Now the morphology. *Affāk* is on the pattern *fa''āl* — the **intensive form**. In Arabic, *fa''āl* names a person whose entire identity is saturated by the action. *Khabbāz* is not someone who once baked bread; it is *the baker*, the one whose life is bread. *Kadhdhāb* is not someone who lied once; it is *the liar*, the one for whom lying is a way of being.

Al-Ṭabarī's transmitters glossed *affāk* as *kadhdhāb bahāt* — a brazen, shameless fabricator, one who lies openly and without flinching. The *bahāt* element is significant: it names someone whose inversions have become entirely unembarrassed, who no longer experiences the internal resistance that once slowed the lie. So *affāk* is **the inverter** — a human being whose habitual orientation toward reality is to turn it upside-down. The default move of his cognition is reversal. He sees the upright and instinctively flips it.

Stay with that. Allah is not describing someone who occasionally lies. He is describing a person whose **interior architecture** is inversion.

#### *Athīm*

The second word. Root *ʾ-th-m*. *Ithm* is sin — but classical lexicographers note something specific about it: *ithm* tends to denote **sin that the doer knows is sin**. It is not the slip, not the inadvertent wrong. It is the sin you commit while seeing it for what it is. The root carries a sense of **slowness** — sin that holds you back, weighs you down, drags at the soul.

*Athīm* is also intensive — *fa'īl* form, expressing settled quality. So this is **someone steeped in conscious wrongdoing**. Not the person who stumbled. The person who knows and persists.

#### Now Watch What Allah Has Built

Allah has given us **two words working together** that map two different dimensions of the human interior:

- *Affāk* — the **cognitive** dimension. How a person handles truth. Whether his mind tends to uphold reality or to invert it.
- *Athīm* — the **moral** dimension. How a person handles right and wrong. Whether his will tends toward conscious obedience or conscious sin.

[PAUSE]

Notice something remarkable. **Both words are intensive.** Allah did not say "every liar and sinner." He said *every habitual-inverter, steeped-in-sin*. Both at maximum.

Why?

Because a vessel for devils is not someone who lies sometimes or sins occasionally. **A devil cannot reliably descend on a person of mixed orientation.** He needs a vessel whose default is reversed and whose conscience is dulled. He needs a *system* that will receive the broken signal and pass it on without correction.

A truthful person, even if he sins, has an internal voice that pushes back. *That doesn't sound right.* A person of clear conscience, even if he occasionally errs in cognition, has a heart that recoils from carrying falsehood to others. The devil cannot use those people. They are bad transmitters. They will garble the corruption into something accidentally good.

But the *affāk athīm* — the habitual inverter, steeped in sin — has both gates open. Cognition is already flipped. Conscience is already deadened. **The devil's whisper enters a system that will faithfully reproduce the distortion.**

[PAUSE]

The classical transmitters were specific about who first inhabits this profile. Qatadah — through a chain al-Ṭabarī preserves — named them plainly: *hum al-kuhhān* — "they are the soothsayers." He continued: "the jinn steal the hearing, then come to their *awliyāʾ* among mankind." That word — *awliyāʾ*, allies, intimate guardians — is deliberate. The kāhin and his jinn do not have a master-and-servant relationship. They have a bond of mutual loyalty. Each has chosen the other. The *affāk athīm* is not a passive victim of demonic interference; he is the active participant in a relationship he has cultivated through his own habitual inversion and his own settled sin.

But the scope does not stop with the professional soothsayer class. Classical commentators explicitly extended the profile to *man jara majrahum min al-fasaqa* — those who follow the same path among the dissolute generally. The *affāk athīm* is not a technical credential belonging only to the *kāhin*. It is a spiritual configuration that can be inhabited by anyone whose moral and cognitive life follows this pattern.

[PAUSE]

Here is the image that makes this concrete. A radio cannot broadcast what it cannot receive. The frequency must match.

The devil broadcasts on the frequency of inverted truth and conscious wrongdoing. To pick up that signal, the receiver must be tuned to that frequency. That tuning is what *affāk* and *athīm* describe. **Sin is not just sin. Sin is antenna-shaping.** It tunes you. And what you become tuned to begins to find you.

This is why these two words come together. Cognitive inversion alone might be a confused person — but their conscience could still flag the whisper. Moral sin alone might be a tormented person — but their love of truth could still reject the whisper. Only when **both gates are reversed at the level of habit** does the devil have a stable transmission line.

[PAUSE]

What this two-word combination reveals: **The Qur'an is not just describing soothsayers. It is describing the precise spiritual physics of how a human being becomes hospitable to satanic influence — and the conditions are not exotic. They are everyday. They are about what we do with truth, day in and day out, and what we do with sin, day in and day out.**

### Element Three: *Yulqūna as-Sam'a* — The Eavesdropping Image

The third ayah:

> يُلْقُونَ ٱلسَّمْعَ وَأَكْثَرُهُمْ كَـٰذِبُونَ
> "They cast hearing — and most of them are liars."

This is one of the strangest constructions in the Qur'an, and we need to slow down to see it.

Let's teach the structure first. The verb *yulqūna* comes from the root *l-q-y*, meaning to throw, to cast, to project something outward. Form IV — *alqā* — is "to throw" or "to cast down."

In normal Arabic, you say "he listened" with *istama'a* — a verb of receiving sound. But here Allah does not say *yastami'ūna*. He says *yulqūna as-sam'a* — literally **"they cast the hearing."** They throw their listening outward.

This is unusual. You don't throw hearing. Hearing is normally something you **receive**, not something you **project**.

What is going on?

The classical commentators read this in concert with the descriptions of devils elsewhere in the Qur'an, particularly in Surah al-Ḥijr (15:17-18) and aṣ-Ṣāffāt (37:7-10), where we are told that devils try to *eavesdrop* on the high assembly of the angels — and are pelted with shooting stars when they get too close.

Classical commentary captures the mechanics with precision: the devils *yataḵhaṭṭafūnahu min al-malā' al-a'lā* — they **snatch** fragments from the High Assembly, from the celestial council where the divine decree is deliberated. The verb *yataḵhaṭṭafūna* — to snatch, to seize in a hurried grab — intensifies the image of desperate, furtive theft. This is not dignified reception. This is scrambling at the edges of something they are not permitted to approach, reaching for whatever can be seized before they are driven back.

The image is this. The devils throw their hearing **upward** — they cast it like a fishing line — trying to catch fragments of the angelic council. Most of what they reach for, they cannot grasp. They are pelted, driven back, and what they manage to snatch is usually nothing or a fragment.

[PAUSE]

Now stay with this picture. Because Allah adds a clause that we cannot afford to miss:

**وَأَكْثَرُهُمْ كَـٰذِبُونَ — "and most of them are liars."**

Let's sit with this. Allah does not say *kulluhum kādhibūn* — "all of them are liars." He says *aktharuhum* — **"most of them are liars."**

Why most? Why not all?

Here is the precision. The classical reading — preserved by scholars like al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī — is this. **The devils sometimes catch a true fragment.** They eavesdrop on something real before they are driven away. Then they descend to their human vessel — the *affāk athīm* — carrying that one true fragment. And the human vessel mixes it with invention — with padding, with the atmospheric embellishments of a mind already tuned to falsehood — and presents the whole package as oracular knowledge.

Classical commentators were remarkably precise about the ratio. The mechanism, as recorded in the tradition: *yaṣduqu aḥaduhum fī kalima, fa-yazīdu fīhā akthar min mi'at kadhiba* — **one of them speaks a true word, then adds more than a hundred lies to it.** One true fragment, snatched before the meteor strikes. A hundred fabrications packed around it — because that is what the vessel's nature demands. The *affāk* does not neutrally relay what he received; his entire architecture of cognition is inversion, and the signal will be distorted before it ever reaches his audience.

[PAUSE]

This is the precision behind *aktharuhum kādhibūn*. The "most" is not statistical imprecision. It is a structural description of the soothsayer's product: **the truth is the one-in-a-hundred that gives the whole enterprise its credibility; the lies are the ninety-nine that constitute its substance.** The single true prediction is the bait. The hundred fabrications are the architecture built around the bait to make the bait feel like proof.

And notice what this means. The danger of the soothsayer — the reason people believed him, the reason the Quraysh had a whole culture built around this institution — is precisely because he was not always wrong. He was mostly wrong. **Mostly wrong, but not entirely.** And that fraction of truth is almost more dangerous than pure fiction, because it gives the listener no simple way to dismiss the whole.

[PAUSE]

Now look at what Allah is saying about the Prophet ﷺ, implicitly, in this structure.

The devils descend on liars who mix true fragments with a hundred inventions. The Qur'an, by contrast, came down across twenty-three years of consistent truth — with no failed oracle, no retraction, no single revision. Not one hundred lies per true word. Not one lie. The *kāhin*'s product is detectable by its ratio: mostly false, occasionally true. The Qur'an's product is detectable by its ratio: entirely true, entirely consistent, no correction needed.

**The very accusation the Quraysh leveled is the comparison that disproves their case.** The moment you look at what soothsayer-reception actually produces — one truth wrapped in a hundred lies, a relationship of mutual alliance with jinn, a vessel defined by habitual inversion and settled sin — the comparison they intended as an insult collapses entirely in their hands.

## SYNTHESIS: The Mirror Completed

Step back and look at what these three ayahs have accomplished together.

The Quraysh came with an accusation: *Muhammad receives from devils, just like our soothsayers.* It was meant to explain away the revelation — to domesticate the extraordinary as something familiar, something known, something they could dismiss.

Allah did not answer defensively. He opened the question wider. *You want to know who receives from devils? Let me give you the full anatomy.*

What He gave them is a portrait that fits themselves.

The *affāk athīm* is the habitual inverter steeped in conscious sin. The kuhhān and their patrons — the very people making the accusation — were practitioners of *ifk* by profession. Their entire commercial enterprise was built on the mixture of one true word and a hundred lies. Their livelihood depended on delivering the distortion with enough accuracy to maintain belief. And their spiritual lives — built around idols, around expedient denial of what they knew — were the portrait of *ithm*: conscious, habitual, deeply settled.

The Quraysh pointed at the Prophet ﷺ. But the profile they described, when laid out precisely, fits the hands holding the finger.

[PAUSE]

This is the divine address at its most precise — giving the mirror without naming who should stand before it. Allah does not say "you are the *affāk athīm*." He says: *shall I tell you who receives from devils?* — and gives a description. He leaves the application to the listener. He lets the mirror do the work.

And the mirror works because Allah's description is not a caricature. It is precise enough that anyone who inhabits the profile will recognize themselves — if their self-perception has not yet been fully inverted.

[PAUSE]

There is one more thing to see. These three ayahs are the closing movement of Surah Ash-Shu'ara. The surah spent more than two hundred ayahs walking through the stories of Mūsā, Ibrāhīm, Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ, and Shu'ayb — messenger after messenger, rejection after rejection. Every story ends the same way: the people who refused the messenger perished. The people who received the message survived.

And now, at the very end, Allah zooms in on the mechanism of rejection. It is not just stubbornness. It is not just tribal pride. At the root of the refusal is a spiritual profile: the habitual inverter who has cultivated his sin so long that his own cognition has reversed. Such a person does not merely fail to receive the truth. He actively reconstructs it as threat, as conspiracy, as sorcery. He cannot see the mirror because the mirror itself looks to him like a weapon.

The surah that opened with rejection closes with the diagnosis of why rejection is possible at all.

## CLOSING: The Question for Your Own Heart

We end where we began. The Quraysh pointed outward. Allah turned the question inward.

The three ayahs are not only about soothsayers in ancient Mecca. They are an anatomy of a spiritual condition that can grow anywhere, in anyone, through the accumulated small choices of what we do with truth and what we do with sin.

Every time we choose to invert — to take a truth we find inconvenient and find a reason to call it false, to take a falsehood we find comfortable and find a reason to call it true — the *affāk* inside us grows a little more settled. Every time we sin while knowing it is sin, and then return to it, and then return again — the *athīm* inside us grows a little more established. The architecture does not appear suddenly. It is built in installments.

*Tanazzalu.* The descent happens in stages. The corruption, too, comes in stages.

[PAUSE]

The question these ayahs leave us with is not "are you like the soothsayer?" The question is: **which direction is your cognition drifting?** When you encounter truth that costs you something — do you hold it upright, or do you feel the pull to flip it? When you sin knowingly — do you stop and return, or do you build a reason why this particular sin is different?

The *affāk athīm* did not wake up one morning as a professional inversion-artist. He arrived there through countless small decisions about truth and about sin. And he arrived there so gradually that by the time the devil found him, the devil found a home that had been under construction for years.

[PAUSE]

Allah ends the surah here, in this place, with this question. Not because the soothsayers of Mecca are our primary concern. But because the soothsayer's condition — the interior architecture of inverted cognition and settled sin — is a warning about the slow, quiet work of the heart's misdirection.

The antidote is the same thing these ayahs define by contrast: a mind that upholds reality as it is, and a will that turns from sin before it settles in. Not perfection. Not the absence of struggle. But the *direction* — tilting toward truth, turning from sin when it is seen for what it is.

That is the vessel the Qur'an wants to find when it descends.
