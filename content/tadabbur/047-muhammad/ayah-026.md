# We Will Obey You in Part
### The Whispered Compromise and the God Who Hears It

---

## INTRODUCTION: The Sentence Spoken Behind a Closed Door

Let me begin by placing the words in front of you. First in Arabic:

> [ayah:47:26] ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ قَالُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ كَرِهُوا۟ مَا نَزَّلَ ٱللَّهُ سَنُطِيعُكُمْ فِى بَعْضِ ٱلْأَمْرِ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِسْرَارَهُمْ

Now in transliteration, slowly:

> *Dhālika bi-annahum qālū lilladhīna karihū mā nazzala-llāhu sa-nuṭīʿukum fī baʿḍi-l-amr — wa-llāhu yaʿlamu isrārahum.*

And now in plain English:

> *That is because they said to those who hated what Allah sent down: "We will obey you in part of the matter." But Allah knows their secrets.*

I want you to notice something before we go anywhere near the grammar. **This ayah is built around a quotation.** Allah is not describing these people in the abstract. He is repeating a sentence they said. And not a sentence they said from a pulpit, or in a public square, or to the Prophet's face.

A sentence they said *quietly*. In private. To the wrong people. Behind a door they believed was closed.

[PAUSE]

### Rebuild the world

To feel the weight of this, you have to stand inside Madinah in those years.

The Muslim community is young, embattled, surrounded. There is an outer ring of declared enemies — those who openly hated what was being revealed. And there is an inner ring, far more dangerous, of people who walked into the masjid, prayed in the rows, called themselves believers — and then went home and made arrangements. People who kept a back channel open. Who told the enemies of the revelation, in lowered voices, *don't worry — when it counts, we're with you. We'll cooperate. Not in everything. Just... in some things.*

This is not a story about armies. It is a story about a **sentence**. A specific, devastating sentence that someone whispered, believing it would never be heard beyond the room.

The surah before this ayah has already told us the symptom: people who *turned back on their heels* after the guidance had become clear to them (47:25). They had it. They saw it. And they reversed. The natural human question is: *why? What broke?*

This ayah is the answer. The reversal in verse 25 did not come from nowhere. It came from a deal struck in verse 26. A private accommodation. A hedge.

### The Hook

So here is what I want you to hold as we begin.

There is a sentence in this ayah that, on its surface, sounds almost *moderate*. Almost reasonable. It does not say "we reject Allah." It does not say "we will obey you in everything." It carves out a careful, limited, partial, sensible-sounding commitment. **"We will obey you in part of the matter."**

And the Quran treats this carefully-hedged, partial, reasonable-sounding sentence as the very mechanism of spiritual ruin.

Why? Why is the *partial* version the dangerous one? Why does the word "part" do so much damage? And what does it mean that the ayah ends not with a threat, not with a punishment — but with a single, quiet, terrifying statement: **wa-llāhu yaʿlamu isrārahum** — *but Allah knows their secrets*?

Let's find out together.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We have three doors to walk through. The first is a word about what they *rejected*. The second is the word that holds the entire poison of the verse — the word "part." And the third is the word that closes the door on the whole game: *secrets*.

---

### Element One: *Nazzala* — What Exactly Did They Hate?

Let me teach you something about Arabic verbs before we open the word.

In Arabic, you can take a simple root and "build it up" into more intense forms. Think of it like the difference, in English, between "to drop" and "to keep dropping," or between "to send" and "to send down piece by piece, deliberately, over time." Same idea — but more force, more deliberateness, more *process*.

The root here is **ن-ز-ل (n-z-l)** — the root of *descending, coming down, being sent down*. The Quran uses this root constantly for revelation. But there are two related forms scholars of the language distinguish: *anzala* (the simpler send-down) and *nazzala* — which is the form right here in our ayah: **مَا نَزَّلَ ٱللَّهُ** — *what Allah nazzala'd*.

This is the intensified, doubled form — what grammarians call Form II. And the classical understanding is that this form carries the sense of **deliberate, sustained, piece-by-piece sending down**. Not a single object dropped from a height. A revelation that *kept coming*, verse after verse, situation after situation, year after year — Allah responding to His community, guiding them through every turn.

So hold the picture. What did these people "hate"?

The word is **كَرِهُوا** — *karihū*, from the root *k-r-h*, which is not mild dislike. It is **deep aversion, repugnance, the recoil of something the self finds hateful.** This is a word the Quran also uses for the things the soul violently resists.

Now put the two together. They felt deep, recoiling aversion toward **the thing Allah was patiently, continuously sending down to guide them.**

[PAUSE]

Sit with the cruelty of that combination. On one side: a Lord sending down guidance, gently, in installments, calibrated to a struggling people's needs — *nazzala*. On the other side: hearts that meet this patient mercy with *karh*, with recoil, with hatred.

And here is the part that should unsettle every one of us. **The verse does not say the hypocrites hated the revelation.** Read it again. The hypocrites are the ones who said the sentence. *To whom* did they say it? **لِلَّذِينَ كَرِهُوا مَا نَزَّلَ ٱللَّهُ** — *to those who hated what Allah sent down.*

So the haters and the speakers are two different groups. The classical commentators — al-Qurṭubī, al-Baghawī, and others — identify the speakers as the hypocrites (and in some narrations, certain factions), and the ones who *hated* the revelation as the open opponents, the mushrikīn.

So what is the hypocrite's actual crime here? **It is not that he hated the revelation. It is that he went and made a private arrangement with those who did.** He himself might still pray. Still recite. Still call himself a believer. His sin is not open hatred — it is a back-channel loyalty to the people of hatred.

What this single word *karihū* reveals: **the most dangerous person in the community is often not the one who hates the truth — it is the one who keeps the truth's haters as his secret friends.**

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Baʿḍ al-Amr* — The Smallest Word That Destroys Everything

Now we come to the heart. To the word the entire ayah pivots on.

Let me teach the concept first, with something ordinary.

Imagine someone says to you: "I will always tell you the truth." Now imagine they say instead: "I will tell you the truth — *in most things*." Has the second sentence reassured you, or has it just terrified you? Because the moment you carve out an exception to honesty, you have not given 90% honesty. You have announced that *there is a category of things about which you cannot trust me at all* — and you have refused to tell me which category. The word "most" did not protect the promise. **It dissolved it.**

There are certain commitments where "partial" is not a smaller version of "total." It is the *opposite* of total. Loyalty. Faithfulness. Trust. Obedience to God. These are not dials you can set to 70%. The moment they become partial, they become something else entirely.

Now look at what the hypocrites said:

> **سَنُطِيعُكُمْ فِى بَعْضِ ٱلْأَمْرِ**
> *"We will obey you — in part of the matter."*

The verb **سَنُطِيعُكُمْ** (*sa-nuṭīʿukum*) is from the root *ṭ-w-ʿ*, the root of obedience, submission, compliance — the very same root used throughout the Quran for obeying *Allah and His Messenger*. And notice the little prefix *sa-*: it is the prefix of the **near future, the firm intention**. Not "we might." *"We will. Soon. Count on it."* It is a pledge.

A pledge of *obedience* — the word that belongs to God — redirected toward the enemies of God's revelation.

But then comes the hedge. **فِى بَعْضِ ٱلْأَمْرِ** — *in part of the matter.*

**بَعْض** (*baʿḍ*) means "some, a part, a portion." And **ٱلْأَمْر** (*al-amr*), from the root *a-m-r*, means "the matter, the affair, the command." Notice it is left **completely unspecified.** *Which* part? *Which* matters? They don't say. They will obey *in some things*.

Now here is the question I want you to truly stop on. Why did they say *"in part"*? Why not "in everything"?

[PAUSE]

I think there are two layers, and they sit on top of each other.

The **first layer** is the obvious one: cowardice dressed as caution. They are hedging. They want to keep a foot in both camps. To the believers they can say "we're with you." To the enemies they whisper "we're with you too — in some things." The word *baʿḍ*, "part," is the exact size of the gap they are trying to keep open. It is the architecture of a divided heart made into a single word.

But there is a **second layer**, and this is what al-Muyassar and the classical readings point us toward. *Sanuṭīʿukum fī baʿḍi-l-amr* — we will obey you in *some* of the matter — meaning *specifically in the matters that go against what Allah and His Messenger command.* In sabotaging the Prophet ﷺ, in dampening people's resolve, in sitting out the struggle. They didn't need to obey the enemy in *everything*. They only needed to obey them in the *one part* that mattered: the part where it counted against the truth.

And here is the devastating logic the verse exposes: **you do not need to betray someone in everything to have betrayed them completely.** You only need one reserved category. One door left open. One "but, in this particular matter..." The traitor never says "I am wholly against you." He says "I am with you — *except in part*." And that exception is the entire betrayal.

[PAUSE]

Do you feel how the word *baʿḍ* — "part" — is the most dangerous word in the verse? Not "all." *Part.* Because "all" would be honest. "All" would be open enmity, and open enmity can be seen and answered. But *part* is hidden. *Part* keeps the mask on. *Part* lets you sit in the prayer row on Friday and undermine the very same prayer on Saturday.

What this single word reveals: **the deadliest disloyalty is never total. It is partial, reserved, and unspecified — a small carved-out exception that quietly contains everything.**

### Element Three: *Isrārahum* — The Word That Closes Every Door

And now the verse turns. After the whispered deal, after the careful hedge, comes the response — not from the believers, not from the Prophet ﷺ, but from the One who was never supposed to be in the room.

> **وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِسْرَارَهُمْ**
> *But Allah knows their secrets.*

Let me first teach you the root, because it is doing something beautiful.

The root is **س-ر-ر (s-r-r)**. It is the root of *sirr* — a secret. The hidden, private, concealed thing. The word for the innermost chamber of a thing. And from this same root, fascinatingly, comes *surūr* — joy — because the deepest joy is something private, held in the secret interior of the heart. The root means: *that which is kept in the most hidden place.*

The word in our verse is **إِسْرَار** (*isrār*) — and here I want to be precise, because the classical reciters themselves preserved two readings, and both are documented in al-Jalālayn, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Baghawī:

- Read with a *fatḥa* — **أَسْرارَهُم** (*asrārahum*) — it is the **plural of *sirr***: *their secrets*, their many hidden things.
- Read with a *kasra* — **إِسْرارَهُم** (*isrārahum*) — it is a **verbal noun (maṣdar)** from the Form IV verb *asarra*, *to keep secret*: *their secret-keeping*, the very *act and process* of their concealing.

Notice the commentators do not force you to choose. This is what we call a *composite* — two camera angles on one reality. One reading says: *Allah knows the secrets themselves — every hidden item.* The other says: *Allah knows the secreting — the entire activity of hiding, the effort, the technique, the very intention to conceal.*

Put them together and the meaning becomes total. **Allah knows both the contents of the secret AND the act of keeping it secret.** He knows *what* you hid and He knows *that* you were hiding and He knows *the labor of the hiding itself.*

[PAUSE]

Now feel the architecture of the whole verse snap into place.

These people lowered their voices for a reason. The entire strategy of *nifaq*, of hypocrisy, depends on a single assumption: **that there is a private room.** That there exists a space — a whispered conversation, a sealed alliance, an unspoken intention — that is sealed off from God. The hypocrite is, at his core, a person who believes in a chamber Allah cannot enter.

The verse demolishes that belief in five words. *Wa-llāhu yaʿlamu isrārahum.*

There is no private room.

The whisper was never private. The deal was witnessed. The closed door was never closed. The very secrecy they were so proud of — the cleverness of keeping it quiet — *that itself* is the thing Allah names and knows. He didn't just overhear. He knew the contents, He knew the effort, He knew the intention before the words formed.

And notice the sound of the word. **Isrār.** Say it. The repeated *s* — *isssrār* — it hisses. It *sounds* like a whisper. The very phonetics of the word imitate the act of secret conversation, the *sss* of voices lowered behind a hand. And then it is placed directly beside the most expansive word in the language: **wa-llāhu** — *but Allah* — and the verb **yaʿlam** — *He knows*. The tiny hiss of human secrecy, set against the boundless knowing of God.

What this word reveals: **the hypocrite's entire enterprise rests on a room that does not exist. The secret was the loudest thing in the verse.**

### Bridge: From Words to Reality

So look at what these three words have built.

*Nazzala* — Allah is patiently, continuously sending down guidance.
*Baʿḍ* — and these people respond by reserving "a part" of themselves for its enemies.
*Isrār* — believing this reservation is hidden.

The grammar has shown us a complete anatomy: patient divine mercy, met by partial human loyalty, concealed in an imagined private room. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about the hypocrites of Madinah — but about the human heart. About *our* hearts. Because the disease this verse names did not die in the seventh century.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The Tyranny of "In Part"

Let me start where you actually live.

You have never said "we will obey you in part of the matter" to the enemies of revelation. Of course not. So let me make this verse find you somewhere closer.

You have made a small compromise. Everyone has. The "just this once." The "in most things I'm a practicing Muslim, just not in *this one area*." The "I'll be honest in everything except this one relationship." The "I follow the deen — except where it touches my money." The carved-out exception. The reserved category. The one door you keep open "in part."

Here is what we tell ourselves about that door: *it's small.* It's *only a part.* The rest of me is committed. Ninety percent of my life is in order. Surely a small reserved exception is... a rounding error. Surely God grades on the whole.

This verse walks directly into that comforting math and overturns the table.

Because the hypocrites of Madinah would have made *exactly* that argument. "We're not against the Prophet — we pray behind him! We only hedged *in part of the matter*. We kept *one* channel open. Ninety percent loyal." And Allah did not record them as ninety percent loyal. He recorded the sentence — *sa-nuṭīʿukum fī baʿḍi-l-amr* — as the definition of who they were.

[PAUSE]

Why? Here is the thing the verse is teaching, and it is hard.

**The reserved exception is not a small region of your loyalty. It is the location of your true loyalty.** Think about it honestly. The 90% you give easily — what does it cost you? The places where obedience is *convenient* prove nothing, because in those places, your desire and God's command happen to point the same way. You are not actually choosing God there. You are choosing what you wanted anyway, and God happens to agree.

It is the *one reserved part* that tells the truth. It is precisely the matter where God's command and your desire diverge — *that* is the only place your loyalty is ever actually tested. And that is the exact part the hypocrite reserves. He gives God every battlefield where there was no fight, and surrenders the only one where the battle was real.

So when you say "I follow everything except this one thing" — the verse whispers back: *that one thing is the only thing the test was ever about.* The exception is not the footnote to your faith. It is the headline. It is the *baʿḍ* — the part — that contains the whole.

[PAUSE]

And notice what makes this so insidious: the partial commitment *feels more honest* than total commitment. The person who says "I'll obey God in everything" sounds naive, absolutist, extreme. The person who says "I'll obey in most things, I'm reasonable, I have my exceptions" sounds mature, balanced, human. **The hedge wears the costume of wisdom.** That is exactly why it is dangerous. The poison comes labeled as moderation.

**So what does this change about tomorrow?** Find your *baʿḍ*. Don't audit the 90% — that's the part designed to reassure you. Find the one reserved category. The door you keep "just slightly" open. The single matter where you've quietly told yourself "I follow Allah — except here." That door is not your exception. It is your address. Name it today. Because that is the one room where your faith is actually being decided.

### Theme Two: The Room That Does Not Exist

Now the second theme. And this one is gentler, and in some ways more frightening, because it removes the one thing we all secretly rely on.

We are people who live double lives — all of us, to some degree. There is the self we perform and the self we hide. There is what we post and what we delete. There is the message we send and the draft we never send. There is the version of us in the room and the version in our own head at 3 a.m. We have built our entire psychological survival around one assumption: *there is a backstage. There is a private self that no one sees.*

And in the age you live in, this is more true than ever — and more false than ever. You believe your DMs are private. Your search history is private. The conversation in the car is private. The thing you said about your friend, to your other friend, is private. We curate a public face precisely *because* we believe the private one is sealed.

The hypocrites of Madinah believed exactly this. Their entire strategy depended on it. The whole genius of *nifaq* is the assumption that there are two rooms — the public room where you perform belief, and the private room where you keep your real arrangements. Nifaq is, at its root, **a theology of the unwatched room.**

And then: *wa-llāhu yaʿlamu isrārahum.*

[PAUSE]

There is no unwatched room.

I want you to feel this not as a threat first, but as a fact. The most private thing you have ever thought — the secret you would die rather than have read aloud — was, in the moment you thought it, **fully known.** Not deduced. Not suspected. *Known.* With more intimacy than you know it yourself. The verse says Allah knows the *isrār* — both the secret and the secreting, both the content and the very act of hiding it.

Now — here is where it can go two completely different directions, and you get to choose which.

**For the hypocrite, this is the end of the world.** The whole point of his double life was the second room. Take away the second room and he has nothing — his public faith was a performance for an audience, and now he learns the real audience was never fooled for a second. The unwatched room was the foundation of his entire house, and it was never there. The house was built on air.

**But for the believer, the very same fact is the greatest mercy imaginable.** Think about it. *There is no unwatched room* also means: **you are never actually alone.** The prayer you prayed that no one praised — He saw it. The charity you gave that you told no one about — He knows. The tears you cried into the pillow that no human will ever know fell — they were never private, never wasted, never unwitnessed. The patience you showed when everyone assumed you were fine. The temptation you walked away from in an empty room where you "could have gotten away with it" — *there is no empty room.* He was there. He knew. It counted.

[PAUSE]

Do you see? The exact same five words — *Allah knows their secrets* — are a terror to the one whose secret life contradicts his public one, and a comfort beyond measure to the one whose secret life is *better* than his public one. The hypocrite's nightmare and the sincere servant's deepest consolation are *the identical sentence.*

The verse names it as *isrārahum* — *their* secrets — as a charge against them. But the same divine attribute, *yaʿlamu*, the One who knows, is also called in the Quran *yaʿlamu khā'inata-l-aʿyun* — He knows the treachery of the eyes — and also the One who knows the smallest atom of good you ever did. **The watching is total in both directions.** Nothing bad is hidden. And — hold onto this — *nothing good is lost.*

**So what does this change about tomorrow?** Two things, and they are two sides of one coin. First: stop investing in the second room. Stop building a private self that contradicts your public faith, because you are decorating a chamber that does not exist. Let the two selves collapse into one. The integrity the verse is calling you toward is *literal* — *integer*, undivided, one. The cure for hypocrisy is not to perform better in public. It is to make the private self *true*, because the private self is the only one God was ever really reading.

And second: take heart. The next quiet good deed you do — the one no one will clap for — do it knowing it lands in the only ledger that was ever real. You were never performing for the room. You were always, only, speaking to the One who knows the secret.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now stand back and look at the whole verse, and watch how perfectly it is built.

> *That is because they said* — [the diagnosis: a sentence]
> *to those who hated what Allah sent down* — [the wrong audience: enemies of revelation]
> *"We will obey you in part of the matter"* — [the disease: partial, reserved, hidden loyalty]
> *But Allah knows their secrets.* — [the collapse: there was no private room]

The verse is a closing trap. It opens by quoting their whispered words — letting you lean in to overhear the secret deal. It walks you through the careful hedge, the clever *baʿḍ*, the reserved exception. It lets you, for three clauses, occupy the hypocrite's own perspective — *here is my secret, here is my hedge, here is my clever arrangement.* And then the final clause turns the lights on. *Wa-llāhu yaʿlamu isrārahum.* The room you thought you were whispering in was never private. The One you were hiding from was the closest listener in it.

The verse *performs* the very lesson it teaches. It lets you build the secret room, clause by clause — and then reveals, in the last four words, that God was inside it the whole time.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are one insight seen from two directions.

The grammar showed us **nazzala** — patient, continuous mercy descending; **baʿḍ** — partial, reserved loyalty offered back; and **isrār** — the imagined private room. The themes showed us that *baʿḍ*, "the part," is not the footnote but the headline of your faith — it is the one place your loyalty is actually tested. And that *isrār*, the secret room, does not exist — which damns the hypocrite and consoles the sincere.

And here is where they fuse: **the reason the reserved "part" is so deadly is precisely because there is no private room to keep it in.** The hypocrite reserves a hidden exception *because* he believes it can stay hidden. Kill the hidden room, and the whole strategy of partial loyalty collapses. You cannot keep a secret reservation from a God who knows the secret *and* the secreting. The two halves of the verse are one lock and its key.

### Questions to Carry

1. Where is my *baʿḍ* — the one matter I have quietly reserved, where I tell myself "I follow Allah, except here"? Have I ever even named it?
2. The 90% of my deen that costs me nothing — am I sure that's loyalty, or is it just convenience that happens to agree with God?
3. If the contents of my "private room" — my messages, my thoughts, my 3 a.m. self — were read aloud, would they confirm my public faith, or expose a second arrangement?
4. Is my secret life *worse* than my public life, or *better*? Which way does my hidden self lean?
5. If there is no unwatched room — what good deed have I been hiding that I can now do with joy, knowing it was never unseen?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**The most dangerous betrayal is never total but partial — a small reserved exception whispered in a room we imagine is private — and the verse demolishes both the smallness and the privacy: the reserved "part" is the only place faith is truly tested, and there was never a private room, because Allah knows the secret and the very act of keeping it.**

### The Closing Invitation

You do not need to root out a thousand sins to answer this verse. You need to find *one* word: your *baʿḍ*. The single reserved part. And you need to surrender one illusion: the unwatched room. Let your private self and your public self become one self — not by performing better where people can see, but by becoming true where only God can. The integrity this verse calls you toward is not a burden. It is a relief. It is the end of the exhausting labor of maintaining two rooms. There was only ever one. Live in it.

### A Brief Du'a

*O Allah, You who know the secret and the secreting — there is no room where You are not. Do not let there be in our hearts a reserved part that we keep from You, or a door we leave open to what You sent down to be obeyed. Make our hidden self truer than our seen self, and let the deeds we hide from people be the ones that weigh heaviest with You. Āmīn.*
