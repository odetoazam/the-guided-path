# The Two Followers

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the words first. In Arabic:

**ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ٱتَّبَعُوا۟ ٱلْبَٰطِلَ وَأَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّبَعُوا۟ ٱلْحَقَّ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يَضْرِبُ ٱللَّهُ لِلنَّاسِ أَمْثَٰلَهُمْ**

*Dhālika bi-anna alladhīna kafarū-ttabaʿū al-bāṭila wa-anna alladhīna āmanū-ttabaʿū al-ḥaqqa min rabbihim. Kadhālika yaḍribu-llāhu lin-nāsi amthālahum.*

"That is because those who disbelieve follow falsehood, while those who believe follow the truth from their Lord. Thus does Allah set forth for people their comparisons."

Now — let me rebuild the room this verse walked into.

### The world behind the verse

Surah Muhammad is a surah of the sword's edge. Many of its verses came down in the years when the believers were no longer just being mocked in Makkah — they were now a community in Madinah, facing armies, facing the real weight of a confrontation that could end them. This is not a surah of gentle parables told over a quiet evening. This is a surah for people whose lives were being decided.

And it opens by sorting humanity into two columns.

Verse one: *Those who disbelieve and obstruct the way of Allah — He sends their deeds astray.* Their actions wander off, lost, like a caravan that walked all night and arrived nowhere.

Verse two: *Those who believe and do righteous deeds — He sets right their state, and forgives them.* Their inner condition is repaired, mended, made sound.

Two groups. Two completely opposite outcomes. One group's entire life-work evaporates. The other group's life is quietly being put back together from the inside.

And the human heart, hearing this, immediately asks the most natural question in the world:

**Why?**

Why these two destinies? What is the actual difference between these two people? They both worked. They both built lives. They both, in some sense, *tried*. So what separates the one whose deeds dissolve from the one whose soul is repaired?

[PAUSE]

Verse three is the answer. And here is what should stop you before we even open it:

Allah does not answer that question the way you would expect. He does not say "this group prayed and that group didn't." He does not list deeds. He does not contrast crimes against good works.

He says something far stranger and far deeper. He reduces the entire difference between salvation and ruin to a single verb — *the same verb* — applied to both of them.

They are both **followers**.

The hook is this: by the end of this verse, you will not be able to ask "do I follow, or do I think for myself?" — because Allah is about to close that exit completely. The only question He will leave standing is the one He wants you to never stop asking.

Let's go and find it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: One Verb for Both — *ittabaʿū*

Before we touch the Arabic, let me teach you something about how language reveals an author's mind.

Imagine you are writing about two rivals — a hero and a villain. The lazy writer gives them different verbs to flatter the reader's bias. The hero "strides," the villain "skulks." The hero "speaks," the villain "hisses." You can feel the author's thumb on the scale.

But there is a rarer, more powerful kind of writing. The writer gives both characters **the exact same verb** — and lets the *object* of that verb carry the entire moral weight. The hero and the villain both "reach." One reaches for a hand. One reaches for a knife. The verb is identical. Everything that matters is in *what* they reached for.

That second kind of writing is much harder. And it is exactly what Allah does here.

Look:

> الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ **ٱتَّبَعُوا۟** ٱلْبَٰطِلَ
> *Those who disbelieve — they followed falsehood.*

> الَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ **ٱتَّبَعُوا۟** ٱلْحَقَّ
> *Those who believe — they followed truth.*

Notice it. **The verb does not change.** *Ittabaʿū* — "they followed" — is used for the disbelievers, and the *very same word*, *ittabaʿū*, is used for the believers. Letter for letter, identical.

Let's sit with this, because it is doing something enormous.

Allah is refusing to flatter the believer.

He could have written it so the believers "were guided to" the truth while the disbelievers "chased after" falsehood — giving the believers a nobler-sounding verb. He does not. He puts both of them in the exact same posture. Both are *followers.* Both are walking behind something. Both have handed the lead to something outside themselves.

[PAUSE]

Here is what that demolishes.

There is a story modern people love to tell about themselves: *"I don't follow. I'm a free thinker. I made up my own mind."* It is the proudest sentence of our age. The believer is mocked as a "follower" — a sheep, a herd-animal, someone who outsourced their thinking. And the unbeliever imagines himself standing alone, upright, beholden to no one, the author of his own conclusions.

This verse walks straight up to that story and dismantles it in one word.

**Everyone is following.** The one who rejected God did not stop following — he just changed what he follows. He followed *al-bāṭil*. The skeptic who threw off religion did not become free of all masters; he picked up a different one. There is no human being standing in the clearing, master of himself, following nothing. That person does not exist. Allah does not even grant him a grammatical category.

Now — the form of this verb deepens it further.

*Ittabaʿa* is **Form VIII** of the root *t-b-ʿ* (تبع). The base meaning of the root is simply "to follow, to come behind." But Form VIII in Arabic — the *iftaʿala* pattern — characteristically pulls the action *inward*, toward the self. It carries the sense of taking the action *up for oneself*, doing it with personal involvement, making it one's own. The simple form means "to follow." This intensified form leans toward "to take as something to follow, to adopt as one's own path, to pursue of one's own engagement."

So this is not sleepwalking. This is not being dragged. Both groups *took up* their following. Both *embraced* a path and made it theirs. The disbeliever is not a passive victim of falsehood any more than the believer is a passive recipient of truth. Each one *reached out and took hold* of what he walks behind.

[PAUSE]

So here is what this single repeated verb reveals, and I want it to land cleanly before we move on:

**The difference between the saved and the ruined is not that one follows and one is free. They are both followers, and both chose their following. The entire weight of their two destinies hangs not on the act of following — but on the one thing the verb leaves blank: the object. What did you take up to walk behind?**

### Element Two: The Falsehood With No Address

Now we go to the objects — to what each group followed. And here is where you must learn to read what is *absent* as carefully as what is present.

Let me teach the principle first.

In Arabic, as in any careful language, when you name a thing you can leave it bare, or you can give it a lineage — a "from," a source, an attribution. "Water" versus "water from a mountain spring." The attribution does two things: it tells you where the thing comes from, and it vouches for it. It gives the thing an *address.* Something you can trace back. Something with a source you could go and verify.

Now watch what Allah gives each group to follow.

The believers followed:

> ٱلْحَقَّ **مِن رَّبِّهِمْ**
> *al-ḥaqqa min rabbihim* — "the truth **from their Lord.**"

The truth comes with a full address. *Min* — "from." *Rabbihim* — "their Lord." Not just any lord — *their* Lord, with the possessive pronoun binding it to them intimately. The truth they follow has a known origin, a named source, a guarantor. You can trace it all the way back to the One who sustains and nurtures and owns them.

And the disbelievers followed:

> ٱلْبَٰطِلَ
> *al-bāṭil* — "falsehood."

[PAUSE]

That's it. That is the whole phrase.

Did you feel the gap?

**Falsehood is given no source.** No *min*. No "from." Nothing comes after it. Allah does not say "falsehood from Shayṭān," or "falsehood from their fathers," or "falsehood from their desires" — even though, as the classical commentators note, *al-bāṭil* here points to Shayṭān and to the path of misguidance. He could have given falsehood an origin. He deliberately does not.

This is asymmetric absence, and it is *theology written in grammar.*

Truth gets an address. Falsehood is left an orphan.

Now — *why* is this perfect? Because of what the word *al-bāṭil* actually means.

The root *b-ṭ-l* (بطل) does not primarily mean "lie" or "wrong." Its core lexical meaning is **to be void, to be vain, to come to nothing, to be null, to vanish without leaving a trace.** *Baṭala* is what happens to a thing that had no reality to begin with and so dissolves. The lexicographers gloss *al-bāṭil* as that which has no fixed reality, no substance, *no stable existence.* It is the opposite of *al-ḥaqq* — and *al-ḥaqq*, from the root *ḥ-q-q* (حقق), means that which is firmly real, established, *true in the sense of solidly existing,* the thing that holds.

So follow the logic all the way down:

How could falsehood have a real source? **A thing with no reality cannot have a real origin.** You cannot trace the address of something that fundamentally *isn't there.* To give *al-bāṭil* a "from" would be to grant it a foothold in reality that, by its very definition, it does not possess. Allah leaves falsehood without an address *because that is precisely what falsehood is*: a path that leads back to nothing, walked behind by people who think there is something there.

[PAUSE]

Let that reorganize the picture for you. The disbeliever, in his own eyes, is the realist. He follows the hard, observable, "real" world and rejects the unseen. And Allah's grammar says: *no — you are the one following the void. You attached yourself to the thing that vanishes. The believer attached himself to the only thing that holds.*

What this single asymmetry reveals — and I want you to carry this exact sentence:

**Truth has a Lord; falsehood has no one. Everything you give your life to is either traceable to a Source that will still be there when everything collapses — or it is an orphan path that leads back to nothing, and you only discover its emptiness when you arrive.**

Major [PAUSE].

### Element Three: *Their* Likenesses — The Coin Allah Mints

One more feature, and it changes the whole frame of the verse.

The verse ends:

> كَذَٰلِكَ **يَضْرِبُ ٱللَّهُ** لِلنَّاسِ **أَمْثَٰلَهُمْ**
> *Kadhālika yaḍribu-llāhu lin-nāsi amthālahum* — "Thus does Allah strike for people their likenesses."

Let me teach two things buried in the Arabic here.

First, the verb. *Yaḍribu*, from the root *ḍ-r-b* (ضرب). We translate the idiom *ḍaraba mathalan* as "to set forth a comparison," "to give an example." That translation is correct but it has gone soft in English. The root verb *ḍaraba* means **to strike, to hit.** It is the same verb used for striking the ground, striking a blow. And — this is the image the Arabs heard — it is the verb used for **striking a coin.** When a mint produced currency, it *struck* the metal: *ḍaraba al-dirham.* The die comes down with force and impresses a fixed, permanent image into the metal that will not wash off.

So when Allah "strikes" a *mathal* — a likeness, a comparison — He is not gently *suggesting* an analogy. He is *minting* it. He is bringing the die down and impressing a permanent, fixed truth into reality, stamping these two groups with their real image so it can never be rubbed away.

Second — and don't miss this — look at the object: *amthālahum.* "**Their** likenesses." Not *al-amthāl*, "the likenesses" in general. *Their* likenesses — with the possessive pronoun *-hum* binding the comparison to the very people it describes.

The classical commentators read this beautifully. *Al-Baghawī* glosses *amthālahum* as *ashkālahum* — "their forms, their types, the shapes that match them." And the meaning that opens up is this: Allah does not assign people a random parable. He strikes for each kind of person **the likeness that matches what they already are.** *Al-Muyassar* makes it explicit: He attaches to every people, from among the likenesses and the forms, *that which suits them* — *mā yunāsibuhu.*

[PAUSE]

Sit with what that means.

The image Allah strikes for you is *yours.* It fits you the way a struck coin fits its die — exactly, with nothing left over. The one who followed the void is *minted* with the likeness of voidness. The one who followed the truth from his Lord is *minted* with the likeness of the real. You are not being described from the outside by an arbitrary metaphor. You are being shown your own face. The parable *is* you, struck into permanence.

And the verb tense seals it. *Yaḍribu* is **imperfect** — present-and-ongoing. Not "Allah struck" once, long ago. **Allah keeps striking.** Right now, in this room, as you follow whatever you are following, the die is coming down. Your likeness is being minted in real time.

What this final phrase reveals:

**Allah is not telling you a story about two distant tribes. He is holding up a struck coin and saying: one side of this is you. Look at which metal you handed Him to strike.**

[PAUSE]

### Bridge

So watch what the grammar has built, brick by brick.

One verb for both groups — *you are a follower no matter what; the only question is the object.* Then the objects: truth with a Lord, falsehood with no address — *one path is traceable to the Real, the other dissolves into nothing.* And then the striking — *Allah is minting your likeness, the one that matches you, in real time, permanently.*

The words have shown us the architecture of every human life. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about language — but about how you are living, today, this hour.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: You Are Already Following Something

Let me start where you actually live.

You woke up this morning and, before you had a conscious thought, you followed something. Maybe you reached for the phone — and followed the feed, the algorithm, the thumb-shaped pull of a thousand strangers deciding what you'd see. Maybe you followed the anxious voice that started narrating your day's obligations before your eyes were fully open. Maybe you followed a craving, a grudge, a fear, a hope. You followed *something* before 7 a.m.

We have built an entire culture on the fantasy that the modern person is autonomous — a sovereign individual who weighs the evidence and chooses freely, beholden to no creed. "I'm not religious, I just follow the science." "I don't need a book to tell me how to live, I follow my own moral compass." "I think for myself."

And here is the quiet thing underneath all of those sentences: *every one of them is a confession of following.* The science. My compass. Myself. Each is a thing walked behind. The person who says "I follow no one" has simply not yet noticed who is in front of them.

This verse saw that fantasy fourteen centuries before it became the official religion of the modern West — and it closed the exit. *Those who disbelieve — followed.* The verb is the same as the believer's. Allah will not let anyone stand outside the category of follower. Not the atheist, not the philosopher, not the influencer, not you.

Why does this matter so much? Because the question changes everything.

If you believe you are a free agent following nothing, then your spiritual life is about *protecting your independence* — and every call to submit to God feels like a threat, a cage, a surrender of the precious autonomous self.

But if this verse is true — if you are *always already following something* — then independence was never on the menu. It was never one of the options. The choice was never "follow God, or be free." The choice is, and has only ever been, **"follow the Truth that has a Lord, or follow the void that has no name."** There is no third door marked "follow nothing." That door is painted on the wall.

[PAUSE]

Feel the relief in that, before you feel the weight.

The relief: you can stop straining to be a self-made island. That project was always going to fail, because the human being is built to walk behind something — it is in the design. You were made to follow. The yearning to give yourself to something larger is not a weakness to be outgrown; it is the deepest true thing about you.

And then the weight: *if I am always following, then I am responsible for what I follow.* I cannot plead neutrality. The man who drifted with his desires did not avoid the choice — *ittabaʿū*, he *took up* the void, of his own engagement, Form VIII, the action pulled inward and owned. There are no bystanders. Drifting is a direction. The current you didn't choose to fight is the current you chose to follow.

**What this changes about tomorrow:** Tonight, before you sleep, name it. Not abstractly — specifically. *What did I follow today?* Trace your actual hours. The first thing you reached for. The voice you obeyed when you were angry. The standard you measured yourself against. You will find, every single time, that you were walking behind something. The spiritual life does not begin when you decide to start following. It begins the moment you become honest about *what you have been following all along* — and turn, deliberately, toward the One whose path has a Lord.

### Theme Two: The Things That Vanish When You Arrive

Now the harder one. Let's talk about *al-bāṭil* — and let's not keep it as an abstraction. Let's make it the thing it actually is in your life.

Remember what the word means at its root: not "lie," but **the void. That which has no reality and so vanishes without a trace.** Falsehood, in the Quranic sense, is not mainly the thing you *know* is false. It is the thing that *feels completely real right up until the moment it disappears.*

Let me show you where you've already met it.

You have, at some point, given a year of your life to something — a relationship, a goal, an image of who you'd become, a number in a bank account, the approval of a particular person — and you were *certain* it was solid. You built on it. You arranged your days around it. It felt like the most real thing in the world; it felt like *the ground.* And then one day it was gone — they left, the number stopped mattering, the goal arrived and was strangely empty, the approval came and meant nothing. And you stood there with a specific, vertiginous feeling: *I built my life on something that wasn't there.*

That feeling has a name. The Quran calls it *al-bāṭil.* And what you experienced in miniature — the ground turning out to be vapor — is what verse 1 of this surah describes happening to an entire life: *He sends their deeds astray.* A whole lifetime of effort arriving, like that caravan, at nothing. Why? Verse 3 tells you: *because they followed al-bāṭil.* They built on the thing that, by its nature, vanishes when you finally arrive at it.

Now hold that against the other phrase: *al-ḥaqqa min rabbihim* — "the truth, **from their Lord.**"

Here is the whole consolation of faith, packed into a single preposition. The thing the believer built on has a *source that outlasts the building.* Even when the believer's own deeds are imperfect, even when he stumbles — verse 2 says Allah *forgives them and sets their state right* — because what he attached himself to is anchored not in his own fragile effort but *in his Lord.* The Truth has a Source that does not move. When everything else in his life collapses — and it will, all of it, everything that is not God will eventually go — the thing he was actually following is still standing, because its address is the One who does not vanish.

[PAUSE]

This is the paradox, and I don't want to rush it: **the disbeliever spent his life on what looked solid and was void. The believer spent his life on what looked uncertain — the unseen, the promised, the not-yet — and it was the only thing that was real.** The realist followed a phantom. The "dreamer" followed the only Reality there is. The whole optical illusion of this world is that *al-bāṭil* wears the costume of the solid, and *al-ḥaqq* wears the costume of the uncertain — and you only find out which was which at the moment of arrival, when one of them vanishes in your hands and the other is finally, fully *there.*

And remember: Allah is *striking* this likeness into you *now*, present tense, *yaḍribu.* Every time you choose what to build on, you are handing Him metal to strike. The void, or the Real. And the coin you are minted into is *amthālahum* — *yours.* It will match exactly what you followed. No one will be assigned a destiny that doesn't fit them. You will receive the likeness of precisely the thing you walked behind.

[PAUSE]

**What this changes about tomorrow:** Take the single biggest thing you are pouring yourself into right now — the thing that, if it failed, you feel would end you. Hold it up to one question: *Does this have a "min Rabbihim"? Can I trace this back to my Lord — or only to the void?* You are not being asked to abandon your work, your relationships, your ambitions. You are being asked to *re-source* them — to stop building them on themselves, where they are *bāṭil* and will vanish, and to rebuild them on Allah, where the very same effort becomes *ḥaqq min Rabbihim* and survives the collapse of everything else. The same deed, re-addressed to its true Source, changes from the coin of the void into the coin of the Real.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing it together

Stand back now and see the whole structure, because the architecture is the proof.

Verses 1 and 2 gave us two **destinies**: deeds sent astray, versus a state set right. Verse 3 — this verse — gives us the single **reason** beneath both, and it builds it in three perfect strokes:

First, **one verb** — *ittabaʿū*, "they followed" — laid identically over both groups, so that no one can escape into the fantasy of the un-following, sovereign self. *You are a follower. Settled.*

Second, **two objects, asymmetrically marked** — *al-ḥaqq min rabbihim*, truth with a full address, traceable to the Lord; and *al-bāṭil*, falsehood, left a deliberate orphan, because the void cannot have a source. *Everything you follow leads either to the One who remains, or to nothing at all.*

Third, **the striking** — *yaḍribu-llāhu lin-nāsi amthālahum* — Allah, present-tense, ongoing, *minting* each person with the likeness that exactly matches what they followed. *Your destiny is not assigned to you from outside; it is struck from the metal you yourself handed over.*

The linguistic discoveries and the themes turn out to be one insight seen from two sides. The grammar said: *one verb, two objects, asymmetric attribution.* The heart hears: *you will follow something; make sure the thing you follow has a Lord, because the alternative has no one and goes nowhere.* The grammar's missing *min* after *al-bāṭil* and the soul's experience of the ground turning to vapor are the **same fact** — one written in syntax, one written in your scars.

### Questions to carry

1. If I traced my last full day honestly, hour by hour — what did I actually walk behind? Not what I believe in. What I *followed.*
2. The thing I am most afraid to lose — does it have a *min Rabbihim*? Can I trace it back to my Lord, or only to itself?
3. Where in my life am I still telling the story that I "follow no one" — and what is that story hiding from me?
4. If Allah is striking my likeness right now, in real time, from the metal of what I follow — what is the coin currently being minted?
5. What is one thing I am building on its own foundation — *bāṭil*, vanishing — that I could re-source onto Allah and turn into *ḥaqq*, lasting?

### One-sentence distillation

**You were never going to follow nothing — so the only question your life has ever really been answering is whether the thing you walk behind has a Lord, or no one at all.**

### The closing invitation

Nothing here asks you to *start* following. You already do; you always have; you were made to. The invitation is smaller and infinitely larger than that: to stop pretending you are the exception, to look honestly at what is in front of you on the path — and then to turn, with the full inward force of that Form VIII verb, taking it up as your own, toward *al-ḥaqq min Rabbihim.* The Truth that has an address. The only path whose end is not vapor.

### Du'a

*O Allah, You who struck this likeness and who strikes it still — we confess that we are followers, and we confess we have walked behind the void and called it solid. Re-source our lives to You. Let every step we take be ittibāʿ al-ḥaqqi min Rabbinā — a following of the Truth that comes from You — so that when everything we built upon vanishes, we find that the thing we were truly following was never going to vanish at all, because it was always, only, from You.* Āmīn.
