# The Two Directions of a Ruined Heart

### Surah Muhammad · Ayah 28

> ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمُ ٱتَّبَعُوا۟ مَآ أَسْخَطَ ٱللَّهَ وَكَرِهُوا۟ رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ

**Dhālika bi-annahumu-ttabaʿū mā askhaṭa-llāha wa-karihū riḍwānahu fa-aḥbaṭa aʿmālahum**

*That is because they followed what angered Allah and hated His pleasure, so He rendered their deeds worthless.*

---

## INTRODUCTION: The Verse That Explains a Death

Let me set the scene first, because this ayah will not open until you are standing where it wants you to stand.

One verse before this, in 47:27, something terrible is happening. The angels are arriving. Not the angels of glad tidings — the other ones. They are taking the souls of a certain kind of person, and the Quran describes it with a detail that makes you wince: **they strike their faces and their backs**. Front and back. Coming and going. There is no angle from which the blow does not land.

And then the surah does something. It stops the scene. And it turns to us — to you, to me — and it says one word: **ذَٰلِكَ**. *That.*

That death. That ending. That blow to the face and the back.

And then it tells us *why*.

Stay with how unusual this is. Allah does not have to explain Himself. He is not obligated to give reasons to His creation for what He decrees. And yet here, at the edge of a soul's worst moment, the surah pauses to say: *here is the reason. Here is what was happening inside them the whole time.* This ayah is the autopsy of a heart. It is Allah opening up the chest of these people after death and showing us the disease that killed them — a disease they carried, fed, and defended while they were alive.

Now here is the thing I need you to feel before we go a single step further.

We tend to imagine that a person who ends badly with Allah was simply *empty*. They didn't pray, didn't give, didn't do good. A blank ledger. But that is not what this ayah describes. Look at the very last word: **فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ** — *He rendered their deeds worthless.* You cannot render worthless what was never there. **These people had deeds.** A ledger full of them. Charity. Kept ties of kinship. Things that, from the outside, looked like the works of righteous people.

[PAUSE]

So the question this ayah is going to force on us is not *"how does someone with no good deeds fail?"*

It is the far more frightening question: **how does someone with a full record of good deeds end up with nothing at all?**

That is the hunger I want you to carry into this verse. Because the answer is not in the deeds. The answer is in two movements of the heart — one outward, one inward — that this verse names with surgical precision. And once you see them, you will start checking your own chest for the same two movements.

Let's begin.

---

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: ٱتَّبَعُوا — Not Just "They Followed," But "They Took It On Themselves"

Let me teach you something about Arabic verbs before we touch the word, because the whole first half of this ayah turns on a shape most translations cannot carry.

In Arabic, a single root — a cluster of three letters — can be poured into different *molds*. The same raw meaning, shaped differently, says different things. Think of it like the difference in English between *"I sat"* and *"I seated myself"* and *"I settled in."* Same basic act. But the second one is more deliberate. The third one says you made yourself comfortable, you took ownership of the place.

The root here is **ت-ب-ع (tbaʿ)** — the idea of following, coming behind, going after.

The simplest mold gives you **تَبِعَ** *(tabiʿa)* — he followed. Plain. A footstep behind another footstep.

But that is **not** the word Allah uses. Allah uses the eighth mold — **ٱتَّبَعُوا** *(ittabaʿū)*, Form VIII.

[PAUSE]

Here is what that mold does. Form VIII — the *iftaʿala* pattern — characteristically folds the action *back onto the doer*. It makes the act reflexive, deliberate, something you take up and carry *for yourself*, with effort and investment. It is the difference between *a path appearing in front of you* and *you choosing the path, stepping onto it, and walking it as your own.*

So **ٱتَّبَعُوا** is not "they happened to follow." It is closer to: **they took it upon themselves to follow. They adopted it. They made it their road and walked it with intention.**

Now notice *what* they took on with such deliberate ownership: **مَآ أَسْخَطَ ٱللَّهَ** — *what angered Allah.*

Let's sit with this. They didn't drift into Allah's anger. They didn't trip and fall into it. The grammar refuses to let us be that generous. The shape of the verb tells us they *pursued* it — they made a project of it, they invested in it, they walked the road that ends in Allah's displeasure as though it were their own chosen direction in life.

Here's what's remarkable, and what should unsettle us: **effort.** The word carries effort. These were not lazy people coasting toward ruin. They were *working*. They were striving. They poured energy and intention into their path. The tragedy of this verse is not the tragedy of the idle. It is the tragedy of the *diligent* — diligent in exactly the wrong direction.

And this is why the surah could rightly say their deeds got nullified. You don't accidentally build a full record of charity and kept kinship. That takes effort too. But here is the horror the word exposes: **the same heart that could muster effort for good deeds was, underneath, deliberately walking the road of what angers Allah.** The effort was real. The direction was fatal.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: أَسْخَطَ and رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ — The Two Opposites, and the Sting Hidden in Their Pairing

Now I want to show you the architecture of this verse's diagnosis, because Allah names **two** movements, not one — and they point in opposite directions.

First movement: **ٱتَّبَعُوا مَآ أَسْخَطَ ٱللَّهَ** — *they followed what angered Allah.*

**أَسْخَطَ** *(askhaṭa)* is from the root **س-خ-ط (sakhaṭ)** — anger, displeasure, wrath. And it sits in the fourth mold, Form IV. Now, I want to be careful here, because the fourth mold in Arabic does several different jobs and we shouldn't lazily slap one label on it. But in *this* word, the function is genuinely **causative-transitive**: *askhaṭa* means *to cause anger, to provoke displeasure, to make someone angry.* So **مَآ أَسْخَطَ ٱللَّهَ** is *"that which causes Allah to be displeased"* — the things that actively provoke His wrath. And those things, the verse says, are exactly what they took up and walked.

That's the first direction: an *outward action.* A road walked. A pursuit.

Now the second movement: **وَكَرِهُوا رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ** — *and they hated His pleasure.*

**كَرِهُوا** *(karihū)* — from **ك-ر-ه (karih)** — is not merely "they neglected" or "they overlooked." It is active aversion. Distaste. To find something repugnant, to recoil from it. And what did they recoil from? **رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ** — *His riḍwān, His pleasure.*

[PAUSE]

I need you to feel the asymmetry the verse has just built, because it is doing enormous work.

Look at the two verbs side by side:

- Toward what angers Allah → **they followed** (an *action*, a doing, a road walked).
- Toward what pleases Allah → **they hated** (a *feeling*, an inner recoil, an aversion of the heart).

Do you see it? **Both the doing and the feeling are corrupted, and they are corrupted in mirror-opposite directions.** It is not enough to say these people did bad things. The verse says: their *hands* moved toward His anger, and their *hearts* turned away from His pleasure. The outer life and the inner life were both inverted. The compass was not broken — it was reversed. North pointed south. What should have repelled them, attracted their feet. What should have drawn them, repelled their hearts.

This is the anatomy of a fully ruined heart: **it pursues what it should flee, and it recoils from what it should pursue.** Not one or the other. Both.

Now let me show you the word **رِضْوَٰن** itself, because Allah did not choose the small word here.

He could have said *riḍā* — pleasure, contentment. That would have been the plain word. Instead He chose **رِضْوَٰن** *(riḍwān)* — the same root, but poured into the fuller mold, the *fiʿlān* pattern, which in Arabic carries a sense of *abundance, fullness, the thing in its largest measure.* **Riḍwān is not just Allah's pleasure — it is His pleasure at its fullest, His supreme good pleasure.**

And here is the detail that should make your breath catch. This exact word — **رِضْوَٰن** — is the word the Quran uses for **the greatest reward in Paradise.** In Surah at-Tawbah (9:72), after Allah lists the rivers and the mansions and the gardens of Eden, He says: **وَرِضْوَٰنٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ** — *"and the riḍwān, the good pleasure, of Allah is greater"* — greater than all of it. The supreme prize. The thing the people of Paradise will treasure above every garden.

[PAUSE]

So read the verse again now with this in your chest:

**وَكَرِهُوا رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ** — *they hated the very thing that is the greatest prize of existence.* They found repugnant the one reward that the dwellers of Paradise will count as worth more than Paradise itself. The treasure was held out to them, and they curled their lip at it. They were repelled by the most beautiful thing there is.

That is not the description of someone who lacked opportunity. That is the description of someone offered the ocean who was disgusted by water.

### Element Three: فَأَحْبَطَ — The Deeds That Swelled Up and Burst

And now we arrive at the last word, and it is the most haunting of all.

**فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ** — *so He rendered their deeds worthless.*

Notice first the small letter at the front: **فَ** *(fa-)* — "so," "and therefore." It is the particle of immediate consequence. It tells us this is not an arbitrary punishment bolted on from outside. It is the *direct result* — the natural, flowing consequence of the two heart-movements just named. Chase His anger, recoil from His pleasure — *and so, as the immediate result*, the deeds collapse. Cause and effect. The ruin grows out of the disease the way a shadow grows out of a body.

Now the verb itself: **أَحْبَطَ** *(aḥbaṭa)*, from the root **ح-ب-ط (ḥabaṭa)**, in the fourth mold — here genuinely causative: *to cause to become void, to nullify, to bring to nothing.*

But I have to tell you where this root *comes from*, because the classical lexicographers preserved its original, physical, visceral meaning — and the moment you know it, this verse will never read flat again.

[PAUSE]

In the oldest layer of the language — recorded in *Lisān al-ʿArab*, explained by al-Rāghib in his *Mufradāt* — **ḥabaṭa** described a specific, gruesome thing that happens to **grazing animals.**

Picture it. An animal — a camel, a sheep — comes upon rich, lush pasture. Green, abundant, irresistible. And it gorges. It eats and eats, greedily, far beyond what is good for it, because the pasture is so plentiful and so sweet. And what happens? Its **belly swells. It bloats.** It puffs up, distended, *enormous* — looking, for a moment, full and fat and thriving.

And then it **dies.** The very gorging that swelled it, kills it. The bloating that made it look so full was the symptom of the thing destroying it from the inside.

**That** is the root Allah chose for what happens to their deeds.

Sit with this. *Iḥbāṭ al-aʿmāl* — the nullification of deeds — is built on the image of **a body that swells up impressively and then bursts into nothing.**

Do you see now why this is the perfect word? Their deeds were not *small*. Their record was not *thin*. Like the bloated animal, their deeds looked **full** — swollen with charity, distended with kept kinship, puffed up with works that, from the outside, looked like abundance, like thriving, like a fat and healthy record. **And it was all the bloat of a dying thing.** The fullness was the symptom of the death. When the moment of truth came, the impressive swelling burst, and what was inside was — nothing. Air. Void.

[PAUSE]

This is the terror at the bottom of the verse, and it is the answer to the question we carried in from the very beginning. *How does a person with a full record end up with nothing?* Here is how: **because deeds can be bloated rather than alive.** They can be impressive on the outside and hollow on the inside. They can swell the ledger and weigh nothing on the scale. A deed eaten greedily from the wrong pasture — done for the wrong reason, by a heart walking toward Allah's anger and recoiling from His pleasure — does not *nourish*. It *bloats*. And bloat is not life. Bloat is the look of life on something that is already dying.

What this single root invites us to consider is devastating: **the size of your record and the life of your record are not the same thing.** You can be full and void at once.

---

### Bridge

So look at what these three words have shown us, laid end to end.

**ٱتَّبَعُوا** — they did not drift; they *deliberately took up* the road that angers Allah, with real effort and ownership.

**أَسْخَطَ / رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ** — their corruption ran in *both directions at once*: feet pursuing His anger, heart recoiling from His pleasure — and the pleasure they recoiled from was *riḍwān*, the greatest prize of all existence.

**فَأَحْبَطَ** — and so, as the direct and immediate consequence, their deeds — full, impressive, swollen — *bloated and burst* into nothing.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself. About *your* heart, and the two directions it can face, and the difference between a record that is full and a record that is alive.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Reversed Compass — When the Heart Wants the Wrong Things

Let me start somewhere very human, very far from theology.

Think of a relationship — a marriage, a friendship, a parent and child — that has gone genuinely bad. Not *cold*. Not *distant*. **Bad.** And let me tell you the precise shape of "bad," because everyone who has seen it knows it.

It is not when one person simply stops doing nice things. That's just neglect — sad, but reversible. *Bad* is something else. *Bad* is when one person starts, almost compulsively, doing the very things they *know* wound the other — and at the same time, finds themselves *irritated* by the things that would please them. You suggest something kind, and they recoil. You offer the very thing they used to want, and it now repels them. Meanwhile, the things they know will hurt — they reach for those almost eagerly.

When you see that in a relationship, you know something has *inverted*. The love has not just cooled. It has **reversed polarity.** What once attracted now repels. What once repelled now attracts. The compass is not dead. It is pointing exactly backwards.

[PAUSE]

That — *exactly that* — is what this ayah is describing about a heart and its Lord.

**ٱتَّبَعُوا مَآ أَسْخَطَ ٱللَّهَ وَكَرِهُوا رِضْوَٰنَهُۥ.**

This is not the portrait of someone who simply forgot about Allah. This is the portrait of an *inverted* heart — feet that have learned to walk toward His displeasure, a heart that has learned to recoil from His pleasure. The polarity has flipped.

And here is why this should make every one of us pause and look inward, rather than point outward at "those people." Because the inversion does not happen all at once. Nobody wakes up one morning with a reversed compass. It happens by **degrees.** It happens the way it happens in a marriage — one small recoil at a time. One small reach toward the wrong thing at a time.

Let me make it concrete, because this is where it stops being about *them* and starts being about *us.*

Have you ever noticed a small reluctance in yourself toward something you *know* pleases Allah? A slight heaviness when the time for prayer comes? A faint irritation when you're asked to give? A quiet relief when an act of worship is cancelled? **That faint recoil is the seed of karihū.** It is the compass *beginning* to drift. It is not yet inversion — but it is the first degree of it.

And on the other side: have you noticed a small pull toward something you *know* displeases Him? A thing you keep returning to, defending, making excuses for? **That pull is the seed of ittabaʿū** — the first footstep on the road.

[PAUSE]

The verse is not describing a faraway monster. It is describing the *destination* of two tiny movements that live in every human chest. And it is doing us the mercy of showing us the destination *now*, while the compass has only drifted a degree or two — while it can still be turned back.

So here is the practical thing to carry, and it is small enough to actually do. **Watch your recoils.** The next time you feel that faint reluctance toward an act of obedience, or that faint pull toward something you know He dislikes — *do not let it pass unnamed.* Stop and say to yourself: *that is my compass drifting.* The drift is reversible at one degree. It is nearly impossible to reverse at one hundred and eighty. The mercy is in catching the *first* degree. This ayah is Allah handing you the instrument to check your own bearing — *before* the angels arrive to read it for you.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: The Full Record That Weighs Nothing — and the Mercy of Knowing Now

Let me take you into a different human experience.

Imagine a man who spends thirty years building the most impressive résumé you have ever seen. Every line is real. The awards happened. The titles were held. The accomplishments are documented, photographed, applauded. By every external measure, it is a *full* life — a thick, heavy portfolio of achievement.

And then imagine he discovers, at the very end, that every single thing on it was done to be *seen* — that there was never, underneath, a real love for the work, never a real care for the people, never anything but the hunger for the applause. And the applause has now stopped. The audience has gone home. And he is left holding a thick portfolio that, in the quiet, weighs **nothing.** It was all bloat. Impressive bloat. Documented bloat. And not one ounce of it was *alive.*

[PAUSE]

We feel the horror of that immediately, because we all half-suspect it could be us. And **فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ** — *He rendered their deeds worthless* — is that horror, told at the scale of eternity, with Allah Himself as the audience that does not clap.

Now, I want to be careful and precise here, because the classical commentators have something important to say about *which* deeds got nullified, and it raises the stakes enormously.

Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported in the tafsīr of al-Qurṭubī and al-Baghawī, explains that "what angered Allah" included their *concealment* — concealing the truth they knew, and turning from it — and that the deeds nullified were the deeds that, on the surface, *looked good*: **charity. The keeping of kinship ties.** Al-Qurṭubī lists them plainly: *ṣadaqah, ṣilat al-raḥim* — giving in charity, maintaining family bonds — "and other than that." And al-Wāḥidī's collection and al-Muyassar's gloss extend the picture to good works in general.

Do you feel what that does to the verse?

**The deeds that were nullified were real good deeds.** Charity that fed someone. Kinship that comforted someone. These were not fake on the surface — they *happened*, they helped, the poor man ate the food. But on the scale that matters, weighed by the One who sees the heart that did them — they bloated and burst. Because a heart walking toward His anger and recoiling from His pleasure cannot, in the end, do a deed that *lives.* It can only do a deed that *swells.*

[PAUSE]

Now — I could leave you in that terror. But that is not where this ayah is meant to leave you, and here is the turn.

Why would Allah *show* us this? Why open the chest, perform the autopsy, name the disease in front of us?

Because **we are not yet dead.**

This verse is an autopsy performed for the benefit of the *living*. The people in it can no longer change their record. But you can. The single most merciful thing about **فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَٰلَهُمْ** is that *you are reading it before your deeds are sealed.* You have been shown, in advance, the exact mechanism by which a full record becomes a void one — and you have been shown it while your own record is still open, still being written, still *changeable.*

This is not a verse meant to make you despair of your deeds. It is a verse meant to make you **go back and check them for life.** Not for *quantity* — you are not asked to count. For *life.* For the heart underneath them.

So here is the practical thing to carry from this theme, and it is a question to ask of your own works, regularly, gently, without panic: *Is this deed I am proud of — is it alive, or is it bloated?* Not *"is it big?"* but *"why am I doing it, and would I still do it if no one — no one at all — would ever know?"* The bloated deed needs an audience. The living deed does not. The bloated deed swells when seen. The living deed is the same in the dark.

[PAUSE]

And the mercy is this: a bloated deed can still be made alive — *while you are alive.* The same charity, redone with a corrected heart, weighs differently. You are not asked to throw away your record. You are asked to *breathe life back into it* before it is sealed. That is the gift hidden inside the most frightening word in the verse. **He told us now. While we can still answer.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at how the two halves of this verse are actually one truth seen from two sides.

The **linguistic journey** showed us a heart facing two wrong directions — *feet* deliberately walking toward His anger (ٱتَّبَعُوا), *heart* recoiling from His pleasure (كَرِهُوا) — and a record that, as the direct consequence (فَ), swelled up and burst (أَحْبَطَ).

The **thematic journey** showed us the same thing in our own lives: the *reversed compass* of a relationship gone bad, and the *bloated résumé* that weighs nothing in the silence.

They are the same insight from two directions. **What the heart faces determines what the deeds weigh.** A heart pointed the wrong way cannot produce a deed that lives, no matter how full the record looks — because the deed inherits the direction of the heart that made it. The compass and the scale are connected. Fix the compass, and the scale takes care of itself. Leave the compass reversed, and no amount of weight on the scale will save you, because it is all bloat.

And underneath both: **He told us.** He stopped the scene of death, turned to the living, and explained. That is not the act of a Lord waiting to catch you. That is the act of a Lord who wants you to catch *yourself* — at one degree of drift, while the record is still open.

### Questions to Carry

Sit with these. Don't rush to answer them. Let them follow you.

1. Where, in the last week, did I feel a small *recoil* from something I know pleases Allah — and did I name it, or let it pass?
2. Where did I feel a small *pull* toward something I know displeases Him — and what am I telling myself to justify it?
3. If I weighed my most impressive deed not by its size but by whether I would still do it with *no one watching* — would it stand, or would it bloat and burst?
4. Is there a good deed in my record right now that I could *breathe life back into* — redo, or re-intend, with a corrected heart — before it is sealed?
5. Which direction is my compass actually pointing — and when did I last check?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**Their ruin was not arbitrary — it was the exact shape of their hearts: feet that chased His anger and a heart that recoiled from His pleasure, so the deeds they piled up swelled into impressive nothing — and He showed us, while we are still alive to turn around.**

### The Closing Invitation

You are not being asked, tonight, to overhaul your entire record. You are being invited to do one small, precise thing: to start *watching your recoils and your pulls* — to treat the faint reluctance toward obedience and the faint attraction toward disobedience not as nothing, but as the compass beginning to drift, and to gently turn it back at the first degree. And to look at one deed you are proud of and ask, without fear, *is this alive?* — knowing that if it is bloated, you still have breath in you to make it live.

The angels in verse 27 read a compass that could no longer be turned. You are reading verse 28 with your compass still in your own hands.

### A Brief Duʿāʾ

*O Allah, You who showed us the disease while we still have life to be cured — turn our hearts toward Your riḍwān before our feet learn the other road. Do not let our deeds swell with bloat and burst into nothing; let the small ones live, done for You alone, the same in the dark as in the light. And let the last thing the angels find in us be a compass pointed home.* Āmīn.
