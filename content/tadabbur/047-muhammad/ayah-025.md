# The Retreat From Clarity

### Surah Muhammad, Ayah 25

> إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱرْتَدُّوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَدْبَـٰرِهِم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ مَا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُمُ ٱلْهُدَى ۙ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ سَوَّلَ لَهُمْ وَأَمْلَىٰ لَهُمْ
>
> *Inna alladhīna-rtaddū ʿalā adbārihim min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā, ash-shayṭānu sawwala lahum wa amlā lahum.*
>
> **"Indeed, those who turned back on their heels after the guidance had become clear to them — Shaytan enticed them, and extended for them a long horizon of hope."**

---

## INTRODUCTION: The World Behind the Words

Before we open a single word, I want you to feel the room this ayah was revealed into.

Madinah. The community is no longer a small persecuted band whispering in Makkah. It is a society now — with weight, with obligations, with cost. And cost is the great clarifier of hearts. When Islam asked only for belief in the quiet of your chest, many could carry it. But Surah Muhammad — the surah some of the early community called *Sūrat al-Qitāl*, the Surah of Struggle — is the surah where the asking gets expensive. It speaks of standing firm when standing firm is dangerous.

And something happens to a certain kind of person when the price tag appears.

Look at the verses just before ours. Allah describes a group who sat among the believers, who heard the Prophet ﷺ, who nodded along — until "a decisive surah is revealed" (47:20). The moment the words turned from comfort to commitment, "you see those in whose hearts is disease looking at you with the look of one fainting at death." The face gives away what the tongue concealed. They had heard everything. They had understood everything. **The problem was never that they didn't know.**

That is the world this ayah lives in. These are not people stumbling in the dark who never found the lamp. [PAUSE]

These are people who *found* the lamp, who saw the room lit up clearly, who saw exactly where the door was — and then, deliberately, started walking backward, away from the light, toward the dark they had already left.

The classical commentators stood in front of this verse and saw two faces of the same act. Qatādah and Ibn Jurayj said: these are the People of the Book, who found the description of the Prophet ﷺ written in what they already had, recognized him the way you recognize your own child, and then denied him. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and al-Suddī said: these are the hypocrites, who held back from struggle after they had read the command with their own eyes in the Qur'an. Two readings — but notice they are not really in conflict. They are two photographs of one human being: **the person who turned away from something they had already seen clearly.**

Here is the hook. Allah does not leave us simply condemning them. In the second half of this ayah, He does something almost unbearable in its mercy and its terror at once: He pulls back the curtain on the machinery. He tells us *how* a human being who has seen the truth gets walked backward away from it. He names the two tools. And once you see those two tools — [PAUSE] — you will recognize them, because they have been used on you. This week. Maybe this morning.

Let us go slowly.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Irtaddū ʿalā adbārihim* — The Doubling-Back

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we touch the word, because the grammar here is not decoration. It is the meaning.

Arabic verbs come in patterns — call them gears. The simplest gear, Form I, just states the action: *radda*, "he returned something," "he sent it back." But Arabic has a reflexive gear, Form VIII, where you add a *t* into the body of the word and the action curves back onto the doer. The plain verb acts on the world; the Form VIII verb acts on *the self*. *Kasaba* is "he earned"; *iktasaba* leans toward "he earned *for himself*, he acquired onto himself." The grammar folds the action inward.

Now the word Allah chose: **ٱرْتَدُّوا۟** — *irtaddū*. The root is *r-d-d*, returning, sending back. But He did not say *raddahum* — "something turned them back," as if a force outside them spun them around. He used the reflexive gear. *Irtaddū.* **They turned their own selves back. They did it to themselves.**

Hold that, because it disarms the excuse before the excuse is born. No one pushed them. There is no villain in the first half of this sentence. The turning is self-administered.

Now listen to the sound of it. *Ir-tad-dū.* There is a *shadda* on the *dāl* — the consonant is doubled, pronounced with a kind of recoil, *tadd-*, the tongue striking twice. Say it and feel it: the word itself doubles back on itself the way the apostate doubles back on his own steps. **The phonetics enact the meaning.** A word about reversal that physically reverses in your mouth.

And then Allah does something the grammar did not require. He adds: **عَلَىٰٓ أَدْبَـٰرِهِم** — *ʿalā adbārihim*, "upon their backs," "on their heels." The root *d-b-r* is the back, the rear, what is behind you.

Stop and ask: why? *Irtaddū* — "they turned back" — already contains the reversal. Why specify "on their heels"? This looks redundant. In the Qur'an, apparent redundancy is never redundant. It is a camera zooming in.

Picture the two ways a person can leave a room. One: you turn around, face the exit, and walk toward it — you are walking *forward*, even if it's forward into something worse, and at least your eyes are on where you're going. That is not this verse. The verse says *ʿalā adbārihim* — **on their backs, walking backward.** Their faces are still pointed at the light. They never stopped seeing it. They are retreating *while still facing the truth*, stepping backward into darkness with their eyes open and fixed on the very clarity they are abandoning. [PAUSE]

That is so much worse than turning your back. To turn your back is to stop looking. To walk backward on your heels is to *keep looking* and leave anyway.

This is the posture of the person who knows. Who never lost sight of it. Who could describe the truth to you in perfect detail even as their feet carry them in the opposite direction.

What this single construction reveals: **the deepest spiritual catastrophe is not blindness. It is sighted retreat.** [PAUSE — major]

### Element Two: *Tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā* — When It Became Clear, Of Itself

Now I want to show you why their retreat is so weighty, and again it is a matter of grammar — the precise gear Allah selects for the word "clear."

The root is *b-y-n*, which carries clarity, separation, distinction — the line between two things becoming visible. From it comes *bayān*, eloquent clear speech. From it comes *bayyinah*, clear proof. And Allah uses Form V: **تَبَيَّنَ** — *tabayyana*.

Form V in Arabic — the *tafaʿʿala* pattern — very often carries the sense of something happening *gradually and of its own accord*, a state that unfolds and settles into place by itself. Not "someone clarified it to them" — that would be a different gear. *Tabayyana* means **the guidance became clear, all by itself, fully, until it stood there undeniable.** It clarified *itself* to them. The truth did its own work. It rose like dawn — you do not argue the sun into the sky; it comes up on its own and there it is.

So read the whole phrase: *min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā* — "after the guidance had made itself clear to them."

And notice the article: **ٱلْهُدَى** — *al-hudā*, "*the* guidance." Definite. Not *a* guidance, not some guidance. *The* guidance — the specific, complete, recognized thing. The very word the hypocrites lacked is the word Allah hands them in full: clarity arrived, settled, became *theirs* — *lahum*, "to them."

Now feel the weight this drops onto the first half of the verse. These people did not retreat through a fog. The grammar has carefully removed every fog. The truth was not partial. It was not ambiguous. It did not need a scholar to decode it. It *tabayyana* — it clarified itself, like a face emerging from shadow until you cannot pretend you don't recognize it.

This is the Qur'an's terrible honesty about a certain kind of human being. There is a despair that comes from never finding the truth — that despair Allah meets with endless mercy and the open door of seeking. But there is another condition entirely: the one who *found* it, for whom it became fully clear, and who walked backward anyway. The verse builds an airtight case before it ever mentions the enemy. No darkness to blame. No confusion to hide behind. Just clarity — and a chosen retreat from it.

What this word reveals: **the guidance is not on trial here. It made its own case, completely. What is on trial is the heart that saw it whole and still turned.** [PAUSE — major]

### Bridge

So now we have the wound in full focus. A self-administered reversal — *irtaddū*, they turned their own selves. A backward walk with the eyes still open — *ʿalā adbārihim*. And no possible excuse of ignorance — *tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā*, the truth had made itself plain.

Which leaves the only real question this verse exists to answer. The question that should be screaming at us: **Why? Why would any human being, having clearly seen, deliberately step back?**

This is where Allah pulls back the curtain. And what is behind the curtain is not what we expect.

### Element Three: *Sawwala lahum wa amlā lahum* — The Two Instruments

Look at the structure of the second half. Allah does not say "and so they turned back because of Shaytan." He builds a clean nominal sentence and *fronts* the enemy: **ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ** — *ash-shayṭān* — first. The Shaytan. Then what he did. By placing the subject first, Arabic spotlights it: *this one — the Shaytan — here is what he did.* The hidden actor steps into the light.

And then two verbs. Only two. The entire mechanism of how a clear-sighted person is walked backward off a cliff reduces, in the divine analysis, to two moves. Learn these two and you have a map of nearly every spiritual failure of your life.

#### The First Instrument: *Sawwala* — Making the Ugly Look Beautiful

The root is *s-w-l*, and it lives in the same neighborhood as *su'l* — a thing the soul *wants*, a desire, a request. Form II — *sawwala* — is the intensive, causative-of-appearance gear: to actively *dress something up*, to make it look the way the soul wishes it looked. The lexicographers and the commentator al-Hasan al-Baṣrī gloss it precisely: *zayyana lahum* — **he beautified it for them**; he made their own ugliness look attractive. *Sawwala* is to take a thing that is genuinely repulsive — a betrayal, a retreat, a sin — and airbrush it, paint it, perfume it, until the soul looks at the same ugly act and says: *that's not so bad. In fact, that looks reasonable. That looks justified. That almost looks good.*

Now hear the sound of the word. *Saw-wa-la.* Soft consonants — the hush of the *sīn*, the liquid glide of the *wāw* and the *lām*. There is no hard edge anywhere in it. It *sounds* like seduction sounds: smooth, low, easy, a whisper that asks for nothing and offers everything. **The phonetics are the temptation.** Allah could have chosen a harsh word for the enemy's work. He chose one that slides.

And here is the detail that should make every one of us go quiet. This exact word — *sawwala* — appears only a handful of times in the entire Qur'an. Twice it is in the mouth of Yaʿqūb, peace be upon him, speaking to his own sons: *bal sawwalat lakum anfusukum amran* — "rather, your own *souls* have *sawwala'd* a matter to you" (12:18, 12:83). There, the one doing the beautifying is **the nafs, the self.** Here, in our verse, the one doing the beautifying is **Shaytan.** [PAUSE]

Sit with that pairing. The same operation — making the ugly look lovely — runs on two engines that are nearly impossible to tell apart from the inside. Sometimes it is the enemy whispering. Sometimes it is your own soul, doing the enemy's work for free. And the deepest danger is precisely that *you cannot feel the difference.* When the retreat looks beautiful to you, you will not be able to say whether that gloss came from outside or from within. Either way, the same lie has been painted over the same ugliness. Either way, you are now looking at a sin and seeing a virtue.

Notice, too, what Allah leaves *unsaid*. The verse says *ash-shayṭānu sawwala lahum* — "Shaytan beautified **for them**" — and then **stops.** Beautified *what*? Arabic expects an object here. We brace for it. And Allah withholds it. There is no stated object. The grammar leaves a deliberate hole.

Why? Because the moment you name the object, you shrink it. If He had said "Shaytan beautified *the retreat* for them," we'd file it under retreat and move on. By leaving the object open, the beautifying expands to cover *everything* — the retreat, the sin, the excuse, the delay, the self-image, the whole package. **The silence is the size of the danger.** Whatever it was that needed to look good for them to take that backward step — the enemy painted it. All of it. The empty space where the object should be is exactly as large as your own capacity for self-flattery. [PAUSE — major]

#### The Second Instrument: *Amlā* — Lengthening the Rope

And then the second verb. **وَأَمْلَىٰ** — *wa amlā.* The root is *m-l-w / m-l-y*, and it carries the meaning of *length* — *malāwah* is a long stretch of time, an extended span. Form IV, *amlā*, means **to extend, to prolong, to give a long rope, to stretch out the horizon.** And in this neighborhood lives the word *amal* — hope, expectation, the imagined future.

The commentators are precise. Al-Hasan again: *madda lahum fī-l-amal* — "he stretched out their hope," *waʿadahum ṭūl al-ʿumr* — "he promised them a long life." *Amlā lahum* is the whisper that says: **there's time. Plenty of time. Later. You'll deal with it later. Tomorrow, next year, when things settle, when you're older, when you've had your fill — then you'll turn back. The horizon is far. Relax.**

Listen to this word's sound against the last one. *Sawwala* was soft and quick. *Amlā* — *am-lā* — ends on a long, open *alif*, a vowel that just... keeps... going. The mouth opens and the sound stretches out and trails off into the distance. **The word elongates exactly as the false horizon elongates.** You can hear the rope being let out. The phonetics are the prolonging.

And now see the two instruments together, and see how they are perfectly engineered to work as a pair — because they cover the two dimensions in which a human being lives. *Sawwala* governs **the present**: it makes the sin *in front of you, right now*, look acceptable. *Amlā* governs **the future**: it pushes the day of reckoning, the day of return, the day of repentance, far enough away that you never feel its breath on your neck. Beautify the now, and lengthen the later. **That is the complete machine.** That is how a person who has clearly seen the truth is kept walking backward, comfortable the whole way down — the present sin looks fine, and the future correction is always still available, always just over there, never quite today.

There is one more layer the reciters preserve. The word *amlā* carries two readings in the tradition. The people of Baṣrah read it *umliya* — passive — "and it was prolonged for them," leaving the prolonger unnamed. Al-Jalālayn notes that the *mumlī*, the one extending the rope, is the Shaytan *by Allah's will* — "so He is the One who lets them stray." This is the doctrine of *istidrāj*, the slow rope: that when a person insists on the backward walk, the rope of time and ease is *let out* for them — not as a kindness, but as the most frightening thing in the Qur'an, a respite that is itself a sentence. The long horizon the enemy promises is, in the deepest reading, *granted* — and that very granting is the trap closing. [PAUSE — major]

What these two verbs reveal together: **the retreat from clarity is never a single choice. It is a present sin made to look beautiful, married to a future reckoning made to look distant. Cut either one, and the machine stops.**

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Sin of the Sighted — Why Knowing Is Not Enough

Let me start where you actually live, not in the seventh century.

You know someone — maybe it's you — who knows *exactly* what they need to do. They could give the lecture. They could counsel a friend through the very same problem with wisdom and clarity. The smoker who can recite the science. The person in the toxic relationship who can name every red flag out loud. The one who knows the prayer is at the center of everything, who has *felt* what it does to a day, who could tell you with total conviction that life is better with it — and who hasn't prayed Fajr on time in months.

We have a quiet, comfortable lie in modern culture: that the problem is always *information*. That if people just *knew better*, they'd *do better*. So we pour everything into knowing. More content, more reminders, more clarity.

And then this ayah walks in and quietly demolishes the whole premise.

Because these people in 47:25 had perfect information. *Tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā* — the guidance had *made itself fully clear to them.* They were not under-informed. They were, if anything, over-informed. They had the *bayinah*, the complete clarity. And they walked backward anyway, eyes open, faces still pointed at the light.

[PAUSE]

This should be a relief and a terror at once. A relief, because it means your spiritual struggle is *not* a knowledge deficit you can never quite fill, and you can stop pretending one more course, one more book, one more reminder is the missing piece. And a terror, because it strips away your favorite excuse. You will stand before Allah not as someone who didn't know — but, for so much of what matters, as someone for whom it had *become clear.*

Here is the movement that the verse forces. The gap between *knowing* and *doing* is not made of ignorance. It is made of two things, and they are the exact two things the ayah just named. The gap is filled with *tasweel* — the present sin dressed up so it doesn't look like a betrayal of what you know — and *imlā* — the comfortable lie that you have time, that today's gap can be closed tomorrow. **Knowledge does not fail you. Knowledge gets *overruled* — outvoted by a beautified present and a postponed future.**

And notice the mercy buried in this diagnosis. If your problem were ignorance, the cure would be endless and exhausting — you would have to *learn* your way to salvation, and the finish line would always recede. But if your problem is *tasweel* and *imlā*, then the cure is not more information. It is two specific acts of unmasking. **Refuse the beautification: look at the sin and force yourself to see it ugly, as it actually is, the way you'd see it in someone else.** And **refuse the long rope: act as though the horizon is today, because in truth you were never promised tomorrow.**

What this changes about tomorrow: stop waiting to *understand* before you *obey*. For the things that have already *tabayyana* — become clear to you — the seeing is done. You are not waiting on more light. You are deciding whether to walk toward the light you already have, or backward away from it. Pick the one clear thing you already know, and do it before the day ends.

### Theme Two: The Long Rope — Procrastination as a Spiritual Weapon

Now let me name the second instrument by its everyday face, because it is so ordinary we don't even flag it as the enemy's work.

Procrastination.

We treat it as a productivity problem — a quirk, a bad habit, something a better calendar would fix. But watch how the Qur'an classifies it. *Amlā lahum* — the lengthening of hope, the stretching of the horizon — is placed *side by side with sawwala* as one of the two master tools of the enemy of mankind. Procrastination of the soul is not a scheduling flaw. **It is, in the Qur'an's own architecture, a weapon of Shaytan.**

Here is why it is so devastatingly effective, and why the enemy reaches for it more than almost anything else. Outright disbelief is a hard sell to a heart that has seen clarity — *tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā*; that heart *knows*. You cannot easily convince it that the truth is false. But you don't have to. You only have to convince it that the truth can be answered *later.* You don't argue against the prayer; you simply move it ten minutes down the road, and then ten more, until the window closes. You don't argue against repentance; you agree it's essential — *so essential that surely you'll do it properly, soon, when the time is right.* [PAUSE]

Shaytan rarely says, "Don't return to Allah." That's too obvious; the sighted heart resists it. He says, *"Return to Allah — absolutely — just not today."* And because you have *agreed* to return, you feel righteous. You feel like a believer with a plan. The long rope feels like a generous road. You cannot see that it is a noose, because *amlā* — the word itself — is so open and stretched and pleasant-sounding that the lengthening feels like *freedom*, like breathing room, like mercy.

And this is where the doctrine of *istidrāj* makes the hair stand up. Recall the passive reading: *umliya lahum* — "it *was* prolonged for them." The rope, in the deepest reading, is genuinely *let out.* The years do come. The horizon does stay distant. The reckoning does keep not arriving. And the person reads all of that as confirmation: *see, there's still time, nothing has happened, the horizon is still far.* The very respite they were granted becomes the proof of their lie. The mercy of delay, when you build your retreat on it, curdles into the machinery of your ruin. Allah says elsewhere with chilling restraint: *"Leave them to eat and enjoy and be diverted by [false] hope — for they are going to know"* (15:3). The same word-family. The same long rope.

Let the paradox sit without rushing to fix it: the time you were *given* as a gift is the same time the enemy uses as a *weapon*. The horizon is real. Whether it is a runway or a trap depends entirely on whether you treat it as *room to return* or *room to delay returning.* Identical span of days. Opposite destinations.

What this changes about tomorrow — and notice I have to say *tomorrow*, even here, and you should feel the irony — is brutally simple. The defense against *amlā* is to collapse the horizon. To live as though the rope is short, because in truth you were shown nothing about its length. The believer's posture toward the future is not "I have time" but "I may not." Not morbid — *clarifying.* The one act of *tawbah* you keep filing under "later," the one relationship you keep meaning to repair, the one command that has already *tabayyana* to you — bring it from the far horizon to *this hour.* Shorten the rope yourself before it is shortened for you.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now that we have done the work, stand back and look at the whole verse as a single built structure, because its shape is the argument.

It divides cleanly in two.

**The first half is the visible act:** *Inna alladhīna-rtaddū ʿalā adbārihim min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahumu-l-hudā* — they turned their own selves backward, on their heels, eyes still open, after the guidance had made itself completely clear. This is what you could *see* from the outside — the hypocrite's face falling when the demanding surah was revealed.

**The second half is the hidden cause:** *ash-shayṭānu sawwala lahum wa amlā lahum* — Shaytan beautified, and lengthened. This is the machinery you could *not* see — the airbrushing of the present, the stretching of the future.

And the hinge between them is the silent, screaming question: *why would the sighted retreat?* The structure itself poses the question in the first half and answers it in the second.

Now look at the thread Allah sews through the entire verse — the single repeated word: **لَهُمْ — *lahum*, "to them / for them."** It appears three times. *Tabayyana **lahum*** al-hudā — the guidance became clear *to them.* *Sawwala **lahum*** — he beautified *for them.* *Amlā **lahum*** — he lengthened *for them.* [PAUSE]

The same *lām*, the same recipients. The exact hands that *received the guidance* are the hands that then *received the beautification and the long rope.* The clarity and the seduction were poured into the very same vessel. There is no neutral ground in this verse. Whatever is given to a human being — *lahum* — is either guidance received or guidance overwritten. The space that does not hold the *hudā* does not stay empty. It fills with *tasweel* and *imlā.* That triple *lahum* is the whole human drama compressed into one syllable, said three times.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same truth seen from two sides.

The grammar of *irtaddū* — the self-administered, reflexive turning — and the theme of the sighted sinner are one insight: **no one is pushed off the path; the catastrophe is always, at the final moment, self-administered, and it happens to those who saw.** And the two verbs *sawwala* and *amlā* are the same thing as the two themes of knowledge-is-not-enough and procrastination-as-weapon: **the gap between seeing and doing is bridged by the enemy with exactly two planks — a beautified present and a postponed future — and the entire spiritual life is the work of pulling up those two planks.**

### Questions to Carry

1. What is the one thing that has already *tabayyana* to me — become fully clear — that I am still walking backward away from, eyes open?
2. When I look honestly at a sin I keep returning to, can I see the *tasweel* on it — the specific way it has been made to look reasonable, justified, "not that bad"? What does it look like with the paint stripped off?
3. What act of return have I filed under "later"? And what, exactly, makes me so confident the rope is that long?
4. Is the time I've been given functioning, in my life, as a runway for return — or as room to delay returning?
5. When the demanding command comes — the costly one — does my face fall the way theirs did? What does my recoil reveal about what I've been concealing?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**The ruin of the soul is not failing to see the truth, but being walked backward away from a truth already seen — by an enemy who makes today's sin look beautiful and tomorrow's reckoning look far.**

### The Closing Invitation

You came into this verse perhaps expecting a warning about other people — the apostates, the hypocrites, the ones who left. Leave it knowing the verse handed you a mirror and a map. The mirror: *you* are, in some corner of your life, one of *alladhīna-rtaddū ʿalā adbārihim* — someone walking backward from something already clear. The map: it is being done to you with exactly two tools, and now you can name them both. Catch the beautification when the ugly thing starts to look fine. Catch the long rope when "later" starts to feel like mercy. **You do not need more light. You have seen.** You need only to stop walking backward, turn your face the way your feet should go, and take one step today — not on the far horizon the enemy keeps pointing to, but in the hour you actually hold.

### Du'ā

O Allah, You who made the guidance clear to us — *tabayyana lanā* — do not let us be among those who saw and turned. Strip the false beauty from every sin we have learned to excuse, and shorten in our hearts the long rope of "later" until we return to You today, while it is still today. Keep our faces toward Your light, and forbid our feet from the backward step.

*Āmīn.*
