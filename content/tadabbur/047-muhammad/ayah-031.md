# Until We Make It Evident

## The Ayah

> [ayah:47:31] وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ حَتَّىٰ نَعْلَمَ ٱلْمُجَـٰهِدِينَ مِنكُمْ وَٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ وَنَبْلُوَا۟ أَخْبَارَكُمْ

*Wa-la-nabluwannakum ḥattā naʿlama-l-mujāhidīna minkum waṣ-ṣābirīna wa-nabluwa akhbārakum.*

**"And We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you and those who are patient, and We will test your affairs."**

---

## Introduction: Rebuilding the World

To stand inside this ayah, you have to feel the air of the surah it lives in.

Surah Muhammad is sometimes called *Surat al-Qitāl* — the Surah of Fighting. It was revealed in the early Madinan years, when the small community of believers was no longer being persecuted quietly in Makkah but was now facing the open question of survival. And in that pressure, something became visible that comfort had kept hidden.

There were people in Madinah who said the words. Who prayed in the same rows. Who sat in the same gatherings. And when the moment of real cost arrived — when standing for the faith meant standing where blades were drawn — a sickness in certain hearts surfaced. The surah just before this ayah describes them with chilling precision: people in whose hearts was a disease, who looked at the Prophet ﷺ "with the look of one fainting at death."

The community could not see into those hearts. The believer sitting next to the hypocrite could not see the difference. From the outside, the sincere and the false wore the same face, spoke the same testimony, made the same prostration.

And then the pressure came. And the faces stayed the same — but the feet did not. Some moved forward. Some held their ground under blows. And some quietly slipped away.

[PAUSE]

That is the world this ayah answers. It is Allah stepping forward to explain what just happened — and to declare it not an accident, not a tragedy, but a **method**. A deliberate divine procedure.

But here is the hook. Here is the thing that should stop you.

Allah says He will test them **"until We know."**

Until *We* know.

The One who knows the secret and what is more hidden than the secret. The One who knew you before your mother carried you. The One from whom not an atom's weight is hidden in the heavens or the earth.

*That* One says: I will test you **until I know.**

What does it mean for the All-Knowing to test in order to know? Does Allah learn something? Does the test give Him information He lacked?

Stay with that question. Because the answer is not a loophole or an embarrassment to be explained away. The answer is one of the most liberating truths in the entire Quran about what your trials are actually *for*.

# Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

## Element One: The Oath You Cannot Hear in English

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we touch the word.

In English, if I want to emphasize a promise, I add words around it. "I will *definitely* do this. I really, truly will." The emphasis is bolted on from outside.

Arabic does something different and far more powerful. It builds the emphasis **into the bones of the verb itself.** It has two specific tools for swearing an unbreakable promise, and it can stack them on a single word.

The first is the **lām of oath** — a *la-* prefix that signals: this is sworn. The second is the **nūn of emphasis** — *the energetic nūn* — a heavy *-anna* ending that hammers the verb shut, as if to say *this will happen, without fail, there is no version of reality where it does not.*

Now look at the very first word of our ayah:

> وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ — *wa-la-nabluwannakum*

Both tools are present. The *la-* at the front. The doubled, weighted *-wanna-* before the ending. This is not Allah saying "We will test you." [PAUSE]

This is Allah **swearing an oath** that He will test you. The grammar carries the full weight of a divine vow.

Let's sit with what that means.

You and I tend to experience our trials as interruptions. As deviations from the plan. The plan was the smooth life, the easy iman, the untested faith — and then *this* happened. The illness. The loss. The fear. The thing that came and broke the surface.

But the grammar of this word tells you the trial was **not** the interruption. The trial was the *promise*. It was sworn into existence before it reached you. The smoothness was never the plan. The testing was the plan — sworn, sealed, certain.

Notice something. Allah does not swear oaths casually in the Quran. When the divine voice takes the form of an unbreakable vow, it is marking something as a fixed law of the universe, as reliable as sunrise. And here, the thing being sworn is: **you will be tested.**

So when the test comes, the believer who has heard this word does not ask the panicked question, *"Why is this happening to me?"* — as though something has gone wrong. He recognizes an oath being kept. *Of course. He promised.* [PAUSE]

What this single word reveals: **your testing is not a glitch in a good life. It is a vow Allah made, and Allah keeps His vows.**

## Element Two: The Verb That Should Not Be There

Now we arrive at the heart of the ayah. The word that creates the entire problem.

First, the concept. There are two completely different things we lazily call by one English word: "to know."

There is the knowing of *information* — learning a fact you did not have. You did not know the answer; now you do. Something entered your mind from outside. Call this **acquired** knowledge.

And there is a second kind. A teacher who has watched a student for a year already knows, in her heart, that he is gifted. But on exam day, the gift becomes a **score**. A grade. A documented, external, undeniable fact that exists now in the world for everyone to see. The teacher learned nothing new. But something that was *interior* became *exterior*. Something known *privately* became known *publicly, in the open, as a manifested reality.* Call this **knowledge of manifestation** — knowing-as-it-becomes-visible.

Hold those two apart. Now look:

> حَتَّىٰ نَعْلَمَ — *ḥattā naʿlama* — "until We know."

The root here is *ʿ-l-m*, the root of *ʿilm*, knowledge — the very root that gives us *al-ʿAlīm*, the All-Knowing, one of Allah's own names.

And the theological alarm should be ringing. Because if this means *acquired* knowledge — Allah learning a fact He did not possess — then it contradicts the entire Quran. Allah tells us plainly that He knows what is in the wombs, knows the secret and the hidden, knew you in the depths before you breathed. The All-Knowing does not wait for an exam to find out who is sincere.

So the classical scholars did not flinch from this. They opened the word — and notice, they did not give us competing guesses. They gave us a single reality seen from several angles.

Al-Baghawi names it directly: *ʿilm al-wujūd* — **the knowledge of actualization.** Not Allah coming to know, but the thing becoming *actual*, becoming *existent in the open*, "so that the one who strives and the one who endures may become **distinct** from the other."

Al-Jalālayn calls it *ʿilm ẓuhūr* — **the knowledge of appearing, of emergence.**

And then the companions add their camera angles. Ibn ʿAbbās said: *ḥattā naʿlama* means *ḥattā numayyiz* — **"until We distinguish, until We separate one from the other."** And ʿAlī ﷺ said: *ḥattā naʿlama* means *ḥattā narā* — **"until We see it made visible."**

[PAUSE]

Do you feel what they are all circling?

The test does not inform Allah. **The test informs reality.** It takes what was already fully known to Allah in the unseen — your sincerity, or its absence — and drags it across the border into the seen, where it becomes an actual deed, a fact in the world, a thing that *happened*, a piece of evidence.

Here's what is remarkable. The knowledge was never the issue. Allah's knowledge was complete and eternal. What the test produces is not knowledge in *Him* — it is **manifestation in *you*.** The potential becomes the actual. The "I would have" becomes the "I did." The claim becomes the deed.

And now — *stay with me* — turn this toward yourself, because here is where it stops being theology and becomes mercy.

You say you love Allah. You say, in the quiet of a comfortable night, that you would sacrifice, that you would be patient, that you would hold firm. Beautiful words. But words about an untested self are only *potential*. They are unbanked currency. They have never been spent.

The test is the moment Allah lets your potential become **real** — lets it cross over from something you *believe about yourself* into something you have *actually done before Him.* The struggle is not Allah finding out who you are.

**The struggle is Allah giving you the chance to actually become it** — to convert the claim into a deed that is now permanently, gloriously yours, written, witnessed, real.

What this word reveals: **Allah does not test you to discover your worth. He tests you to let your worth come into existence as an act, instead of remaining a possibility in your heart.**

## Element Three: The Two Who Survive the Test, and the Record That Remains

The ayah does not say *until We know the believers.* It names two specific kinds of people — and then names a third thing that gets tested. Each word is chosen.

First: **ٱلْمُجَٰهِدِين** — *al-mujāhidīn*, "those who strive."

A point of Arabic. The corpus marks this word as built on **Form III**, the pattern of *mufāʿalah*. Form III is the form of **reciprocal action — of doing something *against* a counterforce that pushes back.** When you wrestle, you are not lifting a dead weight; you are straining against something that strains against you. *Jihād* in this form is not effort in a vacuum. It is effort **against resistance** — against an enemy outside, and against the heavier enemy inside, the nafs that pulls the other way.

So *mujāhid* is not merely the busy or the active person. It is the one locked in struggle with something that fights back.

Then, paired with it: **ٱلصَّٰبِرِين** — *aṣ-ṣābirīn*, "those who are patient, who endure."

Notice the pairing, because it is doing precise work. *Jihād* is the **forward** energy — the push, the advance, the doing. *Ṣabr* is the **holding** energy — the staying, the not-breaking, the absorbing of blows without giving way. One is the verb of motion. The other is the verb of stillness under pressure.

And Allah names *both* — because the test exposes people who can do one but not the other. There is the one who can charge forward in a burst of zeal but cannot *hold* when the struggle becomes long, grinding, and unglamorous. And there is the one who can endure passively but never *moves*. Real faith, the ayah quietly tells us, is the marriage of the two: **the strength to advance and the strength to stay.** To strive *and* to outlast the striving.

[PAUSE]

And now the final word — the one most translations flatten, and the one that completes the meaning:

> وَنَبْلُوَا۟ أَخْبَارَكُمْ — *wa-nabluwa akhbārakum* — "and We will test your *akhbār*."

The root is *kh-b-r*. And from this root comes the everyday Arabic word *khabar* — **news. A report. The story told about a person.** *Akhbār* is its plural: your reports, your record, the **account of you** that exists out in the world.

Look at what Allah does here. He does not repeat "We will test *you*." He shifts the object. He says, "We will test your **news** — the *story* of you."

Al-Baghawi unfolds it beautifully: *nubliya akhbārakum* means **"We will bring your record out, expose it, uncover it"** — make manifest who refused and who advanced, who broke and who held. Al-Muyassar adds: until "the truthful among you is distinguished from the liar."

Do you see the architecture now? The ayah *opens* with a test — *wa-la-nabluwanna* — and it *closes* with a test — *wa-nabluwa*. The whole verse is bracketed, sealed, between two testings. And what sits in the middle? The making-evident, the distinguishing, the bringing-into-the-open.

The first test is the *experience* — the trial you live through. The second test concerns your **akhbār** — what the trial *writes about you.* Because every test does two things at once. It happens *to* you. And it becomes a *report about* you — a line in the account, a piece of news in the registers of the unseen, a fact about who you turned out to be when it cost something.

What these final words reveal: **your trials are not only experiences you undergo — they are testimony being authored. Each one writes a true sentence about you into a record that will be read.**

Three discoveries, one current running through them.

# Part 2: The Thematic Depths

## Theme One: You Do Not Know Who You Are Until You Are Tested

Begin where you already live.

There is a strange and humbling fact about being human that every honest person eventually meets. **You do not actually know what you will do until you are in the moment.**

You can rehearse it a thousand times. You imagine the confrontation and you are brave in it. You imagine the temptation and you walk away from it. You imagine the loss and you are patient through it. In the theater of your mind, you are always the hero of your own story.

And then the real moment arrives — and you discover, sometimes to your horror and sometimes to your relief, that the person who actually showed up was not the person you rehearsed.

Soldiers describe this. They train for years, and they say no one truly knows how they will react under live fire until the live fire comes. People who imagined they would crumble find a steadiness they never knew they had. People who imagined themselves brave find their legs will not move.

The imagined self and the tested self are **two different people.** And only one of them is real.

This is the human ground the ayah stands on. *Ḥattā naʿlama* — "until it becomes manifest." Allah is not the one who needs to find out. **You** are. The test is the only instrument in existence that can tell you the truth about yourself — because it is the only place where the imagined self is forced to step aside and the real self has to act.

[PAUSE]

And here the ayah turns what feels like a threat into a gift.

We dread our trials because we experience them as Allah's judgment falling on us. But re-read the word. *Until We make evident.* The test is not the verdict. **The test is the place where you get to become the person you hoped you were.**

Think of the *mujāhid* and the *ṣābir* again. Before the battle, there was a man in Madinah who believed in his heart he was loyal. He had no way to *prove it* — not to others, and not even to himself. His loyalty was a private feeling, untested, unbanked. And then the test came. And he advanced. And he held. And in that moment his loyalty stopped being a feeling he had and became a **deed he did** — a real thing, weighed, recorded, his forever.

The test did not take something from him. The test let him **cash in his potential and walk away owning the deed.**

Now stand inside your own trial. The thing that is hard right now. The illness, the loss, the fear, the long grinding patience that no one sees. You have been asking, *why is this being done to me?*

The ayah offers a different question: *what is this letting me become?*

Because right now, in this exact difficulty, your patience is being converted from a word into a deed. Your trust is being moved from the realm of "I believe I would trust Allah" into the realm of "I *did* trust Allah, on that day, when it was dark." That cannot be manufactured in comfort. It can only be **earned in the test.** [PAUSE]

What this changes about tomorrow: the next time the hard thing comes, before you ask why, pause and recognize — *this is the moment my claim becomes a deed. This is where the imagined self steps aside and the real one is allowed to act.* And then act like the person you hoped you were. Because that person only gets to exist here.

## Theme Two: Your Trials Are Writing a True Story About You

Begin again where you live.

Every one of us is, quietly, the author of a record we cannot fully read. We call it many things — our reputation, our character, our legacy. But strip the words down and it is this: there is a **story of you** accumulating, one moment at a time, written not by your intentions but by your actions.

And here is what we all secretly know and rarely admit: the story is written most truthfully **in the hard moments, not the easy ones.**

Nobody learns your character from how you behaved on a good day. Anyone is generous when they have plenty. Anyone is calm when nothing is threatened. Anyone is faithful when faith costs nothing. The easy days write a story, but it is a story that could be anyone's. It carries no information.

The *true* sentence about you — the one that actually distinguishes you from every imposter wearing the same words — gets written on the day it cost you something. The day you were afraid and acted anyway. The day you were exhausted and held on anyway. The day the easy exit was right there and you did not take it.

That is what the final word of the ayah names. **أَخْبَارَكُمْ** — your *akhbār*, your record, the news of you. Allah swears that He will test it — meaning He will **bring it into existence and into the open.** He will let the true story of you get written, in the only ink that writes truly: the ink of tested action.

[PAUSE]

Stay with how merciful this actually is.

A world without tests would be a world in which the hypocrite and the believer remained forever indistinguishable — where the one who merely *said* the words and the one who *meant* them stood eternally in the same line, with nothing to tell them apart. The person who quietly meant every word of their faith would never get to have it **acknowledged as real,** not even in the books of the unseen. Their sincerity would die as a private feeling, unrecorded.

The test is what rescues sincerity from invisibility.

It is the divine refusal to let the true and the false be confused forever. It is Allah saying: *I will not let the one who actually loved Me be filed away next to the one who only performed it. I will give the sincere a day to make it real, and that day will be written, and it will be theirs.*

So the surah's hypocrites were not robbed by being exposed. **The sincere were honored by being given the chance to be distinct.** The same test that wrote the hypocrite's true sentence wrote the believer's true sentence too — and the believer's sentence was beautiful, and now it exists.

[PAUSE]

Now bring it home.

The thing you are enduring right now is not just happening *to* you. It is being **written about** you. Quietly, in this moment, a true sentence is being authored — a *khabar*, a report — and you are the one choosing what it says. Not by your feelings. By your response.

You will not remember most of your comfortable days. But the day you held firm when everything in you wanted to let go — that day becomes a permanent line in your *akhbār*. A piece of news about your soul that is **true**, that you **earned**, and that no one can ever take from you or claim was an accident.

What this changes about tomorrow: stop seeing your hardship as merely something to survive and get past. It is a page being written. Ask, in the middle of it — *what sentence am I authoring right now? What will the true story of me say about this day?* And then write the sentence you would be proud to have read back to you.

# Closing Synthesis

## The Architecture

Step back and look at the whole verse as a single shape now, because the work is done and you can finally see how it was built.

> **وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ** ← the test, sworn as an oath
> **حَتَّىٰ نَعْلَمَ** ← until it is made manifest
> **ٱلْمُجَٰهِدِينَ ... وَٱلصَّٰبِرِينَ** ← the one who advances, the one who holds
> **وَنَبْلُوَا۟ أَخْبَارَكُمْ** ← the test, again — now as a record written

The verse is **bracketed by testing.** It opens with *nabluwanna* and closes with *nabluwa* — the same root, *b-l-w*, sealing both ends like two bookends around a single shelf. And what sits on the shelf between them? The **making-evident** — the *naʿlama*, the bringing of the hidden into the open.

That is the entire architecture of a trial, drawn in eight words. It *enters* as an experience you must live. It *exits* as a record about who you were. And the whole purpose, sitting in the center, is **manifestation** — the conversion of the unseen into the seen, the potential into the actual, the claim into the deed.

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes were never two things. They were one truth approached from two doors.

The grammar told you: this word *naʿlama* does not mean Allah learns — it means the inward becomes outward. The themes told you: that is exactly what your trials do to *you* — they take the self you imagine and force the self you are into the open, and they take the faith you feel and write it into a record as a deed.

The oath sworn into the first verb told you the test is a divine promise, not an accident. The themes told you why that promise is a mercy: because only the test lets your sincerity become real and lets your true story get written in the one ink that does not lie.

It is all the same insight. **Allah, who already knows everything about you, tests you not to find out who you are — but to let who you are come into existence, become visible, and be written down as yours.**

2. In the trial you are inside right now — are you a *mujāhid* who advances, a *ṣābir* who holds, or are you waiting to be both? Which one is harder for you, the moving or the staying?
3. If your *akhbār* — the true record of you — were read back, what would today's sentence say?
4. What claim about yourself have you been carrying unbanked, untested, never converted into a deed? What test would convert it?

## One-Sentence Distillation

**Allah, who knows all, swears to test you — not to discover who you are, but to bring who you are out of the hidden and into the open, where your sincerity becomes a deed and your trial becomes a true sentence written about your soul.**

## The Closing Invitation

Let something shift. Not the trials — those were sworn, and they will come; that is settled. Let the *meaning* of them shift. The next time the hard thing arrives, before the panic and the *why me*, let one quiet recognition rise first: *He promised this. And He is not interested in finding out who I am — He already knows. He is giving me the chance to become it, and to have it written.*

Then meet the test as the gift it is: the only place on earth where the person you hope you are is allowed to actually exist.
