# The Ones Who Walk Toward the Light Are Given More Light

## A Reflection on Surah Muhammad, Ayah 17

---

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the ayah first. Hold it lightly for now — we are going to spend the better part of an hour learning to see what is inside it.

> **وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ**
>
> *Walladhīna-htadaw zādahum hudan wa-ātāhum taqwāhum*
>
> **"And those who let themselves be guided — He increases them in guidance, and grants them their God-consciousness."**

Six words. That's it. Six words in Arabic, and the whole thing could be carried in a single breath.

But before we open it, I want you to stand somewhere with me first. Because this ayah does not arrive in a vacuum. It is an answer. And to feel the force of an answer, you have to have heard the question.

### Rebuild the world

Picture the Prophet's gathering in Madinah. The mosque is not yet a building of marble and chandeliers. It is mud-brick and palm-trunk, and the floor is sand. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ is speaking — revelation is coming down, the most precious words a human ear has ever received — and the room is full.

But not everyone in that room is the same.

In the verse just before ours, Allah describes a certain kind of listener. They sit there. The words wash over them. And the moment they walk out of the door, they turn to those who were given knowledge and they ask — and the Arabic of 47:16 captures the sneer in it — *"Mādhā qāla ānifan?"* — **"What did he just say a moment ago?"**

Sit with that for a second. They were *just there*. The words had *just* been spoken. And already — nothing. It has run through them like water through a cupped hand. Allah says of them in that same passage: these are the ones *"whose hearts Allah has sealed."*

That is the world this ayah lives in. A room where the **same revelation** is falling on every head — and in some hearts it evaporates the instant it lands, and in other hearts...

Something else happens.

### The Hook

Here is what I want you to notice, and what we are going to chase for the rest of this reflection.

When Allah describes the second group — the ones it *worked* on — He does not say He gave them guidance. They already have guidance. That is what makes them the second group.

He says He gave them **more**.

And then He says something stranger still. He gives them something — and in the very act of giving it, He calls it **theirs**. He hands them a gift that, somehow, already belongs to them.

[PAUSE]

What does it mean that guidance is not a destination you arrive at, but a momentum that compounds? And what does it mean that the deepest thing Allah gives you is something He insists on calling *yours* — even as He is the one handing it over?

Let's find out.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Verb That Means You Leaned In

#### Teaching the concept first

I want to teach you something about Arabic verbs before we touch the ayah, because once you have it, you will never read this verse the same way.

In Arabic, a single root — a skeleton of usually three consonants — can be poured into different *molds*, and each mold changes the meaning in a precise, predictable way. Scholars number these molds as Forms, one through ten.

Let me make it concrete in plain English. Imagine the difference between these three sentences:

- "He learned." — something happened.
- "He **taught** someone." — he *caused* the action in another.
- "He **took it upon himself** to learn." — he reached in, pulled the action toward himself, made it his own project.

Same idea — learning — but that third one has a particular flavor, doesn't it? It's not passive. It's not something done *to* him. He **leaned into it**. He took it on.

In Arabic, that third flavor — the *I-reached-in-and-took-this-action-into-myself* flavor — has a specific home. It's called **Form VIII**, the pattern *ifta'ala*. The tell-tale sign is a little **ت** that burrows into the middle of the word. And what that ت does, again and again across the language, is turn an action *reflexive* — it folds the action back onto the doer. The doer is not just doing the thing. The doer is **personally taken up in it**.

#### Opening the word

Now look at the first thing Allah says about the people of this ayah.

**وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟** — *walladhīna-htadaw* — "and those who **ٱهْتَدَوْا**."

The root here is **ه-د-ي** (h-d-y) — the root of *hudā*, guidance, the showing of a path. And the word ٱهْتَدَوْا is that root poured into exactly the mold we just learned. **Form VIII.** Can you hear the buried ت? *Ih-**t**-adaw.* There it is.

So this is not the passive word. Allah does not say *walladhīna hudū* — "those who *were guided*," as if guidance were rain that happened to fall on them. He does not even use the simplest form. He reaches for the form that means **they took the guidance into themselves**.

#### Deepening

Let's sit with what that does.

The most natural English translation is "those who are guided." But that's flat. It loses the whole grammatical event. A truer rendering — clumsier, but honest — would be:

**"Those who let themselves be guided."**
**"Those who took the path on."**
**"Those who reached for the guidance and pulled it inside."**

Notice something. The grammar has quietly told us that guidance is **never one-directional**. Guidance, in the Quran's own verb, is not a thing that is simply *poured into* a passive vessel. It is a thing that is *received by someone who reached out their hands*.

And now — now go back to the room.

Remember the first group? The ones who heard the exact same words and walked out asking "what did he just say?" The revelation reached their ears. The sound waves hit their eardrums. By any external measure, they were *exposed* to guidance. The guidance came *to* them.

But it never became *ٱهْتَدَوْا*. They never leaned in. There was no ت folding the action back into the self. It stayed outside them, on the surface, and rolled off.

[PAUSE]

Here is what this single verb form reveals, and I want you to feel the weight of it: **You can be in the presence of guidance and not be guided.** The revelation can be in the air you breathe, in the masjid you sit in, in the surah playing in your car — and none of it lands, not because Allah withheld it, but because the ت was never there. Because you never reached in and took it on.

The guided are not the ones who were *near* the light. They are the ones who **turned toward it and walked**.

[PAUSE — let it settle]

### Element Two: The Increase That Has No Ceiling

#### Teaching the concept first

Here's a small feature of Arabic that does enormous work, and most people read right past it.

In Arabic, a noun can be *definite* — "the guidance," a specific, bounded, known quantity — or *indefinite*, marked by a little sound at the end called *tanwīn*, a soft *-un* or *-an*. And indefiniteness in Arabic often does something beautiful: it throws the doors open. It can signal *vastness*, *some-amount-beyond-reckoning*, *a kind you cannot pin down or measure*.

Think of the difference, in feeling, between "I gave him the money" — a known sum — and "I gave him *wealth*." The second one is bigger precisely because it is *unspecified*. Your mind cannot draw a box around it.

Keep that in your pocket.

#### Opening the word

Allah says: **زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى** — *zādahum hudan* — "He increased them — **هُدًۭى**."

The root **ز-ي-د** (z-y-d) means increase, growth, addition, surplus. And the object of that increase is **هُدًۭى** — *hudan*. Guidance.

But listen to the end of that word. *Hud**an**.* That's the tanwīn. **Indefinite.** Allah does not say He increased them in *the* guidance, a fixed dose. He says He increased them in *guidance* — open, unbounded, unmeasured.

#### Deepening

Let's draw out what's hidden here, because there are two astonishing things folded into these two words.

**The first:** the reward for guidance is *guidance itself*.

Stop and feel how unusual that is. You'd expect the verse to say: those who are guided — He rewards them with Paradise. He gives them peace. He gives them light on the Day of Judgment. All true elsewhere. But here, the reward for taking on guidance is... **more of the same thing.** The prize for walking is the strength to walk further. The reward for the first step is the second step.

This is one of the most important spiritual laws in the entire Quran, and it sits in two words. **Guidance compounds.** It is not a switch that flips once. It is an account that gathers interest. Every act of *ihtidā'* — every leaning-in — is met by Allah with an increase, so that the next leaning-in is easier, deeper, more whole.

The classical commentators saw exactly this and named its many faces. Look at how al-Qurṭubī gathers them — and notice he does *not* make you choose between them, he lets them stack:

> Some said the increase is in **knowledge** (*zādahum ʿilman*). Some said it is that **they acted on what they learned, so they were given more** — knowledge that becomes action that earns more knowledge. Some said it is increased **insight into their religion** (*baṣīra fī dīnihim*) — their certainty *multiplies* (*yataḍāʿafu yaqīnuhum*).

Do you hear that word — *yataḍāʿafu*, "multiplies, doubles upon itself"? That is the language of compound growth, fourteen centuries before anyone graphed an exponential curve. Their certainty doesn't add; it *multiplies*.

And al-Qurṭubī preserves something even more striking. One narration — from al-Farrāʾ — says: what *increased* their guidance was **the mockery of the hypocrites themselves**. *Zādahum iʿrāḍu l-munāfiqīna wa-stihzāʾuhum hudan.* The very turning-away of the sealed-hearted, the very sneering "what did he just say" — for the people who had leaned in, *even that became fuel.* The thing that emptied one group filled the other.

[PAUSE]

**The second** astonishing thing is the tanwīn we put in our pocket. *Hudan.* Indefinite. Unbounded.

There is no ceiling on this. Allah does not say He increased them by a measured amount and then the tap closes. He leaves the quantity wide open, unmeasured, because **the capacity of a heart that keeps leaning in has no known limit.** As long as you keep reaching, the increase keeps coming, in an amount your mind cannot draw a box around.

So here is what these two words reveal: guidance is not a finish line you cross. It is a **momentum you enter.** And once you're in it — once you've genuinely taken that first step inward — Allah's response is not a single reward but an **endless, compounding, ceilingless increase**, where even your obstacles get converted into fuel.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Three: The Gift He Calls Yours

This is the one. If you remember nothing else from this hour, I want you to carry this.

#### Teaching the concept first

Let me set up a small puzzle in plain language.

If I *give* you something, then before I gave it, it was mine and not yours. That's what giving means. The word "give" only makes sense if the thing was *not already yours.* You don't "give" someone their own wallet — you *return* it.

So hold that logic: **giving implies the thing wasn't theirs yet.**

Now also notice how Arabic, like English, attaches ownership with a tiny suffix. **هُمْ** — *-hum* — "their." Tack it onto a noun and the noun becomes *theirs*. *Bayt* = a house; *baytuhum* = **their** house. Simple. That little هُمْ is a flag of possession: *this belongs to them.*

Keep both of those in mind — "give means it wasn't yours" and "the -hum suffix means it's yours" — because Allah is about to put them in the same two words, and they should not be able to coexist.

#### Opening the word

**وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ** — *wa-ātāhum taqwāhum* — "and He **gave them** — **their** taqwā."

Look at it slowly.

**ءَاتَىٰهُمْ** — *ātā-hum*. The root is **أ-ت-ي** (ʾ-t-y), here in Form IV, *ātā*, which means precisely **to give, to grant, to hand over.** The verb of giving. The verb that, by its own logic, means the thing was *not yet theirs.*

And the object He gives them? **تَقْوَىٰهُمْ** — *taqwā-**hum***. Their taqwā. With the هُمْ of ownership stamped right onto it. *Their* God-consciousness. *Their* protection.

#### Deepening

Do you see the collision?

He **gives** them — which means it wasn't theirs.
He gives them **theirs** — which means it was.

Allah could so easily have closed that gap. He could have said *wa-ātāhumu t-taqwā* — "He gave them taqwā," plain, definite, no ownership suffix. Clean. Logical. A gift from outside. He chose not to. He deliberately, against the grain of the very verb He just used, called it **theirs.**

[PAUSE]

Stay with me here, because this is the heart of the whole ayah.

What is *taqwā*? The root is **و-ق-ي** (w-q-y), and its physical, bodily meaning — the meaning an Arab in the desert would have felt first — is **to shield, to guard, to protect from harm.** *Wiqāya* is a barrier you put between yourself and something that would hurt you. Taqwā is the inner shield of God-consciousness — the awareness that guards you from the fire.

And the commentators, reading this very word, point straight at that meaning. Al-Jalālayn says *ātāhum taqwāhum* means **"He inspired them with that by which they guard themselves from the Fire"** — *alhamahum mā yattaqūna bihi n-nār.* Al-Muyassar: **"He granted them success in taqwā and made it easy for them"** — *waffaqahum li-t-taqwā wa-yassarahā lahum.* Al-Baghawī: **"He gave them success in acting upon what He commanded them"** — and then preserves Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's reading: *wa-ātāhum thawāba taqwāhum* — **"He gave them the reward of their taqwā."**

Now watch how every one of those readings lives inside the collision instead of resolving it.

The taqwā is **theirs** — because they are the ones doing the guarding, the fearing, the restraining. It is *their* effort, *their* striving, *their* shield raised by *their* arm. No one can be God-conscious on your behalf. In that sense it could not be more *yours*.

And yet He **gives** it — because the success to do it (*tawfīq*), the inspiration that whispers *guard yourself here* (*ilhām*), the ease that makes the shield light in the hand (*yassarahā*) — that descends from Him. You raise the shield; He is the one who put the strength in your arm and the awareness in your eye and the very impulse to lift it at all.

[PAUSE]

So which is it? Is taqwā something you earn, or something you're given?

**Yes.**

That is the answer the grammar forces on us. It is *both*, fused so tightly that Allah will not let the verb and the suffix be separated. *He gives you your own striving.* The most intimate, hard-won, personal thing about you — your own God-consciousness, the shield you raised with your own hand — even *that*, Allah insists, was a gift from Him. And the gift, the moment it lands, becomes so truly yours that He calls it by your name.

This single phrase dissolves the oldest false choice in the religious heart: *"Did I do this, or did God do this?"* The ayah answers: **stop choosing.** He gave you what is yours. Both are true at once, and only the arrogant or the despairing need to pick.

#### Bridge

Look back now at the three words we've opened. **ٱهْتَدَوْا** — they leaned in. **زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى** — He met every lean with a ceilingless increase. **ءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ** — and what they earned by their own striving, He hands back to them as His gift, calling it theirs.

The grammar has been telling us a story about *how a soul and its Lord move together.* Now let's ask what that reveals — not just about words, but about how you are actually meant to live.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: Guidance Is Not a Door You Walk Through. It's a Current You Step Into.

Let me start where you already live.

Think about the last time you tried to build something real in yourself. Maybe it was fitness. The first week is agony. Every rep is negotiation, every morning the alarm is an enemy. You are *manufacturing* discipline out of nothing, by sheer will, and it is exhausting.

But you've also felt the other side. Somewhere around week six, something shifts. You don't *decide* to go to the gym anymore — you just go. The momentum carries you. The thing that used to cost you everything now costs you almost nothing, and skipping it feels worse than doing it. You've crossed from *forcing* into *flow*.

Or think of learning. The first hundred words of a new language are brutal — each one a separate act of memory. But there is a tipping point, every learner knows it, where suddenly new words *stick on contact*, because you now have somewhere to hang them. The more you know, the easier it becomes to know more. Knowledge **compounds into capacity for more knowledge.**

[PAUSE]

Now hear the ayah again: **وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى.** "Those who leaned into guidance — He increased them in guidance."

This is the same law, lifted from the gym and the textbook and placed at the center of your soul. **Guidance is not binary.** It is not that you are either "guided" — saved, finished, done — or "lost." That's the model most of us secretly carry, and it's why guided people get complacent and struggling people get hopeless. Both have misunderstood the physics.

The Quran's physics is *momentum.* Every single act of leaning in — every prayer you actually showed up *inside* of, every time you read the Quran and let it land instead of letting it run off like the hypocrites' "what did he say," every small *no* to what you knew was wrong — is met by Allah with an *increase.* He makes the next step lighter. He deepens the channel so the water flows faster. You are not maintaining a fixed account; you are in a **current that gathers speed the longer you stay in it.**

And here is the sober flip-side, because the current runs both ways. The hypocrites of 47:16 didn't get sealed in a single dramatic act. They got sealed by *the opposite momentum* — each turning-away making the next turning-away easier, each "what did he say?" hollowing the channel a little more, until nothing could stay. Allah states this elsewhere as the same law in reverse: *"so when they deviated, Allah caused their hearts to deviate"* (61:5). Drift compounds too.

Which means the most important question is never *"Am I guided?"* — as if checking a box. The real question is: **Which direction is my momentum pointing right now?** Am I leaning in, even slightly, today? Because Allah's response to a slight lean is not slight. It is *zādahum hudan* — an unmeasured, ceilingless increase.

**What this changes tomorrow:** Stop waiting to feel "guided enough" to act. You will never feel ready, because readiness is not the entry fee — *leaning in is.* Pick the one smallest act of *ihtidā'* available to you tomorrow — the two rak'ahs, the ten minutes of Quran you actually sit *inside*, the single sin you turn from — and do it *not* because it will fix everything, but because it is a deposit into a compounding account. You supply the lean. Allah supplies the increase. That is the deal this ayah is offering you.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: The Thing You Built Was a Gift All Along

Here's a tension you have felt, even if you've never named it.

There are two ways people relate to their own goodness, and both of them are poisoned.

The first is **pride.** "I pray. I fast. I gave that money, I held my tongue, I built this discipline. *I* did that." And the moment you own it like that — fully, as your achievement — something rots. You start looking down. You start keeping score. Your worship becomes a résumé. You've all met the person whose religiosity has curdled into superiority, and maybe, in honest moments, you've been that person.

The second poison is the opposite: **despair and self-erasure.** "Nothing I do is really mine. It's all just God anyway. My effort is an illusion. So why bother? Why struggle, if I'm not really the one struggling?" This sounds humble but it's actually paralysis. It cuts the nerve of effort. If none of it is yours, then there's no *you* in the room to grow.

Pride says *it's all mine.* Despair says *it's all His and therefore I'm nothing.* And for centuries, serious souls have felt forced to pick a side.

[PAUSE]

Now hold up these four words against that whole exhausting argument: **وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ.** "And He gave them *their* taqwā."

Feel how it refuses both poisons at once.

To the proud one, it says: *that taqwā you're so impressed with? I gave it to you.* The success to do it, the awareness that prompted it, the ease that made it possible — all *ātāhum*, all granted from above. The shield felt light in your hand because I lightened it. So there is nothing — *nothing* — to look down on anyone about. Your best deed was My gift before it was your achievement. Pride has no floor to stand on.

But to the despairing one — in the *same breath*, in the same word — it says: *and yet it is genuinely **yours**.* I stamped your name on it. *Taqwā**hum.*** I did not erase you to give it. I did not reach past you. I worked *through* your striving, your choice, your raised arm, and the result is so truly *yours* that I will call it by your name on the Day you stand before Me. You are not nothing. You are the one I chose to honor by making the gift *look like* an achievement — *your* achievement.

[PAUSE]

Do you see the mercy in that? This is one of the gentlest things in the whole Quran, and it's hiding in a pronoun.

Allah could keep all the credit. He is the source of everything; by strict logic, every good thing is His and His alone. And yet — out of a generosity that should stop your heart — He takes the goodness that He Himself enabled, and He *attributes it to you.* He lets you keep it. He calls your shield *yours*, knowing full well He forged it. As Saʿīd ibn Jubayr read it: *He gave them the reward of their taqwā* — He even rewards you for the gift He gave you.

This is the posture the ayah trains: a person who works as hard as if everything depended on their own arm — *because the taqwā really is theirs to raise* — and who, the instant they succeed, falls into gratitude as if they did nothing — *because the taqwā really was His to give.* Maximum effort and zero pride, fused. The striver who cannot become arrogant, because every rung they climb, they look down and see it was placed under their foot.

**What this changes tomorrow:** The next time you do something good — and feel that little warm flush of *I did that* — catch it. Don't crush it into false humility ("it was nothing"), and don't inflate it into pride ("look at me"). Do the thing this ayah does: hold both. Say *Alhamdulillah* — not as a reflex, but meaning it precisely: *this is mine, and it was a gift; I raised the shield, and You put it in my hand.* Let your good deed make you simultaneously more confident and more humble. That impossible combination is exactly what *wa-ātāhum taqwāhum* is built to produce.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Now that we've done the work, let me show you how the six words are actually built, because the structure *is* the meaning.

Watch the motion:

1. **وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟** — *They* move. The verb is theirs, Form VIII, reaching in. **The human leans.**
2. **زَادَهُمْ هُدًۭى** — *He* moves. He increases them, ceilingless. **God responds with more.**
3. **وَءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ** — *He* gives, and the gift lands as **theirs.** **God's gift becomes the human's possession.**

It's a circle. The human's effort → God's increase → a gift that returns to the human as their own. Their movement opens the door; His generosity floods through it; and what floods through, He hands back to them with their name on it. Effort and grace are not taking turns in this verse. They are *braided.* You cannot find the seam where the human ends and the divine begins — because the ayah was constructed so you couldn't.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the lived themes are the same truth seen from two sides.

The **verb form** (ٱهْتَدَوْا, the lean-in) and the **first theme** (guidance as momentum, not a finish line) are one insight: *you must reach, and the reaching is what gets multiplied.*

The **possessive gift** (ءَاتَىٰهُمْ تَقْوَىٰهُمْ) and the **second theme** (the death of both pride and despair) are one insight: *what you earn is given, and what is given is yours.*

And the **ceilingless tanwīn** (هُدًۭى, the unmeasured increase) holds the whole thing open — promising that this braiding of your effort and His grace has *no known limit*, as long as you keep leaning in.

### Questions to Carry

Sit with these. Don't answer them quickly.

1. The same revelation fell on two groups in this passage. For one it evaporated; for the other it compounded. **When the Quran reaches you this week — do you *lean in*, or does it run off like the hypocrite's "what did he just say"?**

2. Your spiritual life right now — is it a *box you've checked* ("I'm a practicing Muslim") or a *current you're moving in*? Honestly: **which direction is your momentum pointing today?**

3. Think of your single best quality — your most reliable good deed. Can you hold it as the ayah asks — **fully yours *and* entirely a gift** — at the same time, without dropping either?

4. The hypocrites' mockery *increased* the believers' guidance. **What in your life right now feels like an obstacle to your faith — that Allah might be offering you as fuel for it instead?**

5. If taqwā is a *shield you raise but God forged* — what is the one place in your life where you've put the shield down, and what would it mean to pick it back up tomorrow?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**To the one who takes even a single step toward the light, Allah gives more light without measure — and hands back their own striving as a gift with their name on it.**

### The Closing Invitation

You don't have to arrive anywhere to begin. That is the whole liberation of this ayah. You are not waiting at a locked door called "guidance," hoping one day to be let through. You are standing at the edge of a current, and the only thing being asked of you is to *lean in* — even slightly, even today. The moment you do, you are no longer the one doing it alone. *Zādahum hudan.* The increase begins. And whatever you build from there, He will let you keep, and call it yours.

So don't wait to feel worthy. Lean.

### A Brief Du'ā

*O Allah — You who increase the guided in guidance: we have leaned in only a little, so meet our little with Your much. Multiply our certainty as You promised, until even our obstacles become fuel for our path to You.*

*And O Allah — grant us our taqwā. Forge the shield, place it in our hands, and out of Your generosity, let us carry it as our own — so that we strive as if everything depends on us, and thank You as if we did nothing at all.*

*Āmīn.*
