# The Witnesses Before the Verdict

## INTRODUCTION

Let me begin by simply placing the words in front of you.

> وَالشَّمْسِ وَضُحَىٰهَا • وَالْقَمَرِ إِذَا تَلَىٰهَا • وَالنَّهَارِ إِذَا جَلَّىٰهَا • وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰهَا • وَالسَّمَاءِ وَمَا بَنَىٰهَا • وَالْأَرْضِ وَمَا طَحَىٰهَا

*Wash-shamsi wa ḍuḥāhā • wal-qamari idhā talāhā • wan-nahāri idhā jallāhā • wal-layli idhā yaghshāhā • was-samāʾi wa mā banāhā • wal-arḍi wa mā ṭaḥāhā.*

"By the sun and its morning brightness, and the moon when it follows it, and the day when it displays it, and the night when it shrouds it, and the sky and the One who built it, and the earth and the One who spread it out."

Now, I want you to imagine the original scene. Not a page. A *voice*.

Imagine standing in Mecca, before any of this was written down, and a man you have known your whole life — a man whose honesty was so reliable you called him *al-Amīn*, the Trustworthy — begins to speak. And he doesn't open with a teaching. He doesn't open with a command. He opens by **swearing an oath**. And then another. And another. And another. Six of them tumbling out, one after the next, each one shorter and tighter than ordinary speech, each one ending on the same haunting sound — *hā... hā... hā...* — like a drumbeat you can feel in your chest before you've understood a single word.

This is the longest unbroken chain of oaths anywhere in the Quran.

And here is the thing I want you to sit with from the very beginning. **An oath is not information.** When a person swears, they are not telling you something new. They are doing something to you. They are saying: *stop. What I am about to say is so weighty, so consequential, that ordinary speech cannot carry it. I am calling witnesses.*

[PAUSE]

So before we have heard a single claim, Allah has summoned the sun. And the moon. And the day, and the night, and the sky, and the earth. He has assembled the entire visible cosmos into a courtroom. Six witnesses, standing in a row.

And here is the question that should make you lean forward: **Witnesses to *what*?** What could possibly be so heavy that the sun itself must be called to testify before it is spoken?

We are going to spend our time today inside the witnesses — the six oaths — and we are going to discover that they are not random. They have been chosen. They have been arranged. And by the time we understand *why these six and in this order*, we will understand something about ourselves that we spend most of our lives trying not to look at.

Let's begin.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Sun and *Its* Brightness — a Thing Paired With Its Own Becoming

Let me start with something most people read straight past.

The very first oath is not *"by the sun."* It is *"by the sun **and its morning brightness**"* — *wash-shamsi wa **ḍuḥāhā**.*

Before we open the word, let me teach you the shape of what's happening here, because it governs everything that follows.

In ordinary speech, when we swear by something great, we name the thing and stop. "By God." "By my honour." We don't usually swear by a thing *and then by an aspect of that thing.* Imagine someone said to you, "I swear by the ocean — and by its blueness." You would pause. Why mention the blueness separately? Isn't the blueness already *in* the ocean? The very fact that the aspect is named separately tells you the aspect is being **lifted up for its own sake**. It's being made to matter on its own.

That is exactly what happens here. Allah swears by the sun. And then He swears, separately, by its **ḍuḥā**.

So what is this *ḍuḥā*?

Here is where the classical scholars hand us not one meaning but a cluster of them, and I want you to receive them not as competing options where you must pick the winner, but as several photographers standing around a single statue, each capturing an angle the others missed.

Mujāhid and al-Kalbī said *ḍuḥāhā* means **its light, its radiance** — *ḍawʾuhā wa ishrāquhā* — the moment the sun has risen and its light has become *pure*, clean, fully itself. Al-Baghawī preserves this: *the ḍuḥā is when the sun rises and its light grows clear.*

Qatāda added another angle: **its splendour**, its *bahāʾ* — and in another narration, the whole risen day that the sun produces.

Al-Suddī, and a report from Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Ḍaḥḥāk, caught a different facet: **its heat** — *ḥarruhā* — the warmth that pours out of it once it has climbed. Al-Qurṭubī records Ibn ʿAbbās saying Allah *"placed light in it and made it hot."*

And al-Yazīdī saw yet another: **its spreading** — *inbisāṭuhā* — the way the light expands and unfolds across the world.

[PAUSE]

Now step back and look at all those angles at once. Light becoming pure. Splendour. Heat. Spreading. Fullness.

What is the single reality they are all circling?

The *ḍuḥā* is the moment the sun **comes fully into its own.** It is not the timid sun of dawn, half-hidden at the horizon. It is the sun *arrived* — risen high, its light no longer faint but clean and total, its warmth no longer promised but delivered, its presence spread across everything. The *ḍuḥā* is the sun's **completion of itself.** The sun as it was made to be.

So notice what Allah has actually done in five Arabic words. He has not just sworn by an object. He has sworn by an object **and by that object reaching its full purpose.** The thing, and the thing *becoming everything it was created to become.*

Hold that. We will need it.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: The Thread of *Hā* — Six Pronouns That Bind a Cosmos Together

Now I want you to *listen*, because this next feature is one your ear caught before your mind did.

Read the six oaths again and pay attention only to the final sound of each:

> ḍuḥā**hā** • talā**hā** • jallā**hā** • yaghshā**hā** • banā**hā** • ṭaḥā**hā**

Six times. The same syllable. *Hā... hā... hā...*

Let me teach the device before we feel its weight. In Arabic, that little *hā* on the end is a pronoun — *it*, or *its*. It points back to something already mentioned. It is the smallest possible word, a single breath, and yet it is a thread. Every time you hear it, your mind is yanked backward to ask: *it — what is "it"? What does this point to?*

And here is the marvel. **The thread is constant, but what it points to keeps shifting** — and that shifting is the whole drama.

Let's trace it.

*Wash-shamsi wa ḍuḥā**hā*** — "the sun and **its** brightness." Here the *hā* points to the sun. Clear enough.

*Wal-qamari idhā talā**hā*** — "the moon when it follows **it.**" Now the *hā* points... where? The moon follows *it* — follows the sun. So this *hā* reaches across the gap to the sun again. The moon is defined by what it trails behind. The verb here, *talā*, is the same root we use for **reciting** the Quran — *tilāwah* — because reciting is *following*, word trailing word in order. The moon, then, is a reciter of the sun: it has no light of its own to declare; it can only *follow*, repeating at night a dimmer version of what the sun said by day.

*Wan-nahāri idhā jallā**hā*** — "the day when it displays **it.**" The *hā* here — the scholars hold more than one view, and both are luminous. It may point to the sun: the day *unveils the sun.* Or it may point to the whole world, the earth: the day *displays everything*, throws the entire scene into visibility.

*Wal-layli idhā yaghshā**hā*** — "the night when it shrouds **it.**" The same *hā*, now covered. Whatever the day displayed, the night *veils.*

*Was-samāʾi wa mā banā**hā*** — "the sky and **its** building." And *wal-arḍi wa mā ṭaḥā**hā*** — "the earth and **its** spreading."

[PAUSE]

Do you feel what is happening sonically? Six times your ear is pulled to that same soft, open *hā* — and six times your mind is sent searching for an antecedent. The repetition isn't decoration. It is **binding.** Allah is taking six enormous, scattered realities — a star, a satellite, the daylight, the dark, the dome above, the ground below — and stitching them together with one recurring thread, until they stop sounding like six separate things and start sounding like **one woven fabric.** One creation. One hand behind all of it.

And the longer the *hā* repeats, the more your soul starts to expect it. You are being *prepared* — rhythmically, almost physically prepared — for one more *hā* that has not yet come. Hold that feeling. It is the most important thing in the surah, and we will return to it at the very end.

### Element Three: *Mā*, Not *Man* — When Allah Refers to Himself as a "What"

Here is the feature that has occupied the scholars more than any other in these six lines, and I have saved it because it is the strangest.

Look at the fifth and sixth oaths:

> *was-samāʾi wa **mā** banāhā* — "the sky and **mā** built it"
> *wal-arḍi wa **mā** ṭaḥāhā* — "the earth and **mā** spread it out"

In Arabic there are two different words for "who" and "what." *Man* (مَن) is used for **persons** — rational, willing beings. *Mā* (مَا) is used for **things** — objects, concepts, the impersonal.

So who built the sky? Allah did. Who spread out the earth? Allah did. And yet the word chosen is not *man* — "the One **who** built it" — but ***mā***. A word that, on its surface, is used for *things.*

This should stop you cold. Why would Allah, referring to His own act of creation, use the word reserved for the impersonal?

The classical scholars give us a composite here, and again I want you to take all the angles together.

**The first reading** takes *mā* as what grammarians call *maṣdariyya* — a particle that turns the verb after it into a verbal noun, an *action*. On this reading the line means: *"by the sky and **its building**, by the earth and **its spreading-out.**"* The oath is not by the Builder at all but by **the act, the craftsmanship itself** — the sheer engineering of a sky that holds together without pillars, an earth rolled out flat enough to live on. You swear by the masterpiece to point at the Master.

**The second reading** takes *mā* as *mawṣūla* — a relative pronoun meaning "the One who": *"by the sky and **the One who** built it."* And here is the subtle thing: when *mā* is used for a rational being like this, Arabic uses it precisely to express **awe at the magnitude and mystery** of that being — to point at a greatness so total it cannot be reduced to the ordinary category of "someone." *Mā* here doesn't diminish the Builder; it *enlarges* Him beyond the reach of *man.*

[PAUSE]

Notice that both readings arrive at the same destination from opposite directions. The first stares so hard at the *building* that it forgets to name the Builder — and in that very silence, points at Him. The second names the Builder but with a word so vast it refuses to fit Him in a box. Either way, you end up gazing at a greatness too large for the normal grammar of persons.

And there is one more thing I cannot pass without you seeing it. Listen to the **sounds** of those two final verbs. *Banāhā* — "built it" — is a smooth, rising word; building is an *adding*, a lifting-up. *Ṭaḥāhā* — "spread it out" — sits heavier in the mouth, that emphatic *ṭ* pressing the tongue flat against the roof of the mouth, the very gesture of *flattening, spreading, pressing out.* The word for spreading the earth *spreads* in your own mouth as you say it. The sky is built **up**; the earth is pressed **out** and **down**. Even the texture of the two words places you between the two of them — exactly where a human being stands: a built sky above, a spread earth below.

So the construction has shown us something the words alone could not: that all the grandeur of the cosmos — the dome and the floor, the act and the Actor — is being gathered and held up in front of us. But held up *for what*? Now let's ask what these six witnesses reveal not just about words, but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: A Universe Built in Pairs — and the Mirror It Holds to Your Soul

Let me begin not with the sky, but with something you already know in your body.

You have felt, I think, the strange experience of being *two people at once.* There is the part of you that wants to pray, and the part that wants to sleep. The part that resolves, at night, to be patient tomorrow — and the part that snaps at the first provocation by 9am. The part of you that gives quietly and the part that wants everyone to see you give. We tend to think this inner contradiction is a flaw, a glitch, a sign that something has gone wrong with us. We spend enormous energy trying to flatten ourselves into one consistent thing.

Now look again at what Allah swears by. Not random objects. **Pairs of opposites.**

The sun — and the moon. The day — and the night. The sky above — and the earth below.

These are not six unrelated witnesses. They are **three couples.** Light and its lesser light. The bright that reveals and the dark that conceals. The lofty and the lowly. Allah is parading before us a cosmos that is *structured in twos*, a universe whose very architecture is **polarity.**

And watch what the verbs do with these pairs. *Jallāhā* — the day "displays," unveils, throws everything into the open. The verb is Form II, a doubled, intensified shape — not merely "showed" but **thoroughly laid bare, exhibited in full.** And then *yaghshāhā* — the night "shrouds," envelops, draws a covering over the very same thing. Reveal and conceal. Expose and hide. The same world, shown by one and veiled by the other.

[PAUSE]

Here is a detail the grammarians noticed, and I offer it carefully, as something the verb-forms themselves invite us to consider. *Jallāhā*, "displayed it," is in the **completed** tense — the day *did* its unveiling, finished, done. But *yaghshāhā*, "shrouds it," is in the **ongoing** tense — the night *keeps* covering, again and again, an act renewed every evening of your life. As if the *revealing* is a clean, decisive event — but the *covering*, the slipping back into darkness, is the thing that never stops needing to happen, the recurring pull you face every single night.

Does that not sound familiar? The moment of clarity comes once, sharp and complete — you *see* what you must do. But the covering-over, the forgetting, the sliding back into the dark — that is the daily, ongoing labour. Anyone who has tried to change knows this asymmetry in their bones.

Now — *why* is Allah showing us all this polarity before He says anything else?

Because of what comes next. Right after these six oaths, in the very next breaths, the chain finally arrives at its last and greatest oath: *wa nafsin wa **mā sawwāhā** — fa-alhamahā **fujūrahā wa taqwāhā.*** "By the soul and **the One who** proportioned it — then **inspired into it** its capacity for corruption *and* its capacity for God-consciousness."

[PAUSE]

Do you see it now? The sun and the moon, the day and the night, the heaven and the earth — every one of them a **pair, a polarity, a thing and its opposite** — were the rehearsal. They were teaching your ear and your soul the *grammar of twoness*, so that when Allah finally turned the oath inward and swore by *you* — by the human soul — and announced that you too contain a polarity, two roads written into you, *fujūr* and *taqwā*, the pull down and the pull up — **you would already understand it.** You would not be shocked that you are two things at once. The entire visible universe has just spent six oaths telling you: *of course you are. So am I. I was built that way too.*

The contradiction you carry is not a malfunction. **It is the same architecture as the cosmos.** Day and night do not cancel the world; they constitute it. And your light and your darkness do not disqualify your soul; they are the very terrain on which the soul's whole meaning will be decided.

What does this change about tomorrow? It means the next time you catch yourself wanting two opposite things, you do not need to despair that you are broken or hypocritical. That doubleness is the *raw material* you were given. The question the surah is racing toward is never "do you have a dark side?" — the sun has a setting, the day has a night. The question is the one in the lines that follow: *which one will you grow, and which will you bury?* But that is for those ayahs. For now, simply let the six witnesses do their work: they are normalizing your inner war so that you will be ready to fight it honestly.

### Theme Two: The Sun's *Ḍuḥā* and the Soul's *Becoming*

Let me return to where we began — to that lonely little word *ḍuḥā* — because it has been waiting for us, and now you are ready to see what it was for.

Start with something human. Think of a person you have watched **come into their own.** Maybe a student who was timid and uncertain, and then one day you saw them teach, and they were *radiant* — completely themselves, their gift finally arrived. Or a parent who seemed ordinary until the moment their child was in danger, and suddenly a strength poured out of them you never knew was there. There is a moment when a thing — or a person — stops merely *existing* and starts *fulfilling what it was made for.* The Arabs had a word for that moment in the life of the sun. They called it the *ḍuḥā* — the hour the sun stops climbing and starts *blazing*, light purified, warmth delivered, splendour spread. The sun's arrival into its full self.

And Allah did not swear merely "by the sun." He swore "by the sun **and its ḍuḥā**" — by the sun **and by its coming fully into what it was created to be.**

[PAUSE]

Now hold that beside the oath the chain is building toward: *wa nafsin wa **mā sawwāhā*** — "by the soul and **the One who proportioned, completed, perfected it.**" The word *sawwā* means to shape something into balanced completion, to finish it into its proper form. Do you hear the rhyme between the two oaths — not a rhyme of sound, but of *meaning*?

The sun has its *ḍuḥā* — the fullness it was made to reach.
The soul has its *taswiyah* — the completeness it was shaped toward.

The sun's *ḍuḥā* arrives **automatically.** The sun cannot choose to stay dim. It climbs, it blazes, it fulfills its purpose without deciding to, because it has no will. It was given its fullness as a gift and a compulsion at once.

But you — *you* were given something the sun was not. You were given the **two roads.** Your *ḍuḥā*, your coming-into-fullness, your *taswiyah* completed by Allah's own hand — does **not** arrive automatically. It is offered, and then handed to your choosing.

[PAUSE]

This is the quiet devastation hidden in the parallel. The sun was made magnificent and given no say in the matter. You were made — listen — *with the same care*; the One who built the sky and spread the earth and lit the sun is the very same One who "proportioned" your soul. The same craftsmanship. The same hand. But where the sun's fullness was *installed*, yours was *entrusted.* The sun's *ḍuḥā* is its destiny. Your *ḍuḥā* is your **decision.**

And here is the human door back into it. Every one of us carries a *ḍuḥā* we have not yet reached — a fuller, cleaner, warmer version of ourselves that we can feel exists, the way you can feel the sun is meant to climb higher than the horizon. We sense the person we *could* be — more patient, more honest, more generous, more awake to God. The sun reaches that fullness without struggle. We reach it only through the daily labour the rest of this surah describes: *qad aflaḥa man **zakkāhā*** — "he has truly flourished who **purifies** the soul, who grows it, who lets it climb to its *ḍuḥā*" — *wa qad khāba man **dassāhā*** — "and he has lost who **buries** it, who shoves it down into the dark and lets it stay dim."

What does this change tomorrow? It gives you a question to hold against every choice: *am I letting my soul climb toward its ḍuḥā — or am I keeping it dim by my own hand?* The sun does not get to choose between blazing and staying faint. You do. That is the terror and the dignity of being human, and these six oaths have set the whole stage for it.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture, Revealed

Now, at the end, let me show you the building we have been standing inside.

Step back and look at the shape of all seven oaths together — the six we have studied and the seventh they were aiming at:

> The sun — and the moon.
> The day — and the night.
> The sky — and the earth.
> **And the soul.**

Three pairs of cosmic opposites — and then, standing alone at the summit, **one** thing: the human soul. The architecture moves from the largest and most distant — a star — and travels *inward*, oath by oath, closer and closer: from the sky over your head to the earth under your feet to, finally, **the self inside your chest.** The whole cosmos was a funnel, narrowing, until it pours out onto *you.*

And remember the thread of *hā* — *ḍuḥāhā, talāhā, jallāhā, yaghshāhā, banāhā, ṭaḥāhā.* Six times your ear was trained to expect that soft falling syllable. Allah was tuning you, rhythmically, so that the seventh and eighth — *sawwāhā... fa-alhamahā fujūrahā wa taqwāhā* — would land in an ear already shaped to receive them. The sound itself was carrying you home. Every *hā* pointed at "it" — the sun, the moon, the world — until the final *hā* pointed at the only "it" that was ever really on trial: **the soul. *Hers.***

### The Two Discoveries Were Always One

Look at what the words showed us and what the themes showed us, and watch them fuse.

The **grammar** taught us *polarity*: a cosmos stitched together from pairs of opposites — *jallā* and *yaghshā*, reveal and conceal, sky and earth.

The **theme** taught us that *you* are built from the same polarity — *fujūr* and *taqwā*, two roads inscribed in one soul.

The **grammar** taught us *ḍuḥā*: a thing paired with its own fullest becoming.

The **theme** taught us that you, too, were *proportioned* toward a fullness — but yours is not installed, it is *entrusted* to your will.

And the strange word ***mā*** — "the sky and *what* built it" — that refusal to box the Creator into the ordinary grammar of persons? It was telling you, all along, that **the One who engineered the dome and pressed out the earth and set the sun blazing is the very same One who proportioned the soul in your chest.** The Builder of the heavens is the Maker of your *self.* You were fashioned by the hand that hung the stars. That is why your inner life is heavy enough to swear by. That is why the sun must be called to *witness* what becomes of you.

That was the answer to the question we began with — *witnesses to what?* To this: that a creature made with the same care as the cosmos, and given the one thing the cosmos was not — a *choice* — will either grow toward its light or bury itself in its dark. The sun was summoned to watch what you do with the freedom it was never given.

### Questions to Carry

- The sun reaches its *ḍuḥā* without a single decision. What decision is mine to make today, that my soul's fullness is *waiting* on?
- I have always treated my inner contradictions as proof I am broken. What changes if they are instead the raw material I was *given* — the day and night of my own soul?
- The night "keeps covering" in the ongoing tense. Where in my life is the *revealing* already done — I have *seen* what is right — and the daily labour now is simply to stop letting the dark slide back over it?
- If the One who built the sky also proportioned my soul, with the same care — how would I treat this self I carry, knowing whose craftsmanship it is?
- The whole universe was funneled, oath by oath, down onto the human soul. Do I live as though my inner life is that consequential — or as though it is a small thing?

### One-Sentence Distillation

Allah summons the entire cosmos of paired opposites — sun and moon, day and night, sky and earth — as witnesses, narrowing the whole creation down onto the one place where light and dark are not merely arranged but *chosen*: the human soul.

### The Closing Invitation

You do not need to leave this reflection resolving to become a perfect person overnight. The sun did not climb to its *ḍuḥā* in a single leap; it rose. The invitation is gentler and harder than perfection: simply to *stop burying.* To recognize that the self in your chest was shaped by the Maker of the heavens, that it leans two ways by design, and that its fullness — its *ḍuḥā* — is the one thing in all of creation that has been left in your own hands. Let it climb. Even a little, today.

### A Brief Du'a

O You who built the sky without pillars and spread the earth beneath our feet, You who set the sun blazing into its fullness — You proportioned this soul within me with that same hand. Do not let me be the one who buries what You shaped with such care. Let my soul climb toward the light You made it for, and let the sun and the moon, the day and the night, bear witness for me, and not against me, on the Day they testify. *Āmīn.*
