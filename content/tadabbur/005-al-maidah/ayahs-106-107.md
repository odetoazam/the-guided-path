# What the Witness Does Not Own

## Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:106–107)

[ayah:5:106] يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ شَهَـٰدَةُ بَيْنِكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ ٱلْمَوْتُ حِينَ ٱلْوَصِيَّةِ ٱثْنَانِ ذَوَا عَدْلٍ مِّنكُمْ أَوْ ءَاخَرَانِ مِنْ غَيْرِكُمْ إِنْ أَنتُمْ ضَرَبْتُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَأَصَـٰبَتْكُم مُّصِيبَةُ ٱلْمَوْتِ ۚ تَحْبِسُونَهُمَا مِنۢ بَعْدِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ فَيُقْسِمَانِ بِٱللَّهِ إِنِ ٱرْتَبْتُمْ لَا نَشْتَرِى بِهِۦ ثَمَنًۭا وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰ ۙ وَلَا نَكْتُمُ شَهَـٰدَةَ ٱللَّهِ إِنَّآ إِذًۭا لَّمِنَ ٱلْـَٔاثِمِينَ

[ayah:5:107] فَإِنْ عُثِرَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنَّهُمَا ٱسْتَحَقَّآ إِثْمًۭا فَـَٔاخَرَانِ يَقُومَانِ مَقَامَهُمَا مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَحَقَّ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْأَوْلَيَانِ فَيُقْسِمَانِ بِٱللَّهِ لَشَهَـٰدَتُنَآ أَحَقُّ مِن شَهَـٰدَتِهِمَا وَمَا ٱعْتَدَيْنَآ إِنَّآ إِذًۭا لَّمِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ

## Introduction

There is a kind of injustice that almost can't be caught.

It happens at the edge of someone's life — when they are too weak to enforce, and the people around them are not. It happens far from home, where the witnesses speak a different language and the family is days away. It happens at a moment when whatever was about to be transferred — money, property, the small careful arrangements of a person who knew they were leaving — passes through hands the dying person trusted because they had no choice but to trust.

If those hands tell a slightly different story afterward, who would know?

That is the world this passage enters. Not a world of contracts in well-lit offices. A world where a Muslim merchant has died on the road, and the only people who saw what he owned at the end are the people now bringing his belongings home — minus, perhaps, a silver vessel they happened to find useful, plus, perhaps, a story that explains why the inventory looks lighter than expected.

Al-Ma'idah is the surah of contracts. It opened with *awfū bil-ʿuqūd* — *fulfill your covenants*. It has spent forty ayahs on dietary law, on retribution, on judging by what Allah revealed, on the disease of inherited false sacrality. And now, near its end, it turns to the most invisible kind of contract there is — the one written by someone about to die, witnessed at a distance from their family, enforced by no court that can subpoena.

The passage is enormously long. **5:106 alone is one of the longest ayahs in the Quran.** That length is not accidental. Allah is constructing, in slow careful clauses, a fence around an act that almost wants to slip through. He is taking a moment that lives in the dark and putting fluorescent light on every inch of it.

Even the procedural frame is dense with meaning. The witnesses are summoned after the prayer — the classical commentators understood this most specifically as the ʿAsr prayer, the middle prayer, whose time the hadith tradition marks as the moment when both the angels of the day and the night are present before their ascent. The oath is taken, in other words, when the unseen gallery is at its fullest.

But the part of the passage I want to walk you toward is not the procedural fence. It is the way Allah talks about *witnessing* itself — and how, in three small grammatical decisions, He is dismantling everything our age believes about testimony.

## Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

### 1. *ʿUthira* — When Truth Comes by Tripping

Listen to how Allah introduces the possibility that the witnesses lied:

**فَإِنْ عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ أَنَّهُمَا ٱسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا**

*Fa-in ʿuthira ʿalā annahuma istaḥaqqā ithman.*

*If it is stumbled upon that those two became guilty of a sin...*

Stay with that verb. **ʿUthira.** The root is ع-ث-ر, and in Arabic it is the word for tripping. Stubbing your toe on a stone you didn't see. The way your foot finds something hidden in tall grass. *ʿAthara* is what the camel does when it catches its hoof on a root in the sand.

When you want to say in Arabic that something has been *proven*, you have words for that. *Thabata.* *Tabayyana.* When you want to say something has been *uncovered* with intention, you have *kashafa.* When you want to say it was *investigated and found,* you have whole verb-forms built for that — *istaqraʾa,* *baḥatha.*

Allah uses none of them.

He uses the verb that means *tripped over.*

Al-Tabari, in his tafsir, traces the root to its classical ground: *the origin of ʿathr*, he writes, *is landing upon a thing and falling onto it* — the impact of encounter, not the intent of search. He cites the pre-Islamic poet al-Aʿsha, who used the same word of a stumbling camel: when the beast trips, the further stumble claims her before any recovery does. The word sits that deep in the Arabic lexicon — not the stumble of carelessness but the stumble of a foot that has found something buried in its path.

And He puts it in the **passive voice**. *ʿUthira* — *it was stumbled upon.* No subject is named. No investigator is named. The sentence does not say *if you discover,* or *if a judge finds,* or *if witnesses come forward.* It says: *if it gets tripped over.* The reality of the lie comes to light not because the system caught it — but because the system, walking along, happened to catch its foot on something.

[PAUSE]

Notice what this does. **It is theology disguised as grammar.**

Allah is not naïve about concealment. He has just spent an entire ayah constructing the procedural fence — two witnesses, justice, after the prayer, by oath, with the conditional clause about the price and the kinsman built in. He knows how concealment works. He knows that all the procedure in the world cannot guarantee that the witness will not lie. So when He turns to the next clause — *and what if they did lie?* — He does not pretend that human courts will detect it. He uses the verb of *tripping.*

What He is telling the believer is something the modern world has forgotten: **most concealed sin surfaces, when it surfaces at all, by the foot of Providence catching on a root no one planted.** The neighbor's offhand comment. The forgotten letter that turns up in the wrong drawer. The accountant's daughter who happens to mention what her father said. The secret was perfectly buried. And then someone, walking along, *tripped.*

This is not a theory of justice for the strong. It is a theology of justice for the weak. **The dying merchant on the road in the third year after Hijrah cannot enforce his estate.** His widow in Madinah does not have a forensic accountant. The system, on its own, will not catch the witnesses who shaved a silver vessel off the inventory. So Allah does not tell the believer to trust the system. He tells the believer that the system has a foot, and that foot belongs to its Owner, and Allah's foot has never failed to find what was hidden.

The grammar of *ʿuthira* is the believer's quiet confidence: **what is concealed is not safe. It is merely waiting.**

### 2. *Shahādata Allāh* — Whose Testimony Is It?

Now move to the heart of the witnesses' oath:

**وَلَا نَكْتُمُ شَهَـٰدَةَ ٱللَّهِ**

*Wa lā naktumu shahādata Allāh.*

*And we will not conceal the testimony of Allah.*

Read that again. **The testimony of Allah.**

Whose testimony is it?

In Arabic, *shahādata Allāh* is what the grammarians call an *iḍāfa* — a possessive construction binding two nouns. *Bayt Allāh* — the house of Allah. *Rasūl Allāh* — the messenger of Allah. *Kitāb Allāh* — the book of Allah. The first noun belongs to the second.

So when the false witnesses are made to swear that they will not conceal *shahādata Allāh* — they are not swearing about *their own* testimony. Classical commentators have read this possessive construction along more than one axis: some understood it as testimony *belonging to* Allah, entrusted temporarily to the witness like property held in custody; others read it as testimony *enjoined by* Allah, the very act of bearing witness being His command rather than the witness's prerogative; others still as testimony rendered *in the presence of* Allah, sworn before the One who misses nothing. Across all these readings the weight is identical: the testimony is not the witness's own creation or possession, and he has no authority to shape it toward his interest. They are swearing about something whose Owner is not them.

[PAUSE]

Sit with this for a long moment. We live in an age that believes the opposite of this sentence.

Our age believes the witness *owns* the testimony. The eyewitness's account is the eyewitness's *property* — to share if she wishes, to sell if the offer is good, to shape to her interest, to water down if it complicates her relationships, to amplify if it advances her cause. Testimony is treated as a possession. **My truth.** The phrase itself is the quiet creed of a civilization that believes the witnessing of reality is a private asset.

The Quran's grammar refuses this at the root.

The witness does not own the testimony. The testimony belongs to Allah. The witness is a courier. He is carrying something he did not produce, to a destination he did not choose, on behalf of an Owner whose property the truth is. **He has the same relationship to the testimony that a courier has to a sealed envelope.** Opening it is not exercising rights. It is theft.

This is why concealing testimony, in the Quran's grammar, is not silence. It is **embezzlement**. *Lā naktumu* — *we will not conceal.* The verb *katama* is what you do with something that isn't fully yours: you sit on it, you bury it, you keep it from reaching the one who has a claim. Allah uses the same verb in 2:283: *wa man yaktumhā fa-innahu āthimun qalbuh* — *whoever conceals it, his heart is sinful.* It is the verb of a thief sitting on stolen goods.

And notice what this reframing does for the dying merchant on the road. **He no longer has to trust the witnesses' character.** He has to trust *Allah's grip on the testimony.* The witness is a courier of a package that has a recipient. The recipient knows what was in the package. The courier may pretend, may shave a fragment off, may invent a different inventory — but the package was never his. The Owner is waiting at the other end, and the Owner already knows.

This is why the most reliable witness, in the Quran's logic, is the one who has *least* sense of owning the testimony. The journalist who would lose her career before she would shape a story. The doctor who tells you the truth he wishes were not true. The friend who, when asked what happened, refuses to add a single ornament. They are not impressive because they are honest. They are impressive because they have understood **whose testimony it is.**

### 3. The Asymmetric Ending — From *Āthimīn* to *Ẓālimīn*

Now look at how the two oaths close. They are almost identical. They are deliberately not.

The first witnesses, swearing they will not sell or conceal, end:
**إِنَّآ إِذًۭا لَّمِنَ ٱلْـَٔاثِمِينَ**
*Innā idhan la-min al-āthimīn — we would then surely be of the sinful.*

The second pair, the ones who stand in the place of the first pair when the lie has been *tripped over*, end:
**إِنَّآ إِذًۭا لَّمِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ**
*Innā idhan la-min al-ẓālimīn — we would then surely be of the wrongdoers.*

[PAUSE]

Two near-identical sentences. One word changes. Why?

Both *ithm* and *ẓulm* describe sin. They are not synonyms. **Ithm** is the inner mark — the spiritual stain a person carries when they have transgressed against Allah. It is between the slave and his Lord. Its lens is the interior: the stain that settles on the perpetrator's soul, the quality of the transgression as it stands before Allah before its consequences have fully rippled outward. The Prophet ﷺ said *al-ithmu mā ḥāka fī ṣadrika* — *ithm is what hesitates in your chest,* what you would not want others to see. It is sin from the inside.

**Ẓulm** is the outward shape of that sin. It is the displacement of something from its rightful place. Allah uses it for shirk because shirk *displaces* worship from its rightful Recipient. He uses it for oppression because oppression displaces a person from their rightful safety. *Ẓulm* requires a *victim* — someone whose right has been moved out of place.

So look at when each word appears.

In **5:106** — the first oath — there is no victim yet. The witnesses are swearing in advance, before anything has happened. If they were to lie, the lie would be a private mark on their own souls. *Āthimīn.* The word fits the moment.

In **5:107** — the second oath — there *is* a victim. The dead man's family has been wronged. The lie is no longer hypothetical inner stain. The lie has displaced inheritance from where it belonged. **The same act has now grown a shadow that falls on someone else.** *Ẓālimīn.* The word, again, fits the moment.

[PAUSE]

This is one of the quietest, most surgical word-choices in the Quran.

What Allah is teaching, by changing one word at the end of two near-identical clauses, is something we almost never grasp on our own: **a sin grows.** The same lie, told before a victim and told after one, is not the same lie. The hour the false witness held the inventory in his hand and decided to alter it, what stained him was *ithm.* The hour the family in Madinah opened the false inventory and accepted that the merchant's wealth was less than it actually had been, what stained him was *ẓulm.* The same act. The same lie. **Two different sins, separated only by who has been touched by it.**

We have a saying in modern moral language: *no harm, no foul.* The Quran says the opposite. The foul exists at the moment of decision. The harm only changes the *name* of the foul. The witness who decided in his heart to conceal was already an *āthim.* The moment the family received the false story, he became a *ẓālim.* The progression is not from innocence to guilt. It is from guilt-against-Allah to guilt-against-Allah-and-others.

Classical commentary on this passage presses the distinction still deeper. Al-Muyassar glosses *ẓālimīn* here as those who *transgress the limits of Allah* — *al-mutajāwizīn ḥudūda Allāh*. Ẓulm, at its deepest, is not only the displacement of another's right but the overstepping of a boundary Allah declared inviolable. The false witness is not merely a thief of another person's property — he is a transgressor of the divine space itself. The victim is real, and the wrong done to the victim is real. But behind the victim stands the Limit.

This is why the second pair of witnesses takes the second oath in the more severe form. They have stepped into a situation where *ithm* has already become *ẓulm.* They are swearing not to *re-enact* the first sin, but the second one — the one with the family's face on it, and with Allah's boundary behind it.

## Part 2: The Thematic Depths

### 1. The Theology of the Receipt

There is a small, unglamorous practice in the world of customs and shipping. When you accept a package, you sign a receipt. You do not own the package by signing. You acknowledge that you received it in the condition described, and that you are now responsible for delivering it on. The signature is not ownership. **It is custody.**

The Quranic theology of testimony is the theology of the receipt.

When you witness something — when you see what happened, when you hear what was said, when you know the inventory of the dying merchant's bag — you have not *acquired* anything. You have *received custody of* something whose Owner is Allah and whose recipient is the person to whom that truth is owed. The widow in Madinah is owed an accurate inventory. Allah is the Owner of that accuracy. You signed for the package the moment your eyes saw what was inside.

What our age has done is treat every witnessing as an acquisition. *I saw it, so it is mine. I lived it, so it is mine. My experience, my truth, my story, my framing.* The first-person singular pronoun has eaten the act of witnessing whole. We no longer feel that there is anyone on the other side of the package waiting. We feel that the package belongs to the carrier.

The work of becoming a Muslim, in part, is the work of un-doing this. **It is learning to feel testimony as custody.** The conversation you overheard is not yours to spin. The interaction at work is not yours to shape into a story that flatters you. The moment you saw what someone did is not yours to weaponize against them or to whitewash for them. **You are carrying a receipt.**

And the practical landing of this is not abstract. It is what you actually say when you are asked *what happened.* It is whether you let the story drift in the direction your interest wants it to drift. It is whether, when you are alone in your own head retelling the day, you tell it more or less the way it actually happened.

Tonight, when you replay your day in your mind, ask: **am I editing the inventory?**

### 2. The Mercy of Being Tripped Over

There is a strange comfort hidden in *ʿuthira.*

Most of us — if we are honest — have at some point in our lives carried a small concealment. A shaved corner of the truth. A version of events tilted in our favor. Money owed and quietly forgotten. Credit taken for what wasn't ours. We did not call it a sin. We called it *protecting myself.* We called it *not making a big deal.* We called it *the way the world works.*

And we got away with it.

That is the part the soul holds onto. *I got away with it.* The lie did not surface. The corrected inventory was never demanded. The truth, apparently, did not have a recipient who was paying attention.

But Allah has placed in His grammar a quiet warning that is also a quiet mercy. *ʿUthira.* It will be *stumbled over.* Not necessarily here. Not necessarily soon. Not necessarily by anyone we know. **But the foot of providence walks the long road of every life, and roots stick out of soil for a reason.** You may make it to the end of your days with the lie buried. You will not make it past the Day when *ʿuthira ʿalā kulli nafsin mā kasabat.*

The mercy is this: **better to trip in this world.**

Better the embarrassment now than the exposure then. Better the conversation you have to have with the friend you wronged than the one you will have with the Witness you cannot evade. Better to have the buried inventory dug up by your own foot — by your own sudden, gracious moment of *I should make this right* — than to have it dug up at the only excavation that matters.

This is why the believer who has carried a concealment, when grace touches him, *runs* to the surface. He does not wait for the foot of providence. He tells the widow what was actually in the bag. He returns the silver vessel. He says, *I am the witness who shaded the inventory. Please forgive me.* And the moment he does that, **he has done to himself, voluntarily, what the Day will do to him involuntarily.** The trip has happened. He has spared himself the larger trip.

The grammar of *ʿuthira* is therefore both warning and invitation. To the witness who is hiding: it will not stay hidden. To the witness who has been hiding: trip yourself, before something else does.

## Closing Synthesis

### The Architecture

Look now at the passage as a whole.

It begins with the dying believer — vulnerable, far from home, his wealth in others' hands. It ends with the second pair of witnesses correcting the first. Between those two endpoints, Allah has built a structure of **leveraged fear**.

The dying man fears for his estate.
He summons two witnesses — men of *ẓawā ʿadl*, what the classical commentators define not as bare legal credential but as *rashd* (right guidance), *ʿaql* (sound intellect), and *ḥijā* (wisdom): a standard of character, not just of status.
The two witnesses are made to fear Allah by oath, after the prayer, with the conditional clause about the price and the kinsman built in.
If they betray that fear, two others — *closer of kin to the truth* — fear Allah enough to correct them.
And the whole sequence is held in place by *ʿuthira* — by the assurance that what slips through every human net cannot slip through the foot of God.

Notice what is absent. **There is no enforcement clause.** No penalty. No punishment described. The passage does not say *and the false witnesses shall be flogged* or *their estates seized.* The architecture is held together not by sanction but by **fear correctly aimed.** The witness who fears Allah does not need to fear the magistrate. The widow whose husband died on the road does not need to hire investigators. The whole structure rests on a vertical relationship that cannot be evaded — and the procedural fence is just the human shape that vertical relationship takes.

The Quran is not designing a court. **It is designing a society in which courts are the last resort because the first resort has already done its work.**

### The Distillation

Here is the whole passage in one sentence:

**The witness does not own the testimony, the truth has a destination, and what is concealed in this world is only being held for the moment when the foot of providence will trip over it.**

### Questions to Carry

When you tell the story of your day to yourself, are you editing the inventory?

When you witnessed something inconvenient — a colleague's mistake you knew about, a family member's wrongdoing you saw, a moment in which you were the only one who knew what really happened — did you treat the truth as your possession to shape, or as a package you were signed for?

If the foot of providence walked across your life today, where would it trip?

Is there a buried inventory you could dig up yourself this week — before something else digs it up for you?

What would it mean to live as a courier of testimony rather than its owner?

### The Closing Invitation

The merchant on the road has long since arrived at his destination. The witnesses, true and false, have long since stood before the Witness who needs no investigation. The widow in Madinah received her inventory, accurate or shaved, and went on with her grief.

But the grammar Allah used to describe their situation — *shahādata Allāh*, *ʿuthira*, the slow asymmetric slide from *āthimīn* to *ẓālimīn* — that grammar is still here, and it is still describing us. Every conversation you carry forward today is a piece of testimony. Every story you tell about your day is an inventory. You signed for it the moment you saw it.

**Carry it accurately.**

### Du'a

O Allah, You are the Owner of every truth we have witnessed. Make us couriers and not thieves of Your testimony. Trip our feet, in this world, on every concealment we are carrying — before the Day when there will be no foot but Yours, and no excavation but the final one. Make us of those whose inner *ithm* never grows the shadow of *ẓulm* over another, and whose witness, when given, is given as it actually was.

Āmīn.
