# Neither Soothsayer Nor Madman

### *So remind — for you are not, by the grace of your Lord, a soothsayer or a madman.*

---

## INTRODUCTION: The World Behind the Words

Let me put you somewhere before we read anything.

You are a man in Mecca. You have spent forty years among these people. They have a name for you that they use in the marketplace, in private, when they hand you something to keep safe: *al-Amīn.* The Trustworthy. The Sane One. The one whose word does not bend. If there were a single human being in that valley whose mind everyone agreed was sound, it was you.

And then something happens to you that you did not ask for and cannot stop. Words begin to come — words that are not yours, that arrive with a weight that makes you tremble, that say things no man in that valley has ever said in that order. You begin to speak them aloud.

And the same people who called you *al-Amīn* turn and say a new word.

*Majnūn.* He's lost his mind. *Kāhin.* He's gotten in with the jinn, he's running a divination racket like the fortune-tellers at the fairs.

[PAUSE]

Sit in that for a moment. Because there is a particular kind of loneliness here that I want you to feel before we open the ayah. It is the loneliness of being **pathologized for the best thing that ever happened to you.** The thing that is, in fact, the highest honor a human being has ever received — and the people around you have decided it is either fraud or madness. There is no third option in their mouths. Either you are too clever and you are conning them, or you have lost your reason and you cannot help it.

The Prophet ﷺ is carrying this. And in the middle of a surah that has been thundering with oaths — *by the Mount, by the Book, by the Sea that swells* — Allah turns, very suddenly, very personally, and says eight words to His Messenger.

Here is the hook. I want you to notice, before we even begin, where Allah places the comfort in this sentence. He does not put it at the start. He does not put it at the end. He buries it in the **exact center** of the verse — wedged between the command and the insult, between *remind* and *soothsayer-or-madman.* And what He buries there is two words: *bi-niʿmati Rabbik.* By the grace of your Lord.

Why there? Why not anywhere else? Hold that question. We are going to earn the answer.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Fa-dhakkir* — The Command That Assumes You Already Know

Let me teach you something about Arabic verbs before we touch the word, because it changes everything about how this command lands.

In Arabic there is a difference between *teaching* and *reminding,* and it is not a small difference — it is a difference about **what is already inside the person you are speaking to.**

If I *teach* you something, I am assuming it is not in you yet. I am pouring water into an empty cup. But if I *remind* you of something — I am assuming it was already in you, that you knew it once, that it has only been buried, and my words are not water poured in, they are a hand that wipes the dust off a thing you already own.

[PAUSE]

Now look at the first word of our ayah.

### فَذَكِّرْ

*Fa-dhakkir.* "So remind."

The root here is *dhāl-kāf-rā* — *dhikr.* Remembrance. The same root as the *dhikr* you do with your tongue, the same root the Quran uses to name *itself: adh-Dhikr,* the Reminder. And the verb form — listen to it — *dhakkir,* with that doubled, stressed *kāf* in the middle. In Arabic grammar this is **Form II**, and Form II takes a simple root and *intensifies* it, makes it deliberate, makes it something you *do to* someone. The bare root would mean "to remember." This form means **"to actively, deliberately, make another person remember."**

So Allah does not say to His Prophet: *teach them.* He does not say: *inform them of something new.* He says **remind them.** *Dhakkir.*

Let that land, because it is a quiet revolution in how you understand the whole mission.

It means the message the Prophet ﷺ carried was never assumed to be foreign to the human heart. It was assumed to be **already there, asleep.** The truth of one God, the truth of the return, the truth that this life is weighed — these are not bulletins from outside. They are things the soul knew before it forgot. The Quran's word for that buried knowledge runs all through it; here, the command treats every listener — even the mocker calling him *majnūn* — as someone who *had this once.* Reminding only works on someone who already knows.

[PAUSE]

And now notice the tiny letter at the front. The *fa.* "**So** remind." *Therefore* remind.

A *fa* always points backward. It says: *because of what just came, therefore this.* And what just came, in the verses right before, was the Mount, the Book inscribed in unrolled parchment, the frequented House, the canopy raised, the sea set to swell — and the Day, vivid, certain, sworn upon again and again.

So the command is not floating. It is anchored. It is as if Allah says: *Given all of that — given that the Day is as real as that mountain you can see, given that the reckoning is coming as surely as that sea is full — therefore, do your one thing. Remind.*

Here is what is remarkable, and we will return to it at the end. The command is **a single word.** *Remind.* That is the entire job description. Not *convince them.* Not *change them.* Not *succeed.* Just — uncover what's buried, and place it before them. One verb. The outcome is not in the verb.

What this single word reveals: the Prophet's task, and by extension yours, is **disclosure, not manufacture.** You are not installing the truth in anyone. You are wiping dust off a thing they already own.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Bi-niʿmati Rabbik* — The Two Words Buried in the Middle

Now we come to the center. And I need you to see something structural first, with your eyes, before we go into meaning.

Read the shape of the sentence:

> *fa-dhakkir* — **so remind**
> *fa-mā anta* — **for you are not**
> ***bi-niʿmati Rabbika*** — **by the grace of your Lord**
> *bi-kāhin wa lā majnūn* — **a soothsayer or a madman**

Do you see what Allah has done? He has begun a negation — *you are NOT* — and then, before He lets the ugly words *soothsayer* and *madman* arrive, He **interrupts His own sentence** to insert two words: *by the grace of your Lord.*

Grammatically this is a parenthetical, an insertion. The plain sentence would read perfectly without it: *you are not a soothsayer or a madman.* Clean. Complete. But Allah does not leave it clean. He cracks the sentence open right at the seam — right between the *you are not* and the slander — and into that gap He pours: *bi-niʿmati Rabbik.*

[PAUSE]

Why does *placement* matter this much? Let me give you something from your own life.

Imagine your child comes home shaking because the other kids called him stupid. Now imagine two ways you could comfort him.

Version one: "You're not stupid. You're smart, by the way — your teacher told me." Reassurance, then, as an afterthought, the source.

Version two — and feel the difference: "You are not — and I know this from your teacher who watches you every day — you are *not* stupid."

In the second version, the proof is **wrapped inside the denial.** The child cannot reach the word *stupid* without first passing through the evidence that it's false. You have built a wall in the middle of the sentence, and the insult has to climb over the wall to even arrive.

That is precisely what Allah does for His Prophet. The insults *kāhin* and *majnūn* are in this verse — Allah does not pretend they weren't said. But He does not let them stand alone and unguarded. Before they appear, He plants *by the grace of your Lord* directly in their path. The slander cannot reach the Prophet's name without first passing through the declaration that he is held, right now, in the grace of his Lord.

[PAUSE]

Now the words themselves.

*Niʿmah* — root *nūn-ʿayn-mīm* — is grace, favor, blessing; the same root as *naʿīm,* the bliss of the Garden. And *Rabb* — root *rā-bā-bā* — is not the name *Allah* here. It is *Rabb:* the Lord who *nurtures, sustains, raises a thing up stage by stage* the way a gardener brings a seed to fruit. And it is *Rabbika* — **your** Lord. Personal. Yours. The One who has been growing you since before you knew you were being grown.

So put it together. The classical commentators read *bi-niʿmati Rabbika* in two complementary ways, and both are true at once — this is a composite, not a choice. *Al-Jalālayn* and *al-Baghawī* read it as **"by His blessing upon you"** — by the favor of prophethood, by His mercy and His protection (*ʿiṣmah*) over you. *Al-Muyassar* makes it concrete: *by Allah's favor upon you of prophethood and of soundness of mind.* And *al-Qurṭubī* records a further reading: that it carries the force of an oath — *by the grace of your Lord, you are no soothsayer and no madman* — so that the very grace becomes the witness.

Here is what all of these are circling. **The thing they are mocking him for IS the grace.** They look at a man receiving knowledge of the unseen and they say *kāhin* — fraud. Allah looks at the same man and says: that is *niʿmah,* My favor. They look at a man overwhelmed, trembling, behaving unlike any ordinary man, and they say *majnūn* — broken. Allah looks at the same man and says: that is *niʿmah,* and your mind is the soundest in the valley.

[PAUSE]

Stay with this, because it is the hinge of the whole ayah. **The same data — the same observable facts — produce opposite verdicts depending on who is doing the naming.** The crowd sees the symptoms and names them sickness. Allah sees the symptoms and names them *grace.* Nothing about the Prophet ﷺ changed between their verdict and Allah's. Only the One naming it changed. And He chose to embed His naming — *bi-niʿmati Rabbik* — in the dead center, where it can never be cut out.

What this single phrase reveals: **what the world pathologizes in you, Allah may be calling His favor — and He places His verdict closer to you than the insult ever gets.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: *Kāhin* and *Majnūn* — The Two Roads Out, and the Jinn Beneath Both

Now the two words themselves. And I promise you they are not random insults. They are a precise pair, and there is something hidden underneath them that, once you see it, you cannot unsee.

First, what each word actually means.

**Kāhin** — root *kāf-hā-nūn.* A *kāhin* is a soothsayer, a diviner, the fortune-teller who sat at the fairs of Arabia claiming to know the hidden and the future. *Al-Qurṭubī* and *al-Baghawī* give the precise charge underneath the word: a kāhin is one who *fabricates speech and reports what will happen tomorrow with no revelation* — who traffics in the unseen by his own invention or, as the Arabs believed, by snatched whispers from the jinn. To call the Prophet *kāhin* is to say: *your "unseen knowledge" is a con. You're a clever fraud running the oldest game in the desert.*

**Majnūn** — and here is where it gets extraordinary. The root is *jīm-nūn-nūn — j-n-n.* Now, what else comes from that root? *Jinn.* And *jannah* — the garden, hidden under its trees. And *janīn* — the embryo, hidden in the womb. And *junnah* — a shield, a thing that covers. Every single one of these words means **something covered, concealed, hidden from sight.** That is the bedrock meaning of the root: *to cover, to conceal.*

So *majnūn* — the passive form — literally means **"one who has been covered over."** Covered in his mind. His reason veiled, concealed, occluded. The Arabs believed madness came when the *jinn* — the hidden ones — got inside and covered a man's intellect. The word and the cause share a root: the *concealed beings* produce the *concealed mind.*

[PAUSE]

Now look at what these two accusations have in common. Look hard.

The *kāhin* gets his information *from* the jinn — they whisper to him from the unseen. The *majnūn* is overtaken *by* the jinn — they cover his mind. **Both accusations route through the jinn.** Both are the disbelievers' desperate attempt to explain something that has no ordinary explanation: *Where is this speech coming from?* This Quran is so far beyond the speech of men that they cannot file it under "ordinary human words." So they reach in two directions — and only two are available to them.

One direction is **upward, into cunning:** he's too clever, he's tapped a hidden source, he's a fraud with jinn-informants. That's *kāhin.*

The other direction is **downward, into collapse:** he's lost control, the hidden ones have taken him, he doesn't know what he's saying. That's *majnūn.*

Do you see the trap they built? The kāhin has *too much* control — a sly operator working the unseen. The majnūn has *too little* — no control at all, mind covered over. These are **opposite extremes.** And the disbeliever needs *both* on the table because each one, alone, doesn't quite fit. The Quran is too coherent, too structured, too overwhelming to be one man's fraud — so reach for madness. But it's too majestic, too ordered to be raving — so reach for cunning. They keep grabbing, and Allah negates both with a single denial: *bi-kāhin wa lā majnūn.* Not the clever one. Not the broken one.

[PAUSE]

And here is the irony that the root *j-n-n* hands us, and I want you to feel its full weight.

They called the Prophet ﷺ *majnūn* — **"the one whose sight is covered, whose mind is veiled."** But what was actually happening to him was the *exact opposite of covering.* He was the one man on earth at that moment having things **uncovered** for him. *Waḥy* — revelation — is the lifting of the veil. The very root of *kāhin*'s claim and *majnūn*'s slur is *concealment* — and the man they were slandering was the single human being whose veil was being *removed.*

They named him by the word for *hidden,* while he stood there as the most *unhidden-to* person alive.

[PAUSE]

There is one more thing in the sound. Say them: *kāhin... majnūn.* Both land on that nasal, droning ending — the *-in,* the *-ūn,* the nunation that hums through the whole surah's rhyme. These two slurs are made to *sound* like a pair, twinned by their music, so that the ear hears them as the two halves of one accusation — and then hears Allah cleave them both with *wa lā,* "and **not**."

What these two words reveal: **the world will always try to name the divine gift as either fraud or madness — too much or too little — because it has no category for "this came from beyond." And the very word it reaches for, *covered,* is the precise opposite of what is happening: the veil is being lifted.**

---

### Bridge

Look at what these eight words have shown us. A command that assumes the truth is already buried in every heart. A two-word verdict of *grace* wedged into the exact center, where no insult can dislodge it. And a pair of slurs that turn out to be the only two exits a denying mind can find — both routed through *concealment,* both negated at once.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words, and not just about one Prophet in one valley — but about reality itself, and about you.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: When the World Names Your Gift a Sickness

Let me start where you live.

There is a thing that happens to almost every person who carries something unusual. The world does not have a slot for it, so the world reaches for the nearest pathology and files you there.

The child who feels everything intensely is told she is "too sensitive," "too much," "dramatic." The person who will not compromise on a principle is called "rigid," "difficult," "self-righteous." The one who sees a problem coming before anyone else is called "negative," "a worrier," "paranoid." The one who withdraws to think is "antisocial." The one who burns with conviction is "a fanatic," "intense," "obsessed."

[PAUSE]

Notice the pattern in every one of those. The world takes a real trait — sensitivity, integrity, foresight, depth, zeal — and renames it with a word from the clinic or the insult shelf. It does not say *you have a gift I cannot categorize.* It says *something is wrong with you.* Because "something is wrong with you" is easier to live with than "you have access to something I don't."

This is *exactly* the structure of *kāhin* and *majnūn.* The Quraysh did not have a category for "this man is receiving divine speech." That category was unthinkable. So they reached for the two nearest slots — fraud and madness — and filed him there. Not because the evidence pointed there. Because **those were the only slots their worldview contained.**

And here is the first thing the ayah does for you. It does not tell the Prophet to argue with the diagnosis. It does not say *prove to them you're sane.* It re-names him *from above.* *Bi-niʿmati Rabbik* — by the grace of your Lord. It lets the higher authority overrule the crowd's verdict.

[PAUSE]

This is the move, and it is the only move that works. You will never out-argue the crowd's diagnosis on the crowd's terms, because the crowd controls the terms. The kāhin/majnūn frame is rigged — anything the Prophet does fits it. If his speech is coherent: clever fraud. If it overwhelms him: madness. There is no behavior that escapes the frame *from inside the frame.*

The only escape is a verdict from outside it. Someone with more authority than the crowd, who watches you more closely than the crowd, naming the same facts differently. For the Prophet ﷺ, that was *Rabbika* — **your** Lord, the One nurturing you, the One who has been raising you stage by stage and *knows what He is growing.*

And notice — it is *Rabb,* not a distant name. The word for the One who tends a seed into a tree. The crowd looks at the seed cracking open in the dark soil and says *it's dying, it's rotting.* The gardener looks at the identical sight and says *it's germinating.* **Same cracking. Same dark. Opposite verdict.** Because one of them knows what is being grown.

[PAUSE]

So here is the question this theme presses on you. *Whose naming are you living inside?*

When the people around you reach for the nearest pathology to explain the thing you carry — your depth, your conviction, your refusal, your sight — do you accept their filing? Do you start to believe you are "too much," "rigid," "naïve," because that is the word that got assigned? Or do you let the verdict of the One who is *growing* you sit closer to your heart than the verdict of the crowd?

The ayah's structure already answered this. Allah placed *bi-niʿmati Rabbik* **closer to the Prophet than the insult.** Right against him. The slander is in the verse — but it is on the far side of the grace. That is the geometry of a healthy soul: the insult is allowed to exist, but God's naming of you sits *between* you and it.

What this changes about tomorrow: when the next label comes — and it will come — do not rush to defend yourself on its terms. Instead, ask the higher question first: *What does my Rabb name this?* Put His verdict in the center, the way He did, and make the insult climb over it to reach you. Often it cannot.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: Your Job Is One Word — *Remind* — and the Result Was Never Yours

Now let me take you to the other discovery, because the two halves of this ayah are secretly one teaching, and the second half completes the first.

Go back to *fa-dhakkir.* Remind. One word. The whole assignment.

I want to show you the weight that has been *lifted* off the Prophet's shoulders by that single word — and off yours.

Think about how we usually carry the people we love who are far from the truth. The parent whose child has drifted from prayer. The friend who married a faith and watched it slip. The one who keeps trying, gently and then desperately, to bring someone back — and lies awake measuring their *failure* by whether the other person changed. *I couldn't reach him. I said it wrong. If I'd been better, she would have listened.*

[PAUSE]

Hear what Allah did *not* say to His Prophet — the most eloquent, most beloved, most God-supported caller who ever lived. He did not say *convince them.* He did not say *guide them.* He did not say *bring them to faith.* He did not even say *succeed.*

He said one word. *Dhakkir.* Remind.

The verb contains the *action* and stops there. The *outcome* is not in the verb — because the outcome was never the Prophet's to carry. Elsewhere Allah says it flatly: *fa-dhakkir, innamā anta mudhakkir — lasta ʿalayhim bi-muṣayṭir* (88:21-22) — "So remind; you are only a reminder. You are not over them a controller." And again: *wa dhakkir, fa-inna-dh-dhikrā tanfaʿu-l-muʾminīn* (51:55) — "And remind, for the reminder benefits the believers."

[PAUSE]

Now bring back what *dhikr* means — *reminding,* not teaching. Wiping dust off a thing already there.

Do you feel how that completes the relief? Not only is the *outcome* not yours — even the *content* isn't something you have to manufacture and install. You are not building the truth inside the other person. It is already in them, buried under the dust of *ghaflah,* of forgetting. Your job is the smallest possible act: bring it near, say it clearly, wipe the surface. Whether the heart underneath chooses to recognize what it already owns — that is between that heart and its *Rabb.*

This is the mercy hidden in making the command *remind* instead of *teach,* and a single word instead of a paragraph. **It is sized to what a human being can actually carry.** You can always remind. You cannot ever guarantee a result. So Allah made the duty the thing you *can* do, and kept the thing you *cannot* do entirely with Himself.

[PAUSE]

And see, finally, how this loops back to the slander. They called him *majnūn* — the one whose sight is *covered.* But the covering was never on him. The covering — the *jnn,* the veil — was on **them.** They were the ones with the dust on the mirror. The reminder he carried was the very thing that could wipe it off. The accusation they threw — *concealed, veiled, covered* — described their own condition exactly, and named the cure he was offering by its own opposite.

So the caller is told: *remind.* Lift what you can lift. And do not take the listener's veil as a verdict on you. Their covering is theirs. Your reminding is yours. The uncovering — that belongs to *Rabbika.*

[PAUSE]

The question this theme leaves in your hands: *Where in your life have you confused "I reminded" with "I failed to convince"?* Whose distance from God are you carrying as your own guilt, when Allah only ever asked you to *remind* — and kept the hearts for Himself?

What this changes about tomorrow: say the true thing, clearly and with mercy, to the one you love — and then *set down the outcome.* Not with coldness. With trust. You did your one word. The result was always above your pay grade, and it was always in better Hands than yours.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Now stand back and look at the whole shape of these eight words, because the design is the proof.

> **فَذَكِّرْ** — *so remind* — the command (one word, your part)
> **فَمَآ أَنتَ** — *for you are not* — the negation begins
> **بِنِعْمَتِ رَبِّكَ** — *by the grace of your Lord* — **the center**
> **بِكَاهِنٍ وَلَا مَجْنُونٍ** — *a soothsayer or a madman* — the two slanders

It is built as a shield. The two accusations — fraud and madness, too-much and too-little — are pushed to the *outer edge* of the sentence. The command is at the front. And in the protected center, surrounded on both sides, sits *grace.* The structure *enacts* its meaning: God's favor is placed where nothing can reach it, with the Prophet's task in front of it and the world's insults held off at the rim.

The *junnah,* remember, is a shield — from the same root *j-n-n* the slanderers used. They threw a *j-n-n* word, *majnūn,* "covered." And Allah answered by *covering* His Prophet — shielding him — with grace placed at the center of the verse. He turned their root against them.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the two themes are one truth seen from two sides.

The grammar showed us *grace, buried in the center, guarded by the structure.* The themes showed us *let God's naming of you sit closer than the crowd's, and carry only your one word — remind.*

They meet here: **You are responsible for the front of the sentence — the reminding. You are not responsible for the edges — what they call you, or whether they listen. And in the center, between your effort and their verdict, sits the only thing that was ever truly about you: the grace of your Lord, naming you what the world refused to see.**

### Questions to Carry

1. Whose naming am I living inside — the crowd's pathology, or my *Rabb*'s verdict? Which one sits closer to my heart?
2. What gift of mine has the world filed under "too much" or "something's wrong" — and what might Allah be calling it instead?
3. Where have I been grieving a "failure to convince" when I was only ever asked to *remind*?
4. Whose veil have I been carrying as my own guilt?
5. When the next insult comes, can I make it climb over God's verdict to reach me — the way this verse is built?

### One-Sentence Distillation

*Do your one task — remind — and let the grace of your Lord, not the verdict of the crowd, be the name you live under, for what they call sickness He may be calling His favor.*

### The Closing Invitation

You will be misnamed. If the most trustworthy man in Mecca, receiving the speech of God, was called fraud and madman in the same breath — you are not exempt, and you were never promised exemption. What you are offered instead is better: a verdict from above, placed nearer to you than the insult, and a job small enough to actually do. Let that be the shift. Stop arguing on the crowd's terms. Center the grace. Lift what you can lift. Set down the rest.

### Du'ā

O Allah, who named Your beloved by Your grace when the world named him by its blindness — name us by Your favor, and place that naming closer to our hearts than every insult and every label. Make our one task light upon us: to remind, and to trust You with the rest. And lift from our own eyes the veil we are so quick to see on others. *Āmīn.*
