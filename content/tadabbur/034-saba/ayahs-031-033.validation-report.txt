ISSUES:
- [MODERATE] "That word — لن (*lan*) — is the strongest negation Arabic has for the future... We absolutely never will." — The directional claim (لن is stronger than لا for future negation) is broadly correct and accepted by classical grammarians. However, the gloss "absolutely never will" imports a sense of ta'bīd (eternal/absolute negation) that classical grammarians explicitly dispute. Ibn Hisham in Mughni al-Labib refutes the position that لن grammatically entails eternality, treating it as emphatic future negation, not absolute. The word-by-word reference provided here gives simply "Never will" — not "absolutely never will." The contextual reading (these disbelievers are making an emphatic confident refusal) is defensible, but the grammatical claim overstates what لن itself encodes. A careful reader taught "لن means absolutely never" will misapply it elsewhere in the Quran.
