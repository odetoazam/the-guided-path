ISSUES:
- [MODERATE] "The first *baʿḍ* has the pronoun *-kum* attached — *baʿḍukum*, 'some of you'... Even the second group is left ungrouped. The indefiniteness is total." — This is grammatically imprecise. In Arabic, baʿḍukum is *maʿrifa* (definite) because it is in an iḍāfa construction with the definite attached pronoun *-kum*; pronouns always render the preceding noun definite. Only *li-baʿḍin* (second occurrence, with tanwin kasra) is genuinely indefinite/nakirah. The thematic point — that the day erases named categories — is a valid rhetorical reading, but the specific grammatical claim "The indefiniteness is total" misrepresents the syntax of baʿḍukum. A reader with Arabic grammar knowledge would flag this.

- [MODERATE] "They denied with their tongues. Allah responds with their tongues." — This is an interpretive connection between tukadhdhibūn (the verb of denial) and dhūqū (the verb of tasting) via the organ of the tongue. The classical sources provided (Jalalayn, Muyassar, Qurtubi, Baghawi) do not make this link; none mention tongues as the connecting element. The observation is plausible as literary analysis but is presented with rhetorical conviction ("There is an exactness here") that implies documentary grounding it does not have from the tafsir record.

- [PASS — minor note] Transliteration renders ضَرًّا as "ḍarrā" (suggesting a long ā). In connected speech (waṣl), this word carries tanwin naṣb and should be "ḍarran." The pausal form (waqf) justifies "ḍarrā" only if read with waqf on it, which is unusual here mid-verse. Marginal issue; not a root-level error.

SUMMARY: The reflection is substantively sound and well-grounded in its surah-level narrative and tafsir references, but overstates a grammatical claim about baʿḍukum's definiteness, and presents one interpretive literary connection (tongue/denial/taste) as more textually documented than the classical sources support.
