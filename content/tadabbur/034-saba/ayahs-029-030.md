# When the Mockers Asked "When?" — and Allah Refused to Give a Date

## وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَـٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ ۝ قُل لَّكُم مِّيعَادُ يَوْمٍ لَّا تَسْتَأْخِرُونَ عَنْهُ سَاعَةً وَلَا تَسْتَقْدِمُونَ

*Wa yaqūlūna matā hādhā al-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn ۝ Qul lakum mīʿādu yawmin lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn*

**"And they say: 'When is this promise, if you are truthful?' Say: 'For you is the appointment of a Day — you cannot delay it by an hour, nor can you bring it forward.'"**

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The World Behind These Words

Picture late Meccan Mecca. The Prophet ﷺ has been preaching for years. The message has been pressed against the city's chest until it can no longer be ignored. The arguments have all been made. The signs have all been pointed to. The objections — about idols, about ancestors, about wealth, about poverty — have all been raised and answered.

And finally, the mockers arrive at the question they think will end the conversation.

Not "what is your evidence?" — that conversation has run its course. Not "why should we believe?" — they've already decided they won't. They arrive at the question that sounds like inquiry but is built like a trap.

**"When?"**

When is this Day you keep warning us about? When is the resurrection? When is the accountability? You've been telling us about this *waʿd*, this promise, for years now — name the date. Pin it down. Tell us this Tuesday. Tell us next year. Tell us *something*.

Because they know — and we know they know — that no specific date is coming. And so the question is not really a question. It's a closing move. It's the conversational equivalent of a man crossing his arms and saying *prove it now, on my terms, or admit you were never serious.*

[PAUSE]

This is the climate Surah Saba is breathing in. Earlier in the same surah — only twenty-six ayahs before this one — Allah tells us that they have already announced, *lā taʾtīnā al-sāʿah* — "the Hour will not come to us." They have already made their position. The "when?" question here is not the curiosity of someone who might believe if only they had a date. It is the smirk of someone who has decided, and now wants to make believers look foolish.

This is one of the most repeated demands in the entire Quran. *Matā hādhā al-waʿd* — when is this promise? It appears in Yūnus, in al-Anbiyāʾ, in al-Naml, in Yāsīn, in al-Mulk. Across surahs and across years of revelation, the mockers kept arriving at this same closing move.

And Allah keeps answering it.

### The Hook

Here is what should stop you. They asked Him *when*. He had every option available. He could have given a date. He could have said *the Hour is in the year 4000* — and they could not have argued. He could have said *next Friday* — and shaken them to their cores.

He gives them neither.

He gives them something stranger. He gives them an answer about their own powerlessness. He gives them an answer that doesn't tell them the *when* and instead tells them that they cannot *move* the when by so much as an hour in either direction.

Why?

Because they were never asking about time. **They were asking about power.** And He is going to show them, in the cleanest possible way, that the power they were testing for is not theirs to test.

Stay with me. We're going to take this slowly.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Root That Returns Transformed — وَعْد becomes مِيعَاد

Before we look at the ayah, let me teach you something about Arabic that English does not do well.

In English, when we want to say "promise" and "appointment," we use two completely unrelated words. They have separate etymologies, separate associations, separate textures. *Promise* feels like words. *Appointment* feels like a calendar entry.

Arabic does not work this way. Arabic builds families. From a single root — three letters that carry a core meaning — Arabic generates whole constellations of related words, each one rotating that meaning along a slightly different axis. The reader who knows the root sees the whole family at once. The root is the family resemblance.

Now look at what happens across these two ayahs.

In the question, they say: مَتَىٰ هَـٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ — *when is this waʿd?* When is this **promise**.

In the answer, Allah says: لَّكُم مِّيعَادُ يَوْمٍ — for you is the **mīʿād** of a day.

Notice something. **Both words come from the same root** — و-ع-د (*wa-ʿa-da*). They are not synonyms borrowed from different traditions. They are siblings.

But they are not the same word.

[PAUSE]

*Waʿd* (وَعْد) is the simplest noun from this root. It means a promise — the act of pledging that something will happen. It is unspecified in its mechanics. A waʿd can be vague. It can be a hope, a commitment, an intention to deliver.

*Mīʿād* (مِيعَاد) is a different morphological form from the same root. It carries the meaning of an **appointed time, an appointed place, a fixed rendezvous** — a meeting that has been set with specificity. The morphological pattern (*mifʿāl*) often functions in Arabic to denote a locus or instrument — *the place/time at which the root action is realized*. A *mīʿād* is not the promise itself; it is the *fixed encounter* the promise points to.

So watch what just happened.

They said: when is your *promise*?

He said: for you is the **fixed rendezvous**.

They tried to make the conversation about whether the promise was vague. He responded by upgrading the language. *You called it a waʿd, as if there might be ambiguity in it. There is no ambiguity. It is a mīʿād. A meeting. A locked appointment.*

The root is the same. He doesn't even change the subject. He just rotates the same root into a form that closes every door their question tried to open.

Let's sit with this.

They wanted to imply the promise was floating, unfixed, perhaps not real because no date had been named. He responds: *the absence of a date you can see is not the absence of an appointment. The appointment exists. The fixedness is not the issue. The issue is that I have not given the calendar to you.*

Here's what's remarkable. In English, we'd need a paragraph to make this point. In Arabic, He makes it by changing one letter pattern within the same root. **The argument is structural before it is semantic.** A reader who knows Arabic morphology sees the response to the question *before* they parse the meaning of the words. The form itself answers.

This is not decoration. This is divine economy.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: The Plural They Spoke To — كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ

Look again at the question: *matā hādhā al-waʿd in **kuntum** ṣādiqīn.*

"When is this promise, if **you (plural)** are truthful?"

The Arabic verb كُنتُمْ — *kuntum* — is the second-person masculine **plural**. They are not addressing one person. They are addressing a group.

Now this matters. Because just one ayah earlier — in 34:28 — Allah has been speaking to the Prophet ﷺ in the **singular**: *Wa mā arsalnāka illā kāffatan li al-nās* — "And We have not sent **you** (singular) except as a bringer of glad tidings and a warner to all of humanity."

The Prophet ﷺ is sent — singular. He is one man with one mission.

But when the mockers turn around to mock, they say *kuntum* — plural.

Why?

Because by this point in the Meccan revelation, the message has gathered a community. The Prophet ﷺ is no longer one man speaking into a void. There are believers. There are followers. There is a *we* that the mockers can identify and address. And when they aim their question, they aim it at the whole community. Not just the Messenger.

This is more cutting than it looks. Notice what it does psychologically.

If you address the Prophet ﷺ alone — *if you are truthful* — you make the mockery a question of one man's claim. He can answer or not answer. The community of believers is not in the line of fire.

But if you address the believers as a group — *if you (plural) are truthful* — you do something different. **You implicate every individual believer in having to defend a date they were never given.** You turn each believer into a person who is, by their faith, on the hook for an answer they cannot produce. You make every single one of them feel the embarrassment.

This is a tactic the modern world knows intimately. Watch how it works today: a believer holds a position. The skeptic does not engage the strongest scholar of that position. He engages the believer in front of him. *You believe this? Then prove it. You. Right now. With what you have in your hands.*

The strategy is to make the individual the proxy for the whole tradition. And the believer, caught off guard, often has nothing on hand.

The mockers in Mecca discovered this strategy before the internet did. They aimed it at the community of believers, *kuntum*, because they wanted every believer to feel the impossibility of producing a date.

[PAUSE]

And now look at Allah's response. *Qul* — *say*. Singular. Imperative to the Prophet ﷺ.

The mockers addressed the believers as a group. Allah turns and tells His Messenger: *you respond.* The community of believers does not have to scramble for an answer. The Messenger speaks. The community is sheltered behind the response.

This is what it looks like to be loved by Allah. The mockers tried to put every believer on the spot, and Allah said — *no, the answer comes through one mouth, the one I have chosen to speak through.* The believer does not have to be the apologist for what only revelation can answer.

Stay with that for a moment.

When someone today demands that *you*, personally, justify a metaphysical claim with an empirical date — you have not been put on the hook by Allah. You have been put on the hook by them. And Allah, here, is teaching us that the answer does not come from your scrambling. It comes from what He has already revealed through His Messenger.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: The Symmetry of Powerlessness — لَّا تَسْتَأْخِرُونَ ... وَلَا تَسْتَقْدِمُونَ

Now we come to the most architecturally beautiful part of the response. And to see it, you need one more piece of Arabic grammar.

Arabic has ten common verb forms. Form I is the basic shape. Each subsequent form modifies the meaning in a predictable way — Form II often intensifies, Form III often makes the action mutual, Form IV often makes the verb transitive or causative, and so on.

Form X — the form *istafʿala* (اِسْتَفْعَلَ) — frequently carries the meaning of **seeking, requesting, or attempting** the action of the basic verb. From *ghafara* (to forgive) we get *istaghfara* (to seek forgiveness). From *qāma* (to stand) we get *istaqāma* (to seek to stand firm, to be upright). The Form X often adds an element of *wanting it, trying to obtain it.*

Now look at the response. The Day, He says, is one **you cannot delay** and **cannot advance**. The two verbs:

- لَّا تَسْتَأْخِرُونَ — *lā tastaʾkhirūn* — from the root أ-خ-ر (delay, lateness), in Form X.
- وَلَا تَسْتَقْدِمُونَ — *wa lā tastaqdimūn* — from the root ق-د-م (advance, precedence), in Form X.

Both are Form X. Both addressed in the plural — to *them*. And they are perfectly mirrored — one verb pulling time backward, one verb pushing time forward, both in the form that suggests *seeking, attempting, trying.*

Let me show you what this gives us.

Allah is not just saying "you cannot delay the Day and cannot advance it." That alone would be devastating. He is saying something more precise. **He is saying you cannot even seek to delay it, you cannot even seek to advance it.** The Form X strips them of even the attempt. Even the trying has no purchase.

In English, we might say: *try as you might.* The Form X holds that whole phrase inside a single verb pattern.

[PAUSE]

Now notice the symmetry. He uses *both* directions. Why both?

Because the question they asked could have been answered in either direction. *When* is the promise? It could be far away — they would want to delay it further to avoid it. It could be close — they would want to push it back. Or it could be far away and they would want to bring it forward to test it, to see it, to dare it.

He closes both doors.

You cannot push it back by an hour. You cannot pull it forward by an hour. Whichever way you wanted to manipulate the appointment to make it serve your skepticism, **the appointment is not movable in either direction.**

And the elegance is that He gives you both directions in one breath. *Lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn.* The symmetrical impotence of the mocker. They asked one question with one direction of doubt, and He answered by showing them they had no purchase on time in *either* direction.

[PAUSE]

And then — there's سَاعَة. *Sāʿah.* An hour.

In Arabic, the word *sāʿah* means "an hour" of time — the smallest unit of duration the Arabs commonly named. But *al-Sāʿah*, with the definite article, is also one of the standard Quranic names for **the Hour** — the Day of Resurrection itself.

So when Allah says they cannot delay this Day "by a *sāʿah*," there is a play of meaning sitting on the page. They cannot delay the Day by even an hour of time — by even the smallest unit they have a word for. And the word they have for the smallest unit is itself the name of the very thing they are trying to delay.

They cannot delay the Hour by an hour. The smallest amount of time they could request is itself called by the same name as the reality they are trying to push back. The language is collapsing in on itself, demonstrating the impossibility of escape even at the level of vocabulary.

This is what it means for the Quran to be miraculous in its words and not only in its claims.

### Bridge

So we have three things now. We have the root that returned upgraded — *waʿd* became *mīʿād*, vagueness sharpened into fixedness. We have the plural that gathered the believers under fire — *kuntum* — and the singular *qul* that returned the answer to one mouth, protecting the community. We have the symmetrical Form X verbs that stripped them of even the attempt to move the Day in either direction.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Question That Is Not a Question

Have you ever been in a conversation where someone asked you a question, and you knew immediately that the answer did not matter? That whatever you said, they had already decided to use it against you?

Most of us have. The college student whose parent asks "what are you going to do with that major?" — already convinced it was a mistake. The friend who asks "but how do you *know*?" — already certain you don't. The interviewer whose question contains its own answer. The cross-examination disguised as curiosity.

There is a special kind of question whose function is not to gather information. Its function is to perform certainty in the asker and to extract embarrassment from the answerer.

This is the question the mockers asked.

*Matā hādhā al-waʿd in kuntum ṣādiqīn.* When is this promise, if you (plural) are truthful?

Notice everything they did inside one sentence. They turned the indefinite into the demonstrative — *hādhā* — "**this** promise," as if to mock the very act of pointing to it. They demanded a date. They tied the believers' truthfulness to the production of that date. **They constructed a test the believers could not pass and pretended the test was reasonable.**

This is the architecture of bad faith dressed as inquiry.

[PAUSE]

And here is where Allah's response becomes a teacher for every believer who has ever stood in that conversation.

He does not produce the date. He does not even acknowledge that the date was the thing being asked for. He **changes the frame of the question itself.** They asked about the *when*. He answered about their **inability to move the when.**

Watch the move. *You asked when, as if knowing the date were what mattered. I will tell you what actually matters: you cannot push it back by an hour and you cannot pull it forward. The date is irrelevant to your situation. The fixity is everything.*

This is the lesson. **When someone is asking a question whose function is to trap you, you do not have to answer inside their frame.** You can step outside it. You can ask the deeper question their question was avoiding. You can name what they were actually doing.

The mockers were not asking for a date. They were asking for power over the appointment. They wanted to feel that if no date could be named, the appointment could not be real — that their inability to know the time meant time was on their side. They were treating their ignorance as freedom.

Allah's response strips this away in twelve words. *The appointment exists. You cannot move it. Your not knowing the date is not your possession of more time. It is your possession of no power.*

[PAUSE]

The practical application is sharp. The next time someone asks you a question whose function is to trap, you are allowed — even taught, by these ayahs — to refuse the frame. You don't have to scramble for a date that was never the point. You can step back and say: *the question you are asking is not the question you think you are asking. Let me show you what you were actually asking, and what the answer to that is.*

This is not evasion. This is fidelity to the truth at a deeper level than the question was operating on.

### Theme Two: The Hour You Cannot Move

There is a particular human terror that we do not name often, because naming it requires admitting it. It is the terror of being on a moving train you did not board, with a destination you did not choose, with no brake handle you can reach.

Most of us spend our days arranging the illusion that we are driving. We make our calendars. We plan our weeks. We decide what we will do with our Tuesday. We negotiate, we schedule, we postpone.

But sit still for one moment, in the quiet of late evening, and you can feel it. **You are aging at exactly the speed of time and there is no setting on the dial.** Your body is moving toward an appointment you did not write down. Your heart will beat exactly until it doesn't, and the *when* is not in your hands.

The mockers in Mecca wanted to make this appointment feel optional. They wanted a date — so that they could measure their lives against it, plan around it, perhaps mock it into smallness. They wanted to treat the unseen as if it were just another item on the calendar they were running.

And Allah's answer is gentle and brutal at once. *Lakum mīʿādu yawmin lā tastaʾkhirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn.*

**You have an appointment. The appointment is yours. And you cannot move it by an hour.**

The "for you" is the part we often miss. *Lakum* — for you. The Day is *yours*. It is the appointment that belongs to you. It is not a generic cosmic event — it is the meeting you are walking toward. Every person reading this is walking toward their own *mīʿād*. And it is fixed.

[PAUSE]

There is something the human heart resists about this and something the human heart secretly needs.

We resist it because we are used to feeling in charge. We have phones that summon cars. We have apps that move money. We have schedules we set and break. We have built a civilization whose entire promise is that you can move things around if you have the right tool.

And here, in twelve words, Allah is saying: *the most important appointment of your life is the one tool you do not have in your hand.* No app moves it. No money moves it. No prayer for "more time" extends it past the moment it has been set.

But we *need* to hear this. Because the illusion that we are in charge of our time is the same illusion that lets us procrastinate the only things that actually matter.

If the appointment were movable, we could delay our repentance. We could postpone our reconciliation with the person we wronged. We could push back the call to our parent we have been meaning to make. We could defer the moment we finally take our faith seriously.

The unmovability of the appointment is what makes today urgent.

[PAUSE]

The Prophet ﷺ taught us: *iʿmal li-dunyāka ka-annaka taʿīshu abadan, wa iʿmal li-ākhiratika ka-annaka tamūtu ghadan* — "work for your worldly life as if you will live forever, and work for your akhirah as if you will die tomorrow." This is sometimes attributed as a hadith and sometimes as wisdom of one of the early generations. Whatever its precise classification, the meaning lives in these ayahs.

The mockers wanted to know the date because they wanted to plan their disbelief — to keep it just barely in advance of the consequences. *Tell me when, and I will arrange my life to repent the day before.*

Allah refuses to give them this. **The appointment is unmovable, but the date is hidden — and the hiddenness, combined with the unmovability, leaves you with only one option.** You cannot delay the meeting, and you cannot know when it will happen. So you must be ready now. Not tomorrow. Now.

[PAUSE]

This is the gift hidden inside the apparent withholding. They asked when, and they thought knowing the date would be a gift. He withheld the date — and gave them, instead, a structure that makes every moment of every day matter.

The practical application here is simple but heavy. Make today the day you would want to be holding when the appointment arrives. Not because you know it is today — almost certainly it is not today — but because you cannot know it is not.

Make the call. Say the *istighfār*. Mend the relationship. Pray the prayer. Tell the person you love them. Do the thing your soul has been asking you to do for months.

Because the *mīʿād* is yours, and you cannot move it by an hour.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architectural Reveal

Now that we have done the work, look at how perfectly these two ayahs sit against each other.

| What They Said (34:29) | What He Said (34:30) |
|---|---|
| *Yaqūlūna* — they say | *Qul* — say (back to them) |
| *Matā* — when? | *Mīʿādu yawmin* — the appointment of a Day |
| *Hādhā al-waʿd* — this promise | *Mīʿād* — the fixed rendezvous |
| *In kuntum ṣādiqīn* — if you are truthful | *Lā tastaʾkhirūna...wa lā tastaqdimūn* — you cannot delay, cannot advance |

The Q&A is not a casual exchange. It is a precisely engineered response.

They said *yaqūlūna* — they say, present tense, ongoing. He said *qul* — say back, singular imperative. They demanded specifics. He gave fixity instead. They used the indefinite/vague *waʿd*. He upgraded the same root to *mīʿād* — locked appointment. They tried to put every believer on the spot with *kuntum*. He addressed the answer through *qul* — one mouth, the Messenger's. They challenged truthfulness with a date. He demonstrated power by stripping their ability to move time in either direction.

**Every move of the question is countered by a move in the response, and every move in the response uses the same linguistic material as the question — but elevated, fixed, made architectural.**

This is what it looks like when Allah speaks. He does not throw a different topic back at the mocker. He takes the mocker's own words and shows him what those words really meant.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

From the linguistic side: *waʿd* became *mīʿād*. Promise became appointment. The vagueness the mockers tried to exploit was upgraded into the fixedness they could not escape.

From the thematic side: the question that was not a question — the demand for a date — was answered by a refusal to play inside its frame, replaced with the harder truth that the Day is yours and you cannot move it by an hour.

Both reveal the same thing. **The mockers were trying to live in the gap between the promise and the appointment — the gap they thought they could widen by demanding a date.** Allah closed the gap. There is no gap. The promise *is* the appointment. The vagueness *is* the fixedness. The hidden date *is* what makes today matter.

### Questions to Carry

1. Where in your life are you living as if the appointment is movable — postponing what you know you should do, on the unspoken assumption that there is still time?
2. When someone asks you a question whose function is to trap rather than to learn, do you scramble inside their frame — or do you have the patience to step back and ask what they were really asking?
3. The mockers wanted a date so they could plan their disbelief just barely in advance of consequences. Where are you doing this — wanting just enough information to delay obedience as long as safely possible?
4. The plural *kuntum* was used to put every believer on the spot. Have you ever felt obligated to defend, alone and unprepared, what only revelation can fully answer? What would it mean to let *qul* — the Messenger's voice — carry the response, while you stand quiet inside what has already been said?
5. If you knew the *mīʿād* were tomorrow morning, what is the one act, one call, one prayer, one reconciliation you would not delay — and what is keeping you from doing it today?

### The Distillation

When mockers demand a date to test the promise, Allah responds not with a date but with the fact that they cannot move the appointment by an hour — revealing that their demand was never about knowing the time, only about possessing a power over time that they do not have.

### The Closing Invitation

You will be asked questions designed to trap you. You will be asked to produce dates and evidences for things that operate on a different register than calendar and instrument. You will be made to feel that your inability to produce a "when" is somehow a defeat.

These two ayahs are teaching you something different. They are teaching you that the absence of a date you can see is not the absence of an appointment that exists. The fixity is real even when the timestamp is hidden. And the response to the trap-question is not panic. It is fidelity to the deeper truth — *the appointment is yours, the appointment is unmovable, and the time to live like that is true is now.*

Walk today as someone whose *mīʿād* exists, whose hour cannot be moved, whose only freedom is in what you do with the time you are still standing inside.

### Du'aʾ

Yā Allah, You who said *qul lakum mīʿādu yawmin* — for them is the appointment of a Day — make us among those who lived as if today were the day, not because we feared knowing the date, but because we trusted that You had set it with perfect wisdom.

Yā Allah, when the mockers ask their questions and try to put us on the spot for what only revelation can answer, teach us the courage to speak from what You have revealed and the humility to be silent inside what You have hidden.

Yā Rabb, do not let us be among those who used the hiddenness of the appointment to delay what our souls knew they should not delay. Make us ready, in heart and in deed, for the meeting You have set for us. Ameen.
