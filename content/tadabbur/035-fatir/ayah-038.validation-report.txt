- [MODERATE] "the cosmos, vast as it is, is the *easy* part. The hard, hidden, slippery, ever-shifting thing — the thing that requires a knowing so total it needs its own intensified grammatical form — is the inside of a single human being" — the grammatical observation (ʿĀlim = ism fāʿil; ʿAlīm = ṣīghat al-mubālagha / faʿīl) is lexically and grammatically correct and well-attested. However, the causal interpretation built on it — that the intensified form was chosen *because* the chest is somehow harder or more elusive to know than the entire cosmos — is the author's own inferential reading. None of the four provided tafsir sources draw this conclusion. Al-Jalalayn reads the second clause as an a fortiori argument in the opposite direction: "فعلمه بغيره أولى بالنظر إلى حال الناس" (so His knowledge of other things is even more fitting, given the condition of people) — i.e., the heart-knowledge is the harder case that anchors the easier inference about the cosmos, not a claim about cosmological difficulty. The reflection presents this as what the grammar definitively shows rather than as a tadabbur inference.

SUMMARY: The Arabic grammar is accurately identified and the contextual framing (35:37 connection) is confirmed by Qurtubi, but the interpretive payoff — that the ʿAlīm / ʿĀlim shift encodes the chest as a harder object of knowledge than the universe — is stated with doctrinal confidence that exceeds what the provided tafsir sources explicitly support; it should be framed as reflection, not grammatical conclusion.
