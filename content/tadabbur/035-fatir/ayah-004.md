# When They Call You a Liar
### Surah Fatir, Ayah 4

---

## Introduction: The Hand on the Shoulder

Let me read you the ayah first, and then I want us to slow all the way down.

> **وَإِن يُكَذِّبُوكَ فَقَدْ كُذِّبَتْ رُسُلٌ مِّن قَبْلِكَ ۚ وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ**

> *Wa in yukadhdhibūka faqad kudhdhibat rusulun min qablik. Wa ila-llāhi turjaʿu-l-umūr.*

> *"And if they deny you, then certainly messengers before you were denied. And to Allah all matters are returned."*

Eleven words. On the page it looks like a passing remark. You could read it in four seconds and move on to the next verse without feeling that anything happened.

But I want to rebuild the moment this landed, because the words were not written on a page first. They were spoken into a specific wound.

Picture the man who first heard this. He is in Makkah. He has spent years — *years* — carrying a message that he knows, with the certainty of someone who has seen, is true. And the people he grew up with, the people who once called him *al-Amīn*, the trustworthy, the one you'd leave your valuables with — those same people now turn their faces and say: *you are making this up.* *You are a liar.*

Think about what that does to a human being. It is one thing to be opposed. It is another thing to be **called a liar about the most sacred, most certain thing you know.** Rejection of your ideas stings. Rejection of your *honesty* — being told that the truest thing in you is a fabrication — that goes somewhere deeper. It isolates. It makes you feel like you are standing alone on one side of a line, and the entire world is standing on the other, pointing.

And into that exact feeling, Allah speaks. Not with a thunderbolt. Not with an immediate punishment of the deniers. With something quieter and, if we listen, far more astonishing.

Here is the hook, and I'll only tease it: **notice that Allah does not promise the Prophet ﷺ that they will believe him. He does not say "be patient and they will come around." He does something else entirely with the rejection — He picks it up, and He hands it back transformed.** By the end of these eleven words, the loneliness is gone, and it was removed by grammar before it was removed by argument.

Let's find out how.

# PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element One: The Word That Means "To Call Someone a Liar"

Before we touch the ayah, let me teach you something about how Arabic builds intensity, because the whole emotional weight of this verse hangs on it.

In Arabic, most verbs come from a three-letter root, and that root can be poured into different *molds* or *forms*. The simplest form — Form I — just states the bare action. But Form II takes the middle letter and **doubles it**, and that doubling does work. It can intensify the action. It can make it causative. And very often it turns an inner state into a *declaration aimed at someone else.*

Here's an everyday example. There's a difference between *being wrong* and *calling someone wrong to their face.* The first is a private fact. The second is an act done *to* a person — it has a target, it has aggression, it lands on someone. Form II is often the mold for that second kind of word. It points outward. It accuses.

Now hold that, and let's open the verse.

The root here is **ك-ذ-ب (k-dh-b)** — the root of lying, of falsehood. Form I of this root, *kadhaba*, simply means "he lied." A private act. But that is **not** the word in our ayah.

The word is **يُكَذِّبُوكَ — *yukadhdhibūka*.** Form II. The middle letter doubled — you can even hear it: *kadh-dhi*, the tongue catching and striking twice. And listen to what that doubling turns "to lie" into. It no longer means "to tell a lie." It means **"to call *you* a liar. To declare *you* false. To throw the accusation of falsehood at *you*."**

[PAUSE]

Do you feel the difference? The verse is not describing people who happened to disagree. The very word Allah selects carries, baked into its shape, the act of *pointing at the Prophet ﷺ and pronouncing him a fraud.* And the *ka* at the end — *yukadhdhibū-**ka*** — is "**you**." It is aimed directly at him. The accusation has a target, and the target is named.

This is the wound, captured in a single word. Allah does not soften what is happening. He names it at full strength. *If they call you a liar to your face.*

And here's what's remarkable — and it's the first thing I want you to carry out of this verse. **The cure begins by not denying the pain.** Allah does not say "if they merely doubt you" or "if they are confused about you." He uses the harshest, most personal word available. He lets the wound be exactly as big as it is. **You cannot be consoled for an injury that someone insists is small.** The honesty of the word *yukadhdhibūka* is itself the beginning of the comfort.

[PAUSE]

## Element Two: The Mirror — and the Deniers Who Vanish

Now watch what Allah does next, because this is one of the most quietly perfect movements in the Qur'an, and it happens entirely through grammar.

The reply to "if they call **you** a liar" is:

**فَقَدْ كُذِّبَتْ رُسُلٌ مِّن قَبْلِكَ** — *faqad kudhdhibat rusulun min qablik* — "then certainly messengers before you were denied."

I need you to notice three separate things here, and each one is doing structural work.

**First: it is the same word.** *Kudhdhibat* — same root, same Form II, same doubled middle letter. The exact word that was just aimed at the Prophet ﷺ is now placed on the messengers before him. Allah does not reach for a gentler synonym to describe the past prophets. He uses the *identical* verb. The message is wordless but total: **what is happening to you is not a different thing. It is the *same* thing, the same word, the same wound.** You have not been singled out for a unique humiliation. You have been enrolled in a lineage.

**Second — and stay with me, because this is the subtle one — the verb flips into the passive.** The first verb was active: *they* call *you* a liar — *yukadhdhibūka*. The deniers are present, doing the verb. But the second verb is *kudhdhibat* — "were denied." Passive. And in the passive, **the deniers disappear.** Allah does not say "the nations before them called the messengers liars." He erases the deniers entirely. Where did they go?

Think about who is *not* mentioned. The people who denied Nūḥ. The people who denied Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā. They were nations, kings, empires, generations who were utterly certain they were right. And in this verse — they are not even worth naming. They have been grammatically dissolved into a passive verb. **The deniers of every age share one fate: they become anonymous. The messengers are remembered by name; those who called them liars are swept into a passive construction with no subject.** [PAUSE] That is a preview of the Day of Judgment delivered through a verb tense.

**Third: the messengers are made *indefinite*.** The word is **رُسُلٌ — *rusulun*** — "messengers," with no "the." Not *the* messengers, a known closed list. Just *messengers* — an open, indefinite, uncountable company. *Some messengers. Many messengers. A whole unnamed multitude of them.*

Why indefinite? Because definiteness would give you a finite list you could count and close. Indefiniteness throws the doors open. It says: *you are joining a company so large it cannot be enumerated.* The Prophet ﷺ moves, in the space of half a verse, **from "you" — singular, isolated, alone on his side of the line — to one figure inside an innumerable, honored procession** stretching back to the beginning of revelation.

[PAUSE]

Look at what the grammar has accomplished before a single argument has been made. The loneliness that opened the verse — *they are calling **me**, specifically me, a liar* — has been answered not by promising vindication, but by **dissolving the isolation itself.** *You are not the first. You are not alone. The very thing that made you feel singled out is in fact the oldest mark of the ones Allah loves most.*

The rejection didn't shrink. It got *reframed.* It became a membership card.

## Element Three: "And to Allah the Matters Are Returned"

Now the verse turns, and the final clause is where everything resolves.

**وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ** — *wa ila-llāhi turjaʿu-l-umūr* — "and to Allah all matters are returned."

Two grammatical features here, and both matter.

**First, notice the verb is passive again: *turjaʿu* — "are returned."** Not "the matters return" as if they wander back home on their own. They *are returned* — there is an unstated Hand doing the returning. Everything that is happening — the denial, the mockery, the apparent triumph of the liars-callers, the apparent helplessness of the truthful — all of it is being *drawn back*, deliberately, by an agent the verse doesn't need to name because there is only One it could be.

And there's a beautiful detail the early reciters preserved here. Al-Qurtubī records that a whole school of readers — al-Ḥasan, al-Aʿraj, Yaʿqūb, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and others — read this not as the passive *turjaʿu* but as the active *tarjiʿu*: "the matters **return** to Allah." Don't treat these as rivals to choose between. Treat them as two camera angles on one reality. The passive reading — *are returned* — shows you **Allah's hand**, pulling everything back to Himself. The active reading — *return* — shows you the **inevitability**, that all affairs move toward Him the way water moves toward the sea, because there is nowhere else for them to go. One angle shows the Mover; the other shows the motion. Together: *nothing is loose. Everything is on its way back to Him, and He is the One drawing it.*

**Second, look at the object: ٱلْأُمُورُ — *al-umūr* — "THE matters," definite, plural, total.** And here is an asymmetry I don't want you to miss. A moment ago the messengers were *indefinite* — *rusulun*, an open company. But the matters are *definite* — *al-umūr*, **all** of them, the closed totality, every single affair without exception.

[PAUSE]

Sit with that pairing, because it is exquisite. **The company of the rejected is left open** — there is always room for one more truthful soul to be denied and join the procession. **But the matters that return to Allah are sealed shut** — *all* of them, every act of denial, every mockery, every injustice, every hidden intention, the whole ledger, closed and complete, returning to the One who originated it.

So the verse ends not with "and they will believe you" — that promise is never made. It ends with something stronger: **"and every matter, without exception, is on its way back to Allah."** The verdict is not yours to extract from them. It was never your job to win the argument. The case has already been transferred to the only Court that finishes things.

### Bridge

So look at what the language has built. The harshest word for personal rejection — *yukadhdhibūka.* Then a mirror that uses the *same* word for the prophets while making their deniers vanish into the passive and their company swell into the indefinite. And finally, the whole affair lifted out of the Prophet's hands and returned, in its sealed totality, to Allah.

The grammar has already done the consolation. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the shape of our own lives.

# PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme One: The Loneliness of Being Disbelieved — and the Medicine of Company

Let me start where you actually live, far from the deserts of Makkah.

There is a particular kind of pain that almost everyone here has tasted. It is not the pain of failing. It is the pain of **telling the truth and not being believed.** The child who reports something real and is told *stop making things up.* The employee who raises a genuine alarm and is treated as a troublemaker. The person who finally speaks about something that happened to them and watches the room decide, silently, that they are exaggerating. The friend who says *this is who I really am* and feels the warmth in the room cool by ten degrees.

What makes this pain so specific is its **isolating** quality. When you fail, you can at least be sad in good company. But when you are *disbelieved*, the very faculty you'd use to reach for comfort — your word, your account of reality — is the thing under attack. You can't even successfully say *"this hurts,"* because the same people will hear that as one more thing you're inventing. Disbelief seals you inside yourself. It is a solitary confinement built out of other people's doubt.

Modern psychology has a phrase that circles this: the human terror of being **unseen** — of having your inner reality denied by everyone outside you, until you start to wonder whether *you* are the broken one. It is one of the most destabilizing experiences a person can have.

Now bring the ayah back.

Allah's response to His Messenger ﷺ in this exact agony is not *"prove yourself."* It is not *"they'll see eventually."* It is: **"messengers before you were denied."** And I want you to feel why that, specifically, is the medicine — because it tells us something about what disbelief actually does and what actually heals it.

Disbelief's deepest lie is: *you are uniquely false. Something is wrong with **you** specifically.* It individualizes. It makes you feel like the one defective node in a network of sane people.

And Allah's cure is to **break the isolation without removing the difficulty.** Notice — He doesn't make the denial stop. The deniers are still there. The verse does not fix the external problem at all. What it fixes is the *loneliness inside the problem.* *You are not the broken one. You are standing exactly where Nūḥ stood, where Mūsā stood, where every truthful soul has stood. The denial you're experiencing is not the mark of your falsehood — it is, historically, the mark of the **truest** people who ever lived.*

[PAUSE]

This is the reframe that changes everything: **in a world that runs on denial, being disbelieved by the deniers is not evidence against you. It may be evidence that you are carrying something true.** The procession of the rejected is, when you look closely, the procession of the prophets.

And here is the movement that takes this from history into your own chest. The same Allah who consoled His Messenger by saying *"you are not the first"* is saying it to **you** every time you are disbelieved for standing on something true. You were handed, in that moment, not a defect — but a place in a very old line.

**What does this change about tomorrow?** The next time you are disbelieved for telling the truth — really telling it — try, in that lonely moment, to do what the verse does: stop trying to win the disbelief, and instead *locate yourself in the company.* Whisper to your own heart what Allah whispered to His Prophet: *messengers before me were denied.* Not as a complaint — as an enrollment. The isolation is the lie. The company is the truth.

## Theme Two: Releasing the Verdict — "To Allah the Matters Return"

Here is something about the human heart that you can watch in yourself today.

When someone wrongs us — disbelieves us, slanders us, takes credit, dismisses us — we develop a *hunger.* Not just for the wrong to stop. A hunger for **the record to be corrected, now, publicly, in front of the same people who watched us be wronged.** We want the apology. We want the vindication. We want the moment where everyone turns and says *we were wrong about you, you were right all along.*

And notice what that hunger does. It chains you to the very people who hurt you. You replay the conversation in the shower. You draft the perfect reply you'll never send. You audition for the approval of an audience that already decided against you. The injustice happened once, but the *need to be vindicated* makes you re-suffer it a hundred times. You become the unpaid security guard of a wound, standing watch over it for years, waiting for a verdict that the people who wronged you will almost never deliver.

We are, by nature, *outcome-graspers.* We want to hold the resolution in our own hands. And we exhaust ourselves doing it.

Now hear the last four words of the verse again: **وَإِلَى ٱللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ ٱلْأُمُورُ** — *"and to Allah the matters are returned."*

This is not a passive consolation prize. This is a *release order.* It is Allah saying: **the verdict was never your jurisdiction.** The case of who was truthful and who was a liar-caller — that case is being moved, right now, out of the court of public opinion in Makkah and into the only Court whose rulings are final. You do not have to win it here. You were never equipped to. **And to Allah the matters are returned.**

Let's build through this.

The *first* thing this does is **dignify the deferral.** When you entrust a matter to Allah, you are not giving up — you are filing it with a higher authority. Letting go of your need to be vindicated *now* is not weakness or defeat. It is recognizing that some verdicts are too important to be rendered by people who call truth a lie. You are not dropping the case. You are *appealing it upward.*

The *second* thing — and this is heavier — **the passive verb means it returns whether you release it or not.** *Turjaʿu* — the matters *are returned.* This is not contingent on your tawakkul. Every affair is already on its way back to Allah, by His hand, on His schedule. So your grasping changes nothing about the outcome. It only adds your own exhaustion on top of the original wrong. **The matter is going home to Allah regardless. The only question is whether you go on carrying a duplicate of it in your chest the whole way.**

The *third* movement — and here is where the two halves of the verse become one. Why does Allah place "you are in the company of denied messengers" *right next to* "all matters return to Me"? Because the two are a single instruction. **Company removes the loneliness; return removes the burden.** He tells the truthful soul: *you don't have to feel alone (you're with the prophets), and you don't have to settle the score (I will). Your only job is to keep carrying the truth and keep walking. The accounting is Mine.*

[PAUSE]

Let a paradox sit here without rushing it: the way you finally *get* justice is by stopping your frantic attempt to *seize* it. The matters return to Allah most peacefully through the hands of the one who has opened those hands and let go.

**What does this change about tomorrow?** Pick the one injustice you are currently re-litigating in your head — you know the one; it has a specific face and a specific sentence you keep replaying. Tomorrow, when it rises again, instead of drafting the reply or rehearsing the vindication, try saying the verse over it: *wa ila-llāhi turjaʿu-l-umūr* — *and to Allah the matters are returned.* Not to suppress the feeling. To **file** it. To formally transfer that case out of your jurisdiction and into His. Watch how much energy comes back to you when you stop guarding a verdict that was never yours to hand down.

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## The Architecture

Now that we've walked through it, step back and see the shape of the whole verse, because it is built with a symmetry you can only appreciate at the end.

The verse moves in three beats:

1. **The wound, at full strength** — *yukadhdhibūka* — they call **you** (singular, exposed) a liar. Active verb, named target. Maximum isolation.
2. **The reframe** — *kudhdhibat rusulun* — the *same word*, now in the passive (deniers erased) on an *indefinite* company (the lonely "you" becomes one of an uncountable multitude). Maximum company.
3. **The release** — *turjaʿu-l-umūr* — the whole affair, the *definite totality*, lifted out of your hands and returned to Allah. Maximum trust.

Watch the journey of the human being across those three beats: from **alone**, to **accompanied**, to **unburdened.** And watch the deniers travel the opposite way: from **active and named** ("if *they* deny you"), to **erased into a passive verb**, to absorbed into "the matters" that return to Allah for judgment. As the truthful one is consoled, the deniers are quietly dissolved. The grammar enacts the verdict before the verdict is announced.

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

The grammar told us: the *same word* for your denial and the prophets' denial (you are in their company), the *deniers vanish* into the passive (they are nothing), the *matters are definite and returned* (the verdict is Allah's). And the themes told us: the medicine for being disbelieved is *company, not vindication*, and the medicine for injustice is *return, not revenge.*

It is one message: **When you are denied for the truth, Allah does not change your circumstances — He changes your standing and your address. He stands you in the lineage of the prophets, and He hands the verdict to Himself. Your job was only ever to carry the truth and keep walking.**

## Questions to Carry

1. When I am disbelieved, do I reach for *vindication* — or can I learn to reach for *company*?
2. Whose face is attached to the injustice I am currently re-litigating in my mind — and what would it feel like to formally *file* that case with Allah and stop guarding it?
3. Have I been reading my rejections as evidence that something is *wrong* with me — when they might be the oldest mark of the ones who carried something *true*?
4. The deniers in this verse become anonymous; the messengers are remembered. In the things I do quietly for Allah, which list am I living for?
5. If "all matters are returned to Allah" is *already* true, with or without my consent — what am I still carrying that I was always allowed to set down?

## One-Sentence Distillation

When they call you a liar, Allah does not promise you will be believed — He enrolls you in the innumerable company of the denied prophets and quietly transfers the verdict to the only Court that finishes things.

## The Closing Invitation

There is a particular freedom waiting on the far side of this verse. It is the freedom of the person who no longer needs the people who wronged them to admit it. Who no longer stands guard over a wound, waiting for an apology that may never come. Who, when disbelieved for the truth, feels not the cold of isolation but the warmth of a very old company — and who, when wronged, files the case upward and walks on lighter.

You don't have to win the argument. You were never the judge. *And to Allah the matters are returned.*

## A Brief Du'a

*O Allah, when I am called false for standing on what is true, do not let me drown in the loneliness of it — stand me where Your prophets stood, and let me find the warmth of their company. And every matter I am tempted to grasp and avenge, I return to You now; You are enough as the One to whom all things come home. Lighten what I was never meant to carry.*

*Āmīn.*
