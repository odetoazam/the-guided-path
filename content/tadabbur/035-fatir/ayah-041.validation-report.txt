- [MODERATE] "Form IV — *yumsiku* — which adds a hamza at the start" — This accurately describes the *perfect* Form IV (أَمْسَكَ), but the verb under analysis is the *imperfect* يُمْسِكُ, which carries no hamza. In the imperfect, Form IV is identified by the kasra on the middle radical (يُمْسِكُ vs. Form I يَمْسُكُ). The reflection is explicitly "teaching the form" to readers, so this imprecision in a morphological instruction context is worth flagging. The *functional* conclusion drawn (ongoing transitive grip) is correct; only the morphological identification rule is imprecise.

- [MODERATE] "Form IV puts the holding *onto something*, making the object the focus of the action" — This is a non-standard characterization of Form IV function. Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) is primarily causative or makes an intransitive verb transitive; "object focus" is not its classical grammarians' description, and Form I can also take direct objects. The functional analysis is directionally sound but the framing is idiosyncratic and could mislead readers who use it as a morphology reference.

The imperfect/perfect contrast (yumsiku ≠ amsaka as ongoing vs. completed) is morphologically accurate and theologically supported across all four sources.
