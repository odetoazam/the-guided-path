# The One Who Strives Strives Only For Himself

## Surah Al-Ankabut, Ayah 6

> [ayah:29:6] وَمَن جَـٰهَدَ فَإِنَّمَا يُجَـٰهِدُ لِنَفْسِهِۦٓ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَغَنِىٌّ عَنِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

> *Wa man jāhada fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih. Inna-Llāha la-ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿālamīn.*

> "And whoever strives — strives only for himself. Indeed, Allah is free of need from all the worlds."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are In The Surah

To meet this ayah, you have to know what just happened four verses earlier.

Surah Al-Ankabut opens with a question that has the texture of a hand placed on a chest mid-breath:

*Ahasiba-n-nāsu an yutrakū an yaqūlū āmannā wa hum lā yuftanūn?*

"Do people imagine that they will be left alone — just for saying 'we believe' — and will not be tested?"

That is the opening. That is the foundation under everything else in this surah. The very next ayah doubles down: *We tested those before them.* It is Allah informing us, with no softening, that the word *believer* is not a label you put on a coat. It is a load-bearing claim. And load-bearing claims get tested.

Then comes ayah 6. Our ayah.

### What This Means For The Listener

Picture the early Muslim community in Makkah when these words descended. They are being boycotted. Their relatives have crossed the street. Some have been tortured. Some have buried family members because of what they believe. They are exhausted. And the surah opens by telling them: *what did you think? That this would be free?*

Now imagine you are one of them, and you hear that opening, and your heart is already cracking under the weight of *why is this happening to me* — and then ayah 6 arrives.

This ayah is the answer to the silent question every tested heart has ever asked.

*"Why is Allah putting me through this? What does He want from me? What does He get out of my pain?"*

### The Hook

In eleven words — eleven — Allah does something that should not be linguistically possible. He tells you why you are struggling, He removes Himself entirely from the equation of benefit, and He redirects the entire weight of your effort back onto you as a *gift*.

There is one tiny particle in this ayah that flips your entire psychology of worship.
And there is one ending phrase that, if it lands properly, will rearrange how you understand what Allah is.

Let's go slowly. Because this one deserves it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Word *Jāhada* — And What It Actually Means

Let me ask you something before we touch the Arabic.

When you read the word *jihad* in English, what fires in your mind? Almost certainly an image of conflict, of holy war, of swords or rifles. The word has been so battered by political usage on both sides of the table that the actual Arabic meaning has been buried under it.

So we need to dig that out first. Because if you read *jāhada* in this ayah as "fought," the entire reflection collapses.

The root is **ج ه د** — *j-h-d*.

In classical Arabic, this root has nothing to do with weapons. It means **to expend effort to the point of exhaustion**. *Juhd* is the maximum capacity a human being can pour out. When the lexicographers describe this root, they describe it as the *exertion of one's utmost ability against something that resists*. Effort against resistance. That is the heart of the word.

A farmer who works his land until his hands bleed is engaged in *juhd*. A student who studies through the night until her eyes burn is engaged in *juhd*. Someone who is fighting a sickness inside their own body — yes, that is also *juhd*.

Now look at the form Allah uses. He does not use *jahada* (Form I, simple effort). He uses **jāhada** — Form III.

Teach yourself this simple grammar point: Form III in Arabic almost always implies **mutuality** or **a struggle directed against something that pushes back**. *Qātala* (Form III of *qatala*) means "fought against," not just "killed." *Sābaqa* means "raced against." *Khāṭaba* means "addressed someone who can respond."

So *jāhada* is not "he exerted effort." It is **"he exerted effort against something that was actively pushing back against him."**

[PAUSE]

Sit with that for a moment.

Allah did not say *wa man ʿamila* — whoever works. He did not say *wa man saʿā* — whoever strives in motion. He chose the word that specifically means **effort poured against resistance**.

Why does this matter? Because it tells you that Allah already knows. He already knows there is something pushing back when you try to pray on time. He already knows there is something pushing back when you try to lower your gaze. He already knows there is something pushing back when you try to forgive someone who hurt you. He already knows there is something pushing back when you try to wake up for Fajr. He chose the verb that *names* that resistance before you even feel guilty for feeling it.

Notice something. The ayah does not specify *what* you are striving against. The object is left wide open. Are you striving against your own nafs? Against shaytān? Against a hostile society? Against a chronic illness? Against your own doubts? Against the gravitational pull of the dunya?

Yes.

All of it counts.

The ayah is structured so that **any struggle for the sake of staying upright as a believer falls inside its embrace**. The Prophet ﷺ called returning from external conflict "returning to the greater jihad" — the struggle against the self. The word here is large enough to hold every shade of that meaning.

So when Allah says *wa man jāhada* — "and whoever strives" — He is speaking to the woman holding her tongue when she wants to lash back, and the man choosing the halal income when the haram one was easier, and the teenager pulling himself out of bed for Fajr in the dark, and the convert sitting at a family dinner where everything on the table reminds them how alone they are. All of them are inside this word.

**He sees the resistance. He named it before you confessed it.**

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: The Tiny Particle That Flips Everything — *Innamā*

Now look at what comes next.

*Fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih.*

"— then he only strives for himself."

I want you to slow down at one word: **innamā**.

Let me teach this before we use it. In Arabic grammar, *innamā* is what scholars call a **particle of restriction** — *adāt al-ḥaṣr*. It is one of the strongest tools in the Arabic language for taking a statement and locking it down to one and only one possibility.

Think of it like this. If I say in English, "the door is locked," I have made a statement. If I say, "the door is only locked — nothing else," I have made the same statement but I have walled off every other option. *Innamā* does that. It says *this and not that, this and not anything else.*

The grammarians describe *innamā* as combining two operations: it **affirms** what comes after it and **negates** everything that is not stated.

So when Allah says *fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih*, He is not just saying "he strives for himself." He is saying:

**"He strives ONLY for himself. The benefit of his struggle returns to no one else. Not to Me. Not to the cosmos. Not to anyone he is striving alongside. To him. Only him."**

[PAUSE]

Now hold that particle in your hand for a moment. Because something extraordinary is happening here.

Most religious traditions — and most of how human beings naturally think about worship — assume that the deity gets something from the worshipper. The deity is fed by sacrifice. The deity is honored by ritual. The deity is *strengthened* somehow by your effort. So your struggle is essentially a transaction: you pour out effort, the deity receives it, and in exchange you receive favor.

*Innamā* destroys that entire model.

Allah is saying: **whatever you pour out in struggle, it does not flow upward to Me. It loops back into you.**

Notice the word He chose for the destination of the struggle: *li-nafsih*. The *li-* means "for" or "to." The *nafs* is your own self — your inner being, your soul, the seat of your awareness. The pronoun *-hi* at the end attaches it personally to you.

Your struggle is for **your nafs**. Your struggle refines **your nafs**. Your struggle saves **your nafs**.

This is one of the most psychologically liberating sentences in the entire Quran, and most people read past it without feeling its weight. Let me try to make it land.

### A Detour Through Something Human

Imagine you have a child who is learning to walk. They fall, they cry, they stand back up, they fall again. You watch them, your heart breaking with every bruise. And one day someone asks you, *"Why are you making your child go through this? Why don't you just carry them everywhere?"*

You would look at them like they are insane. The falling **is** the learning. The exhaustion **is** the strengthening of the legs. The bruise on the knee **is** the body's record of growth. The child is not falling *for you*. The child is falling *for the child*. You gain nothing from their bruises. They gain everything.

That is what Allah is telling you in this ayah.

**You are the child. The bruises are yours. The legs that are strengthening are yours. None of this flows to Me.**

[PAUSE]

And here's the part that should make your eyes burn. The parent in this analogy at least *enjoys* watching the child grow — there is parental satisfaction, there is paternal investment, there is *something* the parent gets. The next phrase of the ayah is going to remove even that.

But before we get there — stay with the *innamā* for one more moment.

Think about every struggle you've had this week. Every time you forced yourself to do something hard for the sake of Allah. Every time you said *no* when you wanted to say *yes*. Every time you said *yes* when every cell in your body wanted to say *no*. Every prayer you dragged yourself to. Every dhikr you whispered when nothing in you wanted to.

The *innamā* says: **every single drop of that effort came back into you. None of it was lost upward. None of it disappeared. None of it was for someone else's benefit. It was, all of it, a deposit into the account of the person you are becoming.**

Your struggle is not a tax you pay. It is an inheritance you build for yourself.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: *Inna-Llāha La-Ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿĀlamīn* — The Free One

Now we arrive at the closing of the ayah, and we have to walk very carefully, because this is where most people read the words and miss the earthquake underneath them.

*Inna-Llāha la-ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿālamīn.*

"Indeed, Allah is *ghaniyy* — free of need — from all the worlds."

The key word is **ghaniyy**, from the root **غ ن ي** — *gh-n-y*.

Teach the concept first. *Ghinā* in Arabic means **self-sufficiency** — the state of needing nothing outside yourself. The word is used in everyday Arabic for *wealth* because wealth is the everyday picture of being free of need. A wealthy man is *ghaniyy* not because of how much he has, but because of how little he requires from others to maintain himself.

But here is the thing about human wealth: it is always conditional. The richest person on earth still needs oxygen. They still need their heart to beat. They still need the sun to rise. Their *ghinā* is borrowed. It is propped up by ten thousand things outside themselves that they did not create and cannot control.

When Allah calls Himself **Al-Ghaniyy**, He is using this word in a way no creature can. He is saying: **there is nothing — not one thing in existence — that I need from anything outside Myself in order to be what I am.**

And then look at what He attaches it to. *ʿani-l-ʿālamīn.*

The preposition *ʿan* in Arabic carries a sense of **distance from** or **independence of**. And *al-ʿālamīn* — "the worlds" — is the same word from Surah Al-Fātihah, where we call Allah *rabb al-ʿālamīn*, Lord of all the worlds.

The classical scholars understood *al-ʿālamīn* as encompassing **every category of created existence** — the worlds of humans, jinn, angels, animals, plants, the seen, the unseen, every realm that has ever existed or will exist. The word is in the plural form to indicate not just one type of world, but *all* types simultaneously.

So put it together. *Inna-Llāha la-ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿālamīn* means:

**"Allah is independent of every world that has ever existed."**

[PAUSE]

Let me try to make you feel what this is saying.

If every human being on earth right now stopped worshipping Allah this second, it would not diminish Him by an atom's weight. If every angel ceased its tasbih, He would not be less than He is. If every star in the cosmos collapsed, every galaxy went dark, every realm of jinn dissolved, every prophet's name was forgotten — **He would still be exactly what He is.**

Your worship adds nothing to Him.
Your sin subtracts nothing from Him.
Your struggle on the path enriches Him by zero.
Your abandonment of the path impoverishes Him by zero.

In a famous hadith qudsi, Allah says: *O My servants, if the first of you and the last of you, the human of you and the jinn of you, were all on the most pious heart of any single man among you, that would not increase My kingdom by anything. And if they were all on the most wicked heart of any single man among you, that would not decrease My kingdom by anything.*

This ayah is the Quranic verse that hadith is built on.

### Why This Phrase Is Mercy, Not Distance

Now — and this is the move that matters — most people read *Allah is free of need from the worlds* and feel a cold draft. *Oh. He doesn't need me. He doesn't care.*

That is the exact opposite of what is being said.

Watch the architecture of the ayah:

1. **Whoever struggles** — Allah acknowledges your exhaustion.
2. **Strives only for himself** — Allah tells you the benefit loops back into you.
3. **Indeed Allah is free of need from all the worlds** — Allah tells you *why* the benefit loops back into you.

Why does the benefit loop back to you and not up to Allah? **Because there is nowhere up for it to go.** Allah is not a being who can receive deposits. He is not a deity who needs to be fed. The struggle cannot flow to Him because He, by His very nature, has nothing to receive.

So the only place the benefit can go is *back into the one doing the struggling*.

Do you see what just happened?

The *ghinā* of Allah is the *reason* your struggle becomes yours. If Allah needed your worship, your worship would be a payment — and payments leave your hands. Because Allah needs nothing, your worship is a *gift to yourself disguised as worship*. The transaction He commanded you to do is one where the only beneficiary is you.

[PAUSE]

This is not a God who is far. This is a God who is so unconditioned that He has designed an entire system of worship in which **He gets nothing and you get everything.**

A parent who needs their child's love is a parent whose love is conditional — they will eventually demand return. A parent who needs nothing from the child can love the child with no conditions at all, because no demand can ever arise. *Ghinā* is the precondition of pure mercy. Need produces transaction. Self-sufficiency produces gift.

**Allah's independence is the foundation of His generosity.**

[PAUSE]

### Bridge

So look at what these three words have done.

*Jāhada* — Allah named the resistance you feel and validated that your struggle is real.

*Innamā* — Allah locked the door on every model of worship where you pay Him in effort, and redirected every drop back into you.

*Ghaniyy ʿani-l-ʿālamīn* — Allah explained the metaphysical reason: there is no one upward to receive what you are pouring out, because the One above lacks nothing.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself, and about the human heart that needs to hear it.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Quiet Egotism Hidden Inside The Question "Why Me?"

I want to start somewhere uncomfortable. With a question most of us have asked.

*Why is this happening to me?*

It is a fair question. Sometimes it is the question of a person who is genuinely trying to understand the wisdom in their trial. But often — and we have to be honest — it carries a hidden assumption underneath it. The assumption is: *this is happening to me because someone is doing this to me. There is a transaction. There is a sender. I am being charged.*

And that assumption can produce two responses: either bitterness toward the One we think is charging us, or a quiet pride that we are paying the bill.

Allah, in this ayah, dismantles both at once.

He is saying: **the struggle is not a transaction at all.** It is not Me extracting payment from you. It is not Me testing your loyalty for My benefit. The struggle is the *process* by which you become the person you were meant to be. It is the heat that tempers the steel. The steel is yours. The strengthening is yours. The result is yours.

### The Modern Bridge

Think about the way a personal trainer pushes a client through a workout.

The trainer does not need the client to do another rep. The trainer is not getting stronger when the client lifts. The trainer's body is not being shaped by the client's exhaustion. If the client quits halfway, the trainer's life is exactly the same.

But the trainer still pushes. Why?

Because **the client is the one becoming**. Every rep the client does is for the client's own body. The trainer's *ghinā* — his self-sufficiency, his completeness — is what makes him able to push the client honestly. He has no ego in the workout. He is not getting points. He is not being filled by their reps. So he can demand the rep with pure care for the client's growth.

Now imagine if the trainer's own paycheck or self-worth depended on the client's performance. Suddenly the workout becomes contaminated. Now the trainer is pushing the client partly for the client and partly for himself. The instruction becomes mixed.

Allah is telling you: **the workout you are in is pure**. The One running you through it gains nothing from your reps. The reps are entirely yours. Which means the instructions you have been given are not contaminated by His self-interest. They are designed for one thing and one thing only: to make you into what you were meant to be.

[PAUSE]

### Sit With What This Removes

If you really absorb this ayah, watch what falls away from your inner life.

The complaint that Allah is being unfair: gone. Allah gains nothing from your trial, so He has no motive for unfairness.

The sense that you are doing Allah a favor by praying: gone. He needs nothing from your prayer. Your prayer is a favor to your own nafs.

The sense that other people's worship benefits Allah more than yours: gone. He is *ghaniyy* from all of them too.

The sense of cosmic loneliness in your struggle, that no one sees: gone. The One who named the resistance before you confessed it — He sees.

The bitterness of *why is He doing this to me*: gone. He is not doing it *to* you. He is doing it *for* you. The verb itself, in the ayah, says so.

[PAUSE]

### What This Asks Of Us

Tomorrow, when you feel the resistance again — and you will — when the alarm goes off for Fajr and your body refuses, when the same family member tests your patience for the thousandth time, when the doubt whispers in the back of your mind, when the haram option glows and the halal option feels like sandpaper —

Pause and remember this ayah.

The resistance is not a sign that Allah is being cruel. The resistance is the *exact thing* that makes the effort count for you. There is no growth without resistance. There is no *jihad* without something pushing back. The pushing back is *built into the design* — and Allah named it before you complained about it.

And when you push through, the credit goes to one account. *Li-nafsih.* For yourself.

### Theme Two: What It Means To Worship A God Who Needs Nothing

Now I want to go to the second half of the ayah and stay there longer, because something happens in the human heart when this lands properly.

Most of us, even those of us who would never say it out loud, carry a small picture of Allah that looks something like this: a Being who is watching to see if we will obey, who is somehow *invested* in whether we do, who is *waiting* for our worship, who is *pleased* when we deliver and *displeased* when we don't.

And there is truth in some of that — Allah is indeed pleased by our worship and displeased by our disobedience. But underneath that surface, many of us secretly believe that **He needs the worship**. That He is, in some sense, dependent on the response of the worlds.

This ayah rips that picture in half.

### The Modern Bridge

Imagine a billionaire who genuinely needs nothing. He has no debt, no rivals, no enemies. His wealth, in the strange way of compounding, grows whether he works or not. He could vanish for a century and return to find his fortune intact.

This billionaire decides to fund a scholarship program for kids in poverty. He pays for their schools, their books, their food, their housing. He demands that they study hard, attend class, behave with character. If they slack off, they are removed from the program.

Now — when this billionaire demands hard work from a scholarship student, **what is his motive?** He doesn't need their grades. He doesn't need their gratitude. He gains nothing from their success. His net worth does not move based on whether the student gets an A or a C.

His only motive is that *the student becomes someone*.

The strictness is for the student. The standards are for the student. The discipline is for the student. The billionaire's *freedom from need* is precisely what allows the program to be pure.

Now scale that infinitely. The kafīl in this analogy is a human being who still has *some* needs — he might want public recognition, he might want a tax deduction, he might want a legacy. Even his independence is partial.

Allah is **absolute**. *Ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿālamīn.*

His commands to you are utterly uncontaminated by self-interest. Every "do this" and "don't do that" in the religion of Islam is, from His side, *pure design*. He is not feeding Himself with your obedience. He is not licking wounds with your disobedience. He is sculpting *you*.

[PAUSE]

### The Strange Comfort Of Cosmic Insignificance

Here is something that sounds at first like a hard pill and turns out to be a soft pillow.

**You are not cosmically necessary.**

Allah does not need you. The universe does not require your particular worship to keep running. If you had never been born, Allah would still be Allah. If you die tomorrow, Allah will still be Allah.

For some people, hearing this feels devastating at first. *Oh. I don't matter.*

But sit with it. Because if you keep sitting with it, it flips.

The fact that you are not cosmically necessary means that **everything Allah gives you is gift**. Your existence is gift. Your being addressed by the Quran is gift. Your being given the chance to struggle and grow is gift. Your prayer being accepted is gift. Your name being known to the Most Merciful is gift.

If you mattered to Him because He needed you, His attention would be obligation. Because He needs nothing, His attention is *grace*.

A king who must employ a servant because he cannot manage his own life — that servant is necessary. But a king who employs a servant when he could have done everything alone, employs the servant because **he wants the servant near him**. There is no other reason. The nearness is not necessity. It is choice.

You, in your prayer, in your struggle, in your dhikr, in the dragging yourself to Fajr — **you are near a King who chose to bring you near**. There is no extraction happening. There is only invitation.

[PAUSE]

### What This Asks Of Us In Practice

When you sit down to pray tonight, try this.

Before *takbīrat al-iḥrām*, just for a breath, remind yourself: *He does not need this prayer. I do.*

Watch what happens to the prayer.

The performance anxiety drops. The sense that you are auditioning drops. The sense that you have to "produce" khushūʿ to satisfy Him drops. You are not on stage. You are not delivering a service to a Being who is grading you for His benefit.

You are a child taking medicine. The doctor does not benefit. You do.

Now another application. The next time someone praises your worship or your discipline — *mā shā Allāh, you pray on time, you fast a lot, you read so much Quran* — feel how this ayah deflates pride.

Of course you do those things. They are *for you*. You are not earning points for Allah. You are receiving a benefit He has designed entirely for your nafs. Why would you be proud of taking the medicine that was prescribed for your own illness?

And in the other direction — the next time you fall, the next time you slip, the next time the sin happens — let this ayah pull you back. You are not letting Allah down in the sense of damaging Him. You are damaging *yourself*. *Wa man jāhada fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih* — and the implied reverse is also true: *whoever neglects, neglects only against himself.*

The mercy is that Allah is independent. The seriousness is that *you* are not. You are the one being shaped. And no one else can do the shaping for you.

[PAUSE]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Of The Ayah

Now look at the shape of these eleven words.

> *Wa man jāhada* — **the human side: struggle, exhaustion, effort against resistance.**
> *Fa-innamā yujāhidu li-nafsih* — **the redirection: it loops back to you, only to you.**
> *Inna-Llāha la-ghaniyyun ʿani-l-ʿālamīn* — **the divine reason: He is free of all the worlds.**

The first phrase faces *you*.
The second phrase points the arrow of benefit *at you*.
The third phrase explains *why* the arrow had nowhere else to go.

It is a perfect mercy spiral. Your struggle → into yourself → because Allah needs no struggle from anyone. The God who could have demanded payment chose to design a system where the payment is paid to you, by you, for you, while He commands it for nothing other than your becoming.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

The grammar of *jāhada* (Form III, resistance-implied) and the *innamā* (locked restriction) and the *ghaniyy* (absolute self-sufficiency) — they are not three separate insights. They are one insight refracted three ways.

**The insight is this: worship is not a tax you pay upward. It is a gift you receive downward. And the proof is that the One commanding it gains nothing from it.**

This single ayah, properly absorbed, ends the entire psychology of religious resentment. Because resentment requires a transaction. Allah has dissolved the transaction.

### Questions To Carry

1. When you struggle to obey Allah, do you imagine you are doing it for Him — or do you live as if it is genuinely for yourself? Which would your behavior reveal?

2. What part of your worship has the smell of *audition* on it — performance for an audience that you secretly think is grading you for its own benefit?

3. If you knew for certain — felt it in your bones — that Allah needed nothing from you, what would change about how you sit in prayer tonight?

4. What is the resistance you are facing this week that Allah, with *jāhada*, has already named and validated? Have you given it that dignity, or have you been treating it as evidence of your failure?

5. If Allah is *ghaniyy* from all the worlds, then the only reason He invites you to nearness is grace. How does that change the way you receive His commands?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**Every drop of effort you pour into struggle for Allah's sake is, by His own design, a deposit into you and only you — because the One who commanded it is so utterly free of need that He left nowhere else for the benefit to go.**

### The Closing Invitation

If anything shifts after this reflection, let it be this:

Stop treating your worship as a debt you are barely managing to pay. Start treating it as a medicine you are barely managing to take. The first picture exhausts you. The second one heals you. The first one is wrong. The second one is what this ayah just told you.

The next time you feel resistance — the resistance Allah Himself named in this word *jāhada* — pause for one breath and remember:

*This effort is for me. The One asking it of me needs nothing from it. He is asking it because He is shaping me, not because He is being fed by me. The struggle is the design. The design is mercy.*

And go take one more rep.

### A Brief Du'a

*Allāhumma yā Ghaniyy, yā man laysa laka ḥājatun fī shayʾ — make our struggles light because we remember they are for us. Strip from our worship the performance, the audition, the secret belief that we are doing You a favor. Let us see, even for a moment, that every drop of effort we pour out for You loops back into the soul You are shaping, and that Your independence from us is the very thing that lets Your mercy be pure. Ameen.*
