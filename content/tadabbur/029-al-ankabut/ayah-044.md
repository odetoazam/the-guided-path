# The Heavens, the Earth, and the Sign for Those Who Believe

## Surah Al-Ankabut, Ayah 44

> خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ بِٱلْحَقِّ ۚ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَةً لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ
>
> *Khalaqa Llāhu s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa bi-l-ḥaqq. Inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-l-mu'minīn.*
>
> **Allah created the heavens and the earth in truth. Indeed, in that is a sign for the believers.**

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are Standing

Before we open this ayah, we have to feel where it sits.

Surah Al-Ankabut — the Surah of the Spider — is one of the most psychologically searching surahs in the Quran. It opens with a question that should make every one of us pause: *Do people imagine that they will be left alone simply because they said "we believe" — without being tested?* From the very first lines, the surah is doing surgery. It is separating those who claim belief from those who have actually been refined by belief. It tells the stories of Nuh, Ibrahim, Lut, Shuʿayb, Musa — every one of them a man whose iman was proven not in comfort, but in fire, flood, exile, mockery, and loss.

And then, in the middle of all this, the surah reaches its most famous image: *the parable of those who take guardians besides Allah is like the spider that took a house — and the frailest of houses is the house of the spider.* A web. Beautiful from the right angle. Deadly to flies. But it cannot hold a human body. It cannot hold a storm. It cannot hold *you* through a trial.

Now — *just before* that famous image, two ayat earlier — Allah gives us our verse. He gives us this. And the question we have to feel is: **why here?**

If the surah is about the fragility of what people lean on, and the parable of the spider is about to land — what is Allah doing pausing, in this exact place, to remind us that **He created the heavens and the earth in truth**, and that **there is a sign in this for the believers**?

### The Hook

Stay with me, because there is something the surface translation hides.

Allah has just spent forty-something ayat telling us that everyone gets tested. Everyone. And then He says: *the heavens and the earth were created in truth — there is a sign in that for the believers.*

But wait. The heavens and the earth are visible to everyone. The disbeliever sees the same sky the believer sees. The atheist watches the same sunrise the muslim watches. The sun does not rise differently for different hearts.

So why does Allah specifically restrict the sign — *li-l-mu'minīn*, for the believers — when the *thing itself* is universally visible?

[PAUSE]

That single restriction, sitting quietly at the end of an eleven-word ayah, is going to open up something about reality, about perception, and about what iman actually *does* to a person — that I want us to discover slowly, together.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We are going to walk through three doors in this ayah. Each one of them is small. Each one of them changes everything.

### Door One: *Bi-l-Ḥaqq* — "In Truth," or Something Far Stranger

Let me first teach you a feature of Arabic that the English translation cannot carry.

In Arabic, when a word begins with the particle *bi-*, it usually means "with," "by," "through," or "in." It is a preposition of accompaniment, instrument, or mode. If I say *katabtu bi-l-qalam* — "I wrote with the pen" — the *bi-* is telling you the *instrument* of writing. If I say *ji'tu bi-Zayd* — "I came with Zayd" — the *bi-* is telling you what I brought along.

So when Allah says *khalaqa Llāhu s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa **bi-l-ḥaqq*** — what is the *bi-* doing?

Watch this. It is not saying Allah created the heavens and the earth, and *also* truth was a separate thing on the side. It is saying truth is the *instrument*, the *mode*, the *accompanying reality* of the creation itself.

The translators give us "in truth." Some give us "with truth." Some try "for a true purpose." All of these are reaching at something the Arabic holds in a single particle: **the heavens and the earth were not just made — they were made saturated with, carried by, woven through with, *al-ḥaqq*.**

Now let's open the word *al-ḥaqq* itself.

The root *ḥ-q-q* in classical Arabic does not just mean "truth" in the sense of "a true statement." It means something firmer, deeper, harder. It means *what is real*. *What is established*. *What is owed because it cannot be otherwise*. From this root comes *ḥaqīqah* — reality itself. From this root comes *ḥuqūq* — rights, the things that are due to a person because they cannot be denied. From this root comes *istaḥaqqa* — to deserve, to be entitled to, to have it coming as a matter of necessity.

So *al-ḥaqq* is not the opposite of "a lie." It is the opposite of *al-bāṭil* — the false, the hollow, the empty, the about-to-collapse.

Notice what just happened.

[PAUSE]

We are in a surah whose central image is the *spider's web* — the *bayt al-ʿankabūt* — and Allah is about to tell us, two ayat later, that it is *awhanu l-buyūt* — the *weakest* of houses. The Arabic word *wahn* means fragility, hollowness, the thing that looks like structure but cannot bear weight. **It is the opposite of *ḥaqq*.**

So before the surah shows you the web, Allah shows you the heavens.

Before He shows you what is *bāṭil* — hollow — He shows you what is *ḥaqq* — solid, established, real to its core.

Here is what is remarkable: the word *bi-l-ḥaqq* is doing something the English completely loses. It is saying the universe is not a neutral stage on which truth and falsehood compete. **The universe itself is built out of truth. Truth is its material, not its decoration.** When you look at a galaxy, you are not looking at something that *contains* truth. You are looking at something that *is*, in its very fabric, *ḥaqq*. And when you build your life around something other than Allah, you are not just choosing a different decoration. You are choosing *bāṭil* — fragility — in a universe whose foundations are *ḥaqq*. You are trying to weave a spider's web inside a cathedral built of mountains.

Sit with that for a moment.

The mismatch is not theological. It is *structural*.

[PAUSE]

### Door Two: *Āyah* — The Word for Sign, the Word for Verse

The second door is one word: *āyah*.

We translate it "sign." That's not wrong. But again, the Arabic carries more.

The root *ʾ-y-y* in classical lexicons refers to a *mark*, a *token*, an *indication that points beyond itself*. An *āyah* is not the thing — it is what the thing reveals when you read it correctly. A footprint is not the foot. But the footprint testifies that a foot has walked here.

And here is what every Arabic speaker hears that the English reader can miss: the *same word* — *āyah* — is the word Allah uses for **a verse of the Quran**.

A verse of the Quran is an *āyah*. The stars in the sky are *āyāt*. The wind that lifts the clouds is an *āyah*. The infant in the womb developing limb by limb is an *āyah*. Your own self — *wa fī anfusikum, afa-lā tubṣirūn* — is an *āyah*.

So when Allah says *inna fī dhālika la-āyatan* — "indeed, in that is an *āyah*" — He is not just saying "there's a sign there." He is saying: **the heavens and the earth are a kind of verse**. They are a recitation. They are something Allah is *saying*, and the believer is somebody who has learned to *read*.

Stay with that. Think of what it means.

The Quran has two volumes.

One is the Quran *al-matlū* — the recited Quran, the words Jibril brought, the book you hold in your hands.

The other is the Quran *al-manẓūr* — the witnessed Quran, the cosmos itself, every star and every cell.

Both of them are *āyāt*. Both of them are speech from Allah. One is bound in a *muṣḥaf*. The other is bound by the horizon. And the believer is somebody who has learned to read both.

[PAUSE]

Now look at how careful the ayah is. Allah says *la-āyatan* — *a* sign. Singular. Not *āyāt*. Not "signs." Not plural. Just one.

The entire heavens. The entire earth. Galaxies and quasars and oceans and seasons and dust mites and DNA. And Allah calls all of it — together — a single *āyah*.

Why?

Because the whole thing is one verse. The whole creation is one sentence. The point of it, the meaning of it, is *one*. It is all pointing to one thing. Every star is a syllable in one word, and that word is the word the Quran was sent to teach: *Allāh*.

The universe is not a library of separate signs. It is one āyah, written across a billion light-years.

And the *āyah* itself — its purpose, its message, its meaning — is decoded only by one kind of reader.

Which brings us to the third door.

### Door Three: The Restricted Audience — *Li-l-Mu'minīn*

Here is the door I want us to spend the most time at, because this is where the entire ayah turns.

Allah says: *inna fī dhālika la-āyatan **li-l-mu'minīn*** — "indeed, in that is a sign **for the believers.**"

Now hold on.

The heavens and the earth are visible to everybody. The atheist scientist looks at the same Hubble images the believing scientist looks at. The skeptical poet sees the same sunset as the muslim poet. The polytheist farmer plants in the same soil the muwaḥḥid farmer plants in.

So why does Allah *restrict* the sign to the believers?

Let me show you a contrast that the Quran itself sets up. In other places — like Surat al-Baqarah 164 — Allah lists signs in the cosmos and ends with *la-āyātin li-qawmin yaʿqilūn* — "signs for a people who **use their intellect**." Elsewhere it is *li-qawmin yatafakkarūn* — "for a people who **reflect**." Elsewhere *li-qawmin yasmaʿūn* — "for a people who **listen**." Elsewhere *li-qawmin yaʿlamūn* — "for a people who **know**."

But here, in Ankabut 44, the restriction is *li-l-mu'minīn* — for the **believers**.

Notice something.

Every other restriction is a *cognitive* restriction. *Reflect* — that's a mental act. *Use your intellect* — that's a mental act. *Listen* — receive information. *Know* — possess understanding. These are things a careful, honest, unbelieving philosopher could do.

But *iman* is not a cognitive act. *Iman* is a heart's response.

The root *ʾ-m-n* in Arabic carries the meaning of *security*, *trust*, *being settled*. A *mu'min* is not just somebody who has agreed with a proposition. A *mu'min* is somebody whose heart has *come to rest* in the reality of Allah. Their heart has *handed itself over*. Their heart has *found home*.

So Allah is saying something that is going to take us a minute to absorb.

[PAUSE]

He is saying: **the cosmos as an āyah — as a verse, as a sign that decodes — is legible only to those whose hearts have already turned.**

The information is universal. The *meaning* is not.

This is not Allah being unfair. This is Allah describing a real feature of how perception works.

Think about it like this. Imagine two people walk into a room. On the wall is a love letter, in a language only one of them can read. The other one sees the same paper. The same ink. The same shapes. He might even admire the calligraphy. But he doesn't see the *love*. He doesn't see what the letter is *for*. He sees marks on paper. The reader sees the heart of the writer.

The cosmos is a love letter from Allah. The kafir sees the calligraphy. The mu'min sees the love.

And here is what's extraordinary: the *script* in which the love letter is written is *iman* itself. You don't acquire the script by squinting harder at the letter. You acquire it by the heart's surrender. Once the heart has surrendered, the same data becomes a different *experience*.

[PAUSE]

This is why the Quran can say in one place *inna fī khalqi s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ … la-āyātin li-uli l-albāb* — signs for *those of inner core* (Aal Imran 190) — and here, *li-l-mu'minīn*. The signs are objectively there. But what activates them is not the eye. It is what is *behind* the eye.

Let me say this carefully, because it matters.

A telescope can be pointed at the same sky by a believer and a disbeliever. The photons arriving at the lens are identical. The neural processing is identical. But the *seeing* is not the same. Because *seeing*, in the Quranic sense, is not the photons. *Seeing* is the meaning the heart receives from what the eye reports.

The unbeliever sees stars.
The believer sees an āyah.

Same stars. Different seer.

And here is the line that I want us to take with us out of this section:

**The universe does not yield its meaning to the curious. It yields its meaning to the surrendered.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Bridging from Words to Reality

Look at what these three doors, together, have shown us.

*Bi-l-ḥaqq* told us: the universe is built from truth, woven through with reality.
*Āyah* told us: the universe is a verse — Allah is *speaking* it, and we are meant to *read* it.
*Li-l-mu'minīn* told us: the reading is reserved for those whose hearts have turned.

Now let's ask what this means not just about language — but about the way reality itself is structured around the believing heart.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Two Quran's, and Why You Only Read One

Let me start where you live.

You have probably had this experience. You read the same book twice. The first time, in some difficult season of your life — let's say you were nineteen, and stressed, and your heart was scattered — the book was just words. You finished it. You put it down. You barely remembered what it said.

Then years later — older, calmer, having lived through something — you picked the same book back up. And it *exploded*. Every paragraph was alive. Every sentence was telling you about your own life. You wondered: *was this book always this good? Or did I just become the kind of reader it needed?*

That's the experience that this ayah is naming, but at cosmic scale.

The universe is a book. And most of us are nineteen-year-olds with scattered hearts, walking past masterpieces and seeing wallpaper.

The Quran tells us — explicitly, repeatedly — that there are **two recitations** of Allah's speech. There is the one between two covers, *al-Quran al-matlū*, the spoken Quran. And there is the one between two horizons, *al-Quran al-manẓūr*, the witnessed Quran — the cosmos.

Both of them are *āyāt*. Both of them are speech.

But here is what we forget: **most muslims read only one of the two.** We open the muṣḥaf. We recite. We try to understand. We try to live by it. May Allah accept it.

But the other Quran — the one written in galaxies, in seasons, in the heartbeat of an embryo, in the geometry of a leaf — most of us walk past it every day. We see "weather." We see "biology." We see "the sky." We do not see *speech*.

Now hear what this ayah is doing. By calling the heavens and the earth a *single āyah*, Allah is inviting us to do something we have probably never properly done.

He is inviting us to read the *other* Quran.

Practically. What does that look like?

It does not mean become a scientist (though it can mean that). It means: when you walk outside tomorrow, *look up* — and tell yourself, *Allah is speaking to me right now. This is not background. This is foreground. This is His āyah. Let me try to hear what He is saying.*

Try it once.

You will be shocked at how loud the silence has been speaking, for how long.

[PAUSE]

The companions had this. Look at how the early generations talked about the sky. They didn't analyze it. They *wept* at it. Because when you have learned to read the second Quran, looking up at the stars is no different from opening Surat al-Raḥmān. The verses are different. The Speaker is the same.

What does this change about tomorrow?

It changes the meaning of *takhalluf* — of being alone, of being still, of having "nothing to do." For the believer who has been taught to read the second Quran, there is no such thing as nothing to do. The sky is reciting. The trees are reciting. Your own breath is reciting. *Idleness*, in the world of this ayah, is impossible — because Allah is always speaking, and listening is always available.

The practical application is this: tomorrow, spend three minutes — just three — looking at something Allah made, with the explicit intention of *reading* it as an āyah. The sky. A tree. A child. Your own hand. Anything. Do not analyze. Do not name. Do not list facts. Just hold in your heart, *this is His speech. What is He saying to me?*

The Quran promises, in this very ayah, that **there is something there for the believer to find**.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: Why Belief Comes First and Sight Comes Second

Now I want us to sit with the strangeness of the order in this ayah.

The world we live in tells us: *seeing is believing.* You will believe when you have evidence. You will commit when you have proof. You will surrender when you have been convinced.

This ayah tells us the opposite.

It says: **the sign is for the believers.** Not, the sign produces believers. Not, look at this evidence and you will become a believer. It says, *for those who already believe — for them, this is a sign.*

You have to feel how scandalous this sounds to a modern ear. We have been trained to think that belief is the *output* of evidence. Allah is telling us, in this ayah, that for the deepest realities, **belief is the *input* that makes evidence legible**.

Now — please don't misunderstand me. The Quran is full of invitations to think, to reflect, to consider. Allah does not ask anyone to believe without reason. *Bring your proof if you are truthful*, He says elsewhere. The Quran is not anti-rational.

But there is a layer of reality that the bare intellect cannot reach. There is a kind of *seeing* that only opens when the heart has bent.

Let me give you the analogy.

Imagine a deaf person looking at an orchestra. He can see everything you see. The bows moving. The conductor gesturing. The faces of the musicians in concentration. He can describe the entire scene with perfect accuracy. He can even read the score on the music stand and tell you what notes are being played. He can write a doctoral dissertation on the orchestra.

But he cannot hear the music.

And the music is the point.

Now imagine you are sitting next to him, and the *Pastoral Symphony* is playing, and at a particular passage your eyes fill with tears — because something in your spirit has been reached. He turns to you, confused. He says, *the bows are moving the same as they were thirty seconds ago. What changed? Why are you crying?*

You cannot explain it to him. Not because he is stupid. Not because you are smarter. But because the medium of the meaning — sound — is one he does not have access to.

This is what *li-l-mu'minīn* is saying.

The atheist astronomer and the believing astronomer see the same data. But the *music* of the data — the *meaning* it is carrying — the *speaker* behind it — that requires a different organ. That requires *qalb* — the heart that has turned. That requires *iman*.

And the heart-bending is not the *result* of the seeing. The heart-bending is what *makes* the seeing possible.

[PAUSE]

This is, by the way, why daʿwah is so misunderstood.

We sometimes imagine that if we just gave the right argument — if we just produced the perfect proof — the unbeliever would have to surrender. And then when they don't, we are confused. We say, *but the evidence is overwhelming! Why don't they see it?*

This ayah answers that. The evidence is, in fact, overwhelming. The heavens and the earth are screaming. But *seeing* — in the deep sense — is a function of where the heart is pointed. And no argument, however perfect, can re-aim the heart. Only the heart can do that. And the heart only does it when Allah turns it.

Which is why the Prophet ﷺ said *yā muqallib al-qulūb, thabbit qalbī ʿalā dīnik* — O Turner of Hearts, keep my heart firm on Your religion. He didn't say, O Producer of Arguments. He said, *Turner of Hearts*. Because that is where iman actually happens.

What does this change about tomorrow?

Three things.

First: it changes how you do daʿwah. You stop trying to *argue people into iman* and you start trying to *love them toward Allah*. You make duʿā for them — that the One who turns hearts would turn theirs. You display, in your own life, what a heart that has turned actually looks like. The information you give them matters. But the information will only become an āyah for them when their heart has turned. And turning hearts is Allah's work, not yours.

Second: it changes how you respond to doubt in yourself. When the dry season comes — and it will — and you look at the same sky you looked at last year, and it doesn't move you the way it did before, do not panic. The problem is not the sky. The problem is not the evidence. The problem is the position of your heart. The remedy is not more information. The remedy is *iʿādah* — return. *Tawbah*. Stillness. The Quran. Dhikr. *Yā muqallib al-qulūb*. The seeing will come back when the heart re-aims.

Third — and this is the deepest one — it changes what you think iman *is*. Iman is not a checklist of doctrines you have agreed to. Iman is the *posture of heart* that lets reality become legible. When that posture is in place, the universe itself becomes a Quran. When that posture is lost, even the Quran in your hand becomes mere paper.

Iman is, in this ayah, **the act of having one's vision unsealed.**

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Now look back at the eleven words.

*Khalaqa Llāhu* — Allah created.
*As-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa* — the heavens and the earth.
*Bi-l-ḥaqq* — woven through with truth.
*Inna fī dhālika* — indeed, in that.
*La-āyatan* — is a sign.
*Li-l-mu'minīn* — for the believers.

Watch the architecture.

The first half is *cosmic*. The biggest possible subject — Allah. The biggest possible object — the heavens and the earth. The biggest possible mode — truth itself.

The second half is *intimate*. It narrows. *That* — referring back to all of creation — collapses into *a sign*. And the sign collapses, finally, into one small audience: *the believers*.

The ayah is shaped like a funnel. It begins with everything. It ends with you.

And the message of the funnel is this: **all of this — the entire cosmos, every star, every law of nature, every truth woven through every atom — all of it was made so that *your believing heart* could read it.** The universe is not aimed at humanity in general. It is aimed at the *mu'min*. Everyone else is in the room. But the letter is addressed to you.

[PAUSE]

### Linguistic and Thematic, the Same Insight from Two Directions

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are saying the same thing.

*Bi-l-ḥaqq* told us the universe is made of truth. *Āyah* told us the universe is a verse. *Li-l-mu'minīn* told us only the believer reads it.

The themes told us there are two Quran's, and the second one is legible only to the heart that has turned.

Both are saying: **reality has a structure, and the structure is aimed at the believing eye.** You are not a tourist in this universe. You are its intended reader. Everything was set up — galaxies and seasons and seas and your own face in the mirror — so that when *you* look, and your heart is in its right posture, *you would hear Allah*.

This is not metaphor. This is what the ayah is saying.

### Questions to Carry

1. When was the last time you read the second Quran — the one written in stars and seasons — with the same attentiveness you read the first?

2. If iman is the script in which the love letter is written, what is the state of your script right now? Is it sharp, or has it faded?

3. What is the *spider's web* in your life — the thing that looks like structure but cannot bear weight — that is being held up next to the heavens-and-earth of what Allah has actually built?

4. When you make daʿwah, do you treat the other person as a mind to be convinced, or as a heart that needs Allah to turn it?

5. Three minutes tomorrow, looking up — at the sky, at a tree, at your child's face — and asking, *what is Allah saying to me right now?* — what would that change about the day that follows?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**The universe is Allah's other Quran, woven through with truth, and addressed — as a love letter is addressed — to the one whose heart has turned enough to read it.**

### The Closing Invitation

What I want to invite — not demand, not require, just invite — is a small shift.

Tomorrow, before you open the muṣḥaf, step outside. Look up. Look at one thing Allah made. And read it.

Not as nature. Not as science. Not as scenery.

As an *āyah*.

Then come back inside and open the muṣḥaf. And notice that the Speaker has not changed.

That is what this ayah is for.

### Du'ā

O Allah — You who wove the heavens and the earth through with truth — turn our hearts toward You so that Your speech becomes legible to us in both of its volumes. Make us among *al-mu'minīn* for whom Your signs are signs and not just sights. And when the spider-webs of this world tempt us with their false architecture, let us remember the firmness of what You have built, and let us build our lives only there. *Āmīn.*
