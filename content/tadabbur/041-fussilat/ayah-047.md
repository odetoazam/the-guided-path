# The Knowledge in the Covering

## Introduction: The Question Behind the Question

Let me give you the words first, and then let me give you the moment they fell into.

> إِلَيْهِ يُرَدُّ عِلْمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ ۚ وَمَا تَخْرُجُ مِن ثَمَرَٰتٍ مِّنْ أَكْمَامِهَا وَمَا تَحْمِلُ مِنْ أُنثَىٰ وَلَا تَضَعُ إِلَّا بِعِلْمِهِۦ
>
> *Ilayhi yuraddu ʿilmu-s-sāʿah. Wa mā takhruju min thamarātin min akmāmihā wa mā taḥmilu min unthā wa lā taḍaʿu illā bi-ʿilmih.*
>
> "To Him is referred the knowledge of the Hour. And no fruits emerge from their coverings, nor does any female conceive or give birth, except with His knowledge."

Now build the world with me.

The people standing in front of the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah had a question they kept returning to, the way a tongue keeps returning to a sore tooth. *When?* When is this Hour you keep warning us about? When is this Day of standing, this resurrection, this reckoning? Tell us the date. Give us the appointment.

And it was not an innocent question. Al-Qurtubi preserves the reason behind these very words: they came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, in effect, *if you are truly a prophet, then tell us — when does the Hour come?* It was a test disguised as curiosity. Because they believed — they were certain — that no one could know that. And so if he could not name the day, they would call him a fraud. And if he tried to name it, they would catch him in it. The question was a trap with two jaws.

We know this question. We have *become* this question. We are a civilization organized around the demand to know *when*. When will the market turn. When will the diagnosis come back. When will the rain fall, the war end, the test results post, the person text back. We have built entire industries — forecasting, actuarial science, predictive analytics, the whole apparatus of the future-as-data — around a single ache: the unbearable feeling of not knowing what is coming. Not-knowing feels, to the human heart, like a kind of helplessness. So we chase the *when* the way a thirsty man chases water.

And here is the moment. Allah is asked for a date. [PAUSE]

He does not give one.

Instead He does something so strange, so unexpected, that if you read quickly you will miss it entirely. He takes the question about the *end of the entire universe* — the single most enormous, most cosmic unknown there is — and in the very same breath, without pausing, He sets beside it a fruit still sealed inside its husk, and a child still hidden inside a womb.

Why would the timing of the apocalypse and the opening of a flower belong in the same sentence?

Stay with that. Because the answer is going to dismantle the way you carry every unknown in your life. There is something He is telling you about what it means to be *known* — and what it will mean, on a Day that is coming, to discover that no one can witness *for* you except the One who knew you in the dark.

Let's go slowly.

## PART ONE: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: "To Him" — Before the Sentence Even Begins

I want to teach you something about Arabic before we touch the ayah, because if I don't, this first move will slide right past you.

In ordinary, calm Arabic speech, the verb tends to come early and the rest of the sentence arranges itself behind it. *Knowledge of the Hour is referred to Him* — in a neutral ordering, you would expect the verb and the subject to lead, and the little phrase *to Him* to take its normal seat further along.

But that is not what Allah does. He takes the phrase *to Him* — **إِلَيْهِ**, *ilayhi* — and He lifts it out of its ordinary seat and places it at the very front of the sentence. Before the verb. Before the word "knowledge." Before anything.

Here is the everyday version of this, so you feel it in your own mouth. Imagine someone asks, "Who gets the final say on this?" And you could answer flatly: "The final say goes to her." Or you could answer: "*To her* — the final say goes." The second one lands differently, doesn't it? By moving *to her* to the front, you have done something with no extra words: you have made it exclusive. You've said, in effect, *to her and to no one else.*

Arabic grammarians have a name for this — *taqdīm*, the fronting of what would normally come later. And one of its primary functions, attested across the classical rhetoricians, is precisely this: **restriction**. Exclusivity. When you front it, you fence it off.

So now look again:

> إِلَيْهِ يُرَدُّ عِلْمُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ
>
> *To Him is referred the knowledge of the Hour.*

They asked the Prophet ﷺ for the date. And the very first word of the answer — before "knowledge," before "Hour," before any of it — is a wall. **To Him.** To Him *alone* the knowledge of the Hour is returned. The structure has already answered the question before the sentence has finished assembling. You came asking a man for a date that belongs, exclusively, by the architecture of the grammar itself, to God.

Notice something else, and this is where it gets quietly beautiful. The verb is **يُرَدُّ**, *yuraddu* — from the root that means *to return, to refer back, to send home.* And it is in the **passive voice**. Not "He knows the Hour." But "the knowledge of the Hour *is referred* — *is returned* — to Him."

Why passive? Why "is returned" instead of "He knows"?

Let's sit with this. *Is returned* implies a movement. It implies that the knowledge of the Hour is a thing that, no matter where you try to send it, no matter whose door you knock on, keeps getting *sent back* to one address. Picture a letter with only one valid recipient in all of existence. You can hand it to the philosopher, the astronomer, the prophet, the priest — and every one of them, if honest, writes *return to sender* and passes it back up. **يُرَدُّ.** It is *referred* upward. It bounces off every created mind and keeps climbing until it reaches the only One it was ever addressed to.

The passive does something the active voice could not. It puts you, the seeker, in the picture. It describes the *experience* of the question — the way every human attempt to capture the Hour gets returned unfulfilled — rather than just stating the fact of God's knowledge. It is the difference between "she owns the house" and "every claim on the house gets referred back to her." The second one has all the failed claimants standing in it.

[PAUSE]

So before we have even reached the strange image of the fruit, the ayah has already taught us its first lesson in its very grammar: there is a knowledge that does not distribute. It has one address, and the front of the sentence is a closed gate with His name on it.

### Element Two: The Three Coverings — and the Word that Seals

Now comes the move that should stop us in our tracks. Having fenced off the knowledge of the Hour, the ayah does not stay in the heavens. Watch where it goes:

> وَمَا تَخْرُجُ مِن ثَمَرَٰتٍ مِّنْ أَكْمَامِهَا
>
> *And no fruits emerge from their coverings...*

Let me teach you the structure first, because the structure *is* the meaning here.

Arabic has a way of making a statement total — airtight, no exceptions — by building it as a **negation followed by an exception**. You negate everything, sweepingly, and then you carve out the single exception. We do this in English too, but less often, and it always sounds emphatic: "Nobody leaves this room — *except* by my say-so." Notice what that sentence does. By first saying *nobody* and then naming the one exception, you have actually claimed total control more forcefully than if you'd said "I control who leaves." The negation makes the net; the exception shows the one hand that holds it.

Now feel how Allah builds it here. Three negations, then one exception that covers all three:

- **وَمَا تَخْرُجُ** — and *not* a single fruit comes out...
- **وَمَا تَحْمِلُ** — and *not* a female carries...
- **وَلَا تَضَعُ** — and *not* does she deliver...
- **إِلَّا بِعِلْمِهِ** — *except* with His knowledge.

Three of the most hidden processes in creation, swept up in negation, and then handed — every last one of them — to a single knowing. *Illā bi-ʿilmih.* Except with His knowledge.

But I told you we'd go slowly, so let's go to the word that I think is the secret heart of this whole ayah. **أَكْمَامِهَا** — *akmāmihā.* "Their coverings."

The root here is *k-m-m*, and the classical lexicons are precise about it. A **كِمّ**, *kimm*, is the sheath, the husk, the calyx — the green casing that wraps a fruit or a flower *before it opens.* Al-Qurtubi explains it exactly: the *akmām* are the *awʿiyah*, the vessels, the containers of the fruit. And then he gives us Ibn ʿAbbās's beautiful specification — the *kimm* is the **كَفَرَى**, the spathe of the date-palm, *qabla an tanshaqq* — *before it splits open.* Once it splits, once the fruit shows its face, it is no longer a *kimm*. The word names a thing *only* in its hidden state. The moment of disclosure is the moment the word stops applying.

Do you feel what that does? Allah did not say "no fruit grows except with His knowledge." He reached for the most specific possible word for the fruit *while it is still sealed inside its casing, still invisible, still nothing but a swelling under a green hood that has not yet cracked.* The knowledge does not begin when the fruit appears. The knowledge is already inside the *kimm*, in the dark, before the husk has split.

And listen to the word in your mouth. **Kimm. Akmām.** That doubled *mīm* — *kimm* — is a sound that closes the lips and seals them. *Mmm.* You cannot say it without your mouth shutting like a husk. The word *enacts* the covering it names. Compare it to **تَخْرُجُ**, *takhruju*, "comes out" — that *kh* is a sound that scrapes open at the back of the throat, a sound of something forcing its way out of an enclosure. The ayah has a sealed sound and an emerging sound sitting right next to each other: *takhruju min akmāmihā* — *forcing-out from the sealed.* The phonetics are doing the work of a closed husk splitting open.

[PAUSE]

Now hold the three images together, and notice the order, because the order is an argument:

1. **The Hour** — the largest unknown there is. The end of the cosmos. The unimaginably vast.
2. **The fruit in its husk** — the botanical secret. Mid-scale. A green pod on a tree no one is watching.
3. **The female who conceives and delivers** — the most intimate unknown of all. A life beginning, growing, emerging, in a place no eye on earth can enter.

He has drawn a line *from the edge of the universe to the inside of a womb,* and run a single thread of knowledge through all three points. And here is the staggering claim the structure makes: **it is the same knowledge.** The *ʿilm* that holds the timing of the Day the stars fall is the identical *ʿilm* that holds the moment a date-fruit pushes out of its spathe and the moment a hidden child turns in the dark. He did not say "and He also knows about fruits, by the way." He bound them in one grammatical breath, one negation-exception frame, *illā bi-ʿilmih* — so that you could not separate the cosmic from the intimate. The God who is too vast to be asked *when the world ends* is, by the same attribute, near enough to be present at the splitting of a single husk.

This is the answer to the trap. They wanted to corner the knowledge of the Hour as an exotic, far-off, *gotcha* fact. And Allah replied: you do not understand the *kind* of thing My knowledge is. It is not a list of impressive facts I happen to possess, with the Hour as the most impressive entry. It is a single, seamless presence that is *already inside the husk of every fruit on every tree you have ever walked past.* You want to test whether I know the far thing? You are standing inside ten thousand near things I am knowing right now.

### Element Three: "We Make You Hear" — The Witnesses Who Vanish

The ayah turns now, sharply, toward the future. And we have to follow it, because the turn is the whole point.

> وَيَوْمَ يُنَادِيهِمْ أَيْنَ شُرَكَآءِى قَالُوٓاْ ءَاذَنَّـٰكَ مَا مِنَّا مِن شَهِيدٍ
>
> *And the Day He calls to them, "Where are My partners?" they will say, "We announce to You — there is not among us a single witness."*

First the calling. **يُنَادِيهِمْ**, *yunādīhim* — from the root *n-d-w*, and the Leeds morphology confirms this is **Form III**. Form III in Arabic very often carries the sense of *directed engagement* — of doing the action *toward* someone, across a distance, often with raised effort. *Nidāʾ* is not a murmur. It is a *call across a gap* — a summoning that crosses space and demands an answer back. So picture it: on that Day, across the enormous distance between the Creator and these tiny, exposed souls, comes a single ringing question. **أَيْنَ شُرَكَآءِي** — *Where are My partners?*

It is a question that scorches by sarcasm. *Where are the ones you assigned to Me? You spent your lives insisting I had partners — call them now. Where are they?* He calls them "My partners" — *shurakāʾī* — using their own claim against them, the way you might hand someone back the exact words they swore by and ask them to make good on it.

And now their answer. And this is the word I want to put in your hands. **ءَاذَنَّـٰكَ** — *ādhannāka.*

The root is *ʾ-dh-n* — and the Leeds data marks this as **Form IV**. Here I have to be careful and exact, because the methodology demands it: Form IV is not automatically "causative." Its function has to be read from the word itself. And this root is extraordinary, because *ʾ-dh-n* is the root of **أُذُن**, *udhun* — the **ear.** It is the root of *adhān*, the call to prayer that you *hear*. The verb in Form IV, *ādhana*, means: *to make hear, to give notice, to announce, to formally inform.* So the function here is declarative-transitive — *to cause-to-know by way of the ear*, to put something into someone's hearing.

Now sit with the irony, because it is devastating. He **called** to them — *nidāʾ*, a thing for the ears. And they answer with a verb built from the very root of *the ear*: *ādhannāka* — "**we make You hear**, we give You formal notice." The exchange is built entirely out of the vocabulary of sound and hearing — He calls, they announce — as if to underline that on that Day there is nothing left but *testimony,* nothing left but what can be *said aloud and heard.* All the hidden things have been opened. The husks have all split. The wombs have all delivered. Now everything is sound, and the sound they produce is a confession.

And what do they announce? **مَا مِنَّا مِن شَهِيدٍ** — *mā minnā min shahīd.*

Look at the structure: it is that totalizing negation again. *Mā... min* — *not... any.* "There is **not** among us **any** witness." A *shahīd* is one who *bears witness, who testifies, who stands up and says: I saw it, it is true.* These are the people who, in the life of the world, *testified* with their whole lives that God had partners. Their idolatry was a kind of standing testimony — *we bear witness there are others beside Him.*

And on that Day, the testimony evaporates. *There is not among us a single witness.* Not one of us will stand up and say it is true. The witnesses for the false gods cannot find, among their entire number, one mouth willing to testify. Al-Muyassar renders their meaning exactly: *aʿlamnāka-l-ʾāna* — "we inform You *now*" — *there is none among us who witnesses today that You had any partner.*

[PAUSE]

Do you see the architecture now? The ayah opened with the *hidden* — the sealed husk, the closed womb, knowledge no creature can reach. And it closes with the *disclosed* — the Day when everything sealed is opened, and the only testimony left standing is the truth, because every false witness has gone silent. The God whose knowledge was *inside* the husk is the God in front of whom, at the end, no false witness can keep his story.

### The Bridge

So here is what the language has shown us, all three pieces as one. A knowledge that has a single address and is *returned* there from every direction. A knowledge already present *inside the sealed husk,* before disclosure, binding the cosmic Hour to the intimate womb in one seamless presence. And a Day when all of it is unsealed, when even the people who built their lives on a lie cannot find one witness among themselves to swear to it.

That is the grammar. Now let's ask what it does to the way you actually live — because this ayah is not finally about botany or eschatology. It is about two things you carry in your chest right now: **the ache of not knowing,** and **the longing to be known.**

## PART TWO: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Peace on the Other Side of "When"

Let me start where you live.

There is a particular flavor of suffering that has nothing to do with anything bad actually happening. It is the suffering of *not knowing what will happen.* The test you took, sitting ungraded in some server. The biopsy out for results. The relationship in a silence you can't read. The decision someone else is making about your future in a room you're not in. Notice — in those moments, *nothing has gone wrong yet.* The thing you fear may never come. And yet you suffer, fully, *now,* purely from the *not-knowing.*

We are, all of us, allergic to the unknown. And our whole age is one long attempt at a cure. We forecast, we model, we track, we predict, we set up alerts and notifications and dashboards, all so that the future will stop being dark. We treat *not-knowing* as a problem to be solved — a temporary deficiency, a gap in the data we will eventually close.

And into that exact instinct, this ayah lands.

They wanted the date of the Hour. They wanted the unknown converted into a known, the dark future pulled into the lit present. And Allah's answer was not "here it is." His answer was *ilayhi yuraddu* — that knowledge is *referred back to Him.* It has an address, and the address is not you.

Now — your nafs hears that as a *refusal.* As a door slammed. *You won't tell me.* But look again at what He set beside it. He did not leave you with a bare "you can't know." He immediately showed you the fruit in the husk and the child in the womb — and what is the point of those images? **They are things that are unknown to you but perfectly known to Him, and they turn out perfectly.**

Let's sit with this, because it's the whole reversal. The fruit does not need *you* to know the hour it will split from its husk. It splits anyway, on time, held in a knowledge that is not yours. The child in the womb does not need the mother to know the moment of its forming or its delivering — *wa lā taḍaʿu illā bi-ʿilmih* — it all happens, flawlessly, inside a knowing that is not hers. The most important things that have ever happened to you — your own conception, your own forming, your own emergence into air — *all of it occurred in a darkness you had no access to and no control over, and it was not chaos. It was the most exquisitely ordered process in creation, held the entire time in His knowledge.*

[PAUSE]

So the ayah is not saying *the future is dark, deal with it.* It is saying something far gentler and far more radical: **the unknown is not empty. It is full — full of His knowing.** The darkness you fear is not an absence of order. It is the inside of a husk. And husks, in this ayah, are not where things get lost. They are where things are *held until they are ready.*

This is the peace on the other side of *when.* Not the peace of finally getting the date. The peace of realizing you were never the one holding the date in the first place — and that the One who holds it held your own forming in the dark and brought you out whole. You have *already survived* living inside an unknown you could not see into. You did it before you were born. You are doing it right now with every fruit ripening unseen and every cell dividing where no scan reaches.

What does this change tomorrow? It changes what you do with the next "I don't know." When the silence comes — the unanswered text, the pending result, the undecided thing — your trained instinct is to claw at it, to demand it become known *to you,* and to suffer until it does. The ayah offers a different motion: to let that unknown be *referred upward.* *Ilayhi yuradd.* To consciously hand the *when* to the only address it was ever going to reach anyway, and to say: *this is inside a husk. It is not lost. It is held. I do not need to see into it for it to be ordered.* That is not resignation. Resignation says *the dark is empty so I give up.* This says *the dark is full of His knowing, so I can rest.*

### Theme Two: To Be Known in the Dark, and the Day the False Witnesses Go Silent

Now turn the ayah over, because it has a second face, and this one is even more intimate.

We've talked about the terror of not-knowing *the future.* But there is an opposite ache, just as deep, and it is the ache of wanting to *be known.* To be fully seen. There is a reason the loneliest feeling in the world is the feeling that *no one really knows me* — not my surface, not my role, but the actual me, the one in the dark behind my face. We spend enormous energy trying to be witnessed. We perform, we post, we explain ourselves, we build reputations — and underneath all of it is a small, childlike hope: *let someone see who I actually am, and let it be okay.*

Now read the ayah as a description of *you.*

You were once a *thamarah fī kimmihā* — a fruit in its husk. You were once what is *carried by a female* and then *delivered* — *mā taḥmilu min unthā wa lā taḍaʿu.* There was a stretch of your existence when *not one human being on earth could see you.* You were sealed. Hidden. Inside. And the entire claim of this ayah is: **you were fully known the whole time.** Before your mother knew your face, before there was a face — *bi-ʿilmih,* with His knowledge. The most invisible you have ever been, you were the most completely known.

Let that reframe the loneliness. The hunger to be *fully seen* by another human being is a hunger that can never quite be satisfied, because no human can get inside your husk. Even the people who love you most are loving a fruit they've only seen *after* it split open — they never saw you sealed in the dark. But there is One who did. There has never been a sealed moment of you He was outside of. *You have already been completely known* — not for your performance, not for your surface, but in the one place no performance reaches, the inside of the husk, the inside of the womb, the inside of the heart.

[PAUSE]

And now here is where the ending of the ayah turns into something I want you to feel in your bones.

The ayah ends with witnesses *vanishing.* On that Day, the people who built their lives on a false allegiance call out for their witnesses, and *mā minnā min shahīd* — there is not, among all of them, a single one willing to testify. Everyone they counted on to back up their story is gone. The whole structure of *who will vouch for me* collapses.

Bring the two halves together. Through the whole life of this world, we run on *witnesses.* We need people to vouch for us, to confirm our worth, to testify *yes, she matters, yes, he is who he says.* We are addicted to being witnessed-*for*. And the ayah shows us the terrible truth about that economy: **on the Day it counts most, the witnesses disappear.** Every external testimony you spent your life collecting — the reputation, the approval, the network of people who'd speak for you — *mā minnā min shahīd.* Not one of them, in that moment, can carry your case.

So what is left? Go back to the beginning of the ayah. What is left is *the knowledge that was inside the husk.* The only "witness" still standing on that Day is the One whose *ʿilm* was present before you had a face — the One who knew you in the dark when no one else could, and who therefore needs no one to vouch for what you were. He was *there.* He doesn't need a witness; **He was the witness, the whole time, in the only place that ever held the real you.**

This is the most extraordinary comfort and the most extraordinary warning, folded together. Comfort: you do not have to die afraid that the real you went unseen. You were seen, completely, from inside the husk. And warning: you cannot, on that Day, hide the husk-self behind the witnesses, because the witnesses go silent, and the only testimony left is His direct knowledge of what you actually were when the casing was still sealed.

What does this change tomorrow? Two things, pulling in one direction. First — it loosens the grip of *being witnessed by people.* The exhausting performance, the hunger for everyone to vouch for you, the suffering when they don't — all of it is built on an economy that the ayah tells you will be empty on the only Day that matters. You can put some of that down. Second — and this is the heavier one — it asks you to live as someone *already fully known.* Not someone hoping to be seen. Someone who *is* seen, in the husk, in the private hour, in the thing you'd never post. The question stops being *how do I get them to see the good in me* and becomes *what does the One who has always been inside my husk actually find there?* That is a question that reorganizes a life — quietly, from the inside, in the dark, where it was always being known anyway.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now stand back and look at the whole ayah at once, because it has a shape, and the shape is the proof.

It opens with **the sealed**: *to Him alone* is referred the knowledge of the Hour. It moves through **the smaller seals**: the fruit in its husk, the child in the womb — knowledge present *inside* the covering, before any disclosure. And then it pivots, with a single *wa-yawma* — "and the Day" — to **the great unsealing**: the Day when everything hidden is called out into the open, when the question rings across the gap, and the false witnesses cannot produce one testimony among them.

Concealment → concealment → concealment → and then total disclosure. The ayah is built like a husk that splits. It *enacts* its own meaning. It holds the hidden, the hidden, the hidden — and then, at *wa-yawma yunādīhim,* it cracks open and everything sealed comes out into the light.

And the same word ties the two ends together: **knowledge.** *ʿIlmu-s-sāʿah* at the opening — the knowledge of the Hour. *Bi-ʿilmih* in the middle — with His knowledge. And on the Day at the end, the collapse of all *false* knowledge, all false witness, until only His knowing remains. One attribute runs the whole length of the ayah like a spine: *He knows. He knew it sealed. He will be the only knowing left when it is all unsealed.*

### Bringing It Together

The two themes are the same insight seen from two sides.

From one side: *I do not know the future, and that frightens me.* The ayah answers — the future is a husk, and husks are full, not empty; the unknown is held in a knowing that brought you out of the dark once already.

From the other side: *I am afraid no one truly knows me.* The ayah answers — you were known in the husk, before any witness existed, and on the Day the witnesses fail, that knowing is the only one that will still be standing.

Both fears dissolve in the same fact: **there is a knowledge inside the covering.** It is what makes the dark future safe, and it is what makes the hidden self seen. The same *ʿilm* that you cannot reach into is the *ʿilm* that has never stopped reaching into you.

### Questions to Carry

1. When I suffer over *not knowing* what will happen, am I treating the unknown as empty — or can I learn to feel it as a husk, full of His knowing, holding something until it is ready?

2. How much of my exhaustion comes from collecting *witnesses* — people to vouch for me — for a Day when *mā minnā min shahīd,* the witnesses go silent?

3. There is a "husk-self" of mine that no human has ever seen. He has never *not* seen it. What does He find there — and would I live differently if I truly believed He was inside it right now?

4. Can I hand the next unanswered *when* in my life *upward* — *ilayhi yuradd* — instead of clawing at it until it becomes known to me?

5. The fruit splits its husk on time, in a knowledge that is not its own, and it turns out right. What in my life am I refusing to let ripen in the dark because I demand to see it first?

### One-Sentence Distillation

The same knowledge that seals the hour of the world's ending was present inside the husk where your own hidden self was formed — so the future you cannot see into is not empty but held, and the self you fear no one truly knows has been fully witnessed all along by the only One whose testimony will still be standing on the Day every false witness goes silent.

### The Closing Invitation

You came, like they came, wanting to know *when.* The ayah does not hand you the date. It hands you something better: the discovery that you have spent your whole existence — beginning in a womb you couldn't see into — living *inside* a knowing that never once lost you in the dark. Let that be the thing that shifts. Not *I must finally know.* But *I am finally able to rest inside being known.* The husk is not where things are lost. It is where they are held until they are ready to come out into the light.

### A Brief Du'a

*O You to whom the knowledge of the Hour returns, and who knew me when I was sealed in the dark with no face and no name — let me rest in the unknowns I cannot see into, knowing they are full of Your knowing and not empty of it. And on the Day You call out and every false witness falls silent, let the only testimony standing about me be Yours — for You alone were inside the husk, and You alone know what I truly was.* Ameen.
