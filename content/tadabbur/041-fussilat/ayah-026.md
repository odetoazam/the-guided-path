# And Make Noise Therein

## The Strategy That Confesses Its Own Defeat

---

### The Words Themselves

[ayah:41:26] وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَا تَسْمَعُوا۟ لِهَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ وَٱلْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَغْلِبُونَ

*Wa qāla alladhīna kafarū lā tasmaʿū li-hādha-l-qurʾāni wa-lghaw fīhi laʿallakum taghlibūn.*

"And those who disbelieve say: 'Do not listen to this Qur'an, and drown it out with noise, that perhaps you may overcome.'"

---

### Rebuilding the World

Close your eyes and put yourself in Makkah, somewhere in the years before the migration.

The Prophet ﷺ has no army. No state. No wealth to speak of. He has a voice, and a recitation. That is the entire arsenal. He stands near the Kaʿbah, or sits among a few people, and he recites.

Now here is what the leadership of Quraysh has discovered, to their alarm: **the recitation works.** Not because of clever marketing. Not because of social pressure — the social pressure is entirely the other direction; to follow him is to risk being beaten, boycotted, cast out of your own clan. And still, people who simply *hear* it begin to change. A man comes to mock and leaves shaken. A poet comes to refute and falls silent. The words get *in*.

So the chiefs gather, and they think. They are not stupid men. They are the most articulate people on earth — Arabic poetry was the Super Bowl of that civilization, and these were its champions. If anyone could answer the Qur'an with a better Qur'an, it would be them. They tried. They could not.

And so, having failed to *answer* it, they arrive at a different plan. The plan is recorded here, in their own words, for all time. Listen to how small it is. Listen to what it admits.

[PAUSE]

### The Hook

There is a tell in this ayah. A single word the disbelievers *chose* — and three words they conspicuously *did not* choose. And once you see the gap between what they said and what a confident person would have said, you will never read the opposition to truth — out there in the world, or quietly inside your own chest — the same way again.

Because the strategy in this verse is not the strategy of people who think they are right.

It is the strategy of people who are afraid you might be.

Let's find out why.

## PART ONE: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

---

### Element One: لَا تَسْمَعُوا — "Do Not Listen"

Before we touch the word, let's understand a quiet feature of Arabic — and of every language.

There is a difference between *hearing* and *heeding*. In English we feel it too. When a parent says to a child, "You're not listening to me," the child may protest, "I can hear you!" And the parent says, "That's not what I mean." To *hear* is for sound to reach the ear. To *listen* is to let it reach you — to give it standing, to let it have a claim on you.

Arabic carries this distinction inside a single verb. The root here is **سمع** (*samiʿa*), to hear. But notice the construction: it is not "lā tasmaʿū-hu" — "do not hear it." It is **لَا تَسْمَعُوا لِـ** — *lā tasmaʿū li-* — "do not listen **to**," with the preposition *lām* attached.

And the classical commentators stop right here. Al-Qurtubi notes that *samiʿtu laka* — "I listened *to* you," with that *lām* — carries the meaning **"I obeyed you."** *Lā tasmaʿū* here can mean not merely "do not let the sound enter your ears," but **"do not heed it, do not yield to it, do not let it command you."**

So already the prohibition is doubled. They are saying two things at once:

Don't let it in.
And if it gets in — don't let it move you.

[PAUSE]

Now sit with what kind of order this is.

You only forbid people from listening to something **when you are afraid of what happens if they do.**

Think about it from the other side. Imagine the chiefs genuinely believed the Qur'an was the babbling of a deluded man, or the verse of a third-rate poet. What would the confident response be? *"Go listen! Listen all you want — then come back and tell me how foolish it is. Bring your friends. Let him embarrass himself."* That is what you do with a position you are certain is weak. You expose it. You let the sunlight kill it.

You do not build a wall around it.

But these men do not say "go listen and judge for yourselves." They say **"do not listen."** And in that prohibition is a confession they could not help making: *we cannot let our own people be exposed to these words, because we have seen what these words do.*

The command to look away is always an admission that there is something there worth seeing.

[PAUSE]

What this single word reveals — the very first move of the strategy — is **quarantine.** Not refutation. Quarantine. They are not trying to win the argument. They are trying to prevent the argument from ever being heard. And you only quarantine what you secretly believe is contagious.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Two: وَٱلْغَوْا — "And Drown It Out With Noise"

Now we come to the heart of the ayah. The word the whole verse turns on.

**وَٱلْغَوْا فِيهِ** — *wa-lghaw fīhi.* "And *laghaw* in it."

The root is **لغو** (*l-gh-w*), and you need to feel this word the way an Arab feels it. *Laghw* is one of the most contemptuous words in the language. It does not mean "speech." It means **vain speech — empty noise — that which carries no meaning and no weight.** It is the chatter that is worth nothing. The Qur'an uses it elsewhere precisely for what the believers in Paradise are freed from: *lā yasmaʿūna fīhā laghwan* — "they hear no *laghw* therein" (19:62). It is also the word for the meaningless oath your tongue trips over without intending — *laghw al-yamīn.* It is the category of the worthless utterance.

So the command is not "argue with it" or "answer it." The command is: **make laghw at it.** Generate worthlessness. Fill the air with meaninglessness so that the meaning cannot land.

Now — say the word out loud. *Alghaw.* **أَلْغَوْا.**

Feel where it sits in your throat. That hard *ghayn* — غ — a guttural, gargling, scraping sound, deep in the back of the mouth. It is not a clean sound. It is not a beautiful sound. It is, phonetically, *noise* — the very texture of clamor and interference.

[PAUSE]

This is the genius of the word, and you cannot translate it. The Qur'an, describing the strategy of noise, **chose a word that sounds like noise.** *Alghaw fīhi.* The mouth that says it is already making the ugly, scraping clamor it is commanding. The form of the word performs its meaning. You hear the static in the syllable.

And here the commentators fill in exactly what this *laghw* looked like on the ground, and it is almost unbearable in its pettiness. Al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi gather the reports:

Ibn ʿAbbas narrates that **Abu Jahl said: "When Muhammad recites, shout in his face, so that he does not even know what he is saying."** Just — *shout.* Drown the man's voice with your own.

Mujahid says the *laghw* was **al-mukāʾ wa-l-ṣafīr** — whistling and hooting. Stick your fingers in your mouth and whistle over the words.

Al-Dahhak says: **just talk — pile on more and more words — so that it all gets jumbled and confused** and no one can follow the thread.

Al-Baghawi reports they would advise one another: *when you see him reciting, come at it with rajaz and poetry and nonsense.* Recite anything. Recite garbage. Just don't let the silence form in which a person might actually *hear.*

[PAUSE]

Notice what every one of these has in common. **None of them is an argument.** Not one. Whistling is not a counter-claim. Shouting is not a rebuttal. Reciting random poetry over someone is not a refutation of what they're saying.

This is not a debate tactic. It is a **jamming** tactic. In the language of signals: they could not transmit a stronger message, so they decided to flood the channel with static. Destroy the *clarity*. Make it impossible to receive — not by answering the signal, but by burying it in noise.

[PAUSE]

And now lift your eyes for one second to the *name of this surah.*

This surah is called **فُصِّلَت** — *Fussilat.* "Clearly detailed. Made distinct. Separated out and articulated, point by point." It is the surah's own self-description of the Qur'an: a revelation whose verses have been *fussilat* — distinguished, made unmistakably clear (41:3).

And what is the enemy's entire weapon against a Book whose name is *Clarity*?

**Laghw.** Noise. The indistinct. The muddled. Confusion.

Do you see the war that is being fought in this single verse? It is *Clarity* against *Noise.* Articulation against static. The distinct word against the meaningless shout. Allah names the Book "Made Distinct" — and records, a few lines later, that the only counter-strategy His enemies could find was to attack distinctness itself: *make it impossible to make out.*

[PAUSE]

What this word reveals: when falsehood cannot produce a better signal, it attacks the listener's ability to receive *any* signal. It does not defeat the meaning. It assaults the silence in which meaning could be heard. And the weapon it reaches for — *laghw* — is, by its own description, *worthless.* They are fighting the most weighty words ever spoken with the lightest, emptiest thing they own.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Three: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَغْلِبُونَ — "That Perhaps You May Overcome"

We arrive at the end of the verse, and at the tell I promised you in the beginning. There are two words here, and each one is a confession.

The first word is **لَعَلَّ** — *laʿalla.* "Perhaps. So that maybe. In the hope that." It is the particle of hope and expectation, and crucially, it is the particle of **uncertainty.** It is not *kay* — "in order that," clean and confident. It is *laʿalla* — "*maybe* this'll work."

Hold that. These are the chiefs of Makkah. The plan they have settled on — the official, agreed-upon strategy of the entire opposition — is introduced by their own hesitation. **"...that *perhaps* you may overcome."** They are not even sure their plan will work. They have so little confidence in the noise that they hedge in the very sentence proposing it. *Maybe.* If we shout loud enough, *maybe* — *maybe* — it'll be enough.

That is not the language of people who hold the truth. People who hold the truth do not say "let's drown them out and *hope* it works." They say "let them speak, and we will answer." The hedge — *laʿalla* — is the sound of people improvising a defense they don't trust against a force they can't deny.

[PAUSE]

And now the final word. The one they chose — and the three they ran from.

**تَغْلِبُونَ** — *taghlibūn.* From the root **غلب** (*gh-l-b*): to **overcome, to overpower, to prevail by force, to dominate.** *Ghalaba* is the verb of the wrestler pinning his opponent, the army that overruns a city. It is victory by *force.* By overpowering. By sheer dominance.

Now here is the absence. Read it as carefully as the presence.

They did **not** say *laʿallakum tubṭilūnah* — "that perhaps you may **prove it false.**"
They did **not** say *laʿallakum tujībūnah* — "that perhaps you may **answer it.**"
They did **not** say *laʿallakum tardudūnah* — "that perhaps you may **refute it, send it back.**"

Every one of those would have been a verb of the *mind.* A verb of engagement. A verb that meets the Qur'an on the field of meaning and truth and defeats it *there.*

They reached for none of them.

They reached for **taghlibūn** — *overpower it.* A verb of pure force, with the truth-content of the message left entirely untouched. They are not trying to show the Qur'an is *wrong.* They have given up on that completely. They are only trying to make it *lose* — to overpower the man, drown the voice, win the room by volume.

[PAUSE]

Let this land all the way.

The instant you find yourself trying to *overpower* an idea instead of *answering* it — the instant your strategy shifts from "here is why you're wrong" to "I will simply be louder than you" — **you have already conceded the argument.** *Taghlibūn* is the word of someone who has lost on the merits and is now trying to win on the noise.

The choice of *ghalaba* over *ibṭāl* — force over refutation — is the white flag flying over the whole verse. They are telling us, in the very structure of their plan, that on the level of *truth* they have already surrendered. All that is left to contest is the level of *volume.*

[PAUSE]

What this word reveals: when the goal shifts from *being right* to *being louder*, the battle of ideas is already over — and the loud side knows it lost. *Taghlibūn* is not the verb of confidence. It is the verb of defeat, still trying to make some noise on the way down.

[PAUSE — major]

### The Bridge

Look at what these three words, in sequence, have built.

**Lā tasmaʿū** — *quarantine* the message; don't let people hear it, because we've seen what hearing does.
**Wa-lghaw fīhi** — *jam* the message; flood the channel with worthless noise so it can't be received.
**Laʿallakum taghlibūn** — *overpower* the man; not refute him, not answer him — just out-shout him, and even then only *maybe.*

Three moves: quarantine, jam, overpower. Block reception. Generate interference. Win by force. It is, with eerie precision, a complete doctrine of information warfare, recorded fourteen centuries ago in the mouths of men who had run out of arguments.

But the construction of words is only the doorway. Now let's ask what this reveals — not just about a clever rhetorical reading, but about reality itself, and about the war that is being fought, right now, much closer to home than Makkah.

## PART TWO: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The Noise We Make So We Never Have to Hear

I want to start nowhere near this ayah. I want to start with your phone.

You know the moment. There is a small gap in the day — the elevator, the red light, the thirty seconds while the coffee brews, the moment your head hits the pillow. A little silence opens up. And what is the very first thing your hand does, before your mind has even decided anything?

It reaches.

For the feed. For the podcast. For the playlist, the group chat, the news, the scroll. We have built, each of us, a private and portable factory of noise, and we carry it everywhere, and the instant a silence threatens to form, we fire it up. We will do almost anything to avoid being alone with the quiet for more than a few seconds.

Now ask the question we usually never ask: *why?* What is so dangerous about the silence that we sprint from it every single time?

[PAUSE]

Because in the silence, something might reach us.

A thought we've been outrunning. A question about whether this life we're living is the one we actually meant to live. The memory of who we were going to be. The small, patient voice — the one Allah placed in the *fiṭrah*, the one that knows — that can only be heard when nothing else is talking.

And here is the thing I need you to see. **The strategy of Quraysh and the strategy of our own thumbs are the same strategy.**

*Wa-lghaw fīhi.* "Make *laghw* in it." Fill the space with the worthless so the weighty cannot land.

The chiefs of Makkah did it *to* the believers — they shouted over the recitation so the words couldn't get in. And we — we do it *to ourselves.* We are, each of us, both Abu Jahl and his target. We stand over the quiet places in our own lives and we whistle and hoot and scroll, generating just enough *laghw* — just enough empty, weightless, meaningless input — to guarantee that the thing which would actually move us never gets a clear moment to be heard.

[PAUSE]

Think about how exactly the word fits. Reels. Endless scroll. The autoplay. The eleventh hour of a show you won't remember. By the Qur'an's own definition, this is *laghw* — input that carries no weight, leaves no mark, builds nothing, and exists mainly to *fill.* And the Qur'an's promise about Paradise — *lā yasmaʿūna fīhā laghwā* — they hear *no laghw* there — suddenly reads like a description of a soul finally at rest. Imagine. To not need the noise anymore. To be able to sit in a silence and not flinch.

[PAUSE]

So here is the uncomfortable mirror this verse holds up. We read about the disbelievers shouting over the Qur'an and we think, "how foolish, how transparent, how could they be afraid of just *listening?*"

And then the next time the Qur'an is recited — really recited, with attention, in a space where it might actually reach us — watch what your own hand does. Watch the pull to check something, fix something, do something, fill the moment with *anything* other than the bare exposure of your heart to those words.

The Quraysh were at least honest about it. They said "*lā tasmaʿū*" — don't listen — out loud, as a plan. We do the same thing, but we tell ourselves we're just busy.

**The practical step is almost embarrassingly small, and almost impossibly hard.** Once a day, find the silence and do not jam it. When the Qur'an is recited, put the phone face-down and out of reach — physically remove the jamming device — and let the words have an undefended moment. Don't even try to extract a lesson. Just stop generating *laghw* over it, for two minutes, and see what reaches you when the channel is finally clear. The disbelievers understood something we've forgotten: this Book, *if you actually let it land,* does something. They built their whole strategy around preventing that landing. The least we can do is stop building the same strategy against ourselves.

### Theme Two: The Things We Shout Down Are the Things We're Afraid Are True

Let's go somewhere honest.

There is a particular kind of anger — you've felt it — that does not feel like the anger of someone who is *right.* It feels different. It's the anger that flares up too fast, too hot, when someone touches a specific subject. The friend who can calmly debate anything — except this *one* thing, and on *that* one thing they explode, change the subject, raise their voice, walk out.

We have all watched it in others. And if we are honest, we have all *been* it. There is a topic — maybe it's the prayers we keep delaying, maybe it's the relationship we know isn't right, maybe it's the way we've been treating someone, maybe it's a question about death we refuse to let form — and when it gets raised, even gently, even by our own conscience, we do not engage it.

We **shout it down.**

[PAUSE]

Now look back at the chiefs of Makkah with this in mind, because they are the master case study of this exact reflex, written large.

We established the tell in the linguistic journey: they didn't say "*prove it false,*" they said "*overpower it.*" They didn't say "*answer it,*" they said "*drown it out.*" And we said: the moment your strategy becomes volume instead of truth, you've already conceded the argument inside yourself.

Here is the deeper turn. **The intensity of the noise is a measurement.** It is a gauge of exactly how threatened the person is — which means it is a gauge of how much, in some buried place, they suspect it might be *true.*

You do not whistle and hoot to drown out something you find genuinely ridiculous. Ridiculous things you ignore, or you laugh at, and you move on, untroubled. The energy it takes to organize a whole campaign of shouting — to coordinate with your peers, *"when you see him reciting, come at it with poetry and noise"* — that energy only gets mobilized against something you are *afraid* of. Something you cannot afford to let land. Something that, if you sat quietly with it for even a moment, might *win.*

The volume is the fear made audible. The louder the *laghw*, the closer the truth had gotten.

[PAUSE]

And the Qur'an knows this about them. Two surahs over in this same revelation, it records *their* own private admission, the thing they said when the shouting died down: *qad naʿlamu innahu la-yaḥzunuka-lladhī yaqūlūn* — and elsewhere the chilling line where one of their own leaders, after listening in secret, said the Qur'an had a sweetness, a fruitfulness, that it was not the speech of a man. They *heard* it. That's the whole point. They had heard it, and it had reached them, and the noise was the desperate scramble to un-hear what could not be un-heard.

[PAUSE]

So let's bring this all the way home, because Quraysh is not really the subject. *You* are.

What is the subject *you* shout down? Where in your life have you stopped answering your own conscience and started simply out-shouting it? Where have you swapped *"here is why it's okay"* for *just keeping busy enough, loud enough, distracted enough that the question never gets a clear hearing?*

Because by the logic of this verse, **that** — the topic you most reliably drown out — is very likely the exact place where the truth has gotten closest to you. The noise is a map. It marks the spot. The thing you are working hardest not to hear is, with painful precision, the thing you most need to.

[PAUSE]

**The practical step:** identify your one *taghlibūn* — the place you're trying to win by volume instead of by truth. Then do the one thing the chiefs of Makkah could not bring themselves to do. *Stop shouting, and listen.* Sit with the uncomfortable question for ten minutes without reaching for the noise. Ask: what if the reason this won't leave me alone is that it's *right?* The disbelievers were destroyed not because the truth was too strong for them — it wasn't, they admitted its power — but because they chose the noise over the listening, every time, until the time ran out. The verse is preserved as a warning and as an invitation: *don't make their choice.* When the truth gets close, the believer's move is the opposite of theirs. *Lower the volume. Open the ears. Let it land.*

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Now stand back and see the whole structure of the verse, which we can finally read as one piece.

It opens: **وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا** — "*and those who disbelieve say.*" Notice — Allah does not summarize their position. He does not paraphrase. He *quotes* them. He hands them the microphone and lets their own strategy indict them, word for word, forever. The most damning thing in the verse is simply *what they said.*

And then the strategy itself, in perfect descending order:

- **لَا تَسْمَعُوا** — *quarantine.* Block reception. (The admission: it works if they hear it.)
- **وَٱلْغَوْا فِيهِ** — *jam.* Flood the channel with worthless noise. (The admission: we have no better signal.)
- **لَعَلَّكُمْ تَغْلِبُونَ** — *overpower — maybe.* Win by force, not truth, and even that uncertainly. (The admission: on the merits, we've already lost.)

Three commands, and every single one is a surrender disguised as a strategy. Quarantine confesses the message is contagious. Jamming confesses you have no counter-signal. *Ghalaba* over *ibṭāl* — overpowering instead of refuting — confesses you've abandoned the field of truth entirely. And *laʿalla* — "perhaps" — confesses you're not even sure the surrender will hold.

The verse is built so that the harder they fight, the more they admit. It is a self-defeating sentence. The strategy, read closely, *is* the confession.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic journey and the thematic depths are the same insight, approached from two directions.

From the words, we learned: **when you reach for noise instead of an answer, you've already conceded the truth.**

From the themes, we learned the same law operating in two arenas — the noise we generate *outward* to win arguments we've already lost, and the noise we generate *inward* to drown out the conscience we're afraid is right. Quraysh did it to the Prophet's recitation. We do it to our own *fiṭrah.* Same *laghw.* Same flight from the silence in which truth could land.

And underneath all of it, the surah's own name stands as the verdict: **Fussilat** — *the Clear, the Distinct.* You cannot defeat clarity with an argument, because clarity *is* the argument. So falsehood's last and only move is to attack clarity itself — to make it impossible to hear. The whole drama of this ayah is *Clarity versus Noise.* And the next two verses (27–28) tell you who wins.

### Questions to Carry

- Where in my day do I reach for noise the *instant* a silence opens — and what am I making sure I never have to hear?
- Is there a subject I've stopped *answering* in my own heart and started simply *out-shouting?* What does the intensity of my avoidance tell me about how true it might be?
- When the Qur'an is recited near me, what does my body do? Does it lean in — or does it reach to fill the moment with something else?
- If the volume of my resistance is a measure of how close the truth has gotten — where is it closest right now?
- Am I, on my own questions, behaving like the believer who listens — or like the chief who shouts?

### One-Sentence Distillation

When falsehood can no longer answer the truth, it tries to drown it out — and the very noise it makes is its confession that the truth was winning.

### The Closing Invitation

This verse is not, in the end, about the disbelievers of Makkah. It is a mirror, and it is asking one quiet thing of you: *stop jamming the signal.* Find the silence you've been fleeing and don't fill it. Find the question you've been out-shouting and let it speak. The people in this ayah lost everything not because the truth overpowered them, but because they spent their lives making sure they never had to hear it clearly. The whole invitation is to make the opposite choice — to be, in a noisy world, the rare soul who lowers the volume and *listens.*

### A Brief Duʿā

O Allah, You named Your Book *the Clear, the Distinct* — make us of those who *listen* to it and do not drown it out. Quiet in us the noise we generate to flee from You, and give us the courage to sit in the silence where Your words can reach us. Do not let us be among those who shouted over the truth until their time ran out; let us be among those who heard, and yielded, and were changed. *Āmīn.*
