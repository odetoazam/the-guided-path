Not a state they happen to be in — a state that was *decreed* for them, written in advance, the predetermined shape of their being there." — This misreads a grammatical technical term as a theological one. In classical Arabic grammar, *ḥāl muqaddara* (حال مقدرة) means a circumstantial clause whose state is *anticipated/projected* — i.e., it will obtain at a future point (when they arrive in Hell), not simultaneously with the main clause. It does NOT mean "pre-decreed by God." Al-Jalalayn is making a grammatical observation about timing, not a theological statement about divine decree. The theological conclusion (that their remaining was ordained) is defensible from al-Muyassar and al-Qurtubi independently, but the reflection attributes that conclusion to this grammatical term, which overstates what al-Jalalayn is saying.
