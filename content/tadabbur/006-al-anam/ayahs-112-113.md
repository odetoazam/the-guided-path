# Ornamented Speech of Delusion

## Surah Al-Anam (6:112-113)

[ayah:6:112] وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَا لِكُلِّ نَبِىٍّ عَدُوًّا شَيَـٰطِينَ ٱلْإِنسِ وَٱلْجِنِّ يُوحِى بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ زُخْرُفَ ٱلْقَوْلِ غُرُورًا ۚ وَلَوْ شَآءَ رَبُّكَ مَا فَعَلُوهُ ۖ فَذَرْهُمْ وَمَا يَفْتَرُونَ

[ayah:6:113] وَلِتَصْغَىٰٓ إِلَيْهِ أَفْـِٔدَةُ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ وَلِيَرْضَوْهُ وَلِيَقْتَرِفُوا۟ مَا هُم مُّقْتَرِفُونَ

*Wa kadhalika ja'alna li-kulli nabiyyin 'aduwwan shayatina al-insi wa al-jinni yuhi ba'duhum ila ba'din zukhrufa al-qawli ghururan. Wa law sha'a rabbuka ma fa'aluhu. Fa-dharhum wa ma yaftarun.*

*Wa li-tasgha ilayhi af'idatu alladhina la yu'minuna bi-al-akhirati wa li-yardawhu wa li-yaqtarifu ma hum muqtarifun.*

**And thus We have made for every prophet an enemy - devils of humankind and jinn, inspiring one another with ornamented speech of delusion. And if your Lord had willed, they would not have done it, so leave them and what they invent. And so that the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter may incline toward it and be pleased with it and so that they may commit what they are committing.**

## Introduction

Falsehood rarely survives by ugliness alone.

If evil always appeared in its most naked form, fewer souls would follow it. What gives it social power is presentation. It is polished, decorated, emotionally resonant, plausibly phrased, dressed in intelligence, wrapped in beauty, sharpened for appetite. It arrives not only as argument, but as atmosphere.

[PAUSE]

These ayahs pull back the curtain on that atmosphere. Every prophet faces enemies, and those enemies are not only external opponents in the obvious sense. They include *shayatin al-insi wal-jinn* - devils from humanity and jinn - passing speech to one another. And what kind of speech? *Zukhrufa al-qawl*: ornamented, gilded, embellished speech. The point is not simply that it is false. The point is that it is attractive. Then the next ayah traces the full arc of seduction: the heart inclines, then it becomes pleased, then it commits.

[PAUSE]

The hook of this passage is this: **the path to open sin often begins much earlier, when the heart starts leaning toward beautified falsehood because the Hereafter has become too faint to govern what it loves.**

[PAUSE]

## Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

### 1. Enmity to Prophets Is Not an Anomaly

The passage begins:

*Wa kadhalika ja'alna li-kulli nabiyyin 'aduwwan...*

Thus We made for every prophet an enemy.

[PAUSE]

That little word *kadhalika* - "thus," "just like this" - is doing quiet work. The classical commentators read the verse first as consolation. Al-Tabari says the ayah is Allah comforting His Prophet ﷺ over what he endured from the disbelief of his people, and urging him to patience: *just as We tested you, Muhammad, with enemies among the idolaters of your own people, so too did We appoint enemies for every prophet before you.* The line reaches back through Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and every messenger between, and it lands on one shoulder in Makkah as if to say: this weight is not a sign that something has gone wrong. It is the shared inheritance of everyone who has ever carried this message.

[PAUSE]

That framing is a mercy to anyone carrying truth. Opposition is not proof that the prophetic path is failing. It is part of the terrain prophets themselves walked.

Then the enemy is specified:

*Shayatina al-insi wa al-jinn.*

Devils from humankind and jinn.

This is arresting because it tells us that "shaytanic" is not limited to the unseen realm. Human beings can participate in devilish work when they embody its rebellion, seduction, and distortion.

### 2. Falsehood Is Passed Along in Decorated Language

Then:

*Yuhi ba'duhum ila ba'din zukhrufa al-qawli ghururan.*

They inspire one another with ornamented speech as delusion.

[PAUSE]

The verb *yuhi* is striking. It is the same root as revelation, but here it is used for covert passing, suggestive casting, secretive conveying from one to another. This is not wahy in the noble prophetic sense, but it mimics the pattern of transmission. Falsehood has its own whisper-networks.

Then the phrase:

*Zukhrufa al-qawl.*

Gilded speech. Embellished speech. Speech plated with decorative surface.

*Zukhruf* is adornment, ornament, gold-like beautification. The point is not that the content is strong. The point is that the presentation glitters. Al-Muyassar captures the mechanism plainly: it is *speech they have beautified with falsehood, so that the one who hears it is deluded by it and strays from the path of Allah.* The ornament is not decoration for its own sake - it is bait, and the intended catch is the listener's direction, the road he takes home.

Then:

*Ghururan.*

As deception, delusion, beguilement.

The ornament is not innocent style. It is style functioning as spiritual misdirection. And al-Tabari names the concrete aim of this traffic in gilded words: the devils among men and jinn feed one another polished arguments *in order to turn people away, through disputation, from following the prophet and believing in him.* The gilding is weaponized for jidal - for the kind of clever contention that makes surrender look naive and rebellion look thoughtful.

### 3. Allah Still Governs the Field

Then the ayah restores the vertical frame:

*Wa law sha'a rabbuka ma fa'aluhu.*

If your Lord had willed, they would not have done it.

[PAUSE]

Again the surah refuses panic. The existence of hostile rhetoric does not place events outside Allah's sovereignty. Nothing slips beyond Him. Al-Muyassar sharpens this into something more than reassurance: *had your Lord willed, He would have come between them and that enmity - but it is a trial from Allah.* The hostility itself is *ibtila'*, a test deliberately left in place. The decorated lie is not merely tolerated by heaven; it is permitted as an instrument by which hearts are sifted and shown for what they are.

Then:

*Fa-dharhum wa ma yaftarun.*

So leave them and what they invent.

This is not passive approval. It is strategic release. Do not be swallowed by their fabrications. Do not treat every decorated lie as though it deserves your soul's full occupancy.

### 4. The Ayah Maps the Psychology of Seduction

Then comes the next verse:

*Wa li-tasgha ilayhi af'idatu alladhina la yu'minuna bi-al-akhirah.*

So that the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter may incline toward it.

[PAUSE]

The verb *tasgha* means to incline, lean, bend toward. Sin rarely begins with action. It begins with an inward lean. Al-Tabari renders it exactly this way - *li-tamila*, that their hearts might tilt - and he notes a telling division in whom the gilded speech reaches. Its whisperers *aim* to seduce the believers among the prophets' followers and shake them loose from their religion; but where it actually settles and takes root is in *the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter*. The same ornamented sentence brushes against everyone; it lodges only where there is no anchor to resist it.

And notice that the ayah says *af'idah* - the inner, feeling core of a person - not merely *'uqul*, minds. Some classical grammarians connect *af'idah* to a root sense of burning or kindling, reading the word as the seat where desire and perception blaze; whatever weight one gives that etymology, the choice of *af'idah* signals that seduction here is not only intellectual. It reaches the place where a person loves.

Then:

*Wa li-yardawhu.*

So that they become pleased with it.

Leaning becomes liking. Exposure becomes comfort. What first felt questionable becomes emotionally acceptable. Al-Muyassar renders this as *their souls come to love it* - the movement from proximity to affection.

Then:

*Wa li-yaqtarifu ma hum muqtarifun.*

So that they may commit what they are committing.

*Iqtiraf* is acquisition through commission, drawing something onto oneself through deed. The movement is complete: attraction, approval, action. And al-Muyassar closes the verse with a note worth carrying: *in this there is a tremendous threat against them.* The seemingly flat final clause - "so that they commit what they commit" - is not neutral description. It is a warning left ringing: this whole slide, from the first lean to the last deed, is being tallied.

## Part 2: The Thematic Depths

### Theme 1: The Hereafter Is a Defense Against Beautiful Lies

The ayah ties susceptibility to beautified falsehood directly to not believing in the Hereafter.

That is profoundly important. It means attachment to akhirah is not only about hope for Jannah or fear of judgment later. It is an interpretive power now. The one who lives before the Hereafter does not evaluate speech only by how it sounds, flatters, thrills, or liberates in the moment. He asks where it leads, what it costs, what it normalizes, and what it will look like when brought before Allah.

[PAUSE]

This is precisely the fault-line the tafsir traces. The same ornamented speech is aimed at believers and unbelievers alike, but it *incline*s and *settles* in the hearts that have no living Hereafter to hold them. When akhirah fades, ornament becomes stronger than consequence. Style beats truth. Emotional relief beats surrender. Present appetite beats final return. The heart becomes easy prey for *zukhrufa al-qawl* precisely because it has lost the gravitational pull of what comes after death.

### Theme 2: Sin Often Begins With Enjoying the Tone of Falsehood

This passage is useful because it identifies a stage people often ignore: *wa li-yardawhu*.

We usually notice the final outward act. But the Qur'an here points to prior emotional consent. There is a moment when the heart stops resisting the false thing and begins enjoying it. That interior pleasure is already spiritually consequential - and the classical reading that the closing words carry a "tremendous threat" tells us this stage is not overlooked by heaven even when we overlook it in ourselves.

[PAUSE]

So one way to examine ourselves is not only to ask, what have I done? It is also to ask, what kinds of speech have I begun to enjoy? What rhetoric makes me lean? What presentation of disobedience now feels sophisticated, freeing, witty, or deep to me simply because it has been decorated well?

If we only fight the deed and ignore the seduction, we will keep losing upstream.

## Closing Synthesis

These ayahs teach us that prophetic struggle includes contesting language, aesthetics, persuasion, and atmosphere.

There are human and jinn devils. They collaborate. They pass ornamented speech, weaponized for disputation. Hearts without a living Hereafter lean toward it, take pleasure in it, and eventually embody it.

[PAUSE]

But the passage also gives us steadiness. It opens as consolation to the Prophet ﷺ - this is old terrain, not new failure. Allah governs the field, and even the enmity is a permitted trial rather than a breach of His control. Opposition is not new. And not every shining sentence deserves our trust simply because it is well-made.

Perhaps one way to sit with this passage is to ask: what kind of speech has been quietly training my heart to lean before I ever notice what it is training me to do?

[PAUSE]

The architecture of the passage lands here: **falsehood becomes powerful not only when it is believed, but when it is beautified enough to be loved, and the heart most protected from that love is the heart still anchored in the Hereafter.**
