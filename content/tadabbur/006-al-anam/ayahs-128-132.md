# They Benefited From One Another

## Surah Al-Anam (6:128-132)

[ayah:6:128] وَيَوْمَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ جَمِيعًا يَـٰمَعْشَرَ ٱلْجِنِّ قَدِ ٱسْتَكْثَرْتُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ ۖ وَقَالَ أَوْلِيَآؤُهُم مِّنَ ٱلْإِنسِ رَبَّنَا ٱسْتَمْتَعَ بَعْضُنَا بِبَعْضٍ وَبَلَغْنَآ أَجَلَنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَجَّلْتَ لَنَا ۚ قَالَ ٱلنَّارُ مَثْوَىٰكُمْ خَـٰلِدِينَ فِيهَآ إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ۗ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ حَكِيمٌ عَلِيمٌ

## Introduction

Some relationships feel mutually beneficial right up until the Day they are exposed.

One side gains influence, appetite, permission, thrill, or power. The other side gains followers, instruments, bodies, voices, or willing agents. Both call it advantage. Both feel they got something. But the Qur'an shows that some forms of mutual enjoyment are really systems of shared ruin whose full meaning only appears when Allah gathers everyone at once.

[PAUSE]

These ayahs move through that unveiling. The jinn are addressed for having led great numbers of human beings astray. Human allies admit that they benefited from one another. Then the surah immediately gives the governing principle: Allah allies some wrongdoers with others because of what they used to earn. After that comes the great justice question. Were they warned? Did messengers come? Did verses get recited? Yes. They testify against themselves. The dunya had deceived them. And the passage closes by naming the moral architecture beneath all of it: Allah does not destroy communities while they are unwarned, and every being receives degrees according to what it actually did.

[PAUSE]

The hook of this passage is this: **evil alliances survive by making corruption feel useful, but on the Day of gathering every false benefit will be exposed as earned ruin under a judgment that was preceded by warning, witnessed by the self, and measured with total precision.**

[PAUSE]

## Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

### 1. The Day of Gathering Reveals What Mutual Enjoyment Was Hiding

The passage opens:

*Wa yawma yahshuruhum jami'an...*

And on the Day when He gathers them all...

[PAUSE]

*Hashr* is gathering for reckoning, drawing scattered beings into one unavoidable assembly. What was hidden across different lives, eras, whispers, and actions is now brought into a single scene.

Then the address:

*Ya ma'shara al-jinni qad istakthartum mina al-insi.*

O company of jinn, you have misled many of humankind.

The verb *istakthartum* is form X of the root *k-th-r* (to be much or many): literally, "you sought to take much" or "you gathered great numbers" from humankind. But the classical commentators are unanimous that this "taking much" is not neutral accumulation. Ibn Kathir glosses it directly as *"Many did you mislead of men,"* and al-Muyassar reads it as *"you led astray many of mankind"* (*aḍlaltum kathīran min al-ins*). Al-Tabari fills in the mechanism: these were the *shayāṭīn* who whispered to their human followers *zukhruf al-qawl ghurūran* — ornamented, gilded speech offered as delusion — so that the humans would carry it forward and dispute the believers with it. The multiplication, then, is the multiplication of the deceived: not a few isolated individuals tempted, but a harvested human field, cultivated through beautiful lies.

Then the human allies answer:

*Rabbana istamta'a ba'duna bi-ba'din.*

Our Lord, some of us benefited from others.

[PAUSE]

That line is chilling because it is so honest. They do not deny the relationship. They describe it in terms of utility and enjoyment. Ibn Kathir spells out the exchange: the jinn were served by being obeyed, sought out, and treated as objects of refuge; the humans were served by having their appetites indulged and their desires met. Each party drew something from the other. Corruption often persists precisely because both sides feel compensated.

Then:

*Wa balaghna ajalana alladhi ajjalta lana.*

We have reached the term You appointed for us.

The enjoyment had a clock on it. It always does. The verb *ajjalta* (form II of *a-j-l*) is emphatic — a term You *fixed and set*, not one that merely happened to arrive.

Then the sentence turns from Fire's permanence:

*Qala al-naru mathwakum khalidina fiha illa ma sha'a Allah.*

He will say, "The Fire is your dwelling, abiding therein except what Allah wills."

The exception, *illā mā shāʾa Allāh*, is one the classical scholars discussed with care. Al-Muyassar reads it as pointing to the sinful monotheists — those guilty of grave sin but not disbelief — whose stay is not everlasting; others among the mufassirūn refer the exception back to Allah's untrammeled will over His own decree, a reminder that even here nothing binds Him. The reflection need not settle the debate; it is enough to notice that even in the pronouncement of Fire, the sentence bends around the sovereign *mashī'a* of Allah.

### 2. Wrongdoers Are Given to One Another

Then comes the governing principle:

*Wa kadhalika nuwalli ba'da al-zalimina ba'dan bima kanu yaksibun.*

Thus We make some of the wrongdoers allies of others because of what they used to earn.

[PAUSE]

The root *w-l-y* here is sobering, and the classical scholars read it along two lines worth holding together. Qatadah, cited by both Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari, reads it as alliance by resemblance: *"Allah allies people according to their deeds — the believer is the ally of the believer wherever he is, and the disbeliever the ally of the disbeliever wherever he is."* On this reading the wrongdoers follow one another into the Fire as they followed one another in life. Al-Muyassar draws out a second sense: just as Allah set the jinn-devils *over* the disbelievers of mankind, He sets wrongdoers *over one another* in this world, one tyrant handed the reins over another — *taslīṭ*, domination as consequence. Alliance and subjugation are two faces of the same judgment: companions, patrons, and systems become mirrors of what a people have earned.

The ayah does not present corrupt alliances as random accidents. They correspond to acquisition: *bima kanu yaksibun*. Ibn Kathir binds this verse to its sister in Surah al-Zukhruf — *"And whoever is blinded to the remembrance of the Most Merciful, We appoint for him a devil, and he is to him a companion"* (43:36). What we earn inwardly and outwardly shapes what we are handed relationally.

### 3. The Self Ultimately Testifies

Then the address widens:

*Ya ma'shara al-jinni wa al-insi alam ya'tikum rusulun minkum...*

O company of jinn and humankind, did messengers not come to you from among you...?

They recited the verses. They warned of this Day. So the question is not whether there was access to truth. The question is what was done with it.

Then:

*Qalu shahidna 'ala anfusina.*

They say: we testify against ourselves.

[PAUSE]

That is one of the heaviest moments in the Qur'an. No external prosecutor is needed to invent a case. The self knows.

Then the explanation:

*Wa gharrathumu al-hayatu al-dunya.*

The worldly life deluded them.

The verb *gharra* (root *gh-r-r*) is the same deception hidden inside *ghurūr* — the gilded speech the devils whispered in the first ayah. The circle closes: the deception offered by the jinn found its accomplice in the deception of the dunya itself. Its nearness, pleasures, urgencies, rewards, and visible textures distorted proportion until the warning of the Day felt unreal.

### 4. Allah Does Not Destroy the Unwarned

Then comes a principle of divine justice:

*Dhalika an lam yakun rabbuka muhlika al-qura bi-zulmin wa ahluha ghafilun.*

That is because your Lord would not destroy the towns unjustly while their people were unaware.

[PAUSE]

The ayah is remarkable. Punishment does not descend out of divine arbitrariness. Warning precedes reckoning. Clarification precedes consequence.

Allah is not unjust, and ghaflah does not count as informed defiance until the truth has actually come.

### 5. Degrees Correspond to Deeds

Then the passage closes:

*Wa li-kullin darajatun mimma 'amilu.*

For each are degrees according to what they did.

Not vague outcomes. Not flattened judgment. Degrees.

Then:

*Wa ma rabbuka bi-ghafilin 'amma ya'malun.*

Your Lord is not unaware of what they do.

Nothing slips past Him: not the public scheme, not the private pact, not the inner consent, not the outward action.

## Part 2: The Thematic Depths

### Theme 1: Some of the Most Dangerous Relationships Are Built on Reciprocal Use

The confession *istamta'a ba'duna bi-ba'din* is spiritually diagnostic.

Not every alliance is rooted in love of truth or mutual elevation. Some are rooted in exchange: you feed my appetite, I reinforce your rebellion; you give me status, I give you permission; you normalize my darkness, I amplify yours. Each side names the arrangement as beneficial while it is active. And the currency of the exchange, the classical scholars remind us, is often *zukhruf al-qawl* — the gilded word: flattery, rationalization, beautiful cover for what one already wanted to do.

[PAUSE]

These ayahs force us to examine whether some relationships, systems, and loyalties in our lives are held together mainly by reciprocal corruption. The Qur'an is telling us that utility is not innocence. Two parties can both feel served and still both be moving toward Fire. And the warning of 43:36 hangs over it: turn away from the remembrance of Allah long enough, and the companion you are handed is precisely the one your turning-away earned.

### Theme 2: Divine Justice Includes Warning, Witness, and Precision

The closing movement is deeply important because it protects us from imagining Allah's judgment as impulsive.

Messengers came. Verses were recited. Warning was given. The self testifies. Communities are not destroyed in blind neglect while unaware. Then, finally, degrees correspond to deeds.

[PAUSE]

This means the final judgment is not only severe. It is morally intelligible. It is preceded by communication, clarified by testimony, and calibrated with perfect awareness.

For the believer, that should produce two things at once: fear, because nothing is lost; and trust, because nothing is mishandled.

## Closing Synthesis

These ayahs expose a single arc: a false benefit named, its clock revealed, its ruin pronounced, and then the justice that stands beneath the whole scene laid bare. The jinn led many astray through gilded speech; their human allies enjoyed the arrangement; both reached the term Allah had fixed; and the Fire was named as the dwelling. But the passage does not let us walk away imagining an arbitrary God. It doubles back to insist that wrongdoers are handed to one another by what they earned, that messengers came, that the self bore witness, that no town is destroyed in heedlessness, and that every being receives *darajāt* measured to its deeds.

[PAUSE]

What we are meant to carry away is a question about our own alliances. Which of my relationships and loyalties are held together by truth, and which by reciprocal use? Where have I mistaken utility for innocence, or gilded speech for guidance? And underneath the fear this passage rightly stirs, there is rest: the same Lord who will not destroy the unwarned is *ḥakīmun ʿalīm* — Wise in every decree, Knowing every atom — so nothing I do in secret is lost, and nothing done to me is mishandled.

[PAUSE]

## A Closing Du'a

O Allah, our Lord who gathers all and forgets nothing —
guard our companionships from becoming shared ruin,
and do not hand us over to allies our own turning-away has earned.

Let no gilded word deceive us, and let not the nearness of this world distort what is real.

You are Ḥakīm, so place us among the wise;
You are ʿAlīm, so let what You know of us be pleasing to You.

And on the Day of gathering, raise us in the degrees of those who heard the warning and answered it.

Āmīn.
