# Taken in Full: When Heaven Answers Betrayal by Lifting You Up

## The Words Before Us

> إِذْ قَالَ ٱللَّهُ يَٰعِيسَىٰٓ إِنِّى مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَىَّ وَمُطَهِّرُكَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ وَجَاعِلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوكَ فَوْقَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓاْ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ ۖ ثُمَّ إِلَىَّ مَرْجِعُكُمْ فَأَحْكُمُ بَيْنَكُمْ فِيمَا كُنتُمْ فِيهِ تَخْتَلِفُونَ

*Idh qāla-llāhu yā ʿĪsā innī mutawaffīka wa rāfiʿuka ilayya wa muṭahhiruka mina-lladhīna kafarū, wa jāʿilu-lladhīna-ttabaʿūka fawqa-lladhīna kafarū ilā yawmi-l-qiyāmah. Thumma ilayya marjiʿukum fa-aḥkumu baynakum fīmā kuntum fīhi takhtalifūn.*

> "When Allah said: 'O Isa, indeed I will take you in full, and raise you to Myself, and purify you from those who disbelieved, and place those who followed you above those who disbelieved until the Day of Resurrection. Then to Me is your return, and I will judge between you concerning that over which you used to differ.'"

---

## Introduction: Rebuilding the World

Let me put you somewhere before we read a single word again.

Picture a man who has done nothing but heal and teach and call people gently back to their Lord. He gave sight to the blind. He fed the hungry. He never raised an army, never seized a throne, never returned an insult with an insult. And the reward for all of this — the actual reward, in the actual world — is a plot. A coordinated, whispered, deadly plot. The people in power have decided he must be removed. There are knives somewhere in this story. There is a night coming when men will move through the dark looking for him.

This is the moment. The verse just before ours ends with one of the most arresting sentences in the entire Quran: *wa makarū wa makara-llāh, wa-llāhu khayru-l-mākirīn* — "and they schemed, and Allah schemed, and Allah is the best of schemers" (3:54). The plotters think they are alone in the dark. They are not. There is a counter-move already in motion, and it belongs to the Lord of the heavens.

And here al-Ṭabarī catches something easy to read straight past. That little word *idh* — "when" — that opens our verse does not float free. Grammatically it hangs directly on the *makara* of the verse before: *and Allah schemed against them... **when** He said to Isa, "I am taking you in full and raising you to Myself."* In other words, **this sentence is the scheme.** The divine counter-plot against the men with knives was not a thunderbolt and not a trap sprung in the street. It was a few words spoken to a hunted man — and the words were about lifting him up.

Now — here is what I want you to feel before we go further. **When you are betrayed, what do you want?** Be honest. You want them to pay. You want the world to see what they did. You want a horizontal justice — something that travels across the ground from you to them and sets the record straight in front of everyone.

So when Heaven finally speaks — when Allah Himself opens His mouth to answer the plot against His beloved prophet — you brace for the strike. You expect lightning. You expect the schemers to be named and crushed.

[PAUSE]

And instead, the very first thing Allah says is: *innī mutawaffīka wa rāfiʿuka ilayya.* "I am the One taking you in full, and raising you to Myself."

He doesn't start by talking about them at all. He talks about *Isa*. He doesn't strike *downward* at the enemies. He reaches *upward* for the beloved.

Here is the hook, and I want you to hold it the whole way through: **Heaven's first response to betrayal is not retaliation. It is elevation.** The question we are going to live inside today is — *why?* Why, when His servant is most surrounded, most hunted, most alone — does Allah's opening move not bring the enemy down, but lift the servant up? And what does the strange, beautiful word He uses — *mutawaffīka* — promise to anyone who has ever feared being discarded, wasted, or lost?

Let's go and find out.

# PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element One: *Mutawaffīka* — "The One Taking You in Full"

### First, a concept to carry

Before we touch the word, let me hand you a tool, because Arabic does something here that English struggles to do.

In English we mostly think in *verbs* and *nouns* — actions and things. But Arabic has a category that sits exactly between them, called the *active participle* — the *ism al-fāʿil*. It names the *doer of an action as an ongoing description.* Not "he forgives" (a one-time act), not "the forgiver" as a dry title — but something living, like "the One-who-is-forgiving," a description that clings to the person doing it.

Think of the difference between saying "she helped me" and "she is my helper." The first is an event that happened and ended. The second is a *standing reality* — a description that stays true. The active participle works like that second one. It freezes the action into a continuous, defining trait of the doer.

Hold that. It will matter in a moment, because Allah does not say to Isa "I *will take* you" as a single future event. He describes Himself with a participle: *mutawaffī-ka* — **"the One-who-is-taking-you."**

### Now, the word itself

> **مُتَوَفِّيكَ** — *mutawaffīka*

Look at it. The little *-ka* at the end is "you," Isa himself, fused right onto the word. And the body of the word comes from the root **و-ف-ي (w-f-y)**.

Now here is where it opens. We tend to translate *tawaffā* as "to cause to die," because that is how it often appears. But that is the *application* of the word, not its *meaning*. The root *w-f-y* does not mean death at all.

The root *w-f-y* means **fulfillment, completion, taking in full, leaving nothing owed.**

Let's sit with this, because the everyday Arabic is stunning. When a merchant collects a debt and gets *every last coin* — not most of it, not the rounded amount, but the complete sum with nothing left behind — the word is *istawfā*, from this very root. *Wafā* is to *fulfill* a promise. *Awfā bi-l-ʿahd* — "he kept the covenant in full." *Wafāʾ* is *loyalty* — the quality of someone who gives you the whole of what they owe you and never short-changes you.

So the root that we lazily render "death" actually means: **to receive something completely, to take it in full, to leave none of it behind.**

[PAUSE]

Notice what that does to the verse. Death, in this root, is almost a side-meaning — death is *one occasion* on which a soul gets "taken in full." But the *core* of the word is the completeness of the taking. *Mutawaffīka* is Allah saying: **I am the One who is going to receive you entirely. Nothing of you will be lost. Nothing of you will fall into their hands. You will be collected — every part — and the collection will be complete.**

And now feel the form of the word. This is what the grammarians call Form V — *tafaʿʿul* — which carries a sense of the action being *taken to oneself*, drawn inward, done with full involvement. It is not a distant, mechanical taking. It is *Allah drawing Isa to Himself.* The form itself leans the action toward the One who is taking.

### The honest difficulty — and why it deepens the word

Here I have to be straight with you, because the classical scholars themselves stood before this word and differed — and their difference is not a problem to hide. It is a set of camera angles on one reality.

Some of the great commentators — al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, al-Kalbī, Ibn Jurayj, as al-Baghawī reports — read *mutawaffīka* here as **"taking you up alive, without death,"** raising Isa body and soul, and they pointed to Isa's own words on the Day of Judgment in 5:117, *fa-lammā tawaffaytanī* — "when You took me." Al-Jalālayn states it as plainly as a gloss can: *qābiḍuka... min al-dunyā min ghayri mawt* — "taking you from this world without death." Al-Muyassar carries the same sense, and adds the body to it: *qābiḍuka mina-l-arḍi min ghayri an yanālaka sūʾ... wa rāfiʿuka ilayya bi-badanika wa rūḥik* — "taking you from the earth without any harm reaching you, and raising you to Me with your body and your soul."

Al-Qurṭubī records another angle: that the Arabic *wāw* — the "and" — does not force a sequence, so the meaning may be "raising you to Myself *and* taking you in full," the taking coming in its own time.

And al-Baghawī preserves a third, quieter reading that I find piercing — that *tawaffī* here carries its root sense of receiving *wāfiyan*, **in full and loyally** — that Allah took Isa *complete*, "so they got nothing of you," *lam yanālū minka shayʾan.*

Do you see how every one of these is the same light through a different window? Whether the taking is a raising-up alive, or a completion at its appointed time, **the unifying truth is the root itself: Isa is received in full, nothing of him surrendered to the schemers, nothing of him wasted, the whole of him collected by his Lord.** The plotters reached for him in the dark — and the verse tells us their hands closed on nothing, because he had already been *taken in full* by the only One with the right to take him.

What this single word reveals: **the people who plot against the beloved of Allah do not get to write the ending. Allah collects His own — completely — before the knives ever arrive.**

## Element Two: The Three Participles — and the Strange Direction of "Purify"

### The rhythm you can hear

Read these three words aloud, slowly:

> **مُتَوَفِّيكَ ... وَرَافِعُكَ ... وَمُطَهِّرُكَ**
> *mutawaffī-ka ... wa rāfiʿu-ka ... wa muṭahhiru-ka*

Hear it? Three active participles, three times Allah describing *Himself* as the doer, and three times that same *-ka* — "you, Isa" — landing at the end like a heartbeat. *Taking-you. Raising-you. Purifying-you.* Three waves, and every wave breaks on the same shore: *you.*

This is not decoration. Remember what the active participle does — it makes the action a *standing description of the doer.* So Allah is not promising three future favors. He is telling Isa *who He is in relation to him right now*: I am your Taker-in-full, I am your Raiser, I am your Purifier. The threat is horizontal and human; the response is a triple wave of divine action, all of it flowing toward the one being hunted.

And notice the middle wave: *rāfiʿuka **ilayya*** — "raising you **to Myself**." Not "raising you above the city," not "raising you to safety." *To Me.* The direction of rescue is not sideways and away from danger — it is **upward, into nearness.** This is the answer to our hook taking shape. When the world closed in horizontally, Heaven's reply was a vertical axis the plotters could not even see, let alone climb.

### Now read the absence

Here is where I need you to listen for what is *not* said — because in the Quran, the gap is often the message.

> **وَمُطَهِّرُكَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ**
> *wa muṭahhiruka mina-lladhīna kafarū* — "and purifying you **from those who disbelieved.**"

Stay with me. *Ṭahhara*, from the root **ط-ه-ر (Th-h-r)**, is Form II — the intensive, thorough form. It is the word for deep, deliberate cleansing — the purification of *wuḍūʾ*, of *ghusl*, of being made ritually and spiritually clean.

Now — when you hear "purify you," what do you instinctively expect the next word to be? *Purify you from... sin. From... fault. From... impurity.*

But that is not what Allah says. He says *muṭahhiruka **mina-lladhīna kafarū*** — "purifying you **from the people** who disbelieved."

[PAUSE]

Sit in that gap. Isa is a prophet — there is no sin to scrub here. So the "impurity" Allah names is not inside Isa at all. **The impurity is the proximity.** It is the *contamination of being surrounded by rejection*, of breathing the air of mockery and plotting, of living every day among people whose hearts have curdled against the truth.

And the cleansing Allah offers is not "I will change them." It is "**I will remove you from them.**" Purification, here, is *extraction.* It is being lifted *out.*

And this is not a reading pressed onto the verse from outside — it is exactly how the classical commentators heard the word. Al-Jalālayn glosses *muṭahhiruka* with a single, blunt synonym: *mubʿiduka* — "distancing you, removing you far from them." Al-Muyassar reaches for *mukhalliṣuka mina-lladhīna kafarū bik* — "delivering you, rescuing you out of the hands of those who disbelieved in you." Neither of them hears *scrubbing*; both hear *removal.* The cleansing was always about taking Isa *out.*

Notice what this overturns. We almost always assume that if a situation is toxic, the fix is to *fix the situation* — soften the hard hearts, win over the plotters, transform the room. But Allah's purification of Isa works the other way: He doesn't cleanse the room, He cleanses *Isa* — by taking him *out* of it. Sometimes the people will not change. And sometimes Allah's mercy is not that He changes them, but that **He no longer leaves you among them.**

What this word reveals: **there is a kind of purity that only comes from separation. Some environments cannot be cleaned; you can only be carried out of them — and being carried out is itself a mercy and a cleansing.**

## Element Three: *Fawqa* — and the "Above" That Has an Expiry Date

### The word, and its careful borders

> **وَجَاعِلُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوكَ فَوْقَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓاْ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ**
> *wa jāʿilu-lladhīna-ttabaʿūka fawqa-lladhīna kafarū ilā yawmi-l-qiyāmah*
> "and placing those who followed you **above** those who disbelieved **until the Day of Resurrection.**"

*Fawqa* simply means "above, over, superior." The classical commentators fill in *how*: al-Jalālayn says *yaʿlūnahum bi-l-ḥujjati wa-s-sayf* — "above them by proof and by the sword," i.e. in argument and in worldly prevailing; al-Muyassar says *ẓāhirīn* — "prevailing, visible, on top."

So the followers of Isa are promised ascendancy — in evidence and in the affairs of the world. Good. And here the commentators are careful about *who* these followers actually are. Al-Muyassar fills in the phrase: those who followed Isa "upon his religion and what he brought from Allah" — and, strikingly, *those who accepted his glad tidings of Muhammad ﷺ and believed in him when he came.* On that reading the line of "those who followed you" does not end with Isa's own disciples; it runs forward through history to every believer who recognized the prophet Isa himself had foretold. And al-Jalālayn draws the same wide circle from the other side: "those who followed you" are *alladhīna ṣaddaqū bi-nubuwwatik* — all who affirmed Isa's prophethood, *mina-l-muslimīna wa-n-naṣārā*, the Muslims *and* the Christians who held to him — set above those who rejected his prophethood. Between the two glosses you can feel the commentators reaching for the same expansive thing: the "above" is promised not to one generation but to a whole chain of faith that outlives them all — which is exactly why it can stretch the way it does, *ilā yawmi-l-qiyāmah.*

But now read the two boundary-markers Allah builds around this "above," because they are the whole point.

**Boundary one: *ilā yawmi-l-qiyāmah* — "until the Day of Resurrection."** This "above" has an expiry date stamped on it. It is real, but it is *temporary*. It belongs entirely to the timeline of this world. The moment the world ends, this particular "above" ends with it.

**Boundary two — read what comes immediately after:**

> **ثُمَّ إِلَىَّ مَرْجِعُكُمْ فَأَحْكُمُ بَيْنَكُمْ فِيمَا كُنتُمْ فِيهِ تَخْتَلِفُونَ**
> *thumma ilayya marjiʿukum fa-aḥkumu baynakum fīmā kuntum fīhi takhtalifūn*
> "**Then** to Me is your return, and I will judge between you concerning that over which you used to differ."

Notice the word *thumma* — "then." It is not the smooth *wa* ("and"); it is the *thumma* of a clear break, a turning of the page from one order of reality to another. The worldly "above" was the first chapter. *Thumma* — then — comes the *real* settling.

And look where the verb tense finally shifts. For most of the verse Allah has been describing Himself with those timeless participles. But here, at the end, He uses a sharp imperfect verb: *fa-**aḥkumu*** — "and **I will judge**," from the root **ح-ك-م (Ḥ-k-m)**, the root of decisive, final ruling. The "above" of this world was provisional — a matter of proof and prevailing. The *verdict* is something Allah reserves entirely for Himself, for later, *thumma*, in His own court.

[PAUSE]

Do you feel the architecture? Allah gives the followers a real victory *inside* history — but He deliberately *withholds* the final word from history. He hands the believers an "above," and in the same breath He tells them: *this is not the judgment. The judgment is Mine, and it comes after.* The thing people most want when they are wronged — the final, public, settled verdict — is the one thing Allah does not release into this world. He keeps it.

What this reveals: **worldly ascendancy is a gift, but it is never the verdict. Allah lets His people prevail in time, and reserves the truth of who was right for eternity.** Which means the believer is freed from the exhausting need to *be the judge* — because the Judge has already announced Himself, and the case is on His docket, not ours.

And the commentators tell us precisely what is on that docket. Al-Muyassar reads *fīmā kuntum fīhi takhtalifūn* as the disputes *over Isa himself* — *min amri ʿĪsā* — the very arguments about who he was and what became of him; al-Jalālayn widens the lens to *amri-d-dīn*, the whole matter of religion. Either way, the thing humanity fractured over most bitterly — the person of this hunted prophet — is the very thing reserved for God's own ruling. And the next breath of the sūra lifts the lid just enough to show the verdict's two faces: reading on into 3:56–57, Ibn Kathīr notes Allah's promise of *severe torment in this world and the next, with no helpers,* for those who disbelieved, and the full, unstinting reward for those who believed and did righteous deeds. The verse withholds the verdict from history — and then, in the very next line, tells us exactly how it will fall.

## Bridge: From Words to Reality

Look at what these three constructions have shown us, side by side.

*Mutawaffīka* told us: **you will be taken in full — nothing of you wasted, nothing surrendered to your enemies.**

The three participles told us: **the rescue moves upward, into nearness — and there is a purity that comes only from being carried out.**

*Fawqa ... thumma aḥkumu* told us: **the victory you get in this world is real but provisional; the verdict belongs to God and comes later.**

Now let's ask what all of this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself, and about your own life when you are the one surrounded.

# PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme One: The Vertical Answer to Betrayal

Start where you actually live.

Someone wronged you. Maybe a friend who turned, maybe a family member who took a side, maybe a colleague who smiled to your face and worked against you in the meetings you weren't invited to. And here is the thing about being wronged that nobody warns you about: **most of the damage is not the original act. It is what the wound does to your imagination afterward.**

You start rehearsing. You compose the speech that exposes them. You imagine the moment everyone finally *sees*. You want the justice to travel *across* — from you, to them, in front of witnesses. You want it horizontal, public, and now.

I call this the horizontal hunger, and it is exhausting, because it ties your peace to *their* downfall. As long as they are standing, you cannot sit down.

Now bring the verse into that room.

Isa had the most justified horizontal hunger imaginable. He was sinless. They were plotting his murder. If anyone ever had the right to demand a public, leveling justice, it was this man. And Heaven, with all power at its disposal, gives its answer — and the answer does not travel *across* at all.

*Rāfiʿuka **ilayya**.* "Raising you **to Myself.**"

[PAUSE]

The first move is not to drag the enemy down. It is to lift the beloved *up.* The axis of the rescue is vertical — toward God — on an axis the plotters cannot see, cannot reach, and cannot contest.

Let this land in stages. First: when you are wronged, your instinct is to *go toward them* — even if only in your mind, even if only to win the argument. But Allah's pattern with Isa is the opposite motion: **away from them, and toward Me.** The cure for the horizontal wound is a vertical climb.

Second: notice that the vertical answer doesn't *deny* the followers' worldly vindication — it comes, *fawqa*, "above." But it puts that worldly vindication in its proper place: *second.* The nearness comes first; the prevailing comes after, and on a timer. **Allah reorders the believer's priorities by the very order of His sentences.** Closeness to Him is the headline. Winning in the world is the footnote.

And here is the paradox I want you to sit with rather than resolve: the one who stops needing to win horizontally is precisely the one who becomes unconquerable. The plotters of 3:54 organized everything — and the verse tells us, in effect, *they reached and found nothing,* because the prize had already gone up an axis they didn't believe in. **The person whose treasure is vertical cannot be robbed by people who only know how to reach sideways.**

What does this change about tomorrow? When the wound comes — and it will — notice the horizontal hunger the moment it starts. Notice the rehearsing, the imagined courtroom. And then make the deliberate vertical move: turn the energy you were about to spend *on them*, and spend it *toward Him* — in salah, in du'a, in drawing nearer. Not because they don't deserve justice. They might. But because **your healing was never going to come from their fall. It was always going to come from your rise.**

[PAUSE]

## Theme Two: To Be Taken in Full

Let me name a fear most people carry and never say out loud.

It is the fear of being *wasted.* The fear of an unfinished life. You look at what you've built, what you've given, what you've tried — and a cold voice asks: *what if none of it counts? What if I die with the best of me unrecognized, unfinished, scattered? What if I just... get lost?*

We feel it in small ways constantly. The work poured into a project that gets cancelled. The years given to a relationship that ends. The kindness shown to people who never noticed. There's a particular grief in giving the whole of yourself to something and feeling like it just *drained into the sand.*

Now hear the word again, with this fear in the room: *mutawaffīka.* **"The One taking you in full."**

We learned that the root *w-f-y* means completion — to receive the *entire* sum, to leave *nothing* behind, the way a creditor takes back every last coin he is owed. *Istawfā* — he collected it complete. *Wafāʾ* — the loyalty that gives you the whole of what's due.

Now apply that to a human life. To be *mutawaffā* by Allah is to be **collected completely.** Not the edited version. Not the parts the world happened to notice. The *whole* of you — every quiet prayer no one saw, every tear, every restrained anger, every small mercy, every effort that "drained into the sand" — all of it gathered up, in full, by the One who was watching the entire time.

[PAUSE]

Let that overturn the fear. The dread of being wasted assumes there is *no complete accounting* — that the good in you can simply leak away unrecorded. But *mutawaffīka* is Allah's promise that **there is no leak.** The taking is *in full.* The merchant of the universe collects His servant down to the last coin. Nothing true that you ever did falls outside the collection.

And remember the second reading the scholars gave us — that *tawaffī* carries the sense of being received *wāfiyan*, loyally, completely, "so they got nothing of you." Turn that toward your own heart. The world will try to take pieces of you — your reputation, your peace, your years. But the part of you that belongs to Allah, the part you gave *to Him* — that part the world cannot touch, because **He takes it in full first.** What is received by Allah is removed from the reach of everything that wastes and forgets.

Now build to the hardest movement, the one that makes the believer fearless. If you truly believed that your Lord is *mutawaffīka* — that He will, without fail, collect the complete and loyal account of your life — then the terror of *not being seen* dissolves. You stop performing for an audience that misremembers. You stop needing the recognition, the credit, the public verdict. Why would you, when the only Accountant who matters keeps a perfect, total ledger and settles it *in full?*

What does this change about tomorrow? It changes *who you are working for.* The next good deed no one will witness — do it anyway, deliberately, and as you do it remind yourself: *this is not falling into the sand. This is being collected.* Live as someone who knows their account is being kept *complete* — and watch how it frees you from the exhausting hunger to be seen.

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## The Architecture Revealed

Now stand back and look at the whole verse as a single built thing, because its shape is the proof of its message.

Watch the order Allah chose:

1. *Mutawaffīka* — **I take you in full** (you are collected, nothing lost)
2. *Rāfiʿuka ilayya* — **I raise you to Myself** (the vertical rescue, into nearness)
3. *Muṭahhiruka mina-lladhīna kafarū* — **I purify you from them** (extraction as cleansing)
4. *Jāʿilu-lladhīna-ttabaʿūka fawqa...* — **I place your followers above** (worldly vindication)
5. *...ilā yawmi-l-qiyāmah* — **until Resurrection** (and here the timer)
6. *Thumma ilayya marjiʿukum* — **then to Me is your return**
7. *Fa-aḥkumu baynakum* — **and I will judge** (the verdict reserved)

Do you see the climb? The verse *ascends.* It begins with the individual being gathered and lifted (1–3), rises to the community being vindicated in history (4–5), and then — *thumma* — breaks past history entirely into the final court of God (6–7). The grammar itself moves **upward and forward**, from the hunted man, to the prevailing community, to the throne of judgment. The *shape* of the sentence is the very vertical axis it is preaching.

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes turn out to be one insight seen from two sides.

The words showed us a God who **takes in full, lifts up, carries out, and reserves the verdict.**

The themes showed us a heart that can therefore **stop hungering horizontally** (because the rescue is vertical) and **stop fearing waste** (because the collection is complete).

They are the same truth: *When you belong to Allah, the people who reach for you in the dark close their hands on nothing — because He has already taken you up, in full, into Himself.* The plot of 3:54 and the promise of 3:55 are the two halves of one sentence about reality: *they schemed — and Allah took His servant home, complete, before they ever arrived.*

## Questions to Carry

- When I am wronged, where does my energy go — *across* toward the one who wronged me, or *up* toward my Lord? What would it cost me to change the direction?
- Is there a room in my life I keep trying to *clean*, when Allah might be offering to *carry me out* of it instead?
- What good have I done that "drained into the sand" — and can I believe that it was, in truth, *collected in full?*
- Whose verdict am I living for — the public's, which misremembers, or God's, which is complete and final and not yet released?
- If I truly believed I would be *taken in full*, what would I stop performing, and for whom?

## One-Sentence Distillation

When the world closes in on the one Allah loves, His answer is not to strike across at the enemy but to lift the beloved up — taken in full, nothing lost, the verdict reserved for His own court.

## The Closing Invitation

You will be betrayed in some small way before this week is out — a slight, a turning, a discovery of someone working against you. When it comes, you now have a different move available than the one your wound will demand. Not the horizontal rehearsal. The vertical climb. Let this verse be the hand that turns your face *upward, to Him* — and let it quiet the fear that you might ever be wasted. You are being collected. In full. By the One who never miscounts.

## A Brief Du'a

O Allah, You who take Your servants in full and lose nothing of them — collect the whole of me for Yourself, and let nothing true that I have done fall into the sand. When the world reaches for me in the dark, raise me to *You*, and purify me by Your nearness from every room I cannot clean. And on the day You judge between us, let me be found among those You lifted, not among those who only reached across. *Āmīn.*
