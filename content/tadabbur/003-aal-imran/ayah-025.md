# The Question With No End

## فَكَيْفَ إِذَا جَمَعْنَٰهُمْ لِيَوْمٍۢ لَّا رَيْبَ فِيهِ وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍۢ مَّا كَسَبَتْ وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ

*Fa-kayfa idhā jamaʿnāhum li-yawmin lā rayba fīhi wa wuffiyat kullu nafsin mā kasabat wa hum lā yuẓlamūn.*

**"Then how will it be when We gather them for a Day about which there is no doubt, and every soul is paid in full what it earned — and they are not wronged."**

---

## INTRODUCTION: The Sentence That Trails Off

Let me begin not with the verse, but with a feeling you already know.

You are a child. You've done something — you know exactly what. And a parent looks at you, and instead of the punishment, instead of the lecture, they say four words and stop:

*"Wait until you're older..."*

And then — nothing. They don't finish it. They walk away.

[PAUSE]

Notice what just happened inside you. The unfinished sentence is **worse** than any finished one. If they had said "wait until you're older and you'll regret this for a week," you could measure it. You could brace against a known weight. But the blank? The blank you fill yourself, again and again, with everything you fear. The mind cannot leave an open threat alone. It pours itself into the gap.

Hold onto that feeling. Because Allah is about to do exactly this — and He does it on purpose.

### Rebuilding the World

To feel where this verse lands, we have to step back two verses.

In the verses just before this one, Allah is speaking about a particular group — the People of the Book, who had been invited to come to the Book of Allah, to let *it* judge between them. And a faction of them turned away. They walked off. Why?

Verse 24 tells us. They had a sentence they told themselves. They said:

*"The Fire will not touch us except for a number of days."*

A few days. That's all. A short, survivable sentence. We're insiders. We have a lineage, a covenant, a special status. The Fire is for *other* people. For us — a brief inconvenience, then home.

And then Allah says something devastating about that sentence. He says they were **deluded in their religion by what they themselves used to invent** — *mā kānū yaftarūn*. The comfort wasn't revealed to them. They manufactured it. They wrote their own verdict and then believed it because they were the ones holding the pen.

[PAUSE]

This is the world you must stand inside. A group of people who have built — with their own hands — a small, comfortable story about the consequences of their lives. A story that lets them turn away from the Book without anxiety. A story whose entire function is to let them sleep.

And then comes verse 25.

### The Hook

Allah does not argue with their story. He does not list counter-evidence. He does not say "actually, the Fire will touch you for longer than you think."

He asks **one question.** And He builds that question so that, by the time it ends, their entire manufactured comfort has quietly dissolved — not refuted, just... gone. Like fog when the sun comes up.

The question has no stated ending. It trails off — on purpose — exactly like the parent who walks away.

What is in this question that can dismantle a lifetime of self-deception in fifteen words? And why does the *fear* it produces come not from injustice — but from its perfect opposite?

Let's open it slowly.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: فَكَيْفَ — The Question That Refuses to End

Let me teach you something about questions before we touch this one.

In any language, a question usually carries its own subject. "How **are you**?" "How **was the trip**?" The question word points at something — there's a *thing* whose state is being asked about. The sentence is complete. You know what's being measured.

Now here is the first word of our verse:

**فَكَيْفَ** — *fa-kayfa* — "Then how...?"

And then — notice — Allah does **not** tell you how *what*. There is no subject attached to "how." He does not say "how will their *state* be." He does not say "how will they *act*." He says "Then how —" and moves straight into the *when*: "when We gather them..."

The thing that "how" is asking about is **left blank.**

Stay with this, because it is not a small thing. This is an absence, and in the Quran, absence is never an accident.

Look at what the classical commentators do when they reach this word. They cannot help themselves — they reach into the gap and try to fill it. Al-Jalālayn supplies a word that isn't in the verse: *ḥāluhum* — "how will their **state** be." Al-Baghawī does the same: *fa-kayfa ḥāluhum aw kayfa yaṣnaʿūn* — "how will their state be, **or** how will they act?" He offers two fillings because the gap is genuinely open. Al-Qurṭubī adds a third reading: "how will they manage, what will they **do**, when those adornments they claimed in this world have crumbled away from them?"

[PAUSE]

Do you see what just happened? Three of the greatest commentators in our tradition stood at this blank space and each one had to *supply* the missing word — and they didn't fully agree, because **Allah left it for you to fill.**

And here is the thing about a blank you fill yourself: you fill it with your own worst case. Just like the child and the unfinished threat. Allah does not state the horror of that Day. He opens a door marked "how —" and lets your own imagination walk through it carrying everything you most fear about yourself.

A stated punishment can be measured and braced against. An **unstated** one expands to fill the whole of your dread. This is the most merciful kind of warning and the most terrifying — because it doesn't impose a fear on you from outside. It draws out the fear you already carry.

There is one more layer here, and al-Qurṭubī names it precisely. He says this question is asked *ʿalā jihat al-tawqīf wa-l-taʿajjub* — "by way of **making them pause and take stock**, and by way of **astonishment**." *Tawqīf* — to bring someone to a halt. *Taʿajjub* — wonder, the jaw-dropping kind. The grammatical form of the verse is not really seeking information. It is a hand placed flat on the chest of a person walking away — *stop. Just... stop and look at where this ends.*

**What this single word reveals:** The most piercing warning Allah gives here is not a description. It is an open question — *Then how...?* — left deliberately unfinished, so that the comfortable story you built has to finish it for you, and it cannot.

### Element Two: جَمَعْنَٰهُمْ — The Future Spoken as Already Done

Here is something Arabic does that English struggles to imitate.

In English, the future is uncertain by its grammar. "I *will* go" — the very word *will* carries a wobble of possibility. The future tense is the tense of things that *might* not happen.

Arabic has a different move. Sometimes, to express that a future event is **absolutely certain**, the Quran describes it in the **perfect tense** — the tense of completed, finished, already-done action. It speaks of tomorrow as though it were yesterday. The logic is breathtaking: *this is so certain that I will describe it to you as something that has already occurred.*

Now look at the verb:

**جَمَعْنَٰهُمْ** — *jamaʿnāhum* — and the grammar here is **perfect tense**: "We **gathered** them." Past form. Completed action.

But the event is the gathering of all humanity on the Day of Judgment — an event still ahead of every person who has ever read this verse.

[PAUSE]

Feel the collision. The people in verse 24 built their comfort on a subtle bet — the bet that the Day is *far*, that it is *maybe*, that it lives in the soft uncertain haze of the future where you don't have to look. And Allah answers that bet not with an argument about timing, but with a **tense**. He files the future Day under *completed*. From His vantage, *jamaʿnāhum* — it is already done. The gathering they are betting against has, in the only reckoning that matters, **already happened.**

And look at the root itself: **ج-م-ع** (*j-m-ʿ*), to *gather, to collect what is scattered.* This is the language of *al-ḥashr*, the assembly. Every person who ever lived, scattered across every century and every grave and every ocean, collected into a single standing. The deluded faction in verse 24 imagined themselves as a special *group* — insiders, a club with a short sentence. And the verb undoes even that. On that Day there are no clubs. There is one gathering, and everyone is *in* it. The thing they thought made them safe — belonging to a privileged collective — is dissolved into the great gathering where the only category left is the individual soul.

Which is exactly where the next word takes us.

**What this word reveals:** The Day you are quietly betting is far away and maybe-never is, in the grammar of the One who will bring it, *already finished.* You are not waiting for it. You are inside it, walking backward.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: وُفِّيَتْ — Paid In Full, To the Last Atom

Now we arrive at the heart of the verse. And I need to teach you one feature of Arabic verbs first, because everything turns on it.

Arabic verbs come in *forms* — patterns built on the same root that shift its meaning. The simplest form just states the action. But there is a form — we call it Form II — that **doubles** the middle letter of the root, and that doubling does something specific: it **intensifies**. It pushes the action to completeness, to thoroughness, to its full extent. If the simple form is "to break," the doubled form is "to shatter completely." The doubling in the sound *is* the intensifying in the meaning.

Now, our root is **و-ف-ي** (*w-f-y*). In its plain meaning it speaks of **fulfillment, completion, keeping a promise whole** — *wafā'*, loyalty, the thing carried through to its end without shortfall.

And the verb in our verse is the **Form II** — the doubled, intensified form:

**وُفِّيَتْ** — *wuffiyat.*

Not "it was given." Not even "it was paid." It is: **it was paid in full, completely, to the very last measure, with nothing held back and nothing rounded off.** Al-Baghawī glosses it with one word — *wuffirat* — "given in its entirety." Al-Jalālayn: the full *jazā'*, the complete recompense. The doubling in the verb is Allah telling you there will be no shortfall, no approximation, no "close enough."

[PAUSE]

Sit with what this means against verse 24.

The deluded faction wanted a *discount*. "Only a few days." A reduced sentence. A partial reckoning. They built their whole peace on the hope that the account would be *rounded down* in their favor. And the verb that answers them is the one verb in the language that means **the account is settled to the last fraction.** *Wuffiyat.* There is no rounding. There is no discount. Every soul receives the *full* weight of what it carried.

Now look at what this full payment falls upon:

**كُلُّ نَفْسٍ** — *kullu nafsin* — "**every single soul.**"

Not "the people." Not "the groups." Not "the believers and the disbelievers" as blocks. **Every. Soul.** Singular. Individuated. The gathering of *jamaʿnāhum* collected everyone into one place precisely so that each one could be paid out *one at a time.* The crowd assembles and then dissolves into a sequence of individual settlements. There is no hiding in the group. The lineage that the faction trusted — gone. On that Day there is no "us." There is only *this soul,* and *its* account, paid in full.

And what is being paid?

**مَّا كَسَبَتْ** — *mā kasabat* — "**what it earned.**"

The root **ك-س-ب** (*k-s-b*) is the language of the marketplace — to *acquire, to earn, to gain by one's own effort.* It is the word for wages. For what your own hands brought home. Notice: it is not "what happened to it," not "what it was given," not "its luck." It is what it *earned* — what it actively went out and acquired, deed by deed, choice by choice. The payment is not arbitrary. It is the soul's own earnings, handed back to it in full.

The whole image is a final reckoning of wages. You worked. The wages accrued. And on the Day of no-doubt, the envelope is handed over — complete, exact, every hour of labor accounted for, nothing skimmed.

**What this word reveals:** The thing the self-deceiver fears most is not that the account will be too harsh. It is that the account will be *exact.* *Wuffiyat* — paid in full — is the death of every discount we quietly bank on.

### A Bridge

Look at what these three words have built, standing together.

An **open question** that you must finish with your own dread. A **future** spoken as already-completed, so your bet on "later" is void. And a **full payment**, to every individual soul, of exactly what it earned — the death of the discount.

Each of these is a grammatical fact. But notice — they are not really *about* grammar. They are about something much closer to the bone. They are about the stories we tell ourselves so we can keep walking, and what happens to those stories on the one Day none of them survive.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Doubt You Manufacture to Live

Let me start where you actually live.

There is a thing every human being does, and we almost never see ourselves doing it. We *manufacture doubt* — deliberately, quietly — about the things we don't want to face.

You know the medical symptom is there. You feel it. And you tell yourself, "It's probably nothing." You don't *know* it's nothing. You **manufacture** the doubt, because the doubt lets you not go to the doctor. The uncertainty is *useful.* It buys you peace tonight at the cost of the morning.

You know the relationship is dying. You feel it. And you say, "Maybe it's just a phase." The "maybe" is not a discovery. It is a tool. You build it because a definite answer would force a decision you're not ready to make.

This is one of the most sophisticated things the human ego does. It doesn't lie to you with a flat "no." It is far cleverer than that. It manufactures a **"maybe."** It generates *just enough* doubt to disable action — and then it hides, because doubt feels so much more reasonable than denial. Denial you could catch. But who can argue with "we don't really know"?

[PAUSE]

This is *exactly* the faction of verse 24. "The Fire will not touch us except for a few days" — that is not a doctrine. It is a manufactured *maybe.* A doubt about consequence, built deliberately, because the doubt lets them turn away from the Book and still sleep. Allah even names the mechanism: they were *deluded by what they used to invent.* The doubt was self-issued.

Now read how verse 25 answers it. The Day is described as:

**لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ** — *lā rayba fīhi* — "**no doubt in it.**"

Here is the wonder of this. The root **ر-ي-ب** (*r-y-b*) does not mean simple doubt — a neutral "I'm not sure." *Rayb* carries the flavor of *unsettling, anxious, suspicion-laced* doubt — the restless uncertainty that disturbs you. It is, in fact, the very *kind* of doubt the faction was trafficking in. They lived their whole religious life inside a self-made fog of *rayb* — "maybe a few days, maybe it's fine, who really knows."

And Allah takes that exact word — their favorite tool, their entire defense mechanism — and He attaches it to the one Day where it **does not exist.** *Lā rayba fīhi.* The fog they manufactured to survive this life is the one thing that cannot be carried into the next. At the threshold of that Day, every "maybe" you ever built evaporates. The "probably nothing" meets the diagnosis. The "maybe just a phase" meets the verdict.

[PAUSE]

Think about what this asks of you. Not someday — *now.*

Every one of us is running some manufactured doubt right now. Some "maybe it's fine" that is letting us not pray properly, not repent, not repair the thing we know is broken, not turn back toward the Book the way the faction was invited to and wouldn't. The doubt is doing its job. It is keeping you comfortable. And this verse walks straight up to that comfort and says: *there is a Day with no rayb in it, and on that Day you will not have the fog. Why are you spending the only currency that matters — time — buying fog?*

**The core insight:** The doubt that lets you sleep tonight is not a discovery about reality. It is a tool you built. And it has an expiry date — a Day with no rayb in it — after which it returns nothing.

**One practical application:** Name one "maybe it's fine" you are currently running about your own soul. Just one. Don't resolve it tonight — just *catch yourself manufacturing it.* The faction's tragedy was not that they doubted. It was that they never noticed they were the ones making the doubt. Noticing is the first crack of light.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: The Terror Is Not Injustice. The Terror Is Justice.

Now I want to take you somewhere counterintuitive, because the verse ends on a phrase that, read quickly, sounds like pure mercy — and is actually the most sobering line of all.

Let me set it up with how we normally think about fairness.

When we imagine being judged, almost all of our fear is the fear of being **wronged.** Of the unfair boss who doesn't see our effort. Of the false accusation. Of the system that overlooks the good we did and magnifies the bad. Picture standing before any human tribunal — what is your dread? *That they won't be fair. That I'll be punished for what I didn't do, or denied credit for what I did.* Our deepest courtroom fear is **injustice.**

So we comfort ourselves with the hope of fairness. "If they would just be *fair* to me, I'd be fine."

Now read the last clause of the verse:

**وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ** — *wa hum lā yuẓlamūn* — "**and they will not be wronged.**"

The root **ظ-ل-م** (*ẓ-l-m*) is *ẓulm* — injustice, wrongdoing, placing a thing where it does not belong. And Allah, at the climax of this terrifying verse, removes it entirely. *They will not be wronged.* Al-Baghawī spells out the mechanism: *not a single good deed will be diminished from them, and not a single bad deed will be added.* Perfect. Exact. Fair.

[PAUSE]

Now — feel the trap close.

We comfort ourselves with the hope of *fairness.* But look at what surrounds this promise of no-ẓulm. It comes immediately after *wuffiyat kullu nafsin mā kasabat* — every soul **paid in full what it earned.** The "no injustice" is not a softening. It is the *guarantee* that the full payment is **deserved.** It is Allah closing the last escape route. You cannot say "I was treated too harshly," because *lā yuẓlamūn* — there is no harshness in it. Every atom you receive, you earned.

Do you see the inversion? In every human court, our fear is injustice and our hope is fairness. But before Allah, **fairness is the thing to fear.** Because we are not, in truth, hoping to be treated *justly.* We are hoping to be treated *mercifully* — which is to say, *better* than we earned. *Justice* is precisely the discount being revoked. Perfect fairness means *exactly* what you earned, *wuffiyat,* paid in full, *lā yuẓlamūn,* not one atom off in your favor either.

[PAUSE]

This is why the faction's "few days" was such a deadly bet. They were betting on *less than justice* — a reduced sentence, a special discount for insiders. And the verse closes every door: the payment is *full* (no discount), and the scales are *just* (no wrong). The two clauses together are a wall with no gap. *You will get exactly what you earned, and you will not be cheated of a single thing — in either direction.*

And here — right at the bottom of the dread — is where the believer finds something the faction could not. Because *lā yuẓlamūn* cuts both ways. Not one good deed diminished. The secret charity no one saw — counted, in full. The tear shed alone at night for your sins — counted, in full. The patience no one thanked you for — *wuffiyat,* paid in full. The same perfect justice that terrifies the one banking on a discount is the *vindication* of the one who quietly did good and was overlooked by the whole world. Perfect justice is terror to the one who hoped to be *under*-charged, and it is the sweetest mercy to the one who feared being *over*-looked.

**The core insight:** What we fear in judgment is being wronged. This verse removes being wronged entirely — and in doing so reveals that our real hope was never *justice* at all. It was *mercy.* The discount we secretly bank on is not fairness. It is grace. So stop pretending you want to be judged justly. Start begging to be judged *mercifully* — and live like someone who is asking for it.

**One practical application:** When you raise your hands tonight, change one word. Most of us, deep down, plead *"Allah, be fair to me — see how hard I tried."* But *lā yuẓlamūn* means He already will be perfectly fair, and that is not where your safety lies. Plead instead: *"Allah, do not give me only what I earned. Give me from Your mercy what I did not earn."* That single shift — from claiming justice to begging mercy — is the difference between the faction's posture and the believer's.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Step back and see the whole machine of this verse working at once.

The **linguistic discoveries** and the **themes** are the same insight approached from two directions.

The open question — *fa-kayfa,* "Then how —?" — is grammar. But it *is* the manufactured doubt. It is Allah handing the self-deceiver a blank and saying: *finish the sentence yourself, with the story you built.* And the story cannot finish it, because the story was made of fog, and this is the Day of *lā rayba fīhi,* no fog.

The future-as-completed — *jamaʿnāhum,* "We **gathered** them" — is grammar. But it *is* the death of "later." The whole comfort of the faction lived in the haze of the not-yet. The perfect tense files the not-yet under *done.*

And the doubled verb — *wuffiyat,* "paid in **full**" — is grammar. But it *is* the revoked discount, the perfect justice that turns out to be the very thing we should fear and the very thing the quiet, overlooked believer should long for.

One question. One tense. One doubled letter. And a lifetime of self-deception, dismantled in fifteen words — not by argument, but by letting the comfortable story stand in the light of the Day until it simply could not hold its shape.

### Architectural Reveal

Now look at the shape of the whole verse, because it is built with terrible care:

> **Then how will it be —**
>   *(the open wound: you fill the blank)*
> **when We gather them**
>   *(the scattered, collected — no more group to hide in)*
> **for a Day about which there is no doubt,**
>   *(the fog you manufactured: gone)*
> **and every soul is paid in full what it earned —**
>   *(the discount you banked on: revoked)*
> **and they are not wronged.**
>   *(the last door — "but it's unfair!" — sealed shut)*

It moves from the open blank to the sealed door. It begins by opening a wound you must fill, and ends by closing every exit. You enter through *how?* and you leave with no way out except one — the door the verse never mentions, the door the faction walked away from in verse 23: *come to the Book. Throw yourself on mercy before the day of justice arrives.*

### Questions to Carry

1. What is the *"few days"* I tell myself? What is the manufactured *maybe* that is letting me turn away from something I know I should face?
2. If the Day is, in Allah's grammar, *already gathered* — am I living as someone waiting for it, or as someone who knows he is already inside it?
3. When I make duʿā, am I — without admitting it — asking Allah to be *fair* to me? And if He were *exactly* fair, *wuffiyat,* to the last atom — would I want that?
4. The same perfect justice terrifies the self-deceiver and vindicates the overlooked. Which one am I building, deed by quiet deed, right now?
5. Allah left the blank after *"how —"* on purpose. What word does *my* soul put there?

### One-Sentence Distillation

When the doubt you manufactured to live comfortably finally dissolves, you will be paid in full and not wronged by a single atom — and the terror of that Day is not that you will be treated unjustly, but that you will be treated *justly.*

### The Closing Invitation

This verse is not asking you to be afraid of a punishment described in detail. It refuses to describe it. It is asking you, instead, to do one honest thing: to catch yourself in the act of manufacturing comfort — and then to stop spending your days buying fog.

The faction in verse 24 had every opportunity. They were *invited* to the Book. The door of mercy was wide open in front of them. And they preferred their small comfortable story to the open door. The invitation of this verse is simply: *do not be them.* Let the manufactured doubt fall now, willingly, while falling still costs you nothing but pride — rather than have it stripped from you on the Day when nothing can be unsaid and every wage is paid in full.

### A Brief Duʿā

*O Allah, You who will gather what is scattered and pay every soul in full — we confess that we have manufactured our own fog, and called it peace. Strip from us the comforting "maybe" before the Day of no doubt strips it from us. And on that Day of perfect justice, do not give us only what we earned — for we know what we have earned — but cover us with the mercy we did not earn, and let not one atom of the little good we hid be lost. Āmīn.*
