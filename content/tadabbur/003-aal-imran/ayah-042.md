# Chosen, Then Purified, Then Chosen Again

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the ayah first. Sit with the sound of it before we touch a single meaning.

> **وَإِذْ قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصْطَفَىٰكِ وَطَهَّرَكِ وَٱصْطَفَىٰكِ عَلَىٰ نِسَآءِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ**

*Wa idh qālat-il-malā'ikatu yā Maryamu inna-llāha-ṣṭafāki wa ṭahharaki wa-ṣṭafāki ʿalā nisā'i-l-ʿālamīn.*

"And [remember] when the angels said: 'O Maryam, indeed Allah has chosen you, and purified you, and chosen you above the women of the worlds.'"

Now — I want you to notice that you have just heard the word "chosen" twice. **Chosen. Then purified. Then chosen again.** Hold onto that. We are going to spend a long time on why God says it twice, and why He slips the word "purified" in between them. Because that ordering is not decoration. It is the entire anatomy of what it means to be lifted up by God.

### Let us rebuild the world

To feel this ayah, you have to stand in the temple courtyard of Jerusalem, generations before the birth of Isa.

A woman — the wife of ʿImrān — had made a vow. Before her child was born, she dedicated what was in her womb to the service of the sanctuary: *muḥarraran*, freed entirely for God, belonging to no worldly claim. She expected a boy. Boys served the temple. Boys carried that role. And then she delivered a girl.

Listen to her words a few ayahs earlier: *"My Lord, I have delivered a female"* — and then, almost in apology, *"and the male is not like the female"* (3:36). There is a kind of quiet heartbreak in that line. A vow made for a son, fulfilled by a daughter, in a world that did not quite know what to do with a daughter dedicated to God.

And it is into *that* world — a world that ranked her, that wasn't sure she counted, that had a slot for a boy and no obvious slot for her — that the angels descend and say: *yā Maryam.* O Maryam. **Allah has chosen you.** (Al-Jalālayn reads the plural "the angels" here as Jibrīl himself — the very messenger who would soon return to announce her son — so that the same voice that crowns her is the voice that will entrust her.)

[PAUSE]

### The hook

Here is what I want you to wonder about as we begin.

A mother worried her daughter wasn't enough. The world had a hierarchy, and Maryam seemed to fall on the wrong side of it. And God's answer is not to abolish the hierarchy. His answer is to put her **at the top of it** — "above the women of the worlds."

But before He elevates her *above* anyone, He does something to her first. He purifies her. He cleans her before He crowns her.

Why? Why must purification come *before* the elevation — and why does the word "chosen" wrap around it on both sides, like two hands cupping something fragile in the middle?

That is what we are here to see.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One — *Iṣṭafāki*: the word that means "to take the cream"

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we open the word.

Arabic builds meaning from three-letter roots, and those roots almost always begin life in the physical, tangible world — and then the language lifts them into the abstract. So if you want to understand a spiritual word, you find its physical home first.

The root here is **ṣ–f–w** (ص-ف-و). Its physical meaning is **clarity, purity, the clearness of something with nothing murky in it.** Picture water so clear you can see straight to the stones at the bottom. That is *ṣafā*. When the Arabs spoke of *ṣafwat al-shay'* — they meant *the choicest, purest, best part* of a thing. The cream that rises to the top of the milk. The clear part you skim off when everything cloudy has settled below.

Now keep that image — *the cream skimmed from the top, the pure part lifted from the rest* — and let's open the word God actually uses.

He says **ٱصْطَفَىٰكِ** — *iṣṭafāki.*

This is not the simple verb "to choose." This is **Form VIII** of that root — the *iftaʿala* pattern. And what this form does here is take that idea of *skimming off the purest part* and turn it into a deliberate, completed act directed at *her*: **He selected you as the chosen cream. He took the purest portion — and it was you.**

[PAUSE]

Let's sit with this. The English word "chosen" is flat. It just means "picked from a group." But *iṣṭafā* is not picking. It is **refining and then taking the result.** It carries inside its own letters the idea that what is selected is selected *because it is the clearest, the purest, the part with nothing murky left in it.*

And here is something I want you to actually *hear*. Say it slowly: *iṣ-ṭa-fā.* Feel the weight of that **ṣ** — the heavy, emphatic letter, the one that fills the whole mouth. Arabic has light letters and heavy letters, and this root is anchored on one of the heaviest. The word *sounds* like what it means: it has gravity. It has weight. This is not a casual pick. The very sound of the verb carries the solemnity of a divine decree.

Notice something else. The verb is in the **perfect tense** — completed, done, settled. Not "Allah is choosing you" or "Allah will choose you." *Iṣṭafāki* — **it is already done.** Before the angels announced it, before Maryam knew it, before the world recognized it — the choosing was finished. The announcement is not the event. The announcement is the *unveiling* of an event that had already happened in the knowledge and decree of God.

Here is what's remarkable. Maryam did nothing in this sentence. She is grammatically the *object* — the one *to whom* the choosing happens, not the one who does it. *Iṣṭafā-**ki*** — the *ki* at the end is "you," the receiver. **The honor is entirely God's act.** She did not climb to it. She did not earn the elevation by a campaign of striving. The cream does not lift itself to the top by force. It rises because of what it *is*. And what made her the cream — we are about to see — was not her achievement. It was His purification.

The classical commentators are unanimous on the core meaning: *iṣṭafāki* — *ikhtāraki* — He chose you, He selected you (al-Jalālayn, al-Muyassar, al-Qurṭubī, al-Baghawī all gloss it this way). But every one of them preserves the flavor of the root: not chosen at random — **chosen as the choice one.**

And al-Ṭabarī adds the one thing flat "chosen" can never carry: a *direction.* He glosses *iṣṭafāki* as *ikhtāraki wa-jtabāki li-ṭāʿatihi* — "He chose you and selected you **for His obedience**, and for an honor He singled you out with." Al-Muyassar says the same: chosen *li-ṭāʿatihi*, for His service. So the first choosing is not just a selecting *of* her — it is a selecting *for* something. She is skimmed as the cream, yes — but skimmed *toward* a life of worship. Hold that, because it will matter enormously when we reach the very next ayah and find out what the chosen one is told to do with her chosenness.

[PAUSE — let this land]

### Element Two — *Ṭahharaki*: the word in the middle, and the thing it leaves unnamed

Now the second word. And this one teaches us to read by paying attention to a *silence*.

The word is **وَطَهَّرَكِ** — *wa ṭahharaki* — "and He purified you."

The root is **ṭ–h–r** (ط-ه-ر): purity, cleanliness, being free of contamination. But notice the *form*. The simple verb would be *ṭahura* — "to be pure," a state something simply *is*. God does not use the simple form. He uses **Form II** — *ṭahhara* — with the doubled middle letter.

Let me teach you what Form II does. When Arabic doubles that middle consonant, it does something forceful: it makes the verb **intensive and transitive.** It turns "to be pure" into **"to make thoroughly pure, to actively cleanse, to purify completely."** This is not Maryam happening to be clean. This is God *performing* a deep, deliberate, thorough purification *upon* her. The doubled letter is the sound of intensity — the cleansing pressed all the way through.

And again — feel the letter. *Ṭah-ha-raki.* The **ṭ** is another of those heavy, emphatic, mouth-filling letters. So we now have two great pillars in this ayah — *iṣṭafā* with its **ṣ**, *ṭahhara* with its **ṭ** — both built on the weightiest sounds in the language. Selection and purification, the two heaviest acts, carried on the two heaviest sounds. The architecture is in the *acoustics*.

Now — here is the silence I want you to hear.

**Purified you from *what*?**

In Arabic, when you say "He purified her," the ear waits for the next word: purified her *from* — and then the contaminant. *Ṭahharahā min...* — purified her *from* this, *from* that. But God leaves it open. **He does not name what she was purified from.**

[PAUSE]

Stay with me, because this absence is doing enormous work.

Look at what the commentators do with that gap. Because God left it open, they fill it — and they cannot agree, *and that disagreement is the point.* Al-Jalālayn: purified you *from the touch of men.* Al-Muyassar: purified you *from base, lowly character.* Al-Ṭabarī reaches somewhere different again — purified your very *dīn,* your *religion,* *min al-rayb wa-l-adnās:* from **doubt** and from the defilements that cling to faith. (Sit with that one: a purification not of the body or the record but of *belief itself* — cleared of the rayb, the flickering uncertainty, that clouds even sincere hearts.) Al-Qurṭubī reports it from Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan as purification *from disbelief* — and from al-Zajjāj as purification *from every kind of physical impurity.* Al-Baghawī gathers them all: *from the touch of men, from menstruation, from sins* — and notes al-Suddī's report that Maryam was so purified she did not menstruate at all.

Now — do you see what has happened? The scholars are not contradicting each other. They are standing around a single reality and each describing the angle they can see. **God said "purified" without an object precisely so that no single impurity could exhaust the meaning.** Had He said "purified you from sin," you'd think: only sin. Had He said "from the touch of men," you'd think: only that. By leaving it *unnamed*, He made the purification **total** — physical and spiritual, of the body and of the character, of the deed and of the heart. Purified from *everything that could be named, and from everything that wasn't.*

This is what an absence does in the Quran. The unspoken word is larger than any spoken one would have been.

[PAUSE]

And now look at *where* this word sits.

> **chosen** — *purified* — **chosen**

*Iṣṭafāki — ṭahharaki — iṣṭafāki.*

Purification is **in the middle.** It is held between two choosings. And that tells us the relationship between them. The first choosing is not because she was already pure — it is the choosing that *leads to* her being purified. And the second choosing — the elevation — comes *after* the purifying is done. **God chooses, then cleanses what He has chosen, then raises it.** The crown is placed last. The washing comes first. You are not elevated *and then* cleaned up. You are cleaned, and the cleaning is what the elevation rests on.

### Element Three — *Iṣṭafāki ʿalā*: the second choosing is a different kind of choosing

Now look very carefully, because the word "chosen" appears twice but it is **not the same act both times.** And the difference is hiding in one small preposition.

First time: *iṣṭafāki* — "He chose you." Standing alone. No comparison. Just: God selected you, for Himself, for His purpose.

Second time: *iṣṭafāki **ʿalā** nisā'i-l-ʿālamīn* — "He chose you **over** the women of the worlds."

That word **ʿalā** — "over, above, upon" — changes everything. The first choosing is **vertical and private**: God reaching down and selecting her *for Himself.* The second choosing is **comparative and public**: God raising her *above all others.*

Let's sit with the sequence, because it is a complete spiritual map.

1. **First** — *iṣṭafāki*: God chooses her. Pure relationship. Just her and Him. No one else in the frame.
2. **Then** — *ṭahharaki*: He purifies her. He makes her fit for what the choosing was for.
3. **Only then** — *iṣṭafāki ʿalā*: He elevates her above everyone.

Do you see why the elevation comes *last*, and only after purification? **Because being raised above others is dangerous to a soul that has not first been cleansed.** Status without purity corrupts. Elevation without cleansing breeds the very murkiness — pride, self-regard — that *iṣṭafā* skims away. So God will not raise her over the worlds until He has first purified her of everything that would make height poisonous. The order is mercy. The order is protection.

Now — *over the women of the worlds.* Over *which* worlds, *which* women?

Here the scholars give us a real and honest difference, and I want to name it as a difference, not flatten it. Al-Ḥasan, Ibn Jurayj and others (in al-Qurṭubī and al-Baghawī) read it as **the women of her own time** — chosen above all women of her age. But al-Zajjāj and others read it as **all women, of every age, until the Day of Resurrection** — and al-Qurṭubī calls this *the sound view.* Al-Baghawī adds the reason some gave for the universal reading: that she alone, of all women, bore a child with no father — a distinction belonging to no woman before or since.

What this invites us to consider is not which scholar to side with, but the *shape* of the honor. Whether you read it as her age or all ages, the structure is identical: a woman the world wasn't sure how to count is told she is **at the summit of the count.** The hierarchy her mother feared she'd fallen beneath — God places her at its very top.

[PAUSE]

### Bridge

So look at what these three words have shown us. *Iṣṭafā* — chosen as the pure cream, by God's act alone, already completed. *Ṭahhara* — thoroughly cleansed, of an impurity so total it could not be named. And *iṣṭafā ʿalā* — raised above all, but only *after* the cleansing, never before.

That is the grammar. Now let us ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the soul, and about you.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One — The terror of being chosen, and why God cleanses before He crowns

Let me start where you actually live.

You know the feeling of being picked. The promotion you didn't expect. The teacher who singled you out. The person who, of all people, chose *you.* And you know the strange shadow that falls the moment after the joy: **"They don't know the real me. If they saw what's underneath, they'd take it back."**

Psychologists have a name for it now — *imposter syndrome.* The gap between the version of you that got chosen and the version of you that you secretly believe you are. The chosen-you on the outside; the murky-you on the inside. And the fear that the two will one day be found out.

Now bring that human terror to Maryam, and watch what God does with it.

The angels say *iṣṭafāki* — you are chosen. And the human heart, even a heart as pure as hers, might flinch: *chosen — but am I worthy of it?* And God's very next word is the answer. *Ṭahharaki.* **"And I purified you."**

[PAUSE]

Do you feel the mercy in that ordering? God does not say: "I chose you, *because* you made yourself pure." That would leave the burden on her — the imposter's burden, the endless anxiety of *did I do enough to deserve this.* Instead He says: "I chose you, **and I — Myself — purified you.**" The purity is not the qualification she brought. **It is the gift He gave to make her fit for what He chose her for.**

This is the opposite of how the world chooses. The world says: *prove you are clean, and then we will pick you.* Earn it, qualify, present your spotless record, *then* receive the honor. God says: *I pick you, and then I clean you.* The election comes first; the worthiness is something He works *into* you afterward. You do not climb up to God's choosing by your own purity. **His choosing reaches down and purifies you on the way up.**

And notice — this dissolves the imposter's fear completely. The imposter says: "If they saw underneath, they'd take it back." But what if the One who chose you is *also* the One cleaning underneath? Then there is nothing to be discovered, because the Chooser is already at work on the very thing you're afraid He'll find. **You cannot be exposed before a God who is already purifying what's hidden.**

Let this reframe what you carry. The honors in your life, the responsibilities, the moments you were entrusted with something — the dignity of being a believer at all — these are not certificates you earned by being clean. They are *iṣṭafā*: a choosing, followed by a *ṭahhara*, a cleansing God is still performing in you, daily, through your prayer, your repentance, your trials. **You were not chosen because you were pure. You are being purified because you were chosen.**

So tomorrow, when the old voice says *"you're not worthy of this"* — answer it with the order of the ayah. *He chose me, and then He purifies me.* The cleaning is His job, and He is doing it. Your job is simply to stay in His hands and let the murk settle and be skimmed away.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two — Being raised "above," without being poisoned by the height

Now the harder theme. Because there's a second danger, and it's the opposite of the first.

The first danger was feeling *too low* — the imposter, the one who feels unworthy. The second is the danger of being lifted *high.* And it is the more poisonous of the two, because it doesn't feel like a wound. It feels like a reward.

We all know what status does to people. Watch what happens when someone is told they are *above* others. The subtle hardening. The contempt that creeps into the eyes. The way "I was chosen" curdles into "I am better." Elevation is one of the most spiritually corrosive things a human being can receive — because the soul, raised above others, almost always begins to *look down.*

So here is the question the ayah quietly answers: **how do you raise someone above all the women of the worlds without destroying her with the height?**

Look again at the order. God does not go: *iṣṭafāki ʿalā* — chosen above — straight to the top. He inserts *ṭahharaki* first. **He purifies her before He elevates her.** And that is the whole secret. The reason height corrupts is that it amplifies whatever murkiness is already in you. Give a proud man status and you get a tyrant. Give a clean soul status and you get a servant. **So God cleanses first, so that when the height comes, there is no pride left for it to feed.**

This is why the second *iṣṭafā* — the one with *ʿalā*, the one about being *over* others — comes only *after* the purification. The elevation is safe only because the cleansing went first. Maryam can be placed above the women of the worlds without it poisoning her, because there is nothing left in her for the elevation to inflame. The cream has been skimmed; the murk is gone; height has nothing to corrupt.

[PAUSE]

And think about *what she does* with the elevation, right after this. The very next ayah: *"O Maryam, be devoutly obedient to your Lord, and prostrate and bow with those who bow"* (3:43). She is told she is above all women — and immediately told to **put her face on the ground.** Remember what al-Ṭabarī and al-Muyassar told us the choosing was *for* — *li-ṭāʿatihi,* for His obedience. Now the circle closes: she was chosen *for* worship, and the first thing said to the chosen one is *worship.* The elevation does not release her from servitude — it *is* a summons deeper into it. The highest woman in creation is commanded to bow *with* the worshippers, not above them. **Her elevation before God turns instantly into prostration before God.** That is what purified height looks like: the higher she is raised, the lower she puts her face.

That is the test of whether honor has cleansed you or corrupted you. Corrupted honor makes you stand taller over people. Cleansed honor makes you bow lower before God. The murky soul, lifted up, looks *down* at others. The purified soul, lifted up, looks *up* at God and *down at the ground* in sajdah.

So here is the application, and it is uncomfortable. Take the place in your life where you *are* "above" — where you have more knowledge, more wealth, more standing, more righteousness than someone near you. That elevation is a spiritual emergency. And the ayah gives you the only safe way to hold it: **let it drive you to the ground.** The moment you sense yourself standing taller over someone because of what God gave you, that is the murk God meant to skim away. Real chosenness doesn't end in looking down. It ends in sajdah.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture, revealed

Now stand back and look at the whole ayah at once, and watch the shape emerge:

> **ٱصْطَفَىٰكِ** — *chose you*
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**وَطَهَّرَكِ** — *purified you*
> **وَٱصْطَفَىٰكِ عَلَىٰ نِسَآءِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ** — *chose you above the women of the worlds*

It is a ring. *Chosen* on the outside, both times — and *purified* held safe in the center, cradled between the two choosings like something precious held in cupped hands. The two arms of *iṣṭafā* reach around *ṭahhara* and protect it. **Selection embraces purification; purification makes selection safe.** The form *is* the meaning. God built the sentence the way He builds a soul: choose, cleanse, raise — and let the cleansing sit protected at the heart of it all.

### Bringing it together

The linguistic discoveries and the human themes were the same truth approached from two sides.

The grammar showed us: *iṣṭafā* is God's act alone (she is the object, she earns nothing); the unnamed object of *ṭahhara* makes the purification total; and the *ʿalā* of the second choosing comes only *after* the cleansing.

The themes showed us why: because we are torn between feeling *too low* (the imposter, unworthy of the choosing) and being lifted *too high* (the proud, poisoned by the elevation) — and the ayah heals both with one cure. **Purification in the middle.** It tells the unworthy: *you don't have to be clean to be chosen — He cleans you.* And it tells the elevated: *you cannot be safely raised until you are cleaned.* The same word in the center answers both fears.

### Questions to carry

- Where in your life are you still trying to *earn* a chosenness that God is offering as a gift — exhausting yourself to be clean *enough* to deserve it, when He means to purify you *after* He takes your hand?
- What is the unnamed murk in you — the thing you'd never say aloud, the contaminant with no clean label? Can you hand it to the One whose purifying needs no object?
- Where are you "above" someone right now — and is that height making you stand taller over them, or bow lower before God?
- When you were last honored or entrusted with something, did it end in looking down, or in sajdah?
- Could it be that the trial you resent is not a sign you were rejected — but the *ṭahhara*, the cleansing, that always sits between being chosen and being raised?

### One-sentence distillation

God does not wait for you to be pure before He chooses you — He chooses you, purifies you, and only then raises you, holding the cleansing protected at the very center so that you are neither too unworthy to be lifted nor too proud once you are.

### The closing invitation

Let something shift. Stop standing outside God's choosing, auditing yourself for whether you qualify. You were never going to qualify by being clean first — no one is. Step *into* His hands and let the purifying happen. And if He has raised you over anyone in anything — let the height bend your back toward the ground. Be Maryam at the end of the next ayah: told you are above the worlds, and answering by putting your face in the dust.

### A brief du'a

*Yā Allah — You who chose Maryam and purified her before You raised her: choose us, though we bring nothing; cleanse in us the murk we cannot even name; and if You lift us above anyone in this life, do not let the height harden our hearts — bend us instead to the ground, bowing with those who bow. Make our being chosen end, as hers did, in sajdah. Āmīn.*
