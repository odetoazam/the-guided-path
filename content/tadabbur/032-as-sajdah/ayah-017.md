# What No Soul Knows
## A Tadabbur on Surah As-Sajdah, Ayah 17

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The Ayah

**Arabic:**
> [ayah:32:17] فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَّآ أُخْفِىَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ

**Transliteration:**
> Falā taʿlamu nafsun mā ukhfiya lahum min qurrati aʿyunin jazāʾan bimā kānū yaʿmalūn.

**Translation:**
> No soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort for the eyes — a reward for what they used to do.

---

### Rebuilding the World

Before we open this ayah, we need to feel where it sits.

The surah is named **As-Sajdah** — *The Prostration.* It is named for the lowest posture a human body can take. Forehead on the ground. Eyes closed. Spine bent. The point at which a person becomes invisible to anyone standing.

And in the verses just before this one — verse 16 — Allah describes a specific group of people. He says their sides **detach** from their beds. Picture that. The Arabic word for "sides" (*junūb*) is the side of the body that touches the mattress when you sleep. He describes them peeling themselves off the warmth of their beds. In the dark. Before anyone is awake. To call upon their Lord — in fear and hope.

This is hidden worship. Worship no human sees. Worship that has no audience but Allah.

Now — sit with what that does to a person. If you have ever tried to wake before dawn to pray, you know what it costs. You know the negotiation with the body. The pull of the pillow. The silence of the house. The small, secret war you wage with your own self while everyone you love sleeps.

And nobody knows.

Your spouse sleeps beside you. Your children sleep down the hall. Your neighbors sleep. The world sleeps. You alone are awake — and there is no one to witness this except the One you woke up for.

[PAUSE]

This ayah — verse 17 — is what comes next.

Allah has just described the most hidden, most uncompensated, most invisible kind of devotion a human being can offer. And then He answers it.

---

### The Hook

Here is what is stunning about this answer.

Allah does not say *"I have prepared something great."* He does not say *"the reward is gardens beneath which rivers flow."* He does not even say *"only the angels know."*

He says — listen carefully — **no soul knows.**

Not the worshipper. Not the angels. Not the prophets. Not even — and we will have to talk about this — perhaps not even something else.

And the verb He uses to describe what is waiting? It is not "prepared." It is not "promised." It is not "stored." It is a verb that means *deliberately concealed.* **Hidden.**

The question that pulls us in is this: why would Allah, who has described Paradise in such detail elsewhere in the Quran — gardens, rivers, fruits, thrones, companions — choose **this one moment** to throw a veil over the reward and say, *no soul knows what is behind this veil*?

Why does the hidden act demand a hidden reward?

Stay with that question. We are going to walk into the linguistic architecture of this ayah and discover that every single word — the negation, the noun, the verb form, the preposition, the metaphor — is doing the same work. Concealment, answering concealment. A secret, answering a secret.

[PAUSE]

---

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We are going to open three things in this ayah. Each of them, on its own, would be worth a long reflection. Together, they reveal an architecture.

---

### Element One: *Falā taʿlamu nafsun* — "No Soul Knows"

Let's start with how Allah opens this ayah.

In Arabic, when you want to negate something — to say "not" — you have choices. You can say *lā* (no, not). You can say *mā* (not). You can place the negation early in the sentence or late. You can negate the verb or the noun.

These choices are not interchangeable. In Arabic, *where* you put the negation and *what kind* of negation you use changes the **scope** of what is being denied.

Think of it this way in English. There is a difference between:
- "Nobody in this room knows."
- "Nobody knows."

The first one limits the scope — it's about this room. The second is universal — it sweeps the entire category of knowers and empties it.

Arabic does this with more precision. And here, Allah uses a construction that produces the most absolute, most universal sweep available in the language.

**Falā taʿlamu nafsun.**

The word *nafs* — soul, self, person — is indefinite. It has no article. It is not "the soul" or "a particular soul." It is simply **soul** — any soul, every soul, no soul. When you place a negation in front of an indefinite noun like this in Arabic, classical grammarians teach, you produce what is called *istighrāq* — total inclusion. Total sweep.

This is not "most souls don't know." Not "few souls know." Not "you don't know yet but you will." This is: **the category of souls that knows what is hidden — that category is empty.**

[PAUSE]

Now sit with something. Who is included in the word *nafs*?

You are. Every human being is. That is obvious.

But the word *nafs* in the Quran does not only refer to human beings. It is used of jinn. It is used of angels in some contexts. It is used — and here the early commentators paused for a long time — even of prophets. Of Muhammad ﷺ himself.

The classical mufassirūn report a hadith qudsi closely connected to this ayah. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah said: *"I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no heart of man has ever conceived."* And then the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Recite if you wish — falā taʿlamu nafsun mā ukhfiya lahum."*

Notice what this does. The Prophet ﷺ — the one who was taken on the night journey, the one who saw Paradise — is telling us that *even he* is describing something that no soul, including his own, has fully comprehended.

This is what *istighrāq* — that total sweep of the indefinite noun under negation — accomplishes. It doesn't just exclude us. It excludes *every created knower.* The reward is not just hidden from human beings. It is hidden from the cosmos.

[PAUSE]

Here is what is remarkable. The opening negation tells us something not just about the reward — but about the **act** of hiding itself.

If Allah had simply wanted to communicate that the reward is great, He could have done so without the universal negation. He could have said *the reward is more than you can imagine.* The fact that He chooses this construction — the most absolute possible — means the *hiddenness* itself is part of the gift.

The reward is not just unknown because it is too vast to describe. The reward is **deliberately kept unknown.** And that deliberateness is itself a treasure.

What did the worshippers do? They hid their worship.
What did Allah do? He hid the reward.

Hiddenness, answering hiddenness.

[PAUSE — major pause]

---

### Element Two: *Ukhfiya* — "Has Been Hidden"

Now let's look at the verb that holds this entire ayah together.

The verb is *ukhfiya.* The root is *kh-f-y* — the root from which we get *khafāʾ* (concealment), *istakhfā* (to hide oneself), *khufyatan* (in secret).

But the form here is crucial. *Ukhfiya* is in the **passive voice.** Passive — meaning we are not told who did the hiding. The subject is suppressed.

Now, this is grammatically interesting on its own. But here is where Arabic does something English cannot.

Before we open this — let me give you a quick lesson in how Arabic verbs work. Arabic verbs come in patterns called *forms.* There is Form I (the basic root), Form II (intensified or causative), Form III, Form IV, and so on, all the way to Form X. Each form bends the meaning in a specific direction.

The verb *ukhfiya* is in **Form IV** of the root *kh-f-y.* Form IV typically intensifies an action or makes it deliberate and causative. It is the difference between *something happened* and *someone made it happen with intention.*

To feel this in English: there is a difference between "the keys fell behind the couch" and "the keys were carefully placed behind the couch." Both result in keys being hidden. But one is accidental. The other is curated.

*Ukhfiya* is the curated version.

This is not "the reward happens to be unknown." This is **the reward was deliberately, intentionally, with purpose, concealed.**

[PAUSE]

Now layer the passive voice on top of that.

Allah does not say *"I hid"* (*akhfaytu*). He says *"it was hidden"* (*ukhfiya*). The Doer is veiled.

Classical rhetoricians have a name for this. When Allah uses the passive voice in a place where naming Himself as the Doer would be possible — and indeed expected — the construction itself becomes part of the meaning. The grammar is performing the message.

What is the message? **Even the act of hiding is hidden.** The Hider has stepped behind a curtain.

Think about what this does. If Allah had said *"I have hidden for them,"* you would imagine a King who keeps a treasury, who at some point will open the treasury and grant it. There is a Hider, and there is a hidden thing, and the relationship between them is visible.

But when Allah uses the passive — *ukhfiya* — He removes Himself from the visible frame. The reward is hidden. The hiding is hidden. Even the Hand that did the hiding is hidden.

It is hiddenness, three layers deep.

[PAUSE]

Here is what makes me tremble at this construction.

The worshippers in verse 16 — they hid themselves. Their bodies left the bed in the dark. Their voices fell to a whisper. They did not announce. They did not show. They effaced themselves.

And Allah, in verse 17, answers in a perfectly symmetrical voice. He effaces *Himself* from the grammar. He hides His own hand. He uses the passive — the most self-effacing construction Arabic has.

What does it mean when the worshipper hides their worship, and Allah responds by hiding His own response — even hiding His own hiding?

It means this is a private exchange. This is a transaction between a slave and their Lord that has been removed entirely from the public ledger of the universe. No one will see it. No one will be told. It is sealed.

When you fold your hands in the dark and pray a prayer no one will know about — Allah does not write a public receipt. He writes a private one. And then He hides the receipt. And then He hides the hiding.

[PAUSE — major pause]

---

### Element Three: *Qurrati Aʿyun* — "Coolness of the Eyes"

Now we come to perhaps the most beautiful phrase in this ayah — and one of the most beautiful in the entire Quran.

Allah does not describe what the reward is. He uses an idiom. He says it is *qurrati aʿyun* — literally, *coolness of eyes.*

The root *q-r-r* means to **settle, to come to rest, to stop moving.** From this root comes the word *qarār* (a settled place). It also means **to be cool** — because in Arabic understanding, when something settles, it stops generating heat. The cool thing is the still thing.

The root *ʿ-y-n* means **eye.**

So *qurrati ʿayn* literally means: *the settling, the coolness, the stillness of the eye.*

Now — this is an idiom. Idioms must be unpacked, because the literal meaning is not the surface meaning. To understand it, we have to enter the Arab imagination of the eye.

In classical Arab thought, the eye that is restless — that darts, that searches, that longs — is the eye of someone whose heart is not at peace. The eye reflects the soul. When your soul is searching, your eyes are searching. When your soul has found what it was searching for, your eyes **rest.** They stop scanning. They settle.

Tears, too, were understood this way. The Arabs said the tears of grief are hot, and the tears of joy are cool. The grieving eye burns. The fulfilled eye is cool.

So *qurrati ʿayn* is not just "something nice to look at." It is the deepest possible Arabic idiom for **arrival, fulfillment, the end of longing.**

[PAUSE]

Stay with me — this gets deeper.

When the mother of Mūsā places her infant in a basket and sets it on the Nile, Allah promises her: *We will return him to you so your eye may be cool* — *li-taqarra ʿaynuhā* (28:13). When Maryam is grieving in childbirth, the angel tells her to eat and drink — *wa qarrī ʿaynan* — *and let your eye be cool* (19:26). When Mūsā confronts his enemies, the words are: *and We returned you to your mother so her eye would be cool* (20:40).

Notice the pattern. *Qurrat ʿayn* appears at the moments of **reunion after separation, fulfillment after longing, arrival after exile.** It is the Quran's vocabulary for *the thing your soul was made for, when it finally comes.*

The righteous in 25:74 pray: *"Our Lord, grant us from our spouses and offspring qurrata aʿyun."* They are not praying for pretty things to look at. They are praying for the kind of joy that ends the searching of the soul.

[PAUSE]

So when Allah says the hidden reward is *qurrati aʿyun* — He is not promising beauty. He is promising **the end of restlessness.**

Every human being is in motion. Your eyes scan. Your heart searches. You move from desire to desire. Even the satisfactions you achieve never fully settle you — there is always another horizon. You taste, and you are hungry again. You arrive, and you want to leave again. You acquire, and you want more.

The condition of the human soul on earth is restlessness.

What Allah has hidden in Paradise — for those who detached their sides from their beds in the dark — is *the cessation of that motion.* The eye that finally rests. The soul that finally arrives. The longing that finally ends.

You will not be looking for the next thing. There will be no next thing. You have arrived.

[PAUSE]

And notice — Allah does not specify *what* the eyes will be cool *because of.* He does not say "coolness of eyes from gardens" or "coolness of eyes from companions" or "coolness of eyes from My Face." He just says **coolness of eyes.**

Why? Because **the cause is part of what is hidden.** *No soul knows what is hidden.* The reward is concealed; the source of the joy is concealed; the texture of the rest is concealed. All we are told is that whatever it is — when it lands — your eyes will rest.

This is the deepest possible description of unknowable joy. The destination is named only by what it does to you, not by what it is.

[PAUSE]

---

### Bridge

Now look at what these three linguistic elements have done together.

The negation universalizes: *no soul* — the gift is hidden from every created knower.
The passive verb conceals the Hider: *was hidden* — even Allah's hand is veiled in the grammar.
The metaphor refuses specification: *coolness of eyes* — the joy is named only by its effect, never by its substance.

Three layers of concealment, each reinforcing the others. The ayah is not describing the reward. The ayah is **performing the hiddenness of the reward.** The grammar is itself a curtain.

[PAUSE — major pause]

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The Architecture of Reciprocal Secrecy

Let me start somewhere you already live.

Think about the most meaningful relationships in your life. The deepest friendships. The closest bonds. The marriages that work.

What do they all share?

They share **secrets.** Not dark secrets, necessarily. Just the small, accumulated treasury of things only the two of you know. The inside jokes. The shorthand. The look across a crowded room that means something only your spouse will catch. The story you only tell each other.

Intimacy is built out of shared private knowledge. A friendship without secrets is a thin friendship. The depth of a relationship is measured by how much exists between the two of you that exists nowhere else in the world.

Now ask yourself: what would it look like to have a relationship like this — with Allah?

[PAUSE]

This is what verses 16 and 17 are showing us together.

The worshippers in verse 16 are doing something that has no public dimension. Their sides leave their beds in the dark. No one knows. No one will retweet it. No one will remark on it. They are stripping every spectator away from their devotion — except One.

And Allah's response in verse 17 is symmetrical. He preserves the privacy of the exchange. He does not say *"I will reward them publicly so that everyone knows what I have done for them."* He says — **what is hidden for them.** The reward stays in the same key as the worship. Secret meets secret.

What is being built here is not a transaction. It is **intimacy.**

[PAUSE]

Think about what would be lost if this were a public reward.

Imagine a Paradise where, when you entered, you were told: *"Here is your reward, and here is the public record of why you received it. Every act of yours is now on display. Every other inhabitant of Paradise can read your file."*

Even the reward would be diminished. Because the worship was offered in private — as a gift to Allah alone — and if the reward becomes public, the original gift is exposed.

But Allah preserves what you preserved. You kept the worship between Him and you. He keeps the reward between Him and you. The secret you guarded for His sake — He guards in return for yours.

This is what *ihsān* — excellence in worship — actually means at its deepest. Worshipping as though you see Him, knowing He sees you. A relationship where the visibility runs only one direction: He sees, you do not see, and that asymmetry is itself the form of love.

[PAUSE]

There is an old saying among the early Muslims — *"Whoever does an act for Allah and conceals it, Allah will make it more luminous on the Day of Judgment than the sun."* This is not a contradiction of our ayah. It is its completion. The act is concealed in this life. The reward is concealed in this life. And then, on the Day, when the public record is read out — what was hidden becomes the brightest thing.

But notice: even then, the *content* of the reward — the *qurrat aʿyun* itself — remains hidden until it is *experienced.* The hiddenness is not just a delay. It is a property of the gift itself.

Some things cannot be told. They can only be lived. The settled eye is one of them.

[PAUSE]

**What does this change about how we live tomorrow?**

It changes what you do with the small, hidden acts.

There is a temptation, when you do something good that no one saw, to find some subtle way to make it known. To mention it in passing. To create the conditions under which someone will discover it. The nafs hungers for credit.

This ayah is an argument against that hunger. It says: *the value of the hidden act is in its hiddenness.* When you bring it into the light, you have already collected your wage. When you leave it in the dark, you have entrusted it to the One who hides things, and what He hides — He answers with something hidden, infinitely greater.

Tomorrow, when you do a small good that no one will see — pause before you mention it. The mention is the receipt being cashed early. The silence is the receipt being stored in the treasury that no soul knows.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: The Cool Eye in a Restless World

Now I want to turn toward the metaphor itself, because I believe it speaks to something happening in our moment.

The dominant condition of the modern human being is restlessness.

You can feel it in your own day. You wake up and reach for the phone before your eyes have fully opened. You scroll while you eat. You check while you walk. You watch one thing and then need to know what comes next. You arrive at a destination and immediately think about the next destination. You finish a project and immediately wonder what the next project is. You buy the thing and immediately want the upgrade.

Your eyes are *not cool.* Your eyes are *hot* — burning, searching, moving, scanning. There is no settled place.

The data confirms this. The most common modern complaint is some version of: *I have everything I'm supposed to want, and I am still not at rest.* Therapy intake forms catalogue it. Religious counselors hear it. Successful people whisper it.

And the diagnosis — beneath all the specific complaints — is the same: **the eye does not settle.**

[PAUSE]

The Quran's vocabulary for the goal of human existence is, in this ayah, *qurrat aʿyun.* It is not "success." Not "achievement." Not even "happiness," exactly. It is *the settling of the eye.* The end of motion. The arrival.

And it is hidden.

Now ask yourself something difficult. If the settled eye is hidden — *deliberately hidden, behind three layers of concealment* — what does that mean for your search for it on earth?

It means **you will not find it here in its complete form.**

You will catch glimpses. A moment with your child where time slows and your eyes rest on their face and the world quiets for a second. A moment in prostration where you forget to want anything. A moment with a beloved where neither of you needs to speak. These are *qurrat ʿayn* moments. They are real. They are foretastes.

But they are not the full meal. The full meal is hidden. It is in a place you cannot reach by working harder, by buying more, by achieving more, by acquiring the next thing.

[PAUSE]

This is the trap modernity sets for the human soul.

It says: *the settled eye is here. Buy this product. Take this trip. Reach this level. Acquire this title. And then your eyes will rest.*

And every human being who has tried this has discovered the same secret — that the moment of arrival is empty. The thing you wanted, when you finally have it, does not settle you. Within hours, sometimes minutes, the eye is moving again, scanning for the next horizon.

This is not a defect in your particular search. This is a feature of the world. The dunya is constitutionally incapable of settling the eye. It is not designed to. Allah did not design it to. He designed it as a place of motion, and He designed Paradise as the place of rest.

The ayah we are reading is telling you: *stop trying to find here what is hidden elsewhere.*

[PAUSE]

But notice what the ayah also implies.

The worshippers in verse 16 — the ones whose reward is *qurrat aʿyun* — they are not people who have given up on rest. They are people who **have already begun to taste it.**

In the dark, in the private prayer that no one sees, they have found a small foretaste. The hidden act produces a hidden joy in the heart that the rest of the day cannot quite name. Anyone who has prayed *tahajjud* knows this — the strange quiet that lives in your chest the rest of the day after you have whispered to Allah in the dark.

That quiet is *qurrat ʿayn* in miniature. It is a coupon. A handshake. A promise note.

And the full payment is hidden — *what no soul knows* — but the promise note is real. The early Muslims used to say of the night prayer: *its sweetness in the heart is unmistakable.* They were not making it up. They were drawing on the same vein that this ayah names.

[PAUSE]

**What does this change about how we live tomorrow?**

It changes where you look for rest.

The next time you feel the restless eye scanning for the next thing — the next purchase, the next achievement, the next horizon — recognize it for what it is. It is not a problem to be solved by the next thing. It is the constitutional condition of the soul in the dunya.

The temporary rest is available — in the prostration, in the early-morning prayer, in the moment of dhikr where you stop being aware of yourself. The full rest is hidden. The path to the full rest runs through the temporary rest. The cool eye on earth is the foretaste of the cool eye in Paradise.

Tomorrow, when the restlessness pulls you toward the phone, the email, the next thing — try, just once, to go in the other direction. Lower your gaze. Sit. Remember. Let the eye rest, for one minute, on the thought of Allah. That minute is a deposit on the hidden reward.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now look back at what we have walked through.

The ayah begins with **falā taʿlamu nafsun** — a universal negation that empties the category of created knowers. Then it pivots to **ukhfiya** — a passive verb that hides even the Hider. Then it lands on **qurrati aʿyun** — a metaphor that names the reward only by its effect, refusing to specify the cause.

Three layers of concealment, each more profound than the last.

And the entire structure is the divine response to verse 16 — to the worshippers whose sides detached from their beds in the dark. The hidden labor receives a hidden answer. The private worship receives a private reward. The effaced self receives an effaced gift.

The ayah is not describing Paradise. The ayah is **performing the relationship** between the slave who hides and the Lord who hides in return. The architecture is itself the meaning. Concealment is not the wrapping. Concealment is the gift.

---

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the thematic depths are the same insight from two directions.

The linguistic side told us: *every word in this ayah is doing the work of concealment.* The negation is universal. The verb is passive and intensive. The metaphor is unspecified. The grammar performs the hiddenness.

The thematic side told us: *every dimension of meaning in this ayah is the architecture of intimacy.* The hiddenness preserves the privacy of the worship. The unspecified joy preserves the singularity of the gift. The cool eye names the end of human restlessness — which is itself the deepest unspoken hunger of every modern person.

Both are the same insight. **What Allah hides, He hides on purpose. And what He hides on purpose for those who hid their worship for His sake — is something so vast, so singular, so personal — that no created soul can hold it in advance.**

---

### Questions to Carry

Sit with these — they are invitations, not assignments.

1. What is the act of worship in my life that has the smallest audience? When did I last do something good that no one knew about — and what did I do with the impulse to tell someone?

2. When I scan the past week for my restless moments — what was my eye searching for? And was I looking for that thing in a place where it can be found?

3. The worshippers in verse 16 paid the price of leaving their beds in the dark. What is my version of *detaching my side from the bed*? What is the small, costly, invisible labor I have not yet been willing to offer?

4. If the reward is hidden — even from prophets — what does my certainty about *what would make me happy* really reveal about my limits as a knower?

5. When I taste a moment of *qurrat ʿayn* — a settled eye, a quiet heart, an arrival — do I receive it as a foretaste? Or do I try to grasp it and own it, and so lose it?

---

### One-Sentence Distillation

What Allah deliberately hides for those who hide their worship for Him is a joy so personal and so vast that the very act of concealment is part of the gift.

---

### The Closing Invitation

There is a small shift this ayah invites.

It is not a shift of action. It is a shift of orientation. It is the willingness to stop looking for the settled eye in the wrong places, and the willingness to trust that what is hidden is being kept — by the One who hides things on purpose, with intention, with love.

You do not need to know what is behind the veil. You only need to know that the One who placed the veil placed it as an act of mercy, and that the gift behind the veil is shaped exactly to the shape of the worship you offered in the dark.

That is enough. That is more than enough. That is, in fact — *qurrati aʿyun* — already.

---

### Du'a

O Allah, You who hide what You give in proportion to how we hide what we offer — let the small acts we have hidden from the world be received by You in the silence of Your mercy.

Settle our eyes, even now, with a foretaste of what You have prepared. Let the restlessness that pulls our gaze across the dunya be quieted by Your remembrance, until the day You unveil for us what no soul has known.

And let us be among those whose sides leave their beds in the dark, calling on You in fear and hope — and whose hidden longing You answer with hidden joy.

Āmīn.
