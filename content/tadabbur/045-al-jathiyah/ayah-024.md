# Nothing But Time

## The Ayah

> **وَقَالُوا۟ مَا هِىَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَآ إِلَّا ٱلدَّهْرُ ۚ وَمَا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ ۖ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ**

*Wa qālū mā hiya illā ḥayātunā ad-dunyā, namūtu wa naḥyā, wa mā yuhlikunā illā ad-dahr. Wa mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm; in hum illā yaẓunnūn.*

**"And they say, 'There is nothing but our worldly life — we die and we live, and nothing destroys us except time.' But they have no knowledge of that; they are only guessing."**

---

## Introduction: Rebuilding the World

Before we open this ayah, I want you to feel where it sits.

Surah Al-Jathiyah is a surah of *signs*. Read the verses just before this one and you'll find Allah opening door after door onto the visible world. The heavens and the earth. The way you yourself are made. The animals scattered across the land. The night folding into the day. The rain that comes down and brings a dead earth back to green. After almost every one of these, the same refrain lands: **āyātun li-qawmin** — *signs, for a people.* Signs for a people who are certain. Signs for a people who reason. Signs for a people who use their minds.

So the surah has been building a case. It has been saying, over and over: *look, and you will see. The evidence is everywhere. Reality is signed.*

And then comes verse 24 — and it is the voice of the other side. It is the creed of the people who looked at all of that and said: **no.** It is the single, compact sentence that denies everything the surah has been pointing at.

Now here is what I need you to understand about *when* this was said. This was not a quiet philosophical murmur from a lecture hall. This was the confident, public, almost contemptuous reply of the people of Makkah to a Prophet ﷺ who was telling them: *you will be raised, you will stand, you will answer.* And they laughed. They said the thing that men have been saying in every century since: **"This is it. You live, you die, and that's the end. There's no one keeping score. There's no second act. Time takes us all — nothing more mysterious than that."**

Sit for a moment with how *modern* that sounds. You could hear this sentence, almost word for word, on a podcast tonight. "We're here for a blink. We're stardust. Entropy gets everyone in the end. Make the most of it."

The Quran did not treat this as a fringe idea to be ignored. It quoted it. It put the words in the text and preserved them for fourteen centuries. Why? Because Allah wanted us to look closely at *how* the sentence is built — not just what it claims, but the **shape** of the claiming.

And here is the hook. Here is the thing I want you hungry for:

The people in this ayah speak with total, airtight, three-times-repeated **certainty**. The grammar they use is the grammar of *absolutes*. And by the end of the same verse, Allah takes that exact same grammar — the very same construction — and turns it back on them like a mirror, to say something that should stop us cold.

[PAUSE]

Let's go find it.

# PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element One: The Grammar of "Nothing But" — *mā... illā*

Let me teach you one small structure first, because once you see it, you cannot un-see it in this ayah.

In Arabic, one of the strongest ways to make a claim is called **qaṣr** — restriction, or exclusivity. You build it with two pieces: a negation, and then an exception. **مَا** (*mā*, "not") followed by **إِلَّا** (*illā*, "except / but").

You do this in English too, but you feel it as emphatic. There's a difference between saying:

*"This is our life."*

and saying:

*"There is **nothing but** our life."*

The first is a statement. The second is a wall. It doesn't just assert something — it *forbids* the alternative. It slams every other door shut. "Nothing but." "Only." "Except for this — nothing."

That's *mā... illā*. It is the grammar of a closed universe.

Now watch what these people do with it. Watch how many times they reach for this wall in one short breath.

**First wall:**

> **مَا هِىَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا**
> *"It is **nothing but** our worldly life."*

**Second wall:**

> **وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَآ إِلَّا ٱلدَّهْرُ**
> *"**Nothing** destroys us **except** time."*

Two absolute statements, back to back, each one a locked door. *Nothing but this life. Nothing but time.* They are not offering an opinion. They are declaring the boundaries of all of reality. They have, in effect, drawn a box around existence and announced: *outside this box — nothing.*

Notice something about the confidence here. There is no "maybe." No "we think." No "as far as we can tell." The structure itself radiates certainty. These are people who feel they have *settled the question.* The case is closed. The matter is known.

[PAUSE]

Now stay with me, because here is where Allah does something extraordinary.

After they have built their two walls of "nothing but," Allah responds. And He does not respond with a different *kind* of sentence. He does not lecture them with new vocabulary. He reaches for **the very same construction they just used** and builds a third wall — except this time, the wall is around *them*:

> **إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ**
> *"They are **nothing but** guessing."*

Do you feel that? **إِنْ** (*in*) here is another negative particle — "not" — and then **إِلَّا** again — "but." *In hum illā* — "they are nothing but..." It is the identical exclusive structure. *Mā... illā. Mā... illā. In... illā.*

They said: "There is **nothing but** this life."
They said: "**Nothing** destroys us **except** time."
Allah says: "They are **nothing but** guessing."

[PAUSE]

The grammar is a mirror. They used the language of total certainty — the airtight, no-exceptions, case-closed construction — to make two enormous claims about the nature of all existence. And Allah takes that same airtight construction and uses it to define **them**: to say that the only thing that is *actually* total and exceptionless here is **their conjecture.**

They thought they were the ones drawing the box around reality. And Allah quietly draws a box around *them* — and the only thing inside it is a guess.

This is what this single construction reveals: **the form of certainty and the substance of certainty are not the same thing.** You can speak in absolutes and stand on nothing. The grammar of "I know" can sit on top of "I'm guessing," and from the outside the two sound identical. It is only when the mirror is held up that the difference shows.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## Element Two: *Ad-Dahr* — Naming the Destroyer, Leaving the Giver Unnamed

Now look very carefully at the middle of their claim, because there is an absence here that, once you see it, you will never read this verse the same way again.

They make two claims about life and death. Let's lay them side by side:

> **نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا** — *"We die and we live."*
> **وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَآ إِلَّا ٱلدَّهْرُ** — *"And nothing destroys us except time."*

I want you to ask one question of this. A simple question.

For *death* — for being destroyed — they name a cause. They say it explicitly: **ad-dahr.** Time. The aeon. The grinding of nights and days. "What ends us? *Time* ends us." They are very clear about the agent of destruction.

But for *life* — for **نَحْيَا**, "we live" — they name **no cause at all.**

[PAUSE]

Sit with that asymmetry. Because it is the whole tragedy of this worldview in a single grammatical gap.

They have a confident answer for *why things end.* Time. Entropy. The clock. The cause of death is fully accounted for. But they have *no account whatsoever* for why there is life in the first place. Life simply appears in their sentence — *naḥyā*, "we live" — bare, unexplained, a fact with no author. They will name the destroyer. They will not, and cannot, name the **Giver**.

This is the permanent signature of the materialist creed, and you can hear it today as clearly as in Makkah. The closed-universe worldview is *excellent* at explaining how things break down. Aging. Decay. The second law of thermodynamics. The heat death of the universe. It has a rich, confident vocabulary for *ending.* But ask it the other question — *why is there something rather than nothing? why is there life rather than lifeless matter? who gave the gift?* — and the sentence goes quiet. There is a *naḥyā* with no subject behind it.

The classical scholars caught something here too. Look at what they say about **نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا**. Al-Baghawī and al-Jalalayn explain it as: *the fathers die and the children live* — generations rolling over, an endless impersonal churn. Al-Qurṭubī notes a reading where the order is even reversed — *we live and we die* — pure cycle, with no beginning that needs explaining and no end that means anything. In every reading, the picture is the same: **a process with no Person behind it.** A wheel turning, with no hand on the wheel.

And *that* is what **ad-dahr** really is in this verse. It is not just "time." It is **time installed in the place where God should be.** It is the impersonal substitute for the personal Cause. They needed *something* to be the master of life and death — every human heart knows that life and death have a master — so they took the most faceless, blameless, unanswerable thing they could find. Time. You cannot pray to time. You cannot be judged by time. Time asks nothing of you. Time is the perfect god for someone who wants a god with no demands.

[PAUSE]

Now — I want to show you something, and I want to be careful and exact about its status, because it matters.

The Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah, the Most High, says — and this is a *ḥadīth qudsī*, authentically narrated in both Bukhārī and Muslim, a saying traced to the Prophet ﷺ in which he conveys the words of his Lord:

*"The son of Adam injures Me by cursing time (ad-dahr), for I am Time (ad-Dahr): in My hand is the command; I turn the night and the day."*

Listen to what that does to this ayah. These people said, *"Nothing destroys us except ad-dahr."* They thought they had named an impersonal force. And the answer comes back: **behind what you are calling "time," it is I.** The nights and days you blame — *I* am the one turning them. You reached for the most faceless thing in the universe to be your destroyer, precisely so there would be no one to answer to — and the One you were avoiding was standing exactly there, *in* the thing you named, the whole time.

There is nowhere to put the steering wheel where His hand is not already on it. They handed the controls to "time" to be rid of God. And God says: *time is in My hand.*

This is what the word *ad-dahr* reveals: **you can rename the Cause, but you cannot un-cause it.** Call it time, call it nature, call it chance — the moment you admit that *something* governs life and death, you have admitted a Governor. The only question left is whether you will give that Governor His name, or hide Him behind a faceless one so you never have to kneel.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## Element Three: *ʿIlm* Against *Ẓann* — Knowledge and the Guess Wearing Its Clothes

Now we reach Allah's verdict, and it turns entirely on two words. I want you to feel the gap between them, because the whole human drama lives in that gap.

> **وَمَا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ ۖ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ**
> *"They have no knowledge of that; they are nothing but guessing."*

Two words are at war here. **عِلْم** — *ʿilm.* And **ظَنّ** — *ẓann* (the verb *yaẓunnūn*).

**ʿIlm** is knowledge — real, grounded, established. Knowing a thing *as it is.* The kind of certainty that has a foundation under it.

**Ẓann** is its quieter, more dangerous cousin. *Ẓann* is supposition. Conjecture. The leaning of the mind toward something it has not verified. It is "I'm pretty sure" dressed up to look like "I know." It is the guess that has gotten comfortable and started to call itself a fact.

Now notice the architecture of Allah's response, because there are two precise blades in it.

**The first blade — مِنْ عِلْمٍ:**

Look at the little word **مِنْ** (*min*) before **عِلْمٍ** (*ʿilmin*, knowledge). In Arabic, when you place *min* before an indefinite noun inside a negation like this, it does something specific: it negates the **entire category.** Grammarians call it *min al-istighrāqiyyah* — the *min* of totality. It doesn't say "they don't have *much* knowledge." It says **they don't have so much as an atom of it.** Not a trace. Not a particle. The whole genus of knowledge is absent from their claim.

So before Allah even reaches the word *ẓann*, He has already swept the floor completely clean: *of knowledge — there is not a single grain in their hands.*

**The second blade — إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ:**

And now, into that swept-clean emptiness, the exclusive structure returns one final time. **In hum illā yaẓunnūn** — "they are *nothing but* guessing." Having removed every speck of *ʿilm*, Allah now states the *only* thing that *is* there: *ẓann.* Conjecture. And conjecture *exclusively* — nothing else.

[PAUSE]

Do you see the full shape of it now? Trace the whole arc of the verse:

They began with the grammar of total certainty — *nothing but this life, nothing but time.* They *sounded* like people standing on *ʿilm.* They had the *posture* of knowledge: the confident voice, the closed box, the case-is-settled tone.

And Allah dismantles it in two moves. First He empties the box — *not one grain of knowledge.* Then He names what was actually rattling around inside it the whole time — *nothing but a guess.*

[PAUSE]

Here is the thing that should unsettle every one of us, and I don't want us to escape it by pointing only at the people of Makkah. The terrifying lesson of these two words is this: **ʿilm and ẓann can sound identical from the inside.** The deniers were not *lying.* They were not consciously bluffing. They genuinely *felt* certain. Their *ẓann* felt, to them, exactly like *ʿilm.* That is the whole danger. A guess that has dressed itself in the clothes of knowledge does not feel like a guess. It feels like the obvious truth. It feels like "everyone knows this."

The single most important question this verse asks you is not "are you certain?" Certainty is cheap; these people had it in abundance. The question is: **"On what does your certainty stand? Is it ʿilm — or is it ẓann that has simply gotten loud?"**

That is what these two words reveal: **the loudness of a conviction tells you nothing about its foundation.** You can build your entire life on a guess, feel completely sure the whole way, speak in airtight absolutes — and be, in the eyes of the One who actually knows, *nothing but guessing.*

[PAUSE — major pause]

## Bridge

So look at what the construction has shown us. The grammar of "nothing but," used three times, became a mirror that turned the deniers' certainty back on themselves. The word *ad-dahr* showed us a Cause renamed but never un-caused — a destroyer named, a Giver hidden. And the gap between *ʿilm* and *ẓann* showed us that the volume of a belief is not the same as its foundation.

Now let's ask what all of this reveals — not just about the words, but about *us*, and about reality itself.

# PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme One: The Reduction — When You Make the Box Smaller Than the Truth

Let me start where you already live.

There is a feeling — and almost everyone in the modern world has felt it, including believers, in our weaker hours — that the universe is, fundamentally, *indifferent.* That we are a brief flicker of warmth between two long darknesses. That when the heart monitor flatlines, that's it: the lights go off and the room is empty. That the cosmos is vast, cold, and not *watching.*

This feeling has a kind of grim glamour to it. It feels brave. It feels grown-up. *I can look into the void and not flinch. I don't need a comforting story. I'll take the hard truth: we live, we die, time wins.* It even has its poetry — "we are stardust," "make the most of your one wild life."

Notice something: **this is the exact sentence of verse 24.** "There is nothing but our worldly life." Fourteen hundred years, and the creed has not changed a syllable. It has only changed costumes.

Now here is what the Quran does that I find breathtaking. It does not argue that this worldview is *sad.* It argues that it is **too small.** It is not that the deniers believed something dark — it is that they drew the box of reality *smaller than reality actually is*, and then mistook the walls of their box for the edges of the universe.

[PAUSE]

Think about what it takes to say *"there is nothing but this life."* Think about the sheer scope of that claim. To say "nothing but" — to use that exclusive *illā* — you would have to have *checked.* You would have to have surveyed the entire territory of existence, seen, completely, and confirmed that the unseen is empty. *"Nothing but"* is a claim of total knowledge. It is, secretly, one of the most arrogant sentences a human being can utter — because it claims to have mapped the whole of being and found the borders.

And that is precisely why Allah's answer is so devastatingly precise: **مَا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ** — *they have no knowledge of that.* You said "nothing but." But to say "nothing but," you would need to *know* the whole. And you don't. Nobody standing inside the box can see the box from the outside. You have mistaken *the limit of your seeing* for *the limit of what exists.*

This is the quiet con at the heart of every reductionism. *"It's just chemistry." "It's just neurons." "It's just time."* That little word "just" — that's the *illā* again. It is the move that shrinks the real down to the size of what the speaker happens to be able to measure. And then it presents that shrinking as humility — *I'm just being scientific* — when it is, in fact, the opposite. It is the boldest possible claim: *what I cannot see does not exist.*

The believer's posture is actually the *more* humble one, and we should reclaim that. The believer says: *I do not know the whole. I have seen signs — the heavens, the rain, the alternation of night and day, the fact that I exist at all — and the signs point beyond the box. I will not pretend the box is everything just because the box is all I can measure.* That is not credulity. That is refusing to mistake the edge of your instruments for the edge of the truth.

[PAUSE]

So what does this change about how you live tomorrow?

It changes what you do with the word **"just."** Watch for it — in others, and in your own heart. *It's just a coincidence. It's just biology. It's just how things are. This is just my life.* Every time that "just" appears, a box is being drawn, and the universe is being shrunk to fit inside it. The practice is simple and lifelong: when you feel reality being reduced to "nothing but," pause and ask — *am I describing reality, or am I describing the edge of what I can currently see?* Reality is not obligated to be as small as your instruments. Keep the box open.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## Theme Two: The Guess That Got Loud — Building a Life on *Ẓann*

Now I want to take the second theme somewhere closer to home, because it is easy to hear "they are only guessing" and feel safe. *That's them. The deniers. The materialists.* But the verse is doing something to *all* of us, and I don't want us to miss it.

Start here, in your own life. How many of your deepest, life-shaping convictions have you actually *verified* — and how many are *ẓann* that simply got loud enough, repeated enough, ambient enough, that they began to feel like *ʿilm*?

Think honestly. *I'm not the kind of person who could ever change. They'll never forgive me. I've left it too late. God is probably disappointed in me. Success will finally make me feel okay. I'll start being serious about my faith later — there's time.* Each of these *feels* like knowledge. Each is spoken, in the heart, with the airtight grammar of certainty — "there's *no way*," "it's *too* late," "I *always*." But trace them back to their foundation and you find... a guess. A *ẓann* you adopted somewhere, that hardened, that you stopped questioning, that now runs your life as if it were established fact.

This is the universal human condition the verse exposes: **we are all walking around inside conclusions we never actually examined, and we mistake our familiarity with them for evidence.** The denier of the Hereafter is just the largest, loudest example of a thing we *all* do in miniature, every day.

[PAUSE]

Now here is the movement I want you to follow, because the Quran is not just diagnosing — it is offering a way out.

The way out is not to swing to the opposite error — to become someone who *knows nothing* and floats in fashionable doubt. That is not what Allah praises. Look again: He does not condemn them for being *uncertain.* He condemns them for being *falsely certain* — for dressing *ẓann* in the robes of *ʿilm.* The sin is not the gap in their knowledge. The sin is **pretending the gap isn't there.**

So the cure is a kind of fierce honesty about your own foundations. It is the willingness to ask, of any conviction running your life: *what is this actually standing on?* And to be brave enough to say, when the answer is thin: *this is ẓann. I have been treating a guess like a fact.*

And then — this is the beautiful part — once you've named the guess as a guess, you can ask the question that actually leads somewhere: *where could I find ʿilm instead?* Where is there real ground? For the deniers, that ground was right in front of them the whole surah long — sign after sign after sign, the whole of Al-Jathiyah laid out like evidence on a table. They had access to *ʿilm.* They chose the comfortable *ẓann* because the *ʿilm* came with a demand attached: if the signs are real, then the Signer is real, and if the Signer is real, then *you will kneel.*

[PAUSE]

And that is the hidden engine of the whole verse, isn't it. Why would someone choose a *guess* over *knowledge* that is sitting right in front of them? Because the guess is *free* and the knowledge is *costly.* "There's nothing but this life, time takes us all" — that belief asks *nothing* of you. No prayer, no restraint, no accounting, no kneeling. But "there is a God who gives life and gives death, and you will stand before Him" — that *ʿilm* reorganizes your entire existence. So the human being, again and again, reaches for the cheaper *ẓann*, not because the evidence demanded it, but because the *implications* of the truth were too heavy to carry.

That is the quiet confession buried in *in hum illā yaẓunnūn.* They are not guessing because the evidence is absent. They are guessing because guessing lets them off the hook.

[PAUSE]

So what does this change about tomorrow?

Take one belief — just one — that is currently steering your life, and that you have never actually examined. Maybe it's about yourself. Maybe it's about whether change is possible. Maybe it's about God and whether He'd take you back. And ask it Allah's question: *do I have ʿilm of this — or am I only guessing?* And if it's a guess, ask the harder follow-up: *am I clinging to this guess because it's true, or because the truth would cost me something?* Then go looking for the actual ground. Open the surah. Read the signs. Don't let a guess that got loud keep running a life that deserves *ʿilm.*

[PAUSE — major pause]

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## The Architecture

Now stand back and look at the whole verse as a single built thing, because its structure is the proof of everything we've said.

It is built as a **mirror**, and the hinge is in the middle.

**The first half** is the human voice, full of false certainty, using the exclusive construction twice:
- *mā hiya illā ḥayātunā ad-dunyā* — "nothing but this life"
- *wa mā yuhlikunā illā ad-dahr* — "nothing but time"

**The second half** is the Divine voice, and it answers the false certainty in two exact moves:
- *wa mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm* — He empties the box: not one grain of knowledge
- *in hum illā yaẓunnūn* — He fills it with the truth, using *their own grammar*: "nothing but a guess"

They reached for the construction of absolute certainty three times. The first two times, *they* were the speakers, drawing a box around reality. The third time, *Allah* is the speaker — and the box closes around *them*, and inside it is only their conjecture. The form of the verse *is* its meaning: the grammar of certainty, held up as a mirror, reveals the emptiness behind their certainty.

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes turn out to be one insight seen from two directions.

From the side of *language*, we found: the construction of certainty is not the same as the substance of certainty; you can rename the Cause but you cannot un-cause it; the volume of a claim says nothing about its foundation.

From the side of *life*, we found: the materialist box is drawn smaller than reality, and the "just" that shrinks it is the boldest claim of all; and every one of us is at risk of building a life on *ẓann* that got loud, choosing the cheap guess over the costly knowledge because the truth would ask us to kneel.

They are the same truth. **A guess can wear the perfect grammar of knowledge — and the only thing that finally tells them apart is whether you were willing to pay what the knowledge costs.**

And the surah's own name seals it. They said *"nothing destroys us except time."* The surah is called **Al-Jathiyah** — *The Kneeling* — because of the verse that comes soon after: *"and you will see every nation kneeling."* The very thing they said could never happen — standing, answering, accounting — is written into the title of the chapter that records their denial. Time did not have the last word. The One whose hand turns the night and the day did.

## Questions to Carry

1. Where in my life do I say "nothing but" or "just" — and have I actually *checked*, or am I drawing a box and calling it the universe?
2. I am quick to name what *ends* things. Have I ever stopped to name, and thank, the One who *gives* — the unexplained *naḥyā*, the bare gift of being alive at all?
3. Which of my loudest, most certain beliefs about myself is actually *ẓann* that simply got familiar?
4. Is there a truth I am keeping at arm's length — not because the evidence is thin, but because accepting it would cost me a change I don't want to make?
5. If "time" is in His hand, what am I actually afraid of when I am afraid of time passing?

## One-Sentence Distillation

They wrapped an empty guess in the grammar of total certainty — naming time as their destroyer while leaving the Giver of their life unnamed — and Allah answered their "nothing but" with His own: *they are nothing but guessing.*

## The Closing Invitation

You are not being asked to manufacture certainty. You are being invited to do something quieter and braver: to stop mistaking the loudness of your convictions for their truth, and to go looking for *ʿilm* — real ground — even when that ground asks something of you. The signs are not hidden. They fill this surah; they fill your own existence. The only question is whether you'll let a comfortable guess keep the box closed, or whether you'll open it and let the truth back in, knees and all.

## A Brief Du'a

*O Allah, You who give life and give death, whose hand turns the night and the day — do not let me build my life on a guess and call it knowledge. Open the box I have drawn too small. Give me the courage to seek the ʿilm that costs me something, and the humility to kneel before the truth I cannot fully see.* Āmīn.
