# When Every Parable Has Been Brought

## Surah Ar-Rum, Ayah 58

**Arabic:**

> [ayah:30:58] وَلَقَدْ ضَرَبْنَا لِلنَّاسِ فِى هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍ ۚ وَلَئِن جِئْتَهُم بِـَٔايَةٍ لَّيَقُولَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا مُبْطِلُونَ

**Transliteration:**

*Wa laqad ḍarabnā lin-nāsi fī hādhā-l-qur'āni min kulli mathal(in). Wa la-in ji'tahum bi-āyatin la-yaqūlanna-lladhīna kafarū in antum illā mubṭilūn.*

**Translation:**

> And We have certainly struck for the people in this Qur'an from every kind of parable. But if you bring them a sign, those who disbelieve will surely say, "You are nothing but falsifiers."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are Standing

To understand this verse, you have to understand what has just happened in the surah leading up to it.

Surah Ar-Rum opens with something almost unbelievable in its time — a prediction. The Romans have been defeated, and Allah declares they will return victorious *within a few years*. That is the opening signal: **the Qur'an reads what no one else can read**. From there, the surah builds outward into a cathedral of signs.

He speaks of how He created you from dust. How He made spouses from your own selves so you could find tranquility. How He stretched languages and skin-tones across your species like deliberate brushstrokes. How sleep is a kind of dying, and waking is a kind of resurrection. How lightning is *both* fear and hope at once — the same flash. How rain falls on a corpse-earth and the earth answers green.

By the time we arrive at verse 58, the surah has been doing one thing relentlessly: **building a case.** Sign upon sign upon sign. Cosmic, biological, psychological, meteorological, agricultural, sociological. Every domain of human experience has been turned toward the heart and asked to speak.

And now — pause — Allah steps out from behind the signs and says something about them.

He says: *We have struck for the people, in this Qur'an, from every kind of parable.*

And then He says something that should stop you in your tracks. He says: **even so** — even after all of this, even after every parable, even after every sign — *if you bring them an āyah, those who reject will say: "You are nothing but falsifiers."*

[PAUSE]

### The Hook

Now here is what should make us lean forward.

This verse isn't describing the *beginning* of the argument. It's describing what happens *after the argument has been completed*. After the diversity of languages. After the night and the day. After the rain on the dead earth. After the Romans. After everything.

What this verse is naming is not a person who has *not yet seen* evidence. It is naming a person who has seen it all — and whose response is not to engage with the evidence but to attack the *messenger*.

Stay with that. Because if you have ever wondered why some hearts hear the Qur'an and weep, and others hear the same Qur'an and call it a forgery — this verse is about to tell us exactly what is happening. Not on the level of information.

On the level of *will*.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Ḍarabnā mathalan* — "We have struck a parable"

#### The everyday image first

In English, we say "give an example." We say "draw a parallel." We say "make a point." All of those are gentle, almost passive verbs. *Give. Draw. Make.*

Arabic does something different.

When Allah introduces a parable in the Qur'an, He almost always uses the verb **ḍaraba** — which in its plainest, most physical meaning is **to strike**. To hit. To drive something into place the way a carpenter drives a nail. The same root that describes a hammer-blow, a heartbeat, a tent-peg pounded into hard ground.

Think about that for a moment. Before we go anywhere theological, think about the *physical motion*.

A carpenter doesn't "place" a nail. A carpenter *strikes* it. And he strikes it because something in the wood is resisting. The wood doesn't *want* the nail. The nail goes in only because force has been applied through repeated, deliberate impact.

Now hear the verse again with that in your ear:

> **ḍarabnā** lin-nāsi fī hādhā-l-qur'āni min kulli mathal

*We have struck — for the people, in this Qur'an — from every kind of parable.*

[PAUSE]

#### Why this verb and not another

This is not Allah saying *we offered* parables, *we presented* parables, *we suggested* parables. He is telling us about the **force** with which a parable is meant to enter a heart.

A parable in the Qur'an is not decoration. A parable is a hammer-blow against something hard.

Notice what this tells us about the *intended audience* even before we get to the disbelievers. Allah is telling us that human hearts are not soft pine. Human hearts are not waiting, open, receptive. Human hearts have grain. They have knots. They have layers of varnish built up over years. To get something *in* — to drive a single truth deep enough that it actually changes the wood — requires *striking*.

So when He says *ḍarabnā mathalan* — "We have struck a parable" — He is acknowledging, gently, that the heart of the listener is *resistant terrain*.

#### The plural that hides a singular generosity

Now notice something else. He doesn't say *we struck a parable*. He says **min kulli mathal** — "from every kind of parable."

The grammatical word *min* here is what classical Arabic grammarians call **tabʿīḍiyyah** when it works this way — a "min" of partition, meaning *some-of*. *From every type.* He is saying: We have not limited Ourselves to one register. We have brought parables from every domain — agricultural parables, architectural parables, parables of trade, parables of fire and water, parables of the mosquito and the spider, parables of orphans and gardens and mountains.

Why?

Because hearts have different grains. A merchant's heart resists differently from a farmer's heart. A poet's heart resists differently from a soldier's heart. A philosopher's heart resists differently from a child's. So Allah, knowing this, **strikes** with *every kind* of parable — meeting each heart at its own seam, finding the one place where the wood is willing to let a nail in.

[PAUSE]

Sit with the mercy of that.

He did not say: I have given them *one* proof and they may take it or leave it. He said: I have hammered, from every angle, every kind of parable, into the dense wood of the human heart — because I want this to *land*.

#### What this single word reveals

So before we go any further: the verb *ḍarabnā* alone is doing massive work.

It tells us the Qur'an is not a passive offering on a table. It is a *driven thing* — a deliberate, repeated, varied, forceful, merciful campaign of impact aimed at the human heart. And it tells us that **the resistance is expected**. Built into the very vocabulary. Allah does not pretend the heart is soft. He picks up the hammer.

That changes everything about what follows.

### Element Two: *La-qad* and *la-yaqūlanna* — the architecture of certainty

#### Teaching the concept first

In Arabic, there is a family of small particles that exist for one purpose: **emphasis**. They are called *adawāt al-tawkīd* — instruments of stress. They are how the language *insists* on something.

Imagine you tell a friend, "I locked the door." Now imagine you tell them, "I *did* lock the door." Now imagine you tell them, "I *absolutely, definitely did* lock the door." Each version says the same factual thing. But the second and third versions are doing something the first isn't — they are anticipating, and pre-empting, a doubt.

You only say *I absolutely did lock the door* when you sense that someone might not believe you, or when you yourself need the statement to *hold* against pressure.

Arabic builds this directly into its grammar. It has stacked emphasis. You can put one stress particle on a sentence, or two, or three — and each layer announces: *this statement is meant to stand against resistance.*

#### Now look at the verse

Watch what Allah does:

**wa la-qad ḍarabnā** — at the start.

The *wa* is a connector. The *la* is an emphatic *lām*. The *qad* with a perfect-tense verb after an emphatic *lām* turns the verb into a declaration of *certainty* — "We have, **most certainly,** struck." That's already two layers of emphasis stacked on a single verb.

Now look at the second half:

**wa la-in ji'tahum bi-āyatin la-yaqūlanna-lladhīna kafarū**

The *la-in* is *if* preceded by the emphatic *lām* — meaning "and even if, definitely if." Then the verb *yaqūlanna* carries the heavy **nūn al-tawkīd** — the suffix of emphasis — which classical grammarians describe as the strongest form of verbal stress the language possesses. It is the difference between "they will say" and "**they will most surely say** — there is no possibility otherwise."

[PAUSE]

So the architecture of this verse — *grammatically* — is two pillars of certainty holding up a single arch.

**Pillar one:** *We have, most certainly, struck every kind of parable.*

**Pillar two:** *They will, most surely, say: "you are nothing but falsifiers."*

#### Why both pillars need that weight

Here is what is remarkable. The first pillar carries Allah's own action. *We struck. We did this.* It needs emphasis because Allah is testifying to the *thoroughness* of His own evidence. He is, in effect, closing the case for the defense: *do not let anyone say I did not bring proof. I brought every kind.*

The second pillar carries the disbeliever's response. *They will most surely say it.* It needs emphasis because Allah is testifying to the *predictability* of refusal. He is saying: *I know — with the certainty of the One who reads hearts — exactly what the response of the rejecter will be, even before the new sign arrives.*

So this verse is **two certainties facing each other.** The certainty of divine evidence. The certainty of human denial. And the symmetry of the grammatical weight on each side is not accidental. The verse is *built* like a balance — and it is showing us that the problem in this exchange is not on the evidence side of the scale.

The evidence side is *overflowing*.

The problem is on the other side.

[PAUSE]

#### Linguistic honesty

The grammatical fact is straightforward and uncontested: this verse is loaded with one of the densest emphasis structures in the Qur'an. *Laqad*, *la-in*, the *lām* of response, the *nūn al-tawkīd al-thaqīlah* — all stacked. What this *invites us to consider* — and here we step from grammar into reflection — is that Allah is using the maximum possible linguistic weight on a verse about the maximum possible human stubbornness. The form is matching the content. The language is *itself* a kind of hammer-blow.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: *Mubṭilūn* — "falsifiers"

#### Opening the word

Now we come to the word that ends the verse — and ends it with a sting.

**Mubṭilūn.** "Falsifiers."

This word comes from the root **b-ṭ-l**, which carries the core meaning of *something being void, empty, baseless, fraudulent, fake.* The famous Qur'anic phrase **jā'a al-ḥaqqu wa zahaqa al-bāṭil** — "the truth has come and the false has vanished" — uses the noun form of this same root. *Bāṭil* is what has no substance. What collapses when tested. What is hollow at the core.

But notice the *form* of the word here. It is not *bāṭilūn* — "false ones." It is **mubṭilūn** — and this is a form-IV active participle.

#### Why the form matters

In Arabic morphology, form IV is what grammarians call the **causative**. It takes a meaning and turns it *active*. It is the difference between *being asleep* and *making someone fall asleep*. Between *being correct* and *correcting someone*.

So *bāṭil* by itself means "false."
But *mubṭil* — form IV active participle — means **"one who makes things false."** *One who falsifies.* *One who actively produces falsehood.*

[PAUSE]

This is the accusation the disbelievers are throwing at the messengers. They are not merely saying: "what you brought is wrong." They are saying: "you are *manufacturers* of falsehood. You are *producers* of fraud. You are not mistaken — you are *deliberately fabricating*."

Stay with the venom of that.

After every parable has been struck. After the cosmic signs and the meteorological signs and the biological signs and the sociological signs. After Allah has demonstrated, from every angle, the truth of His message — the person whose heart is closed does not say: *I am still unsure*. Does not say: *I need more time*. Does not even say: *I disagree*.

They say: *you are deliberate falsifiers*.

#### The mirror that this word holds up

Here is what is stunning. The accusation **mubṭilūn** — falsifiers, manufacturers of fraud — is exactly what the disbelievers themselves are doing in the moment they say it.

They are *fabricating a charge of fabrication*.

The messengers brought the truth. Allah has just spent the entire surah documenting the evidence. And the response of the rejecter is to invert reality and call the messenger the very thing the rejecter is being.

Classical scholars noticed this. They speak of how disbelief, when it hardens, develops a kind of *projection* — *isqāṭ* in some commentaries — where the person fleeing the truth accuses the truth-bearer of the very vice they themselves are committing. It's not a modern observation; the Qur'an is naming it directly.

#### The restriction particle

And there is one more thing in this little phrase that we should not miss.

**in antum illā mubṭilūn.**

That *in... illā* construction is called **al-ḥaṣr** — restriction. It is grammatical exclusivity. It means: *you are nothing but...* / *you are only...* / *there is no other category you fit into except...*

So the rejecter doesn't say: "you sometimes get things wrong." Doesn't say: "you have a mistaken streak." Says: **you are only — and nothing else but — manufacturers of fraud.**

They have collapsed the messenger's entire identity into one accusation. There is no other dimension they will allow him to occupy. He is not a man. Not a relative. Not a neighbor. Not even a *mistaken* sincere person. He is *only* a fraud.

[PAUSE]

What this single word and its restriction reveal is the **final move** of a heart that has refused to be moved. When evidence has piled up to the ceiling, when every parable has been struck, when the case has been closed — the heart that will not yield does not negotiate with the evidence. It eliminates the messenger by collapsing him into a single, totalizing accusation.

[PAUSE — major pause]

---

### Bridge

So we have three linguistic discoveries.

*Ḍarabnā* — Allah does not place parables, He **strikes** them, with force, into resistant wood, from every angle, because He wants them to land.

*Laqad... la-yaqūlanna* — two pillars of grammatical certainty, one on each side. Certain evidence. Certain denial. The balance reveals where the problem actually lives.

*Mubṭilūn* — the rejecter's final move is not to engage the evidence but to invert reality and turn the messenger into a manufacturer of fraud.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself. About what is actually happening when a heart refuses to be moved by what should move it.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Refusal That Doesn't Need More Evidence

#### Begin where you already live

You know this experience.

You have been in an argument with someone — maybe a sibling, a parent, a friend — where you are *certain* you have laid out the facts. You have the timestamps. You have the receipts. You have walked them through it step by step. And at the end, instead of conceding, they say: *"You're just trying to make me look bad."*

You did not get any closer to resolution. In fact, you got further away. Because somewhere in the middle, the conversation stopped being about *the facts* and started being about *something else entirely* — something underneath the facts. Their pride. Their fear. Their sense of self.

This is what psychologists today call **motivated reasoning**. The discovery — confirmed across decades of research — that human beings do not, in general, gather facts and *then* form conclusions. We form conclusions, and *then* gather facts. When evidence arrives that supports our existing conclusion, we accept it. When evidence arrives that threatens our existing conclusion, our minds work harder — not to integrate the evidence, but to *neutralize* it.

[PAUSE]

#### Now hear the ayah

> *We have certainly struck for the people, in this Qur'an, from every kind of parable. But if you bring them a sign, those who disbelieve will surely say: "You are nothing but falsifiers."*

Allah is naming this exact dynamic — and naming it fourteen centuries before any psychology department in any university would discover it.

He is saying: *the issue is not the supply of evidence*. The supply is overwhelming. *Min kulli mathal* — from every kind. The issue is what happens when evidence meets a will that has already decided.

#### The grammar of *wa la-in ji'tahum bi-āyatin*

Notice the wording. Not *if you bring them another sign*. Not *if you bring them more proof*. Just **bi-āyatin** — "with a sign." Indefinite. Singular. *Any* sign.

The verse is saying: *it doesn't matter which one you bring*. Bring the sign of the splitting moon. Bring the sign of healing the leper. Bring the sign of the unlettered man reciting unmatched literature. Bring a new sign no one has seen yet. *The response is already written.*

This is the chilling part. The rejection is not waiting on the next piece of evidence. The rejection is *already in place*, and the evidence will be processed *through* the rejection.

#### What this means for our own hearts

It would be easy to read this verse from a comfortable distance, as if it describes only *those people, back then*. But the ayah is not narrow. The verse opens with **lin-nās** — "for the people." All of them. Humanity as such.

So the question this verse pushes back at us is: **where in my own heart have I done this?**

Where have I — before the evidence even arrived — already decided what the evidence would mean?

Maybe it is a fiqh question I would prefer to avoid. Maybe it is a relationship I would prefer to keep. Maybe it is a habit I would prefer to protect. Maybe it is a worldview I have spent twenty years building. And every time evidence arrives that threatens it, I do not engage the evidence. I find a reason to dismiss the *source*.

The verse is asking us to notice the dynamic before it becomes a habit. Because the people in this verse did not arrive at *mubṭilūn* in a single day. They arrived there by *practicing dismissal* — for years — until dismissal became their reflex.

#### The practical application

Tomorrow, when a piece of evidence arrives that threatens something you hold — a critique of a position you've defended, a Qur'anic passage that cuts against a habit you love, a story from someone whose worldview you've already decided about — try this: **wait three seconds before you respond.**

In those three seconds, ask: *am I about to engage what they said, or am I about to discredit the one who said it?*

Because the ayah is teaching us that the second move — the move to discredit the source — is the signature of a heart that does not want to be moved. And the first work of īmān is not adding new beliefs. It is **noticing when we are about to refuse**.

### Theme Two: The Burden of the Messenger, and the Mercy in This Verse

#### Begin where you already live

There is a particular kind of exhaustion that anyone who has ever tried to help someone they love will recognize.

You can see what is happening to them. You can see the pattern. You can see the cliff edge. You have tried — with patience, with stories, with examples from your own life, with the wisdom of older relatives, with verses, with hadith, with quiet conversations late at night. And nothing is landing. And eventually, you start to wonder: *is something wrong with me? Am I saying it wrong? If I just found the right angle, the right metaphor, the right sentence — would it finally break through?*

This is the silent wound of every parent, every teacher, every elder sibling, every daʿwah carrier, every imam — and yes, every prophet.

#### Now hear what Allah is doing in this verse

Place yourself in the Prophet's ﷺ position when this verse came down. Years into the message. Years of striking parable after parable. Years of laying out signs from every angle. And the rejection is not softening. The mockery is sharpening. The accusations are escalating.

What does the messenger naturally begin to feel? *Maybe I am failing. Maybe if I tried harder. Maybe if I were more eloquent. Maybe if I brought one more sign.*

And then this verse arrives.

> *We have struck — most certainly — from every kind of parable. And even if you bring them a sign, they will most surely say: "You are nothing but falsifiers."*

[PAUSE]

Read what Allah is *actually doing* here.

He is taking the weight off the messenger.

He is saying: **the case is not still open**. The case has been made. The parables have been struck — not by you, by Us. The evidence is sufficient — not because you decided so, because We decided so. And the rejection — when it comes — is not a verdict on your effort. It is a description of a heart that has already chosen.

#### The two pillars again, but seen differently

Remember the two pillars from the linguistic journey? *Laqad ḍarabnā* on one side. *La-yaqūlanna* on the other. Certainty of evidence. Certainty of denial.

Read those pillars now not as architecture, but as a *gift*. Allah is telling His Messenger ﷺ — and through him, every carrier of truth after him — *there is no missing parable you have failed to deliver. There is no perfect sentence you have failed to find. The evidence is complete. The response is what it is. You are not responsible for the response. You are responsible for the delivery.*

This is *raḥmah*. Mercy folded inside what looks, on the surface, like a verse about rejection.

#### The narrow door this opens for the listener too

There is a second mercy here, and it is for the listener — for us — not the messenger.

Because if the verse is saying *some hearts will refuse no matter what is brought*, the verse is *also* saying: **and some hearts will receive even a small sign — because they were never refusing.**

The same parable that one heart calls falsification, another heart hears and weeps. The same Qur'an that hardens one chest opens another. The signs do not change. The hearts do.

So if your heart, today, is one that is willing to be moved — even a little — by the words you are sitting with right now: that is not a small thing. That is a mercy that the people described in this verse have lost access to.

The fact that you are still asking questions of the Qur'an means the Qur'an still has access to you. The fact that you are sitting with an ayah and letting it work on you means you are not on the side of the pillar that says *mubṭilūn*.

[PAUSE]

#### The practical application

Two practices fall out of this theme.

**For when you are the messenger** — whether to a child, a student, a sibling, a parent, a friend: deliver the truth, deliver it well, deliver it kindly, and then **release the outcome**. The verse is your permission to do so. You are not God. You are not the one striking the parables. You are passing along what was struck. The response belongs to the heart it is striking against, and that is between that heart and Allah.

**For when you are the listener** — and you feel a verse, a sermon, a piece of advice landing in you in a way that *uncomfortable* — do not rush to dismiss. Do not jump to the *mubṭilūn* move. Sit with it for one extra minute than you would normally sit. Let the parable strike. Let the wood receive the nail. That extra minute — that single extra minute of *not refusing* — is the work of a lifetime.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Step back and look at the verse as a whole.

> *And We have certainly struck for the people, in this Qur'an, from every kind of parable.*
> *But if you bring them a sign, those who disbelieve will surely say: "You are nothing but falsifiers."*

It is built as a **balance**. Two halves. Two emphatic structures. Two pillars.

On one side: divine action, divine completeness, divine certainty. *We struck. Every kind. Certainly.*

On the other side: human refusal, human inversion, human totality. *They will say, surely. You are only. Nothing but. Fraudsters.*

And the verse is showing us, by its very symmetry, where the imbalance actually lives — not in the evidence, which is overflowing, but in the will, which has shut.

### Linguistic and Thematic Convergence

The two halves of our reflection — the words and the themes — are saying the same thing from different directions.

The verb *ḍaraba* told us Allah expected the wood to be hard. The theme of motivated reasoning told us why the wood is hard.

The emphasis particles told us the verse is built to stand against denial. The theme of the messenger's burden told us why such standing is needed.

The word *mubṭilūn* told us what the final move of refusal looks like. The mercy in the verse told us what the alternative — the heart that does not make that move — gets to receive.

One insight, two directions.

### Questions to Carry

1. Where in my life have I already decided the conclusion, and where am I now just gathering evidence to defend it?

2. When I encounter a critique of something I hold dear — a Qur'anic challenge to a habit, a perspective from someone I disagree with — is my first move to *engage* what they said, or to *discredit* who said it?

3. If I am someone who carries the truth to others — as a parent, teacher, friend, imam — am I taking responsibility for *the delivery*, or am I trying to take responsibility for *the response*? And what would change if I let go of the second?

4. The verse names a heart that has hardened over time. What small dismissals am I practicing today that, repeated for twenty years, would harden mine?

5. The same Qur'an that produced *mubṭilūn* in some hearts produced tears in others. What is in my heart today that lets a parable strike, or refuses it?

### The One-Sentence Distillation

*Allah has not stopped striking parables — and the only thing standing between any of us and being moved by them is what we have decided, in advance, to refuse.*

### The Closing Invitation

The invitation of this verse is not to be smug about the *mubṭilūn* of someone else's tongue.

The invitation is to notice that the move they made — the move to dismiss the messenger so we don't have to deal with the message — is a move *available to every human heart*. Including mine. Including yours. And the verse is asking us, with the maximum grammatical weight the language possesses, to **not make that move today.**

When the next parable strikes — and one will, today, before this day is over — let the wood receive the nail.

### Du'ā

O Allah, You have struck for us, in this Qur'an, from every kind of parable. Do not let our hearts be the wood that refuses every nail. Soften the grain. Let what You strike, land. And when we carry Your message to others, let us carry it well, and then let us release the outcome to You — the way Your Messenger ﷺ was taught to release it. Āmīn.
