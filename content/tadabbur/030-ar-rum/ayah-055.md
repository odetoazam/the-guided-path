# The Oath of the Deniers — When Lying Becomes Reflex

## The Ayah

**Arabic:**
وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ يُقْسِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ مَا لَبِثُوا۟ غَيْرَ سَاعَةٍ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ كَانُوا۟ يُؤْفَكُونَ

**Transliteration:**
Wa yawma taqūmu as-sāʿatu yuqsimu al-mujrimūna mā labithū ghayra sāʿah, kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn.

**Translation:**
"And on the Day the Hour stands, the criminals will swear they did not linger more than an hour. That is how they used to be deluded."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are in the Surah

Surah Ar-Rum is a surah of signs.

Have you noticed? It's been building this case the whole way through. The Romans were defeated, and they will be victorious. That's a sign. Look at the heavens and the earth — sign. Look at how He created you from dust, then from a drop — sign. Look at your spouse, the tranquility He placed between you — sign. Look at sleep, look at lightning, look at rain falling on land that was dead and waking it back up — sign, sign, sign.

The surah keeps using one phrase: **"wa min āyātihi"** — "and among His signs." Over and over. It's almost like Allah is walking us through a gallery, pointing at exhibit after exhibit, asking: what more do you need?

And the underlying question this whole surah has been asking, beneath every sign, is one question:

**Can the One who started this — start it again?**

Because everything in this surah is a rehearsal for resurrection. Dead land becoming alive — that's a rehearsal. Sleep and waking — that's a rehearsal. Sperm becoming a person — that's a rehearsal. The whole surah is whispering: He's already done this. He's doing it right now. In front of your eyes. Every day.

### The Moment We're Entering

Now, near the end, the surah does something different.

It stops showing us signs from this world. It opens a door into the next.

And what we see when the door opens is not what you might expect. We don't see fire first. We don't see scales first. We don't see the bridge first.

We see **a courtroom scene where the defendants, the moment they stand up, start lying.**

[PAUSE]

That's the picture. The Hour has come. The earth is shaking. The trumpet has sounded. The dead have come out of their graves. And the very first thing the criminals do — the very first reflex — is to swear an oath. Not to confess. Not to weep. Not to fall silent.

To swear.

About what?

About **how long they were dead**.

### The Hook

Stay with me here. Allah is going to tell us, in twelve words, something so strange that we need to slow down before we even begin.

He's going to tell us that on the Day the universe ends, the first words the deniers will speak are a lie. And then He's going to use one single word twice — in two completely different meanings — to show us exactly *why* they're lying. And then He's going to end the ayah by saying something that should stop us cold:

**This wasn't new behavior. This is what they were already doing.**

The lie on the Day of Judgment is not a new lie. It's the *same lie* they were telling their whole lives. They just didn't know they were telling it.

Let's open this carefully.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Word That Means Two Things

Before we go into the ayah, I want you to think about a word in English. The word "minute."

A *minute* of time — sixty seconds. A *minute* detail — something tiny. Same spelling, two meanings. In English, we usually consider this a coincidence, a quirk of the language.

In Arabic, when the same word appears twice in the same ayah with two different meanings — that's never a coincidence. That's architecture. That's Allah turning a word around so you can see both faces of it.

Now look at the ayah. Look carefully.

**"Wa yawma taqūmu as-sāʿatu yuqsimu al-mujrimūna mā labithū ghayra sāʿah."**

Did you catch it?

**As-sāʿah... ghayra sāʿah.**

The same word. Twice. Once at the beginning, once at the end. And it means two different things.

[PAUSE]

The first **as-sāʿah** — "the Hour." Capital H. This is the technical Quranic term for the Day of Judgment, the moment when the universe folds up. It comes from the root س-و-ع, and in classical Arabic it refers to a portion of time, but when Allah says **"as-sāʿah"** with the definite article in this context, it means **the** Hour. The end of everything.

The second **sāʿah** — no definite article. Just an indefinite, ordinary "hour." An hour of clock time. A short period.

Now feel what Allah is doing.

The deniers stand up on the Day of **the Hour**. And they swear: "We didn't linger more than **an hour**."

The same word. Used by Allah at the cosmic scale. Used by them at the human scale.

It's as if Allah is saying: *they cannot even perceive what's happening to them at the right register.* The Hour — the great, all-encompassing, time-ending Hour — has arrived. And the only frame they have to describe their experience is the small hour they were used to in dunya.

Their vocabulary is too small for what just happened to them.

[PAUSE]

Here's what's remarkable. This wordplay isn't a poet's trick. It's a diagnosis.

The human mind, when confronted with infinity, **shrinks it to fit**. We cannot hold what's actually happening, so we reach for the closest familiar word. The deniers in their graves, then standing on the plain of resurrection, reach for the only "hour" they know — the hour on a sundial, the hour between meals, the hour they used to fill with distraction. *That's* the hour they bring to this moment.

And Allah, by using **the same word twice**, is showing us the gap. The gap between what's actually happening (**as-sāʿah** — the Hour) and what they can perceive happening (**sāʿah** — just an hour).

Their perception was the problem in dunya. It's still the problem on the Day.

### Element Two: The Verb of Swearing

Now look at what they *do* in this moment.

**"Yuqsimu al-mujrimūna"** — the criminals swear.

The verb **yuqsimu** comes from the root ق-س-م (q-s-m), and in Form IV, the form Allah uses here, it carries the meaning of *taking an oath*, *swearing an emphatic oath*. Not just saying something. Not just claiming something. **Swearing it.**

Now here's what I want you to notice. The classical grammarians point out something about Form IV verbs — they often carry the sense of *making* something happen, *causing* it, *bringing it into being*. To "take an oath" is not a passive thing. It is an active, deliberate, intentional act of binding your word.

Let's sit with this.

The Hour has just started. The earth is splitting. The heavens are folding. And the criminals, in this moment, **deliberately, actively, intentionally** bind themselves to a falsehood.

Why?

Why would anyone, standing inside the ending of the universe, choose **swearing** as their first act?

[PAUSE]

Here is something worth pausing on. Notice what Allah did *not* say.

He did not say "yaqūlu al-mujrimūn" — the criminals will *say*.
He did not say "yazʿumu al-mujrimūn" — the criminals will *claim*.
He did not say "yaḥsibu al-mujrimūn" — the criminals will *reckon*.

He said **yuqsimu** — they will **swear an oath**.

There is a particular psychology to swearing. We swear when we feel we won't be believed. We swear when we're trying to convince. We swear when our ordinary words feel insufficient. An honest person stating an obvious fact does not, generally, need to swear. The verb of oath-taking is reserved for moments when there's resistance — when we're pushing against being doubted.

So picture this. The deniers stand up. There's nobody arguing with them yet. No questioning has started. No charges have been read. They have not been accused of anything.

**And the first thing out of their mouths is a defensive oath.**

It's the reflex of a guilty man entering an interrogation room — before any question is asked, he starts swearing. "I swear to God, I wasn't even there. I swear I was only there for an hour."

The oath gives them away.

Their oath is the confession.

### Element Three: The Word That Names Them

Now look at the word Allah uses for them.

**Al-mujrimūn** — "the criminals." From the root ج-ر-م (j-r-m).

In classical Arabic, **jarama** carries the sense of *cutting off*, *severing*, and from there, *committing a serious act* — a crime, a transgression. Lisān al-ʿArab notes that **jurm** is what cuts a person off from their proper place. It's not the word for a small mistake. It's the word for the kind of action that severs you from where you should have been.

So when Allah calls them **al-mujrimūn**, He's not just calling them "wrongdoers" in a general sense. He's identifying them by what their actions did to them: their actions **cut them off**. They are the people-who-have-been-severed.

Severed from what?

Severed from the very signs the surah has been showing us this whole time. They walked through a gallery of signs, in marriage and sleep and rain and language and lightning, and at every single sign, they cut themselves off.

[PAUSE]

Now hear the ayah again with this in your ear:

**"On the Day the Hour stands, the severed-ones will swear..."**

Of course they swear. They've been severed from reality. The only thing they have left is performance. Their oath is a performance because their whole life was a performance — a performance of certainty about a story they were inventing.

### Element Four: The Verb That Indicts Them — Kadhālika Kānū Yuʾfakūn

And now the ayah ends with the line that should stop us cold.

**Kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn.**
"That is how they used to be deluded."

Let's open this slowly.

**Kadhālika** means "thus" or "like that." It's a demonstrative that points back at what was just described. So Allah is saying: "That — that exact thing you just saw them do, the swearing, the lying about how long they were dead — *that* is how they used to be..."

To be what?

**Yuʾfakūn.** From the root ء-ف-ك (ʾ-f-k). Now this is one of the most striking roots in the Quran, and I want us to handle it carefully because almost everything depends on it.

The root **ʾ-f-k** in classical Arabic carries a specific image: **turning something away from its proper direction**. Lisān al-ʿArab and the Mufradāt of al-Rāghib are explicit on this. **Ifk** is not just any lie. **Ifk** is the lie that **flips** something. It takes a thing that's pointing one way and turns it the other way.

And **yuʾfakūn** is in the passive voice. They are **being turned**. **Being flipped**. Not just turning themselves — being turned.

[PAUSE]

Stay with me here. This is everything.

The deniers, on the Day of the Hour, swear they were dead for only an hour. They're wrong. They were dead for centuries. Some of them for millennia. The earth covered their bones and forgot their names and rebuilt cities on top of their graves.

But in their experience, it felt like an hour.

Allah says: **kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn** — "that is how they used to be flipped."

Their perception of time on the Day of Judgment — the perception that says "this huge thing is small, this long thing was short, this catastrophic thing was minor" — **that's the same perception they had in dunya.**

In dunya, they were being flipped, too. They looked at the signs in the heavens and saw nothing. They looked at marriage and saw biology. They looked at rain on dead land and saw weather. They looked at their own death approaching and felt it was far away.

**The flipping was already happening.**

On the Day of Judgment, the same disposition just continues. The mind that minimized the signs of Allah in dunya now minimizes the duration of death itself. The mind that turned the big things into small things now turns the cosmic Hour into an ordinary hour.

[PAUSE]

And here is the part that should make us tremble.

The verb is **passive**. **Yuʾfakūn.** They were *being* turned. Not just turning themselves. Being turned by something. Being flipped by something.

By what?

The classical commentators read this with care. The flipping has a *cause* — and it is themselves. Their own choices. Their own preferences. Their own appetite for the small over the great, for the immediate over the eternal, for the comfortable lie over the difficult truth. Every choice they made was a small flip. Every flip strengthened the disposition to flip again. By the end of their lives, they were no longer making decisions — they were being *carried* by the disposition they had built.

That's why the passive voice is so terrifying. By the end, **they aren't even doing the flipping anymore.** The flipping is doing them.

### The Bridge

So look at what these four words have shown us.

**As-sāʿah / sāʿah** — their vocabulary is too small for what's happening.
**Yuqsimu** — they swear before they're even accused, because lying has become reflex.
**Al-mujrimūn** — they have been severed from reality by their own actions.
**Kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn** — and none of this is new. The flipping was their whole life. Today is just the last frame.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Person You're Becoming Is The Person You'll Wake Up As

Have you ever noticed how a person who lies frequently begins to lie automatically?

There's a moment, early in a life of small dishonesty, where the lie still costs something. You feel the pause before it. You feel the slight friction. You feel the internal note: *that wasn't true.*

But over time, the friction wears down. The pause shortens. Eventually, the lie comes out before the truth does. The mouth has been trained. The mind, instead of reaching for the truth and then deciding to bend it, reaches directly for the bent version. The bending has become the first thought.

This is something psychologists describe in studies of habit formation. A behavior, repeated enough, no longer requires conscious choice. It moves from deliberate to automatic. From "I decide to" to "I just do." And once a behavior is automatic, it surfaces in moments of stress — when there's no time to think. When the body is overwhelmed and the mind reaches for its default.

Now hold that picture, and look at the ayah again.

The Hour has come. The maximum-stress moment in the history of the universe. There is no greater overwhelm than the standing-up of the dead. And in *that* moment, what surfaces?

**An oath.**

A lie.

Why? Because that's what was *trained*. That's the default. That's what comes up automatically when there's no time to construct anything. The criminals don't *decide* to lie on the Day of Judgment. **They wake up already lying.**

[PAUSE]

This is one of the most sobering teachings in the Quran. Not because it's harsh — but because it's *honest*.

Allah is telling us: the disposition you build now is the disposition you'll wake up as. Death is not a transformation. Death is an unveiling. Whatever was being built in you, in private, in the quiet repetition of small choices, **that's what walks out of the grave.**

The person who spent a lifetime training the muscle of "I didn't do it" walks out of the grave still flexing that muscle.

The person who spent a lifetime training the muscle of "subḥān Allah, that's a sign" walks out of the grave still flexing *that* muscle.

The Day of Judgment is not where character is determined. The Day of Judgment is where character is **revealed**. The character is being made right now.

### Where This Lands

If this is true — and the ayah insists that it is — then the most consequential thing in our day is not the dramatic decision. It's the boring one.

The small honesty when nobody would catch the lie.
The small admission of fault when you could have pivoted.
The small "I don't know" when "I know" would have made you look better.
The small "subḥān Allah" when you saw something beautiful and could have just scrolled past.

These small moments are not small. They are the studio where the next-life version of you is being assembled. Every honest moment lays down a thread in the disposition you'll wake up as. Every dishonest moment lays down a thread in the disposition the ayah is describing.

And here's the practical change. We tend to think: *I'll get serious before I die. I'll change at the end. When the time comes I'll be ready.*

The ayah closes this door gently and firmly. **There is no "rising to the occasion" on the Day of Judgment.** You will rise as you have lain. The trumpet does not summon hidden reserves of honesty that you never used. It summons whatever you actually were.

The work is now. The work is the boring small honesty. The work is the small refusal to flip a thing in your head from what it actually is.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: The Compression of Time Reveals What You Were Living For

Look back at the ayah one more time.

**"Mā labithū ghayra sāʿah"** — "they did not linger more than an hour."

They were dead, in some cases, for thousands of years. And the experience compressed to "an hour."

Now here's a question we don't often ask: **why does time compress?**

You already know the answer from your own life. Think about it.

A vacation flies by. A long workday lasted forever. A conversation with someone you love passed in minutes. A traffic jam took an eternity.

Time compresses or expands based on what we *invested* in it. The hours we filled with things we cared about, things we were present for, things we loved — those hours feel short in retrospect, because they were *full*. There was so much living packed in that the duration barely registers.

The hours we filled with distraction, with absent-mindedness, with going-through-the-motions — those hours feel long *during* and **completely empty** in retrospect. You can't remember Tuesday afternoon because there was nothing there to remember.

[PAUSE]

Now apply that to the ayah.

The deniers spent their lives — their entire lives — accumulating *nothing*. Not money. Money was there. Not pleasure. Pleasure was there. But nothing that *registers in the soul*. Nothing that the soul could carry across the threshold of death.

So when they wake up, and they try to count what they did with all that time — there is nothing to count. The decades of their lives compress to an hour because **an hour is roughly how much actual living happened**. The rest was filler.

This is one of the most devastating implications of the ayah, and it sits underneath the words quietly.

**The compression of time is the testimony of how empty the time was.**

A believer who spent their life in remembrance, in service, in the small honesties that the previous theme described — that believer might still feel the time was short. But the time was *full*. And in another ayah (10:45), Allah says something similar even about believers — that the day of resurrection feels like "an hour of a day." Even the people who used their time well feel the compression, because the next life is so much larger that everything before it shrinks.

But there is a difference. The believer's "hour" is full of substance. They wake up *having lived*. The criminal's "hour" is full of nothing. They wake up *having existed* — but not having lived.

### Where This Lands

Here is the question this leaves us with, and I'll say it plainly:

**If your life ended right now, and you had to swear how long you had lived — not been alive, but actually lived — what number would you swear to?**

Not how many years on a calendar. How many years that were *full*. How many years where the soul was awake. How many years that the soul could carry forward.

For most of us, the honest answer is shorter than we'd like. And the gap between the calendar years and the *full* years is the gap the ayah is pointing at. The deniers' gap is enormous — decades reduced to an hour. Our gap is smaller, but it's there. And every day either widens it or narrows it.

The practical shift is this. Instead of asking *how much time do I have left*, ask *how much of what I have is full*. A small life that is full is longer, in the only register that matters, than a long life that is empty. The deniers had long lives. They will swear to an hour. That is the verdict on a long, empty life. Allah is showing it to us so we don't have to live it to find out.

[PAUSE]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### How The Pieces Fit Together

Look at what we've seen.

Allah uses one word, **sāʿah**, twice — once for the cosmic Hour, once for an ordinary hour — and the gap between those two uses *is* the diagnosis. The deniers cannot perceive the great because their whole life was the practice of shrinking the great into the small.

He says they will **swear** — and the oath is the confession. You don't swear when you're confident; you swear when you're trying to push back against a truth you know is closing in.

He calls them **al-mujrimūn** — the severed-ones — and names them by what their own actions did to them.

And He closes with **kadhālika kānū yuʾfakūn** — "thus they used to be flipped" — revealing that the lie on the Day of Judgment is not new. It's the continuation of a disposition. They were being flipped their whole lives. On the Day of Judgment, the flipping just keeps going.

The linguistic insight and the thematic insight are the same insight from two directions:

**You will wake up as the person you trained yourself to be.** And the training is happening right now, in the small moments, in the small honesties or the small flips. The cosmic Hour does not change the character of the moment we are living in. It reveals it.

### Questions to Carry

1. What is one small flip — one small turning of something away from what it actually is — that has become automatic in my day? Where does my mouth or my mind reach for the bent version before the true one?

2. If I were standing on the plain of resurrection right now, and asked to swear how long I had actually *lived*, what number would I say? And where is the gap between that number and my calendar age?

3. The deniers' oath was reflexive — it came out before they thought. What is *my* reflex when I'm cornered or stressed? Is it honesty? Or is it a smaller, bent version of what's true?

4. The signs throughout Surah Ar-Rum — marriage, sleep, rain, language — what's one sign I walk past every day without seeing? What would change if I started seeing it?

5. If the disposition I'm building today is the one I'll wake up as, what is one small honesty I can practice today that I usually skip?

### One-Sentence Distillation

The criminals' lie on the Day of Judgment is not a new lie — it is the final frame of a lifetime spent flipping the great into the small, and Allah is showing it to us now so we can stop the flipping before it becomes the only thing we know how to do.

### The Closing Invitation

This ayah is not, in the end, about *them*. It's about the disposition. And the disposition is something we can still affect.

Tomorrow, when you catch yourself about to shrink something — a duty, a sign, an honesty, a relationship — pause. Just for a second. Let yourself see the gap between what it actually is and what you were about to turn it into. That second of seeing is the opposite of being flipped. That second is where the alternative life is built.

You don't have to be dramatic about it. You don't have to do something heroic. Just notice the flip, and refuse it once.

Then again the next day.

That is how the disposition the ayah warns us about gets dismantled — one refusal at a time.

### Brief Du'a

Yā Allah, You have shown us the lie that comes out automatically on the Day the Hour stands. Do not let us be among those who built that lie in private. Train our tongues toward the truth in the small moments so that when the great moment comes, the truth is what surfaces. Do not let us be flipped. Keep us facing You.

Āmīn.
