# The Question They Never Asked Themselves

## Surah Ar-Rum, Ayah 8

### Arabic

> أَوَلَمْ يَتَفَكَّرُوا فِي أَنفُسِهِم ۗ مَّا خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَآ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى ۗ وَإِنَّ كَثِيرًۭا مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِلِقَآئِ رَبِّهِمْ لَكَـٰفِرُونَ

### Transliteration

> *Awalam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim? Mā khalaqa Allāhu as-samāwāti wal-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā bil-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā. Wa inna kathīran min an-nāsi bi-liqā'i rabbihim la-kāfirūn.*

### Translation

> "Have they not reflected within themselves? Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them except in truth and for an appointed term. Yet indeed, many of the people, in the meeting with their Lord, are disbelievers."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are in the Surah

To understand this ayah, you have to understand what just happened.

Surah Ar-Rum begins with one of the most astonishing openings in the Quran. The Byzantine Empire — the great Christian superpower on Arabia's northern horizon — has just been crushed by the Persians. The pagans of Mecca are celebrating. To them, this is cosmic confirmation: the idol-worshippers (Persia) have defeated the people of the Book (Byzantium). The Muslims are demoralized. The "people closest to monotheism" have just been humiliated.

And in this moment, the Quran does something audacious. It announces: *the Byzantines will return. Within a few years. And on that day, the believers will rejoice.*

A prophecy. Made publicly. About a defeated empire. From inside a city where Muhammad ﷺ and his followers are themselves persecuted, marginalized, mocked.

Then the surah pivots. It doesn't linger on the empires. It asks a question that has nothing to do with Persia or Byzantium on the surface — and everything to do with them underneath.

> *Awalam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim?*
>
> *Have they not reflected within themselves?*

### The Pivot

[PAUSE]

This is the hinge of the entire surah.

The Meccans were so busy reading the surface of geopolitics — which empire is rising, which is falling, who is winning, who is losing — that they could not read the surface of *themselves*. They could not see that the cosmos they lived inside was not random. They could not see that they themselves, the very bodies they were walking around in, were evidence.

The surah is essentially saying: you are looking at empires and missing the universe. You are reading headlines and missing your own pulse.

### The Hook

Listen carefully to what this ayah does. It asks a question — but it doesn't tell us what answer that question would have produced. It tells us instead what Allah created the heavens and the earth *for*. And then it ends with a verdict: *many people, in the meeting with their Lord, are deniers.*

Three things side by side. A question. A purpose. A denial.

Why are they next to each other? What is the link between reflecting within yourself, the truth that the cosmos was built on, and the meeting that most people refuse to believe in?

That link is what we're going to spend this hour uncovering. And by the end of this reflection, I want you to feel something you may not have felt before: that the question Allah is asking is not about the universe at all. It is about you.

[PAUSE]

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We are going to open three doors in this ayah. The first is a question. The second is a location. The third is a noun that ties a knot the listener cannot untie without facing their own death.

---

### Element One: *Awalam yatafakkarū* — The Question That Is Not Really a Question

#### The Concept First

In every language, questions can do two different things.

Sometimes a question is a real request for information. *What time is it? Where did you put my keys?* The asker doesn't know and wants to know.

But sometimes a question is something else entirely. It is the form of a question doing the work of an accusation, a rebuke, or a wake-up. *Did you really think nobody would notice? Have you ever even tried? Don't you have a brain?*

Notice what these questions share. The asker already knows the answer. The listener also knows the answer. The question is not asking for information. It is forcing the listener to *confront* what they already know. It is putting them face to face with something they have been avoiding.

Arabic grammarians have a name for this. They call it *istifhām inkārī* — interrogation by way of denunciation. A question whose purpose is to expose, not to inquire.

Hold that idea. We will come back to it.

#### Opening the Word

Now look at what Allah opens the ayah with:

> *Awalam yatafakkarū*
>
> *Have they not reflected?*

That little particle at the beginning — *a-* — is the Arabic interrogative. And the *lam* that follows is a negation. *A* + *lam* together literally form the construction: *Did they not...?*

This is the same form as: *Did you not see what your Lord did to the people of the elephant?* It is the same form as: *Have they not traveled in the land?*

It is never a request for information. It is always a confrontation with something the listener should have seen, should have done, should have known — and didn't.

[PAUSE]

Notice something. Allah is not asking the Meccans, *"Did you reflect within yourselves and reach the wrong conclusion?"* He is asking, *"Did you not reflect at all?"*

The accusation is not that they thought badly. The accusation is that **they never thought about it**.

#### Going Deeper Into the Verb

Now the verb itself: *yatafakkarū*. From the root *f-k-r*.

The basic root carries the meaning of mental motion — turning something over in the mind, going back and forth across it, examining its sides.

But Allah did not use the simple form. He used Form V — *tafakkara*. In Arabic morphology, Form V often carries a sense of *taking on*, *adopting*, *engaging with effort*. It is reflexive. It is sustained. It is not a passing thought.

*Fakkara* in the basic sense would be *to think*. *Tafakkara* is closer to *to keep thinking. To do the work of thinking. To dwell.*

So the question is not, *Did the thought ever cross their minds?* The question is, *Did they ever sit with it? Did they ever do the labor of looking?*

[PAUSE]

Let's sit with this.

A passing thought about your own existence is not what Allah is asking about. Everyone has those. They flicker through the mind in traffic, in the shower, on the edge of sleep — *what am I doing with my life, what is this all for, what happens when I die* — and then the moment passes, and we return to our phones, our errands, our anxieties about smaller things.

What Allah is asking about is not the flicker. It is the *sitting*. The deliberate turning toward. The refusal to look away. The thought you actually let land.

And the rebuke — *did you not?* — is the diagnosis of an entire civilization. They were busy. They were sophisticated. They knew the trade routes and the genealogies and the poetry. They were watching empires rise and fall. They were doing many things.

They had not done the one thing.

They had not, even once, sat with themselves.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Two: *Fī Anfusihim* — Inside, Not Outside

#### The Concept First

When someone tells you, *Look at the evidence*, your mind reaches outward. You look at the world. You look at the data. You look at what is in front of you.

But there is another kind of evidence — the kind that is not in front of you because it *is* you. The pulse in your wrist. The hunger that arrives every six hours and refuses to be reasoned with. The fact that you did not choose to be born, did not choose your face, did not choose the era, did not choose the language you first dreamed in. The fact that your heart has been beating, without your permission, since before you knew what a heart was.

The strangest evidence is always the closest. We walk past it every day because we *are* it.

#### Opening the Phrase

Allah does not say *fakkarū fil-āyāt* — reflect on the signs. He does not say *fakkarū fī khalqis-samāwāti wal-arḍ* — reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth. Both of those constructions exist elsewhere in the Quran. They are valid invitations.

Here He says something else:

> *yatafakkarū fī anfusihim*
>
> *reflect within themselves*

The preposition *fī* means *in / inside*. Not *about*. Not *concerning*. *Inside.*

This is striking. The classical commentators noticed it. Some read it as: *reflect inside their own selves* — meaning, do the reflecting *within the inner space of the self*. Others read it as: *reflect upon their own selves* — meaning, make the self the *object* of reflection.

Don't choose between these. Both are present. Let them be two angles on the same instruction.

Reading One: **the place where reflection must happen is inside you.** Not in a debate. Not in a market. Not in a council of elders. Inside the self, in the silence where no one else is watching.

Reading Two: **the thing reflection must be about is you.** Your own existence. Your own body. Your own arrival into being.

[PAUSE]

Notice the asymmetry between what they were doing and what they were being asked to do.

The Meccans were looking at Persia and Byzantium. Outward, far away, geopolitical. Allah pulls the camera all the way in. Closer than the empire. Closer than the city. Closer than the tribe. Closer than the family. Closer than the body.

Inside.

#### The Sign They Walked Past

Here is what is remarkable. The ayah is about *reflecting within yourself*. But then immediately Allah lists what such reflection would reveal:

> *Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them except in truth and for an appointed term.*

Wait. He told us to look inward. And then He pointed outward — to the heavens, the earth, everything between.

How do these connect?

This is the genius of the construction. Allah is saying: *the path to seeing the truth of the heavens and the earth runs through the inside of you.* You cannot read the cosmos correctly while you are estranged from yourself. The same Hand that built the galaxies built the cells you are made of. The same purpose that ordered the orbits ordered your heartbeat. The same *appointed term* that will end the universe is written, in smaller script, into your own body.

You don't need a telescope to see this. You need a mirror. You need silence. You need to *stop*.

The Meccans had not stopped.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Three: *Bil-Ḥaqq wa Ajalin Musammā* — Built on Truth, Sealed With an Hour

#### The Concept First

Imagine you walk into a building. You don't know who built it or why. But you notice the walls are square. The doors swing on hinges. The pipes carry water. The lights have switches. Every detail has a *function*. Nothing is decorative without also being structural. The whole thing is *for* something.

You would never conclude, looking at this building, that it had assembled itself by accident. The very *fittedness* of it tells you something. It was made *for*. There is an intention woven into every joint.

Now imagine you also notice something else. There is a notice on the wall. *This building will be demolished on a date that is set but not announced.* The function of the building does not change. The walls are still square. But the meaning of being inside it does change. You are not simply *in* the building. You are in the building *during a particular window*. The window will close.

#### Opening the Phrase

Allah says:

> *mā khalaqa Allāhu as-samāwāti wal-arḍa wa mā baynahumā illā bil-ḥaqqi wa ajalin musammā*
>
> *Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them except in truth and for an appointed term.*

The structure here is the strongest negation Arabic can produce. *Mā... illā* — *Did not... except.* It is the construction of total exclusion. It is not, *Allah created them, among other things, in truth.* It is, *He created them — and there is no other reason. The only reason. The exclusive reason.*

And He names two things that are joined by a single *wa* — and:

**One: *bil-ḥaqq* — in / with truth.**

*Ḥaqq* in Arabic is a vast word. It carries: truth, reality, rightness, what-is-due, what-is-real-and-not-illusion, the genuine as opposed to the false. To say something exists *bil-ḥaqq* is to say it exists *for a reason*, *with rightness*, *as a genuine purpose-bearing thing*, not as decoration, not as noise, not as accident.

The cosmos is not random. The cosmos is *deserved* of itself — it is *for* something. There is a *due* that the universe is paying.

**Two: *ajalin musammā* — an appointed term.**

*Ajal* is the named-end of a thing. It is the deadline. The expiration. The moment built into the substance of a thing from the beginning that says: *and then, this stops.*

*Musammā* means *named, specified, given a name.* Not vague. Not approximate. Not "someday." It is named. By whom? By the One who created it.

[PAUSE]

Now feel the weight of these two joined together.

The universe was built on **truth** — meaning it has a purpose, it means something, every piece is doing structural work.

And the universe was built with **an appointed term** — meaning the entire structure has a closing date, written in from the beginning, known by name to its Maker.

The two are inseparable. A purposeful thing must end, because purposes are completed. An eternal accident has no purpose. An endless structure with no closing date is a structure without a destination. The *ajal* is not the enemy of purpose — it is the *evidence* of purpose. The universe will end because the universe is *for* something. When the thing is delivered, the package is no longer needed.

#### Now Connect It Back

Remember what we said: the cosmos and the self are governed by the same principles.

If the heavens and the earth were created *bil-ḥaqq wa ajalin musammā* — then so were you.

You were created in truth. You exist *for* something. You are not decoration in the universe.

And you were created with an appointed term. You have a *musammā* — a named end. Allah knows the name of your last day. He has spoken it. You have not heard it, but it has been said.

[PAUSE]

This is what reflecting within yourself reveals. This is what the Meccans had not done. They had not faced the two halves of this sentence written across their own chest: *you are here for a reason, and you are leaving on a date.*

#### One More Layer

There is something else here. Notice that Allah says *the heavens and the earth and what is between them.* The phrase *and what is between them* is not filler. In Arabic rhetoric, *wa mā baynahumā* is the technical phrase that covers *everything that is not the two great structural endpoints — everything that fills the middle.*

You and I are *mā baynahumā*. We are not the heavens. We are not the earth. We are what is in between. We are the middle. We are the inhabitants of the corridor.

And the ayah is telling us: *the corridor was built for truth and for a term, too.* Don't think the rule applies to the sky and the ground but not to you because you are small. You are part of the same construction. You are governed by the same *bi-l-ḥaqq*. You are sealed with the same *ajal musammā*.

[PAUSE — major]

---

### The Bridge

What we have seen so far:

- A question that is not a question — a rebuke about an entire civilization that never sat with itself.
- A location that is not outside but inside — the reflection Allah asks for happens within the self.
- A pair of nouns that ties the cosmos and the self together — *built on truth, sealed with an hour.*

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The Loneliest Question Is the One You Never Asked Yourself

Let me ask you something. When was the last time you sat in silence — no phone, no music, no audiobook, no podcast, nothing in your ears, nothing in your hands — and just *thought about your own existence*?

Not the to-do list. Not the next thing. Not your worries about money, kids, work, the future. Not the replay of yesterday's argument.

Just: *I am here. I did not choose to be here. I am breathing. My heart is beating. I will die. What is this?*

Be honest. For most of us, the answer is *I can't remember.* Or *not in years.* Or *not since I was a teenager.* Or *never, really.*

There is a researcher named Timothy Wilson at the University of Virginia who ran an experiment a few years ago. He put people alone in a room with nothing — no phone, no books, no distractions. Just them and a chair and a button that would deliver them a mild electric shock. He told them they could sit there quietly for fifteen minutes, *or* they could press the button.

Sixty-seven percent of men, and twenty-five percent of women, pressed the button. They preferred to *hurt themselves* rather than sit alone with their own thoughts for fifteen minutes.

[PAUSE]

This is what the Quran is describing, in Mecca, fourteen hundred years ago.

*Awalam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim.*

*Have they not reflected within themselves?*

The Meccans had not. The shock buttons of their day were poetry, drinking, competition, status, raids, lineage boasts, the gossip of the marketplace. The shock buttons of our day are smaller and more elegant. We carry them in our pockets. We can press them at any moment. We do press them — every red light, every elevator ride, every walk to the bathroom, every transition. Anything to avoid the silence in which the question would arrive.

Because here is what we are afraid of. We are afraid that if we sat with ourselves, even for a moment, the question would land. *What am I doing? Why am I here? What is this all for? What happens when it ends?* And we have no answer ready. So we keep the noise on.

The Quran is not asking us to become philosophers. It is asking us to stop running.

[PAUSE]

#### Where This Leaves Us

Notice something cruel and beautiful about the ayah. Allah does not actually tell the Meccans *what* they would have discovered had they reflected. He says: *had they reflected, they would have seen that the heavens and the earth were created in truth and for a term.* He gives them the conclusion. He skips the journey.

Why?

Because the conclusion, given as information, does not transform. The conclusion, *arrived at by sitting in silence with your own existence*, does transform. Allah is showing us where the journey ends, but He is also telling us — *you have to walk it.* You cannot inherit this answer. You have to find it in the mirror of yourself.

This is why so many people have read the Quran without being moved by it. Not because the Quran is not moving. Because they have not done the prerequisite. The verse asks them to *reflect within themselves* — and they read the verse without doing the thing the verse asks them to do. They want the conclusion delivered. The Quran will not deliver it. The Quran will say: *you sit down with yourself first. Then come back and read.*

#### What This Changes Tomorrow

Here is the practice. It is not complicated. It does not require a retreat. It does not require a teacher.

Find five minutes. Put the phone in another room. Sit somewhere where you are not going to be interrupted. And ask yourself, slowly, the question this ayah is asking: *Did I choose to be here? Did I make my own heart beat? Will this body last forever? What is it for?*

Do not try to answer. Just let the question sit on your chest like a weight. Let it land. Let it stay.

You will feel an urge to escape. Notice it. That urge is what the Meccans surrendered to. Allah is asking you to do what they did not. Stay. Sit. Breathe. Let yourself feel that you are a creature, made by another, on borrowed time, for a reason that has not yet been named in your own mouth.

That is *tafakkur*. That is what the ayah asks for. Five minutes a day is not a small thing. The civilization Allah was rebuking had not managed even that.

### Theme Two: The Meeting You Have Already Been Scheduled For

Now look at the way the ayah closes:

> *And indeed, many of the people, in the meeting with their Lord, are deniers.*

The Arabic is striking. *Bi-liqā'i rabbihim la-kāfirūn.* The word *liqā'* means *meeting, encounter, coming face to face with.* It is not an abstract belief. It is a *meeting.* An appointment. A face that you will be in front of.

And the verb at the end — *kāfirūn* — is usually translated *disbelievers,* but in Arabic the root *k-f-r* literally means *to cover, to bury, to conceal.* A *kāfir* in the original sense is one who *covers over* a truth they have access to. The farmer who buries a seed in the ground is called a *kāfir* in classical Arabic, because he covers the seed with soil. Disbelief, in the Quranic sense, is not a neutral absence of information. It is an active burying. A covering-over.

So put the whole sentence back together.

*Many people, when it comes to the meeting with their Lord, are burying it.*

[PAUSE]

#### The Modern Bridge

You know this. You have seen this. You have done this.

There is a meeting on your calendar that you are dreading. So what do you do? You don't look at the calendar. You scroll past Tuesday. You schedule things on top of it. You let yourself "forget." When someone reminds you, you change the subject. You are not pretending the meeting doesn't exist — you know it does. You are *covering it.* You are *burying it.* You are practicing strategic non-attention.

This is what most of humanity does with death.

We know we will die. We are not deluded. We have been to funerals. We have seen the bodies. We have buried our parents. We *know.* But knowing is one thing. Letting the knowledge sit on the surface of consciousness is another. Most of us bury it. We cover it. We schedule on top of it. We let ourselves forget. We treat the future like a calendar with no Tuesday in it.

This is *kufr* in the deep linguistic sense. Not always the *kufr* of someone who has explicitly rejected Allah. Often it is the *kufr* of someone who has functionally rejected the *meeting* — who lives every day as though it is not coming.

[PAUSE]

#### How This Connects to Everything Else in the Ayah

Now feel the whole ayah click together.

Allah asked: *Did they not reflect within themselves?*

He told them what such reflection would reveal: *the cosmos was built on truth and for an appointed term.* Which means: *you were built on truth and for an appointed term.*

And then He named the consequence of *not* reflecting: *many people are burying the meeting.*

Do you see the architecture? **The reason most people deny the meeting with Allah is that they have never sat with themselves long enough to feel the appointed term written into their own body.** They have never done the internal work that would force the calendar to open. They have stayed on the surface of their lives — busy, productive, entertained, distracted — and the meeting has remained safely buried under the noise.

The Meccans were not deniers because the evidence was missing. They were deniers because they had never looked at the evidence that was *them.*

The ayah is not asking us to add new information to our worldview. It is asking us to *uncover* what we have buried. The verb *tafakkara* and the verb *kafara* are opposites in this ayah. To reflect is to *uncover*. To deny is to *cover over*. The whole spiritual life happens in this binary.

You are either uncovering or you are covering. You are either sitting with the truth of your existence or you are burying it under tasks.

[PAUSE]

#### What This Changes Tomorrow

Here is what shifts. The meeting is not a metaphor. It is a real appointment, scheduled by the One who created you. He knows the date. He has named it. *Ajalin musammā* — the *named* term. You don't know the name. But the name has been spoken.

So tomorrow, when you feel yourself reaching for the phone to escape the silence, name what you are doing. Say it to yourself: *I am burying the meeting again.* Not as guilt. As awareness. The act of *naming* the burial is the beginning of un-burying it.

And here is the gift: the meeting is not only fearsome. It is also a meeting with your *Lord*. *Rabbihim.* The One who made you, sustains you, has been generous to you every breath of your life. The meeting is with the One who already loves you enough to keep your heart beating right now, while you read these words. You are not preparing to meet a stranger. You are preparing to meet the One you have always been in front of, finally face to face.

The Meccans had not even gotten to the question of *how* to prepare. They had not yet admitted the meeting was happening. *Tafakkur* is the first step. It is the un-burying.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture of the Ayah

Look at how this ayah is built. Three movements:

**Movement One:** *Have they not reflected within themselves?* (The question — the diagnosis of failed introspection.)

**Movement Two:** *Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them except in truth and for an appointed term.* (The content — what reflection would reveal: cosmos and self both built for purpose, both sealed with an hour.)

**Movement Three:** *And indeed, many people, in the meeting with their Lord, are deniers.* (The consequence — what happens when reflection is refused: the meeting is buried.)

The three movements form a single line of causation. *Failure to reflect → missing the truth of creation and the appointed term → burying the meeting.*

It is also a line of repair, read in reverse. *To stop denying the meeting → recover the truth that you were made for a reason and you will end → return to reflection within yourself.* The path out is the path in, reversed.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

From the linguistic side, we saw that Allah opens with a rebuke-question, locates reflection inside the self, names the cosmos and the self as joined by truth and term, and ends with a verb of *burying* — the opposite of reflecting.

From the thematic side, we saw that the deepest spiritual problem of any age is the inability to sit with oneself, and that the burial of death is the consequence of that inability.

Both are saying: *the spiritual life is the practice of uncovering what your busyness has buried.* The Meccans had buried it under empire-watching and tribal poetry. We bury it under screens and noise. The medium changes; the burial does not.

### Questions to Carry

1. When was the last time I sat alone with my own existence — no phone, no input — for even five minutes?
2. If someone read my schedule, would they conclude that I believe in a meeting with my Lord?
3. What am I *covering over* in my life that I already know to be true?
4. If the *ajal* of this body has been named, what does it change about the conversation I am avoiding, the apology I am delaying, the prayer I keep meaning to start?
5. What is the noise I reach for first, when silence threatens to bring the question?

### One-Sentence Distillation

> *The Meccans were not deniers because the evidence was missing — they were deniers because they had never sat with themselves long enough to feel that the same truth and the same appointed term written across the heavens are also written across their own body.*

### The Closing Invitation

You don't have to become a scholar. You don't have to learn Arabic grammar. You don't have to memorize anything new tonight.

You only have to sit down. Once. For five minutes. Without your phone. And do what an entire civilization had not done — the thing the Quran rebuked them for skipping.

Reflect within yourself.

Feel the heart that is beating without your permission. Feel the lungs that are filling without your effort. Feel the body that is aging at a rate you did not set. Feel the calendar that is closing toward a date you have not been told.

And then ask, slowly: *What is this all for? Who made it? Who am I going to meet?*

The Quran has given you the answers. But the answers will not transform you until you have asked the questions. The asking is yours to do. No one else can do it for you.

### Du'a

> Allah, You who created the heavens and the earth and what is between them in truth and for an appointed term — pull us out of the noise we have buried ourselves under. Let us sit with what You have made us, even when it is uncomfortable. Let us not be among those who reach the meeting having never thought about it. Uncover for us what we have covered over. Make our days of *tafakkur* the foundation of our days of action. And when our own *ajal musammā* arrives — let us meet You as those who knew the meeting was coming, and prepared, and longed for it.
>
> Āmīn.
