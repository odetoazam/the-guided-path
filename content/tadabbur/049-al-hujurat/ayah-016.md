# Would You Teach God Your Religion?

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the verse first. Sit with the sound of it before we touch a single word.

> [ayah:49:16] قُلْ أَتُعَلِّمُونَ ٱللَّهَ بِدِينِكُمْ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

> *Qul a-tuʿallimūna-llāha bi-dīnikum, wa-llāhu yaʿlamu mā fī-s-samāwāti wa-mā fī-l-arḍ, wa-llāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.*

> **Say: Would you inform Allah about your religion — while Allah knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth, and Allah is Knowing of all things?**

[PAUSE]

### Rebuilding the world

To feel the weight of this verse, you have to stand in the room where it was first heard.

A group of Bedouin Arabs — the Qur'an calls them *al-Aʿrāb*, the desert dwellers — have come to Madinah. Islam is ascendant. The community of believers is no longer a persecuted minority hiding in Makkah; it is a rising power. And these men arrive, and they say to the Prophet ﷺ: **آمَنَّا** — *āmannā* — "We have believed."

Notice what they have done. They have not just submitted. They have not just shown up. They have made an **announcement**. They have come to declare their inner state — to put their faith on the record. *We believe. We are of the believers now. Count us.*

And there is something in that announcement that is asking for something. It is asking to be recognized. It is asking to be credited. It carries, underneath it, the faint expectation: *acknowledge what we have given you.* The verse just before this one (49:17) will name it exactly — they regard their entry into Islam as a **favor they have done**, a *mann*, something the Prophet ﷺ should be grateful for.

And so Allah responds. First, in 49:14, He corrects the very word: *Do not say "we have believed"; say rather "we have submitted," for faith has not yet entered your hearts.* And then — in our verse — He does something sharper. He does not argue with their claim. He does not say "you are lying." He turns their announcement into a single, devastating question.

*You came here to tell Me what is in your hearts. You came to inform Me of your faith.*

**Are you teaching God your religion?**

[PAUSE]

### The Hook

Here is what I want you to feel before we begin. Every one of us, at some point, has done what those Bedouins did. We have stood before God in prayer and — somewhere in the back of the mind — narrated our own righteousness to Him. *Look, I came. Look, I gave. Look how far I've come.* We have, without quite admitting it, treated our faith as **news we deliver upward**.

This verse asks one question that dismantles that entire posture. And the astonishing thing is *how* it dismantles it — not with a rebuke about arrogance, but with a single verb, repeated three times in three different shapes, that turns the speaker's own tool against him.

Let's find out what Allah placed in these sixteen words.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The verb that means *to teach* — أَتُعَلِّمُونَ

Let me teach you a piece of Arabic before we use it.

In Arabic, a simple three-letter verb can be "doubled" in its middle to create what grammarians call **Form II**. You take the bare root and you intensify it, you make it deliberate, you make it *done-to-someone*. The bare verb *ʿalima* (عَلِمَ) means "he knew." But double the middle letter — *ʿallama* (عَلَّمَ) — and it no longer means "he knew." It means **"he taught."** He took his knowing and he transmitted it. He stood as instructor over a student.

This is the verb of the classroom. It is the verb of the one who has, facing the one who lacks. *ʿallama* is what a teacher does to a pupil, what a master does to an apprentice, what the knowing does to the ignorant. It is, by its very structure, a relationship of **higher to lower**.

Now look at the word Allah chooses.

> **أَتُعَلِّمُونَ** — *a-tuʿallimūna*

That little **أ** at the front (*hamza*) is a question: *do you...?* And the verb is *tuʿallimūna* — the Form II. Not *do you know*, not *do you tell*. **Do you teach.** Do you instruct. Do you stand as the one who has knowledge, transmitting it down to the one who lacks it.

And the object of this teaching — the student in this sentence — is **ٱللَّهَ**. *Allah.*

[PAUSE]

Stay with this. Let the picture form fully. The grammar is placing these desert Arabs in the seat of the **teacher**, and it is placing *Allah* in the seat of the **student**. It is drawing, with one word, the most inverted image imaginable: the creature standing at the front of the room, chalk in hand, explaining the lesson — and the Creator seated before him, receiving the instruction.

The audacity isn't stated. It's built into the morphology. You don't have to be told it's absurd. The verb form *shows* you the absurdity by seating everyone in their chairs.

Now — here is where the classical scholars add a layer that makes it even more precise. Al-Jalālayn and al-Baghawī both note that *ʿallama* here carries the specific sense of **إعلام** (*iʿlām*) — **to inform, to notify, to bring news to someone who didn't have it.** That is exactly why the verse says *bi-dīnikum* — "**about** your religion" — with that little *bi* attached. You don't normally "teach someone *about*"; you teach them a subject. But you do **inform** someone *about* something. As al-Baghawī puts it plainly: *al-taʿlīm hāhunā bi-maʿnā al-iʿlām* — "teaching here means informing." And then he delivers the verse's whole thrust in five words: *lā yaḥtāju ilā ikhbārikum* — **"He has no need of your reporting."**

So the picture sharpens. It isn't only "do you presume to instruct God." It is: **do you imagine you are bringing Him news?** Do you think your faith is *information* that reaches Him — a report filed, a fact disclosed, a status update He receives from you?

What this single verb reveals: **the moment you announce your religion to God as though it adds to His knowledge, you have, in the very structure of your speech, claimed the teacher's chair over your Creator.**

[PAUSE — let that land]

### Element Two: The root that appears three times — ع-ل-م

Here is something you cannot see in translation, and it is the heart of this verse.

There is one root — **ʿ-l-m** (ع ل م), the root of *knowledge* — and it appears in this short verse **three separate times**, in three different forms. The translation scatters them into different English words, so the connection vanishes. But in the Arabic, the same root is struck three times like a bell.

Listen for it:

1. **أَتُعَلِّمُونَ** — *a-tuʿallimūna* — "do you **teach** / inform" — root ʿ-l-m
2. **يَعْلَمُ** — *yaʿlamu* — "He **knows**" — root ʿ-l-m
3. **عَلِيمٌ** — *ʿalīm* — "All-**Knowing**" — root ʿ-l-m

[PAUSE]

Do you see what is happening? The verse takes *your* claim — the verb of knowing-transmission that you presumed to aim at God — and then it answers you twice with the same root, but each time elevated.

You said: *I will make-known (ʿallama).* A Form II verb — deliberate, effortful, the act of a creature who acquired knowledge from somewhere and now passes it on.

He answers: *wa-llāhu **yaʿlamu*** — *and Allah **knows*** — the simple, primordial form. Not knowledge acquired and transmitted. Knowledge that simply *is*, eternal, underived.

And then, lest you think His knowing is an act He performs, He answers a third time — not with a verb at all, but with a **name**: *wa-llāhu bi-kulli shayʾin **ʿalīm***. *ʿAlīm* — the All-Knowing — is not something Allah *does*. It is something Allah *is*. It is His description, His attribute, woven into His very being.

So watch the trajectory the root traces across sixteen words:

- From your **manufactured, transmitted** knowing (Form II verb — the teacher's effortful act),
- to His **essential, eternal** knowing (Form I verb — knowledge that simply exists),
- to His knowing **as identity itself** (the name *ʿAlīm* — not an act but a being).

The tool you picked up to wield *toward* Him turns out to be **His own name.** You arrived holding "knowledge" as your credential, and the verse shows you that the thing you were holding belongs to Him absolutely — it *is* Him. You were trying to teach knowledge to the One whose name is *Knowledge*.

This is irony built into the consonants. Not stated. *Structured.* The same three letters, ascending from your fragile claim to His infinite reality.

What this triple-strike reveals: **whatever you presume to bring God, you will find He already is — more completely, more eternally, more essentially than you could ever hold it.**

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Three: The asymmetry between *your religion* and *the heavens and the earth*

Now read absence as carefully as presence. Look at *what* you are reporting versus *what* He already knows.

You came to inform Him of — what? **بِدِينِكُمْ** — *bi-dīnikum* — "**your** religion." Something particular. Something internal. Something small. The state of one heart, in one chest, in one body, standing in one room in Madinah. A sliver of reality. *Your* din.

And against this small thing you wish to report, the verse sets His knowledge:

> **وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ**
> *And Allah knows whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.*

Feel the scale collapse. You wanted to update Him on the contents of *your* chest — and He is the One who knows the contents of **the heavens** and **the earth**. Every star and the space between stars. Every seed underground and the unborn life inside every womb. Every falling leaf, every grain in the dark of the soil, every whisper of every thought in every mind that has ever existed.

And your din — your little private religion that you came to announce — *is one item inside that totality.* It is already on the inventory. It was on the inventory before you walked into the room. It was on the inventory before you were born.

[PAUSE]

And then the verse does not even let you keep "the heavens and the earth" as the boundary. It pushes past it:

> **وَٱللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ**
> *And Allah is Knowing of **every single thing.***

*Kulli shayʾin.* Every. Thing. The word *shayʾ* (شَيْء) is the most universal noun in the language — it means "a thing," literally *anything that can be referred to at all.* He knows it. All of it. The category has no exceptions and no outside.

So here is the asymmetry laid bare: **You came to add one drop to an ocean that already contains every drop that exists — and then some drops the ocean of "heavens and earth" doesn't even name.** Your report is not just unnecessary. It is *already inside the thing you imagine you are adding to.*

There is also a structure here you should hear out loud. Listen to the hammer of the divine Name:

> *...wa-**llāhu** yaʿlamu... wa-**llāhu** bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.*

**Allah... and Allah... and Allah.** Three times in one breath. The Name is not pronouned away into "He." It is restated, and restated, and restated — each time pressing the same point harder: *it is Allah you are speaking to. Allah. The One whose knowledge is the heavens and the earth and everything. That Allah. Do you really mean to inform Him?*

What this asymmetry reveals: **your most private, most carefully announced faith is a single known item inside a knowledge so total it has no edge — and the announcing adds nothing to it except, possibly, to expose the heart of the announcer.**

[PAUSE]

### The Bridge

So look at what the construction has shown us. A verb that seats the creature as God's teacher. A single root struck three times, ascending from your manufactured claim to His essential name. And a scale that sets your private din against the contents of all the heavens and the earth — and then past them, to *every thing.*

The grammar has been making one argument the whole time: **faith reported upward is faith fundamentally misunderstood.**

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the way we actually live before God.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The performance of faith — when religion becomes a report we file

Let me start where you already live.

You know the feeling of curating yourself. We all do it now more than any generation in history. There is a version of you that exists for the feed — the photographed version, the captioned version, the version that posts the milestone and waits for the acknowledgment. And there is a quieter, less flattering thing underneath the posting: the need to be *seen as* something, which is not always the same as the need to *be* it.

Now bring that instinct into the prayer mat. Because it follows us there.

There is a way of being religious that is really a way of **performing religiousness** — even, and this is the subtle part, performing it *for God Himself.* We stand in prayer and somewhere a quiet narration runs: *Look, I'm here. Look, I made it to Fajr. Look how much I've changed, how far I've come, what I gave up for You.* We treat our worship as a transmission — a message sent upward, a report filed with the Divine, a status update on our spiritual progress. *Allah, here is my faith. Noting it for the record.*

That is exactly what the Bedouins did. They walked in and said *āmannā* — *we believe* — and the announcement was doing work. It was asking to be credited. It was treating faith as a **deliverable**.

And the verse stops them with a question that should stop us too: **Are you informing God of your religion?**

[PAUSE]

Here is the first thing this opens. The moment faith becomes a *report*, two corruptions enter at once.

The first corruption: a report can be **inflated.** The Bedouins said "we believe," but Allah said in the previous verse that belief *had not yet entered their hearts.* The gap between the announcement and the reality — that gap is the whole disease. When faith is something you *declare*, you start managing the declaration. You round up. You present the better number. You say *āmannā* when the truth is, at most, *aslamnā.* The performance and the reality drift apart, and you start believing your own press release.

The second corruption: a report assumes the receiver **didn't already know.** And that assumption, aimed at God, is quietly blasphemous. To "inform" Allah of your piety is to imagine a version of Allah who was waiting on your update — who learns of your fasting when you mention it, who discovers your charity when you announce it. The performance of faith *requires* a smaller god. A god you can manage with information.

And the verse demolishes exactly that smaller god: *wa-llāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.* He knew before you formed the word. He knew the prayer before you stood for it, and He knew the precise quality of it — the wandering mind, the half-presence, the real sincerity buried under the show — better than you knew it yourself.

[PAUSE]

So what does this change tomorrow?

It means the single most useful question you can ask before any act of worship is not *how does this look?* but *who is this actually for?* If your religion has become a thing you **announce** — to others, to yourself, to God — the verse invites you to stop announcing and start *doing it in the dark.* Pray a prayer no one will know about. Give something no one will see. Let there be, in your week, at least one act of faith that you will never *report* to anyone, including the running narrator in your own head. That is the act that proves your faith is a **reality** and not a **press release.** Because the only audience left is the One who was always the only audience that mattered.

[PAUSE — re-enter through a human door]

### Theme Two: Being fully known — the terror and the mercy of *ʿAlīm*

Now let me take you somewhere more tender.

Think about what it actually feels like to be *fully known* by another human being. There is almost no one — perhaps no one alive — who knows the whole of you. Not the curated parts. The whole. The thought you'd never say. The motive behind the good deed that wasn't as clean as it looked. The fear you've never named to anyone. We spend enormous energy making sure no single person ever assembles the complete picture, because we are afraid that if someone truly saw all of it, they could not love what they saw.

And now hold the second half of this verse against that fear:

> *wa-llāhu yaʿlamu mā fī-s-samāwāti wa-mā fī-l-arḍ — wa-llāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.*

There is One who has assembled the complete picture. Who has every thought you never said. Every motive you disguised even from yourself. Every fear you never named. He has *kulli shayʾ* — every thing — and not a fragment is hidden. The same verse later in this surah's neighbor (50:16) says it directly: *We are closer to him than his jugular vein.*

Now — here is the paradox, and I want you to feel both sides of it, because the verse holds both.

**Side one: this is terrifying.** Because it means there is no performance that works. There is no curated version that gets through. The whole apparatus of self-presentation we run, all day, every day — it is *useless* before the *ʿAlīm.* He sees the announcement *and* He sees the gap behind the announcement. He hears *āmannā* and He knows the exact temperature of the heart that said it. You cannot manage Him with information. You cannot win Him with a better caption. Every mask is glass.

[PAUSE]

**Side two — and stay with me, because this is the mercy hidden inside the terror:** if there is no performance that works, then *you can finally stop performing.*

Think about what an exhausting, lifelong labor it is to be partially known and to manage the rest. To be loved for the version you show, and to live in low-grade dread that the unshown version would end the love. Now imagine standing before One who **already has the whole picture** — every ugly motive, every failure, every hidden thing — *and who invites you to turn to Him anyway.* Who, knowing all of it, opens the door of repentance every single night.

That is not the relationship of a judge waiting to be informed. That is the relationship of One before whom **you have nothing left to hide and therefore nothing left to protect.** The *ʿAlīm* is the only audience in your entire existence in front of whom you can completely, totally stop pretending — because pretending was never going to work in the first place. The performance was always only ever fooling *you.*

[PAUSE]

This is why the verse that begins as a *rebuke* — *do you presume to teach God?* — ends as an *invitation.* Because the moment you accept that He already knows everything, the entire false economy of religious performance collapses, and something cleaner can grow in its place: faith that is no longer aimed at an audience, because the only One watching already saw it all and stayed.

What does this change tomorrow? It means you can bring God the parts of yourself you've been hiding *from Him* — as if hiding were possible. The sin you've been too ashamed to name in duʿāʾ. The doubt you've been performing your way around. The motive you know isn't clean. He already has the file. The shame that keeps you from turning to Him is built on the fantasy that He doesn't already know — and this verse burns that fantasy down. **You are fully known. Turn to Him as the fully-known thing you already are.** That is the only honest worship there is.

[PAUSE — major]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing it together

Look at how the two directions meet.

From the **linguistic** side, we found a verb that seats the creature as God's teacher; a single root — *ʿ-l-m* — struck three times, climbing from your manufactured claim to His essential Name; and a scale that dwarfs *your religion* beneath *the heavens and the earth and every single thing.*

From the **thematic** side, we found the human instinct to perform faith as a report filed upward — and the shattering, liberating truth that the receiver of that report already knows everything, so the performance was never anything but self-deception.

These are the same insight from two directions. **The grammar's whole argument and the soul's whole lesson are one:** you cannot inform the *ʿAlīm.* And the day you stop trying to inform Him is the day your religion stops being an announcement and finally becomes *real* — done in the dark, for the One who sees in the dark, who saw it all before you ever formed the word.

The Bedouins came to file a report. The verse handed it back to them unopened — *lā yaḥtāju ilā ikhbārikum*, "He has no need of your reporting" — not because the report was unwelcome, but because there was never anyone on the other end waiting to be told.

### Questions to carry

1. When I stand in prayer, is there a quiet narration running — *look how far I've come* — and who exactly is that narration *for*?
2. Where in my religious life have I confused **announcing** faith with **having** it? Where am I saying *āmannā* when the honest word is *aslamnā*?
3. Is there a single act of worship this week that no one — not one person, not even the narrator in my own head — will ever know about?
4. What am I hiding *from God* — in shame, in performance — under the secret fantasy that He doesn't already know?
5. If I truly believed that I am **fully known and still invited**, what would I finally stop pretending about?

### One-sentence distillation

**Faith is not news you deliver to God — it is a reality He already sees more truly than you do, so stop reporting your religion and start living it before the One whose very Name is Knowing.**

### The closing invitation

Let something shift. Not a resolution to do more — an invitation to *perform* less. To let at least one corner of your worship go completely dark, completely unwitnessed, completely unreported — and discover that in that darkness, you were never actually alone, and you never actually were hidden. The audience you were performing for was always the audience that already knew. Stop teaching Him your religion. Just live it in front of Him.

### Brief duʿāʾ

*O Allah, You who know what is in the heavens and the earth and every single thing — You know my faith better than the words I use to announce it, and You know the gaps I hide even from myself. Strip the performance from my worship until nothing is left but what is real. And since You already see all of me, give me the courage to turn to You as the fully-known thing I am — hiding nothing, presenting nothing, only seeking the One who knew, and stayed.* Āmīn.
