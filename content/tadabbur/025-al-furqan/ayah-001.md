# The One Who Came Down: Why the Criterion Was Sent in Pieces

## Opening the Ayah

**Arabic:**
> تَبَارَكَ ٱلَّذِى نَزَّلَ ٱلْفُرْقَانَ عَلَىٰ عَبْدِهِۦ لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَٰلَمِينَ نَذِيرًا

**Transliteration:**
> *Tabāraka alladhī nazzala al-furqāna ʿalā ʿabdihi liyakūna lil-ʿālamīna nadhīrā.*

**Translation:**
> Blessed is the One who sent down the Criterion upon His servant, that he might be a warner to all the worlds.

---

## Rebuilding the World Before the Words

Before we open this ayah, you have to understand what the Quraysh were actually saying.

It was not just "we don't believe you." It was something more specific, more cutting, more designed to wound. They were saying:

*"What kind of book is this? It dribbles down a few verses at a time. A real divine book — like Mūsā's tablets — would come all at once. This isn't revelation. This is a man making things up as he goes along, and when he gets stuck, he waits a few weeks and produces more."*

That objection is going to be quoted **explicitly** in this same surah. Look ahead a few verses to 25:32 — *"And those who disbelieve say, why was the Quran not sent down upon him all at once?"* The whole surah is structured as a response to the mockery of the Quraysh. Mockery of the messenger — *"What is with this messenger that he eats food and walks through the markets?"* (25:7). Mockery of the message — *"a man bewitched"* (25:8). Mockery of the method — *why piece by piece?*

[PAUSE]

So picture this: the Prophet ﷺ is in Mecca. He is being laughed at in the streets. His followers are being tortured. The objections are not theoretical — they are killing the morale of the community. And now Allah is going to open a surah that addresses every single one of these mockeries.

But notice **how** He opens it.

Allah does not start with a defense. He does not start with a counter-argument. He does not start by listing the proofs. He starts with one word that, before any argument is made, has already answered every objection they will ever raise.

*Tabāraka.*

That is the hook. We will return to it.

# PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element 1: *Tabāraka* — The Word That Cannot Be Conjugated

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we open this word.

In Arabic, most verbs can move freely through time. *Kataba* (he wrote), *yaktubu* (he writes), *uktub* (write!). Past, present, command — the verb flexes to whatever you need.

But there is a small category of verbs that exist **only in the past tense**. They have no present form, no imperative form, no future. They sit frozen in the past. Grammarians call these *afʿāl jāmida* — "frozen verbs."

And here is what is fascinating about them: they are frozen *because their meaning cannot be contained in time at all.* They describe realities so absolute that to put them in a tense would be to limit them.

*Tabāraka* is one of these.

[PAUSE]

Now look at the word itself. The root is **b-r-k** — *baraka*. You know this word. We say it constantly. *Bārak Allāhu fīk.* *Mubārak.* *Barakah.*

What does *barakah* actually mean in classical Arabic?

The lexicons describe it as **abundance that increases despite being drawn from**. A well of *barakah* gives water and the water level does not drop. Food that has *barakah* feeds many and there is more left over. The Arabs used the word for the kneeling of a camel — *baraka al-baʿīr* — because when the camel kneels, it settles in a place and *stays*, and goodness accumulates there.

So *barakah* is not just blessing. It is **stable, settled, self-replenishing goodness that does not deplete**.

Now look at the form Allah uses. Not *bāraka* (Form III). Not *barraka* (Form II). But ***Tabāraka*** — Form VI.

In Arabic, Form VI typically carries a meaning of **the action being intrinsic to the doer**, of mutuality folded back into the self, of a quality being so saturated in something that it overflows on its own without needing an object.

Stay with me here.

When you say *tabāraka Allāh*, you are not saying "Allah is blessed" the way you say "the bread is blessed." You are saying something untranslatable: **He is the one in whom blessing inheres so absolutely that it pours from Him without source, without limit, without depletion, and without requiring anyone else to confer it.**

He is the one who is *Tabāraka* — and there is no past, present, or future to it. The verb is frozen because the reality is timeless.

[PAUSE]

Now here is what is remarkable.

Allah does not open this surah with *Al-ḥamdu lillāh*. He does not open with *Subḥān alladhī*. He opens with *Tabāraka alladhī.*

Why?

Because the Quraysh are about to spend an entire surah trying to **diminish** Him. They are going to say His messenger is too ordinary. They are going to say His book arrives too slowly. They are going to say His method is wrong, His timing is wrong, His choice of prophet is wrong.

And before any of that begins, Allah opens with: ***Tabāraka.*** The One whose goodness *cannot be diminished by your objections*. The One whose blessing *does not depend on your approval*. The One who is so saturated in *barakah* that whether you affirm Him or deny Him, the blessing keeps overflowing.

It is the perfect word to silence mockery before mockery has even begun.

This single word has already answered them.

[PAUSE]

## Element 2: *Nazzala*, Not *Anzala* — The Grammar of Coming Down in Pieces

This is the most important grammatical observation in the entire ayah.

Let me teach you what is happening before I show you what Allah did with it.

In Arabic, there is a verb form called **Form II** — the *tafʿīl* pattern. You take a basic verb and you double the middle root letter. *Kasara* (he broke) becomes *kassara* (he shattered into pieces). *Qaṭaʿa* (he cut) becomes *qaṭṭaʿa* (he cut repeatedly). *Naṣara* (he helped) becomes *naṣṣara* (he helped persistently).

Form II carries one of two meanings, and often both at once:
1. **Intensification** — doing the action with greater force.
2. **Repetition or distribution** — doing the action again and again, or spread out across multiple instances.

Now here is the grammatical fact that changes everything.

The simple verb for "he sent down" is *anzala* — Form IV. *Anzala al-Qurʾāna* would mean "He sent down the Quran" as a single event, all at once.

But Allah did not say *anzala*. He said ***nazzala*** — Form II. With the doubled middle letter. The form that means **sent down piece by piece, portion by portion, gradually, over time.**

[PAUSE]

This is not a stylistic choice. This is a load-bearing grammatical statement.

The Quraysh objected: *"Why did this Quran not come down all at once?"*

And Allah's answer, encoded into the very second word of the surah that addresses their objection, is: ***Nazzala.*** **Yes. Of course it came down in pieces. That is precisely the verb I am using to describe what I did. I did not** *anzala* **it — I** *nazzala* **it. I sent it down gradually, on purpose.**

The grammar is the argument.

Stay with this. Notice what is happening. The objection is anticipated and refuted *before it is even stated in the surah*. By verse 32 the Quraysh will say *"why was it not sent down all at once?"* And by then Allah has already replied — in verse 1, in the choice of a single Form II verb — that gradualness is not a defect of the revelation. **Gradualness is the design.**

Why?

Think about it human-to-human. If someone you love has to deliver hard truth to you, do they hand you a 600-page book and walk away? Or do they sit with you, give you what you can hold today, let it settle, and come back tomorrow with more?

Revelation that arrives over twenty-three years is revelation that **walks alongside the human community as it grows**. It responds to their questions. It addresses their wounds in real time. It builds them in stages. The Quraysh saw gradualness as weakness — *a real god would just dump the book on you*. Allah uses one verb to reveal that gradualness is **mercy**, that the dribbling-down they mocked is the very form love takes when it teaches.

Look ahead to 17:106 — *"And it is a Quran which We have separated, that you may recite it to people at intervals, and We have sent it down progressively."* Same root. Same grammar. Same defense.

The Quran is not a book that was dropped on humanity. **It is a book that came down to live with them.**

[PAUSE]

## Element 3: *ʿAbdihi* — The Title Higher Than Prophet

Look at how Allah names His messenger here.

Not *nabiyyihi* — His prophet.
Not *rasūlihi* — His messenger.
Not *Muḥammad* by name.
Not *khalīlihi* — His intimate friend.

He says: ***ʿAbdihi.*** **His servant.**

Now you have to understand something about the word *ʿabd* in Arabic.

The root is **ʿ-b-d**. From it comes *ʿibādah* (worship), *ʿubūdiyyah* (servanthood), and the Kaʿba is from a related sense of being a focal point of devotion. The classical lexicons explain that an *ʿabd* in its deepest sense is **one who has surrendered the will entirely to the master, not from compulsion but from love**.

In the Arab world of that time, the word *ʿabd* could mean a slave — and that meaning carried shame. To call a man *ʿabd* was often an insult.

But when applied to the relationship between the human and Allah, the word **inverts**. *ʿAbd Allāh* — servant of Allah — becomes the highest possible station a human can attain. To be Allah's *ʿabd* is to have surrendered to the only Master worth surrendering to, which means you are a slave to nothing else. No king. No coin. No fear. No reputation. No mob. **No mockery in the streets of Mecca.**

[PAUSE]

Now hold all three of these in your mind at once:

The Quraysh are mocking him in the markets. They are saying: *what kind of messenger is this? He eats food. He walks among us. He is a man.*

And Allah, opening the surah that responds to their mockery, calls him ***ʿabdihi.*** **His servant.**

Notice what Allah does **not** say. He does not say *malakihi* — His angel. He does not pretend the Prophet ﷺ is something other than human. He affirms the very thing the Quraysh thought disqualified him — **his humanness, his servanthood, his ordinariness in their eyes** — and reveals it as the highest title in the cosmos.

The Quraysh said: *he is just a man*. Allah said: **yes. He is My servant. And nothing in existence ranks higher than that.**

This is the same word Allah uses at the most exalted moments of the Prophet's life ﷺ. *Subḥāna alladhī asrā bi-ʿabdihi* — Glory to the One who took His servant by night (17:1) — at the Night Journey, the highest moment of human ascent. *Wa annahu lammā qāma ʿabdullāhi yadʿūhu* — and when the servant of Allah stood calling upon Him (72:19). Every time the Prophet ﷺ is at his most elevated, Allah names him *ʿabd*.

Because servanthood **is** elevation. The lower you bow before Allah, the higher you rise above everything else.

[PAUSE]

Now hear what is said. *Tabāraka alladhī nazzala al-furqāna ʿalā ʿabdihi.* Blessed is the One who sent down the Criterion, piece by piece, **upon His servant**.

The Quraysh wanted a king. They wanted an angel. They wanted someone too dazzling to walk among them.

Allah gave them a **servant**. And He did so on purpose. Because the message was meant for servants — for ordinary humans, eating food, walking in markets, living lives — and only a servant could carry it to them.

[PAUSE]

## Bridge: From the Words to the Reality

Look at what these three words have shown us.

*Tabāraka* — the One whose blessing cannot be touched by mockery.
*Nazzala* — the gradualness the Quraysh mocked, revealed as the very design of mercy.
*ʿAbdihi* — the humanness they thought disqualified the Prophet ﷺ, revealed as the highest title in existence.

Three words. Three objections answered before the objections are spoken.

Now let us ask what this reveals not just about words — but about how Allah teaches a human heart.

# PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme 1: The Mercy of Slow Disclosure

You already know this in your life. You just have not connected it to revelation.

Think about the hardest truth someone you love has ever had to tell you. A diagnosis. A divorce. A betrayal. A loss. Did they tell you everything in the first sentence? Or did they say one thing, then sit with you, let you absorb it, and only later — sometimes weeks or months later — give you the next piece?

If they did it well, they did it slowly. Not because they were hiding things from you. Because they knew that **truth dropped on a human heart all at once does not land — it crushes**.

[PAUSE]

This is what *nazzala* is doing.

The Quran is not a download. It is not a file transfer. It is a relationship that unfolds across twenty-three years. The verses about wine come in stages — *first* there is some good and some harm in it, *then* do not approach prayer while drunk, *then* avoid it entirely. The verses about fighting come after years of being told to be patient. The verses of inheritance come after the community is ready to honor them. The verses of consolation come at the moments of grief.

A book sent down all at once is a book sent down to no one in particular. A book sent down piece by piece is a book sent down **to a community Allah is walking with**.

Stay with this for a moment.

What does it mean about Allah that He chose this method? It means He is not the distant lawgiver who hands down a tablet and disappears. It means He is **the One who stayed**. Who responded to questions. Who comforted after losses. Who answered the slander against ʿĀʾisha (RA) when the community did not know how to bear it. Who corrected the Prophet ﷺ gently when correction was due. **Twenty-three years of staying.**

And here is the part that should reach into your chest:

If Allah taught the first community this way — slowly, in pieces, in response to where they were — then this is also how He teaches **you**. You are not supposed to understand the whole religion in one night. You are not supposed to fix every sin in one weekend. You are not supposed to absorb every ayah on the first reading.

The same Allah who chose *nazzala* over *anzala* for the original community is the One walking with you now. The verse that breaks open for you this year was always there — but you were not ready for it last year. The reflection you cannot enter today may open in five years. **The gradualness is not a flaw in your iman. It is the shape of the mercy.**

[PAUSE]

What does this change?

It means the question is no longer *why don't I get it all yet?* The question becomes: **what is Allah giving me to hold today?**

Practical application: pick one ayah this week — not a chapter, one ayah — and live with it. Let it be your *nazzala*. Allah did not flood the Prophet ﷺ. Stop trying to flood yourself.

[PAUSE]

## Theme 2: The Servant Who Walks Through the Market

Here is where the second theme opens.

The Quraysh objected to the Prophet's ﷺ humanness. *"What is with this messenger that he eats food and walks through the markets?"* (25:7). And modern hearts — even Muslim hearts — sometimes carry a faint version of the same complaint. We want our spiritual figures to be **otherworldly**. We want them to glow. We want them to never have struggled, never doubted, never been hungry, never been mocked. We measure their authority by how *unlike us* they were.

Allah's answer to this is one word: ***ʿabdihi.***

[PAUSE]

The most exalted human in creation walked through markets. Got hungry. Cried at the loss of his son. Bled at Uhud. Was rejected at Ṭāʾif. Sat with companions and listened to their complaints. **Was a servant.**

And what Allah is saying to the Quraysh — and to us — is that the form of revelation **had to match its purpose**. If the Quran was going to be guidance for *people who walk in markets*, then it had to be carried by *someone who walked in markets*. An angel cannot teach you how to be a husband, a parent, a friend, a neighbor. An angel cannot show you how to forgive when forgiveness is hard, because no angel has ever been wronged the way you have been wronged. **Only a servant can teach servants.**

This is one of the most overlooked mercies in the entire Quran.

You think Allah made the Prophet ﷺ a man because that is what He had to work with? No. Allah could have sent angels. He could have sent a being of light. He chose a man — *ʿabd* — because the message was for men and women, and the messenger had to be *near*.

[PAUSE]

Now bring this back to your own life.

There is a part of you that thinks Allah cannot reach you because of how ordinary you are. How distracted. How tangled up in markets — literal markets, metaphorical markets, the marketplace of attention and money and opinion. You think the people who matter to Allah are the ones with no marketplaces.

But the highest title Allah gave to His most beloved was *ʿabd*. Servant. **A servant whose ordinariness was the qualification, not the disqualification.**

You do not have to leave the market to be Allah's. You have to **walk through it as His servant**. The Prophet ﷺ did not stop being in the world. He carried his servanthood through it. His shopping was worship. His greetings were dhikr. His listening was patience. He was *ʿabd* in the market.

This is what the verse is offering you.

[PAUSE]

What does this change?

Stop waiting to be otherworldly to be useful to Allah. He chose a servant who walked in markets to deliver the Criterion to humanity. **He can use you in your market too.**

Practical application: tomorrow, in whatever marketplace you walk through — your office, your group chat, your school, your kitchen — do one small act with the inner posture *I am here as ʿabd*. One small surrender. One moment of remembering. The Prophet ﷺ did not transcend markets. He **transformed** them.

[PAUSE]

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## The Architecture of the Opening

Now look at what Allah has built in nine words.

*Tabāraka alladhī* — He is beyond your evaluation.
*Nazzala* — and the gradualness you mocked is My mercy.
*Al-furqāna* — and what came down is the **Criterion** itself, the very thing that separates truth from falsehood, so the One who criticizes the Criterion has placed himself outside of truth.
*ʿAlā ʿabdihi* — and the man you mocked is My servant, which is the highest rank in existence.
*Liyakūna lil-ʿālamīna nadhīrā* — and his audience is not just you Quraysh; he is a warner **to all the worlds**, so your local rejection cannot stop a universal mission.

Every objection the surah is about to address is **already answered in the opening verse**. The Quraysh thought they were the audience deciding whether to accept this messenger. Allah reveals: they are not the judges. They are one tribe in one valley, mocking a *warner sent to all the worlds*. The *ʿālamīn* — every world, every age, every people — is the audience. And here you are, fourteen hundred years later, in a different *ʿālam*, reading these words. **The opening verse predicted you.**

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic and the thematic are saying the same thing.

*Nazzala* says: I teach you slowly because I love you enough to walk with you.
*ʿAbd* says: I send you a teacher who is one of you because I love you enough to come near.

Slowness and nearness. Gradualness and humanness. **Mercy walking with you, in a body like yours, over the years it takes for a heart to grow.**

That is the opening of Surah Al-Furqan.

## Questions to Carry

1. What is Allah giving me to hold *this week* — and am I trying to grab more than that?
2. Where in my life do I think I need to be more "otherworldly" before Allah can use me — and is that actually a refusal of the model He gave me in the *ʿabd* who walked through markets?
3. What objection do I carry in my heart against how slowly my growth is going? And could that objection be the same one the Quraysh raised against the Quran's gradualness?
4. If the Prophet ﷺ was titled *ʿabd* at his highest moments, what does that say about where my own elevation lies?
5. What would change in how I read the Quran if I read it as a book that *came down to live with me* rather than a book *I have to master*?

## One-Sentence Distillation

**The opening of Al-Furqan answers every objection to Allah's method by revealing that the slowness, the humanness, and the ordinariness the world mocks are not flaws in the revelation — they are the shape of mercy itself.**

## The Closing Invitation

Stop measuring yourself against a download speed Allah never used. He sent the Criterion *piece by piece* upon a *servant* who walked through *markets*. If that was good enough for the most beloved human in creation, then your slow, ordinary, market-walking servanthood is not a falling-short. **It is the road.**

## Du'a

*O Allah, You are Tabāraka — the One whose blessing pours forth without depletion, untouched by my failures and undiminished by my doubts. Teach us at the pace of nazzala, one verse at a time, walking with us across the years it takes our hearts to grow. And let us be content to be Your ʿabd — servants in our markets, ordinary in the eyes of others, beloved in Your sight. Āmīn.*
