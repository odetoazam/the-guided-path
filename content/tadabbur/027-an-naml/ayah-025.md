# The Hoopoe's Theology

## Surah An-Naml, Ayah 25

> [ayah:27:25] أَلَّا يَسْجُدُوا۟ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى يُخْرِجُ ٱلْخَبْءَ فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تُخْفُونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ

> *Allā yasjudū lillāhi alladhī yukhriju al-khabʾa fī as-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn.*

> "[Satan turned them away] so that they would not prostrate to Allah — the One who brings out what is hidden in the heavens and the earth, and who knows what you conceal and what you reveal."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are

To understand this ayah, you have to picture the scene.

Sulayman — the prophet-king with armies of jinn, men, and birds — is reviewing his ranks. He notices the hoopoe is missing. The hoopoe — that small, crested bird — has gone off without permission. When it returns, it brings news from a distant kingdom in southern Arabia: Saba. A queen rules there. Her throne is magnificent. Her people are prosperous. And they prostrate — *yasjudūna* — to the sun.

The hoopoe, this bird, has flown across deserts and seen something that disturbs it theologically. It comes back with a report. And in the middle of its report — right at the heart of its diagnosis of what is wrong with these people — comes our ayah.

[PAUSE]

Notice what's happening. A small bird has just delivered a more precise theology than the entire civilization of Saba could produce. It identifies the *exact* mechanism by which sun-worship is wrong. Not by saying "your god is small and our God is big." Not by appealing to power or majesty alone. But by naming a specific divine attribute that *cannot* belong to the sun by definition.

What the hoopoe says — or what Allah says through this verse, scholars differ — is one of the most theologically dense corrections in the Quran. And inside it lives a question that should haunt every one of us: **what do you actually mean when you prostrate?**

### The Hook

Here's what's strange. There are fifteen ayat in the Quran where, when recited, the listener is recommended or required to prostrate. This is one of them. **A verse about people who prostrate to the wrong thing — becomes itself a verse that calls us to prostrate to the right thing.**

The medicine is hidden in the diagnosis.

And the medicine is one specific word — *al-khabʾ* — a word so rare in the Quran that it appears only here, in this single verse, in the entire revelation. It is a hapax legomenon. A word Allah used once.

Why this word? Why here? Why never again?

Stay with me. We'll get there.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Al-Khabʾ* — The Word Used Only Once

#### Teaching the concept first

Before we open the word, let's think about how language works. Every language has *common* words — water, bread, sky — and *rare* words used only when something specific is meant. In English, you can say "hidden," "concealed," "secret," "buried," "stashed," "stored." Each one paints a slightly different picture.

The Quran uses many words for hidden things. *Ghayb* — the unseen. *Sirr* — a secret. *Khafiyy* — that which is concealed. *Maknūn* — that which is preserved or sealed. Each appears multiple times. Each carries its own texture.

But *khabʾ* — the form Allah uses here — appears only once in the entire Quran. **One time. This verse. This place.**

When the Author of revelation reaches into His vocabulary and pulls out a word He uses nowhere else, you stop and pay attention.

#### Opening the word

The root is خ-ب-أ (kh-b-ʾ). Classical lexicographers — Ibn Manẓūr in *Lisān al-ʿArab*, al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī in his *Mufradāt* — explain *khabaʾa* as: to store something away, to hide it for safekeeping, to put something in reserve.

This is critical. *Khabʾ* is not just "hidden" in the abstract sense. It carries the texture of **hiding-with-purpose**. Treasure buried for a reason. A seed placed under soil. Water stored deep in rock for a future thirst. Something put away by an Owner who knows where it is, why it is there, and when it will come out.

The Arabs called the storeroom of a household *makhbaʾ*. The places where rain hides in the clouds, where minerals hide in the earth, where life hides in the dead seed — all of these are *khabʾ*.

[PAUSE]

Let's sit with this. The verse is not saying Allah brings out random hidden things. It's saying Allah is the One who brings out **what has been deliberately stored**, what has been placed in reserve, what is being held — by the Owner — until the moment of its emergence.

#### Deepening — what is being said about the heavens and the earth

Now look at the phrase: *yukhriju al-khabʾa fī as-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi* — He brings out the *khabʾ* in the heavens and the earth.

Notice the universality. Not "in this place" or "from this thing." In the heavens *and* the earth. Everywhere. Every dimension of existence, above and below.

What is hidden in the earth that comes out? Water from rocks. Plants from seeds. Metals from ore. Children from wombs. The dead from graves on the Day of Resurrection.

What is hidden in the heavens that comes out? Rain from clouds. Lightning from stillness. Stars from the dawn-blackness. Angels who descend with what was decreed in secret.

[PAUSE]

Here's what's remarkable. Every single thing the people of Saba can perceive — including the sun they prostrate to — is itself something that *was once hidden*. The sun rises out of the hidden. It sets back into the hidden. It is itself a revealed-thing. It is, in the language of this verse, a *khabʾ-that-has-come-out*.

**You cannot prostrate to a thing that is itself in the process of being brought out.** You prostrate to the One bringing it out.

This is not a power argument. It is a category argument. The sun is in the wrong category to receive prostration. It is among the *brought-out*, not the *Bringer-out*.

#### The hapax — why this word, why only here

So why does Allah use *khabʾ* only here, only once?

Because the diagnosis of Saba's sun-worship requires a precise tool. *Ghayb* would have been too cosmic — the unseen as a vast unknown. *Sirr* would have been too small — a private secret. *Khafiyy* would have been too generic — the merely concealed.

What Saba needs is the word that says: **what you call the sun is what Allah calls the* khabʾ*-now-emerged.** What you treat as ultimate is what Allah moves in and out of His storeroom at will.

The hoopoe — through whatever inspiration — has reached for the one word that puts the sun back in its place.

And once that word has done its work, the Quran never uses it again. It was a single, precise instrument, deployed once, for one civilization that had mistaken the contents of the storeroom for the Owner of the storeroom.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Two: *Yukhriju* — The Active Participle of an Ongoing Bringing-Forth

#### Teaching the concept

In Arabic, verb tense is more than past, present, future. The form *yukhriju* — what grammarians call the *muḍāriʿ* — is often translated as "He brings out," but it carries a meaning closer to **"He keeps on bringing out, continuously, again and again, in the present and in every moment that will come."**

Think of the difference in English between "the sun rose" and "the sun is rising and rises and will rise again." The first is a single event. The second is a pattern, a habit, an ongoing reality.

The Quran uses the *muḍāriʿ* form for divine actions that are continuous truths about how reality works. *Yukhriju* is one of these.

#### Opening the verb

The root is خ-ر-ج (kh-r-j) — to come out, to exit, to emerge. The form *yukhriju* is Form IV (*afʿala*), which adds **causation**. *Kharaja* means "it came out." *Akhraja* means "He brought it out, He caused it to emerge."

So *yukhriju* says: **Allah continuously, actively, in this very moment, is causing hidden things to emerge.**

[PAUSE]

Stay with this. The verse is not describing something Allah did once at the beginning. It is describing what Allah is doing right now, as you listen. Right now, somewhere in the earth, a seed is splitting open. Right now, a cloud is releasing rain. Right now, a child is being born. Right now, in your own chest, a thought you didn't know you had is rising into awareness.

*Yukhriju.* Continuous. Unceasing. Active. *Now.*

#### The contrast that the verb sets up

Here is what the grammar is doing.

The sun, to which Saba prostrates, *is itself brought out*. It is acted upon. It is a passive object in this divine sentence — a thing that comes out because something else brings it out.

Allah, in this verse, is the active subject. *Yukhriju* — He brings out. He acts. He never passes from the role of subject to the role of object.

**Worship belongs to the One who never moves from subject to object — never from actor to acted-upon.**

[PAUSE]

A god who is acted upon — who rises and sets, who needs orbit and gravity, who is moved by laws greater than itself — cannot be Allah. The grammar of the verse, in a single verb, does what theological argument takes pages to do.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: *Mā Tukhfūna wa Mā Tuʿlinūn* — What You Conceal and What You Reveal

#### Teaching the concept

The verse pivots in its second half. Until now, we have been hearing about the heavens and the earth, the cosmic *khabʾ*. Suddenly, without warning, the address turns inward.

*wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn* — and He knows what you conceal and what you reveal.

Notice the shift. The first half of the verse is about the cosmos. The second half is about *you*. The first half says Allah brings out what is hidden in the macrocosm. The second half says Allah knows what is hidden inside you.

This is a rhetorical structure the Quran uses repeatedly: the outer pointing to the inner, the cosmic pointing to the personal. *Istidlāl al-maʿqūl bil-maḥsūs* — proving the unseen by appeal to the seen. The same God who brings the seed out of the dirt knows what is buried inside your chest.

#### Opening the asymmetry

Look closely at the pairing: *tukhfūna* (you conceal) and *tuʿlinūn* (you reveal). At first glance, they look balanced. Two opposites, named together.

But notice which one is named first. **Conceal comes first. Reveal comes second.**

[PAUSE]

Why? Why not "what you reveal and what you conceal"? Both orders would have rhymed. Both would have scanned. The Quran chose this order.

Here is what the order is teaching. **Most of who you are is concealed.** What you reveal is a small surface — a smile, a word, a public posture. What you conceal is the larger ocean — the resentments, the longings, the prayers no one hears, the pride no one sees, the fears you don't say out loud.

The verse names the bigger reality first. *Mā tukhfūn* — what you conceal — is the larger half of your life. And it begins with *kh-f-y* — the same texture of hiddenness we just met cosmically. The hidden things in the heavens and earth. The hidden things in you. **The same God knows both.**

#### The asymmetric absence

Here is something to notice. In the first half of the verse, Allah names His action: He *brings out* the hidden in creation.

In the second half, Allah names only His knowledge: He *knows* what you conceal and what you reveal.

He doesn't say *yukhriju* — He brings out — what you conceal. He says *yaʿlamu* — He knows — what you conceal.

[PAUSE]

What is this asymmetry teaching?

In the macrocosm, the hiddenness is structural. Seeds are *meant* to come out. Rain is *meant* to fall. The hidden things in the heavens and earth are stored to be brought forth — that is their purpose.

But in the human heart, what is concealed is not necessarily meant to be displayed. Your private prayer, your inner repentance, the secret tear you cry at 2 a.m. — Allah does not need to bring these *out*. He already knows them. The cosmic hiddenness is for revelation; the human hiddenness is for relationship. **Allah meets you in your concealment without exposing it.**

He brings out the seed because the seed must grow. He does not bring out your secret because your secret is between you and Him.

[PAUSE]

But there is a flip side. If you are concealing something that *should* come out — a wrong, a debt, a harm, a hypocrisy — then Allah's *knowledge* without His *bringing-out* is His mercy and His warning. He could expose. He doesn't. Yet.

The Day will come when *what you concealed* and *what you revealed* will both be brought out. On that Day, the verb shifts. *Yawma tubla as-sarāʾir* — the Day when the secrets will be tested. (Surah at-Ṭāriq.) On that Day, the second half of our verse becomes the first half. Knowledge becomes manifestation.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Bridging from Linguistic to Thematic

Look at what these three elements have shown.

*Al-khabʾ* — Allah owns the storeroom. Everything visible is something He keeps in reserve and brings out at His will.

*Yukhriju* — He is the unceasing actor, never the acted-upon. His role is permanent subject; never object.

*Mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn* — what is true cosmically is true psychologically. The same God who brings the seed out of dirt knows what is buried inside you.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself, and about the small life you and I are living right now.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: Your Inner Life Is the Larger Half of You

Let me start where you live.

You are walking down a street. A person passes. You smile at them. The smile is one second long. Inside that one second, there is — let's be honest — a whole world. Maybe you noticed they were dressed in a way you wished you could afford. Maybe you remembered an argument from this morning that hasn't left your chest. Maybe you said *bismillah* under your breath. Maybe you didn't.

The smile is what you revealed. *Mā tuʿlinūn.*

The whole world inside is what you concealed. *Mā tukhfūn.*

[PAUSE]

Now imagine this multiplied across every interaction of your day. Every word you said, every word you swallowed. Every time you looked at your phone and felt something you didn't name. Every prayer you almost made and didn't.

The "you" that other people meet is the *muʿlin* — the public surface. The "you" that lives inside is the *mukhfī* — the concealed mass underneath.

The Quran is telling us, in this verse, that **the larger and more real "you" is the concealed one.** And that this larger "you" is fully known to Allah.

This should do two things to you at once.

First, it should produce *taqwā* — a careful awareness. Because if Allah knows what is in your concealed half, then there is no such thing as a "private sin" or a "private resentment" that doesn't matter because no one sees it. The witness who matters most is already there, in the room of your chest, watching.

But second — and this is what often gets missed — it should produce **profound relief**. Because if Allah already knows what you conceal, then there is no need to perform for Him. There is no need to dress up your du'a. There is no need to be impressive in your dhikr. He sees the longing under the words. He sees the desperation under the polite phrasing. He sees the *real* prayer that you didn't know how to say.

[PAUSE]

Think about what this means for the person who feels their worship is shallow. Who says, "My prayer is empty. My heart is dry. My du'a feels rehearsed."

Allah knows what you conceal. Including the buried longing under the dryness. Including the part of you that *wants* to want Him more, even though you can't summon the want itself. **The hidden longing is itself heard.**

This is the comfort hidden in the same phrase that should produce trembling.

#### The practical application

Tonight, when you make du'a, try this. Do not perform. Do not phrase. Open your hands and say: *Yā Allāh, You know what I conceal and what I reveal. You know the prayer I don't know how to say. Hear the prayer underneath the prayer.*

Then sit in silence. Let Him hear what is *khabʾ* in you.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: You Are Prostrating to Something — The Question Is What

Here is what the verse, taken as a whole, is doing. It is naming the people of Saba as people who prostrate. They are not non-worshippers. They are not nihilists. They are devout. They have a religion. They have rituals around the sun. They are sincere.

And the verse, gently, surgically, says: *your prostration is going to the wrong category of thing*.

[PAUSE]

This is more uncomfortable than it sounds, because the truth is **everyone prostrates to something.** Every human heart bows. The only question is — to what?

Some of us prostrate to a number in a bank account. Some of us prostrate to a relationship. Some of us prostrate to a career. Some of us prostrate to our own image — the version of ourselves we want others to see. Some of us prostrate to a feeling — the high of recognition, the relief of approval, the rush of being right.

Saba prostrated to the sun because the sun was the most luminous thing they could see. We prostrate to whatever is the most luminous thing in our own field of vision.

And the diagnostic in this verse is razor-sharp. **Whatever you are prostrating to — is it a *brought-out* thing, or the *Bringer-out*?**

The number in your bank account — was it brought out? Yes. By Allah. From the *khabʾ* of provision He stores in the heavens and earth.

Your relationship — was it brought out? Yes. He brought that person into your life. He could bring them back into the hidden tomorrow.

Your career, your reputation, your identity — all of these are things *brought out from hiddenness*. None of them is the One who brings.

[PAUSE]

To prostrate to any of them — to organize your life around protecting them, fearing their loss, pleading with them for security — is to do exactly what Saba did. It is to confuse the contents of the storeroom with the Owner of the storeroom.

#### The reframe

Now here is what is beautiful.

When you understand that everything in your life is *al-khabʾ* — something Allah is bringing out and may return to hiddenness — your relationship to those things changes. You stop clutching them. You stop prostrating to them. You start receiving them.

The job is not your god. It is something Allah brought out for a season.
The relationship is not your god. It is a *khabʾ* He revealed.
Your health is not your god. It is something currently brought out from the storehouse of His mercy.

When the storehouse closes — and one day, on every gift, the storehouse closes — you do not lose your God. You only lose what He had brought out for a time.

**The one thing that never returns to the *khabʾ* is Allah Himself.**

He is *al-Awwal wa-l-Ākhir, wa-ẓ-Ẓāhir wa-l-Bāṭin* — the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden (Surah al-Ḥadīd 57:3). He does not get brought out and put back. He brings out and puts back. **He is the only legitimate destination of a sajdah.**

#### The practical application

This week, identify one thing in your life that you have started to prostrate to without realizing it. One thing whose loss feels existential. One thing you organize your day around protecting.

Then, in your next sajdah, name it. Say silently to Allah: *Yā Rabb, this thing — I had begun to bow to it. I am taking my forehead off of it and putting it where it belongs. On the ground. To You alone.*

This is what it means to prostrate at this ayah. It is not just the physical sajdah recommended for the recitation. It is the inner sajdah of relocation — moving your worship from a *brought-out* thing back to the *Bringer-out*.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture of the Verse

Now look at how the verse is built.

It begins with negation: *allā yasjudū* — that they would *not* prostrate.
It continues with description: *lillāhi alladhī yukhriju al-khabʾa fī as-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi* — to Allah, the One who brings out the *khabʾ* in the heavens and the earth.
It pivots inward: *wa yaʿlamu mā tukhfūna wa mā tuʿlinūn* — and who knows what you conceal and what you reveal.

The verse moves: outward (cosmos) → inward (heart). And the same word-family of hiddenness — *khabʾ*, *tukhfūn* — bridges them. **What is true of the universe is true of you.** The God who manages the macrocosmic hidden manages the microcosmic hidden.

And in the center of this architecture is the verb *yukhriju*. The continuous, active, causative bringing-out. The pulse of the verse. The unceasing breath of the One worthy of sajdah.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are saying the same thing from two directions.

From the linguistic side: Allah is the only legitimate object of prostration because He alone is never *brought out* — He only brings out.

From the thematic side: every other candidate for your prostration is something Allah has temporarily released from the *khabʾ*, and your inner life — which is the larger half of you — is fully known to Him without needing to be displayed.

The verse is a mirror and a map. A mirror to show you what you've actually been bowing to. A map to relocate your sajdah.

### Questions to Carry

1. What in my life have I quietly started prostrating to without naming it?
2. If Allah already knows what I conceal, why do I keep performing for Him in du'a instead of just opening?
3. When I lose something I love, am I losing my God, or am I losing something He had brought out for me from the *khabʾ*?
4. What is hidden in me right now — a longing, a fear, a regret — that I have never spoken aloud, that He already hears?
5. When I make sajdah today, will I make it with the awareness that this is the one thing I owe to no created object — only to the Bringer-out of all created objects?

### One-Sentence Distillation

Prostration belongs only to the One who never moves from Bringer-out to brought-out — and who knows the larger, concealed half of you better than the public half you show the world.

### The Closing Invitation

This is one of the verses of *sajdah* in the Quran. When you recite it, you are recommended to physically prostrate.

But know what you are doing when you do.

You are not just performing a ritual. You are taking your forehead off of every *brought-out* thing in your life — every reputation, every fear, every idol — and placing it on the ground in front of the only One who is not a content of the storeroom.

You are joining your concealed self to your revealed self in a single act, in front of the only Witness who sees both.

You are learning, with the hoopoe, the precise theology of the universe: **the sun rises and sets; only the One who brings it out and puts it back deserves the bowing of a head.**

### Du'a

Yā Allāh — You who bring out the *khabʾ* in the heavens and the earth, bring out from the *khabʾ* of my own heart the longing for You that I have buried under everything else. You know what I conceal — including the love for You that I have not yet learned to feel. Make me a person whose sajdah goes only to You, and whose concealed half is more honest than my revealed half. Āmīn.
