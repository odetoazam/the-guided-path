# An-Naml 27:24 — The Sun Worshipped in Place of the One Who Made It

## The Ayah

**Arabic:**
> [ayah:27:24] وَجَدتُّهَا وَقَوْمَهَا يَسْجُدُونَ لِلشَّمْسِ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَزَيَّنَ لَهُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ أَعْمَـٰلَهُمْ فَصَدَّهُمْ عَنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ فَهُمْ لَا يَهْتَدُونَ

**Transliteration:**
> Wajadtuhā wa qawmahā yasjudūna li-sh-shamsi min dūni-llāhi wa zayyana lahumu-sh-shayṭānu aʿmālahum fa-ṣaddahum ʿani-s-sabīli fa-hum lā yahtadūn.

**Translation:**
> "I found her and her people prostrating to the sun instead of Allah, and Shaytan has made their deeds beautiful to them, and so turned them aside from the path — and they are not guided."

---

## Introduction — A Bird's Diagnosis

Step into the scene for a moment.

Sulayman, peace be upon him, has been given something no human has ever been given before — language with the birds, dominion over the wind, command over jinn. He is reviewing his troops, and he notices that one bird is missing. He says he will punish the hoopoe severely — unless it brings him a clear excuse.

The hoopoe arrives. And it has news.

It tells Sulayman that it has flown over a kingdom in Saba — what we today call Yemen. It found a queen there. She has been given everything — *min kulli shay'* — every kind of provision, and a throne so magnificent that the Quran uses the word *ʿaẓīm* to describe it. Wealth. Power. Order. A civilization in full bloom.

And then comes our ayah. The hoopoe — *the bird* — diagnoses what is wrong.

[PAUSE]

Notice something before we even open the words. The diagnosis does not come from a prophet. It does not come from a scholar. It does not come from an angel. It comes from a creature that, in the human imagination, sits beneath us. Allah is teaching us, through the architecture of who delivers the verdict, that the disease the hoopoe is about to name is *visible to creation itself*. Even a bird can see it. The only ones who cannot see it are the people living inside it.

Hold that question — how can an entire civilization, with every sign of intelligence and prosperity, fail to see what a bird flying overhead can see in a single afternoon? — and let's enter the words.

---

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We are going to slow down on three things in this ayah:

1. The phrase **"min dūni-llāh"** — "instead of Allah," but more precisely, "*beneath* Allah."
2. The verb **"zayyana"** — "made beautiful." Form II of the root z-y-n. Causative, intensive.
3. The final clause **"fa-hum lā yahtadūn"** — "and they are not guided." Read against what is grammatically *missing* in it.

Three doors. Let's open them one at a time.

---

### 1. *Min dūn* — Beneath, Beside, Beyond

Before we touch the ayah, let me teach you what *dūn* actually means.

In English, when a translator writes "they prostrated to the sun *instead of* Allah," your mind hears a clean either/or: they chose A instead of B. Two equally available options, and they picked the wrong one.

Arabic does not say that.

The word *dūn* (دُون) in classical Arabic, as documented in Lisān al-ʿArab and Mufradāt al-Rāghib, carries a sense of *what is below, short of, less than, on this side of.* When something is *dūna* something else, it sits *underneath* it in the order of things. Imagine a man trying to reach a high shelf, and on the way his hand passes a lower shelf and rests there. He never reached the higher one. He stopped at *dūn*.

So *min dūni-llāh* does not mean "instead of Allah" as a horizontal choice. It means "*on the near side of* Allah." "*Short of* Allah." "*Beneath* Allah."

[PAUSE]

Now look at the ayah.

> *yasjudūna li-sh-shamsi min dūni-llāh* — they are prostrating to the sun, *short of* Allah.

Stay with me. The grammar is doing something the English translation cannot carry. The Quran is not saying these people had a clear choice between Allah and the sun and they picked the sun. It is saying their reach **fell short**. They were reaching for *something*. They knew there was something worthy of prostration. Something high. Something that deserved their forehead on the ground.

But on the way up, their hand stopped at the brightest thing they could see.

The sun.

The sun is genuinely high. The sun is genuinely powerful. The sun gives life. The sun is, in our visible world, the most awe-inspiring thing the eye can rest on without going blind. Their instinct was correct: *something deserves my forehead.* But their reach was short. They mistook the most magnificent created thing for the One who created it.

This is what *min dūn* is telling us. Idolatry, in the Quranic vocabulary, is rarely the sin of choosing nothing. It is the sin of choosing *something high* — but not high enough.

And here is what should land on us. The Quraysh, when they heard this ayah recited, were idolaters who knew better. But they were not the only ones being addressed. **Every single human being** has the impulse to prostrate. The forehead wants the floor. The question is never *whether* you will worship — the question is whether your reach will extend all the way to Allah, or whether it will stop at *dūn* — at something brilliant, something high, something close, but ultimately created.

The sun is the most generous thing the eye can see. And it is still *dūn*.

[PAUSE]

This is the first door. Hold it open while we walk to the second.

---

### 2. *Zayyana* — Beautified

The next phrase is one of the most psychologically precise things in the Quran:

> *wa zayyana lahumu-sh-shayṭānu aʿmālahum*
> "And Shaytan made their deeds beautiful to them."

The verb is *zayyana*. Root: z-y-n. The base form means "to be adorned, to be beautified." But Allah does not use the base form here. He uses Form II — *zayyana* — which in Arabic morphology is the causative-intensive form. It does not mean "it was beautiful." It means "*he made it appear beautiful*. He decorated it. He dressed it up."

Let me give you the everyday image first, before we go inside.

Imagine an empty room. Bare walls, cold floor, dim light. Now imagine someone walks in and lights candles, hangs paintings, lays a rug, brings flowers, plays soft music. The room has not changed in its bones. The walls are the same walls. The floor is the same floor. But the *experience* of the room has been transformed by the act of decoration.

That is *zayyana*. The Form II carries that exact texture: not "to be beautiful," but "to *dress something up* so that the one looking at it experiences beauty."

[PAUSE]

Now apply that to the ayah. The Saba civilization is prostrating to the sun. Their *aʿmāl* — their deeds, their religious system, their rituals, their entire life-architecture — is being *zayyana* by Shaytan. He is not making the deeds *good.* He is making them *appear good.* He is decorating the empty room.

Stay with this. Because here is what is so dangerous, and so accurate:

When a person is in a clear sin — drinking, stealing, lying — there is at least the possibility of guilt. The conscience can still register: *this is wrong.* The deed has not been decorated. The walls are still bare.

But when an entire civilization prostrates to the sun and *experiences it as worship* — when they feel the warmth on their faces and call it presence, when they organize festivals and call it devotion, when they build temples and call it submission — Shaytan has done his work. The deed has been *zayyana*. The empty room has been decorated. The worshipper does not feel the lack. The worshipper feels *beauty.*

This is why the ayah needed this verb. Without *zayyana*, you might ask: how could anyone prostrate to the sun and not feel something missing? The Quran answers: because Shaytan dressed the act in beauty. The participants experience reverence, awe, community, transcendence — all the *feelings* that proper worship produces.

But the object is wrong.

[PAUSE]

And here is what should make us tremble. *Zayyana* is not a word reserved for sun-worshippers in ancient Yemen. The Quran uses this verb across many surahs, applied to many different deeds. Allah is teaching us that this is *Shaytan's primary mechanism*. He does not usually argue with you. He does not usually say "do this evil thing." He decorates. He stages. He lights the candles.

What does this mean for us? It means the hardest sins to detect are not the ones that feel ugly. They are the ones that feel *good*. They are the ones that come pre-decorated. Self-righteousness feels like devotion. Showing off feels like teaching. Cutting someone out of your life feels like dignity. Spending hours on something useless feels like productivity.

The worshippers of the sun did not feel they were worshipping the wrong thing. They felt they were worshipping *beautifully*.

This is the second door.

---

### 3. *Fa-hum lā yahtadūn* — And They Are Not Guided

The ayah ends with a clause that, on the surface, is simple:

> *fa-hum lā yahtadūn*
> "And they are not guided."

But the Arabic is doing something we have to slow down for.

The verb *yahtadūn* is from the root h-d-y, which is the root of *hidāyah* — guidance. But it is not the simple Form I (*yahdī* — "he guides"). It is Form VIII — *yahtadī* — which in Arabic morphology carries a *reflexive* sense. It means "to seek guidance for oneself," "to allow oneself to be guided," "to take the guidance and walk by it."

Now read the ending again with that in mind.

It does not say *Allāhu lā yahdīhim* — "Allah does not guide them." That would be a statement about Allah's withholding.

It says *fa-hum lā yahtadūn* — "*they themselves* are not taking the guidance."

[PAUSE]

This is staggering. Let me say what the grammar is telling us.

The ayah has just described two things: (1) they are prostrating to the sun *short of* Allah, and (2) Shaytan has decorated their deeds. And now, the conclusion. You might expect: *and Allah has abandoned them.* You might expect: *and they are misguided.* You might expect a passive verb, something done *to* them.

But Allah uses the active reflexive. **They are not guiding themselves toward guidance.** They are not reaching out for it. The guidance is, in some sense, available — but the hand that should be reaching is at rest. They are not even *seeking.*

Why is this happening? Read backward through the ayah. Because Shaytan has made their deeds beautiful. Why are they not seeking guidance? **Because they do not feel lost.** A person who feels lost asks for directions. A person who feels found does not.

The decoration of *zayyana* and the failure of *yahtadūn* are not two separate things. They are cause and effect. When your room has been decorated by Shaytan, you do not feel the bareness of the walls. When you do not feel the bareness, you do not seek the architect. When you do not seek, you do not find.

[PAUSE]

Notice the absence here — and this is crucial. Throughout the Quran, when Allah describes those who *seek* guidance and don't find it, He still mentions Allah. *Allāhu yahdī man yashāʾ* — Allah guides whom He wills. There is a relationship described, even if asymmetric.

But in this ayah? Allah is not in the second half. The second half is *Shaytan, them, the path, them.* Allah's name appears once — in *min dūni-llāh* — and then the rest of the ayah is the closed loop of a person, their decorated deeds, the satanic hand on the rudder, and a path they have been steered away from.

The grammatical absence of Allah's name in the second half *enacts* the spiritual condition the ayah is describing. **Allah is no longer in their frame of reference.** Not because He is absent — He is never absent — but because their attention has been so thoroughly redirected that He no longer registers in the ecosystem of their thought.

This is the third door. And now the bridge.

---

### Bridge

We have walked through three pieces of grammar:

- *Min dūn* — they reached, but stopped short.
- *Zayyana* — Shaytan dressed up their stopping place.
- *Lā yahtadūn* — and so they no longer reach at all.

Notice how these three are a *sequence*. They describe how a soul ends up in spiritual stasis. First the reach falls short. Then the stopping place is decorated until it feels like the destination. Then the seeking dies entirely.

This is not just a description of pagan Saba. This is the anatomy of how any human heart ends up in a place that looks beautiful from the inside and is *dūn* from the outside.

Let's now ask what this reveals about reality itself — and about us.

---

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One — The Decorated Cage

Let me start somewhere uncomfortable.

Have you ever known someone who is destroying their life and is *certain they are flourishing*?

You watch a friend in a relationship that is corroding their soul, and they describe it to you as the deepest love they've ever known. You see a colleague burning everything to climb a ladder, and they tell you with shining eyes how aligned with their purpose they feel. You watch someone who is angry every day, alienated from everyone who loves them, and they explain that they have finally *cut out the toxic people* and feel free.

You can see the cage. They cannot.

Why?

Because the cage has been *zayyana*. Decorated. The bars have been hung with curtains. A vase of flowers sits on the floor. The light has been adjusted. From the inside, it doesn't look like a cage at all. It looks like a room.

[PAUSE]

This is what the ayah is describing — not just for sun-worshippers in ancient Saba, but for anyone whose deeds have been arranged in such a way that *the experience of doing them feels like the right thing.*

Now here is the question that should land hard:

**Whose room has been decorated for them?**

Mine? Yours?

Don't answer too quickly. Because the whole architecture of the ayah is that the worshippers in Saba did not feel deceived. They felt religious. They felt magnificent. The hoopoe sees the truth of their condition because the hoopoe is *outside* the room. They are inside it.

Modern psychology has a concept for this — *ego-syntonic*. A behavior or belief is ego-syntonic when it feels like *me*, when it harmonizes with my self-concept, so I do not experience it as a problem. Pathological narcissism is ego-syntonic. Many addictions are ego-syntonic. The reason they are so hard to treat is not that the person cannot stop. It is that the person *does not feel anything is wrong.*

The Quran named this fourteen hundred years ago. *Zayyana lahumu-sh-shayṭānu aʿmālahum.* Shaytan's signature move is not to make you feel guilty. It is to make sure you don't.

[PAUSE]

So how do you know if you are inside a decorated room?

You can't ask yourself "am I doing the right thing?" — because the decoration *is* your answer to that question. It will feel right. That feeling is exactly what is being manufactured.

You have to ask a different question. The Quran gives us the test, hidden in the same ayah:

**Is what I am doing taking me toward Allah, or stopping me at *dūn*?**

Not *does it feel meaningful.* Not *does it feel beautiful.* Not *do other people approve.* Those metrics have already been compromised. The only metric that survives is direction.

If your career, your relationships, your opinions, your aesthetics, your activism, your religiosity — *if any of them* — are giving you a beautiful experience but not bringing you closer to the One who made the experience possible, then something has been decorated, and it is not what you think it is.

**Practical application:** Pick one thing in your life right now that gives you the most pride. The one accomplishment, identity, or relationship that you most strongly associate with "I am a good person because of this." Hold it up against this question: does it bring me to *Allah*, or has it become a place I rest *short of* Him? If you cannot honestly answer, that itself is data. The decoration is so good that you cannot see the room.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two — The Reach Is Built Into You

Now turn the ayah inside out.

If you stand back from the verse, you notice something the translation cannot capture. Allah is not describing the people of Saba as godless. He is not describing them as having no religious instinct. The opposite — they are *prostrating*. Foreheads on the floor. The full physical posture of submission. They have not lost the impulse to worship. **They have only lost the address.**

This is one of the most hopeful and one of the most terrifying truths in the Quran:

**The reach is built into you.**

Every human being is born with what the Quran elsewhere calls *fiṭrah* — the original instinct to seek the High. Atheists feel it. Polytheists feel it. The most secular person you know feels it. They will deny it in language, and then live it in their behavior — they will worship something. A career. A nation. A romantic partner. An ideology. A version of themselves they are trying to become.

The forehead always finds a floor. The question is what is under the floor.

This is why the ayah uses *yasjudūna* — they are *making sujūd*. The Quran does not say "they had no religion." It says they had a religion, and the religion fell *dūn*.

[PAUSE]

Hear this carefully, because it changes how you look at the world around you.

Every form of worship you see in the modern world — every cult of celebrity, every devotion to productivity, every mystical attachment to ideology, every collapsing into a relationship as if it were salvation — is a forehead reaching for a floor. The reach is correct. The address is short.

You don't need to despair at how irreligious the world looks. The world is not irreligious. **The world has misaddressed its sujūd.**

And this is the work of daʿwah — and the work of your own life. Not to teach people to worship. They already do. But to extend the reach. To say: *the One you are reaching for is higher than you have stopped. Don't stop at the sun. Don't stop at the success. Don't stop at the lover. Don't stop at the cause. There is more above. Reach.*

[PAUSE]

There is also a personal application here, and it is intimate.

When you feel yourself sliding into despair, into addiction, into obsessive attachment to a person or an outcome — the Quranic diagnosis is not "you are weak." It is "*your sujūd has misaddressed itself.*" You were built to prostrate. The energy is correct. It has just landed on something that cannot bear the weight of being worshipped.

The sun cannot hold the weight of your worship. The sun is itself a created, dependent, command-following servant of Allah — *fa-lā tasjudū li-sh-shamsi wa lā li-l-qamar* (41:37) — "do not prostrate to the sun or the moon." Neither can a person. Neither can your career. Neither can your reputation. They will all collapse under the weight of being worshipped, because they were not designed to bear it.

Only Allah is *Aḥad* — singular, indivisible, not contingent on anything. Only He can bear the full weight of a forehead pressed all the way down. Everything else, when you give it your full sujūd, breaks under the load. And then so do you.

**Practical application:** Notice this week, in real time, where your sujūd is being misaddressed. Not the explicit sujūd in ṣalāh — the implicit ones. What are you most afraid of losing? What approval are you most addicted to? What outcome would devastate you if it failed? That is where your forehead is pressing. If it is anything *dūn-Allāh*, the practice this week is to redirect the *same energy* — not less of it, the same — toward the One it was always meant for. Pray about that fear. Pray about that loss. Take the heat of your attachment and aim it upward.

[PAUSE]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at what we have walked through.

The ayah is an X-ray of how a human soul ends up worshipping the wrong thing while feeling religious about it.

The reach falls short — *min dūn*.
The stopping place is decorated — *zayyana*.
And so the seeking dies — *lā yahtadūn*.

Three pieces of grammar. One spiritual disease. And the same disease, in its outer signs, is what we see across the entire modern world — in others, but more urgently, in ourselves. Not the worship of the sun, but the same architecture: a high reach that landed short, a stopping place that has been decorated until it feels like home, and a soul that has stopped looking for anything higher because it does not feel anything is missing.

The hoopoe could see what the queen could not — because the hoopoe was outside the room.

The Quran is, for us, what the hoopoe was for Saba. It is the voice from outside the decorated room, telling us what we cannot see from inside it. The work of tadabbur is to *let the voice in* — to let the diagnosis land before we explain it away.

[PAUSE]

### Questions to Carry

1. Where in my life have I stopped at *dūn* — at something high, but not the Highest? What is the brightest created thing I have allowed to take the place of the Creator in my heart's economy?

2. What is the most decorated room in my life right now — the place where everything *feels* aligned with my deepest values, but I have never honestly tested whether the direction is toward Allah or away from Him?

3. Where am I prostrating without realizing it? What is the floor my forehead keeps finding when I am not paying attention?

4. If a hoopoe flew over my life today and reported back to Sulayman, what would it say I worship?

5. *Fa-hum lā yahtadūn.* Am I actively seeking guidance — *yahtadī*, the reflexive — or have I gone quiet in my asking, because I no longer feel anything is missing?

### One-Sentence Distillation

> The deadliest form of misguidance is not the one that feels like sin — it is the one that feels like worship, performed toward the most magnificent thing your eye can see, while the One who made the eye and the magnificence is just one degree higher than your reach.

### The Closing Invitation

You do not need to find a sun to worship. The world has handed you many. They are well-decorated. They feel like home.

The invitation of this ayah is not to add more religiosity to your life. It is to *audit the direction* of the religiosity you already have. To ask, of every place your forehead presses — literally and metaphorically — *is this taking me to Allah, or stopping me on the way?*

Let the hoopoe speak. Let the diagnosis land. Then let the reach extend.

### Du'a

> Yā Allāh, we are people whose foreheads always find a floor. Do not let our sujūd land short of You. Strip the decoration from the deeds Shaytan has dressed up in our lives, so we can see plainly what is *dūn* and what is You. Make us of those who not only are guided, but who reach for guidance — *al-muhtadīn*. Ameen.
