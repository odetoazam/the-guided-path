# When Is This Promise? — The Question That Became Its Own Answer

---

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the words first. Sit with them before we go anywhere.

> [ayah:67:25] وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَـٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ
>
> *Wa yaqūlūna matā hādhā-l-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīn.*
>
> "And they say: *When* is this promise, if you are truthful?"

> [ayah:67:26] قُلْ إِنَّمَا ٱلْعِلْمُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَإِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ
>
> *Qul innamā-l-ʿilmu ʿinda-llāhi wa innamā ana nadhīrun mubīn.*
>
> "Say: The knowledge is only with Allah, and I am only a clear warner."

> [ayah:67:27] فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهُ زُلْفَةً سِيٓـَٔتْ وُجُوهُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ وَقِيلَ هَـٰذَا ٱلَّذِى كُنتُم بِهِۦ تَدَّعُونَ
>
> *Fa-lammā raʾawhu zulfatan sīʾat wujūhu-lladhīna kafarū wa qīla hādhā-lladhī kuntum bihī taddaʿūn.*
>
> "But when they see it drawn near, the faces of those who disbelieved will be grieved, and it will be said: This is that which you used to call for."

---

### Rebuilding the world

To feel what is happening here, you have to stand in Makkah, before the migration, when the Muslims were few and mocked and the message was new.

Picture the scene. The Prophet ﷺ has been telling them — patiently, for years — that there is a Day. A Day when the dominion they fight over, the wealth they hoard, the status they kill for, all of it dissolves, and every soul stands alone before the One who owns it all. Surah Al-Mulk has been building this case the entire chapter. *Tabāraka-lladhī biyadihi-l-mulk* — Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion. The whole surah is an argument about ownership. Who really holds the sky from falling? Who really feeds you? Who holds the birds up in the air? Who could bring you water if the springs sank into the earth tomorrow?

And the disbelievers of Makkah have run out of counter-arguments. You can feel it in the text. They don't say "the sky holds itself up." They don't offer another god who feeds them. They have no rebuttal to the ownership argument.

So they change the subject.

They reach for the one move that always feels like winning when you're losing: **they demand a date.**

"Fine. There's a Day. When? Give us the day. Give us the hour. You claim you're truthful — *prove it.* Tell us *when.*"

It is a taunt dressed as a question. And notice — this is not an honest seeker asking for information. The classical commentators are clear: al-Qurtubi calls it plainly *istihzāʾ* — mockery. They are not asking because they want to prepare. They are asking because they think the question has no answer, and an unanswerable question feels, in the moment, like a victory.

And notice *who* they aim it at. Al-Jalalayn and al-Muyassar both read the taunt as flung not only at the Prophet ﷺ but at the believers as a body — *"if* **you** *are truthful,"* plural, the small mocked community as a whole. It is the jeer thrown at a group that has staked everything on a claim: *go on then, the lot of you — name the day.* And the "promise" they mock is no vague threat; the mufassirūn identify it precisely — *waʿd al-ḥashr,* the promise of the **Gathering,** the day all creation is assembled for reckoning. That is the specific thing they pretended could never arrive.

---

### The Hook

Here is what I want you to hold onto as we walk through these three verses.

They asked a question — *matā hādhā*, "when is **this**?"

And the Quran does something staggering with that question. It does not just refuse to answer it. It picks up their exact words, carries them across the verses like a thread, and hands the words back to them — in the one place, at the one moment, where the answer arrives in full.

Their question becomes the instrument of their own answer.

Watch the word *hādhā* — "this." Watch where it appears and where it returns. Because by the time we reach the end of verse 27, the mocking "when is **this**?" has become a sentence spoken over them: "**This** is the one..."

Let's earn it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The question that asks *when* to avoid asking *whether*

Let me teach you something about questions before we touch the verse.

There is a kind of question that is really a refusal wearing the costume of curiosity. You know this kind. A person says, "Okay, but *when* exactly is this going to happen?" — and they are not actually asking for a calendar date. They are signaling that they have decided it *won't* happen, and they want you to fail to produce the date so they can feel justified in their disbelief.

Notice the difference between two questions:

- "**Is** this true?" — that question is dangerous, because it might end with *yes.*
- "**When** is this?" — that question feels safe, because you can always demand more precision than anyone can give, and walk away looking like the reasonable one.

Hold that distinction. Now look at what they actually said.

> **مَتَىٰ هَـٰذَا ٱلْوَعْدُ**
>
> *Matā hādhā-l-waʿd* — "When is this promise?"

The first word is **مَتَىٰ** — *matā* — "when." Not *a-ḥaqqun hādhā* — "is this real?" Not *kayfa* — "how could this be?" They have skipped clean past the question of *whether* the Day is real and jumped straight to *when.*

Let's sit with this, because it is more revealing than it looks.

To ask "*when* is the promise" is to grammatically concede that there *is* a promise. You cannot ask for the timing of a thing whose existence you reject. If I sincerely believed there was no meeting, I wouldn't ask "what time is the meeting?" — I'd say "there is no meeting."

So buried inside their mockery is an admission they didn't mean to make. **The very structure of their taunt assumes the thing they're taunting.** They have moved the goalposts from existence to schedule — and the moment you argue about a thing's schedule, you've stopped arguing about whether it exists.

Now look at the next word: **هَـٰذَا** — *hādhā* — "this." A demonstrative. "*This* promise." Not "a promise." Not "some promise you keep mentioning." **This** one. The definite, specific, already-known promise.

You only point and say "*this*" at something already present in the room of the conversation. Their own choice of word betrays them again: the promise of the Day was so thoroughly established by the Prophet's message that they could gesture at it — *this* one, the famous one, the one you keep warning us about. The mufassirūn fill in the referent without hesitation — *hādhā-l-waʿd* is *waʿd al-ḥashr,* the promise of the Gathering, the reckoning so often named that a single demonstrative was enough to summon it. The mockery only works because the thing being mocked is already fixed and clear in everyone's mind.

[PAUSE]

So here is what this single opening clause reveals: **they were closer to belief than their tone admitted.** Their grammar believed more than their hearts were willing to. The "when" conceded the "whether." The "this" conceded its reality. All that remained between them and faith was the surrender they refused to make — so they buried the surrender under a demand for a date.

That is the anatomy of a certain kind of disbelief. Not the honest "I don't think this is real," but the evasive "prove the details to me on my terms, and until you do, I'm free."

---

### Element Two: "If you are truthful" — the conditional that was already decided

Now the sting in the tail of their question.

> **إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ**
>
> *In kuntum ṣādiqīn* — "if you are truthful."

Let me first teach the structure. **إِن** — *in* — is the conditional particle, "if." And **كُنتُمْ** — *kuntum* — is a verb from the root *kāna,* "to be," but notice its tense: it is **perfect** — a completed form, "you *were* / you *are* in a settled state of being." Then **صَـٰدِقِينَ** — *ṣādiqīn* — "truthful ones," from the root *Ṣ-D-Q,* truth, the same root that gives us *ṣidq* (sincerity) and *ṣadaqah* (the charity that proves your faith is true).

Here is what's remarkable about adding *in kuntum ṣādiqīn* — "if you are truthful" — to the end.

They did not need it. The question "when is the promise?" is complete without it. The addition does no informational work. It does *emotional* work. It is the smirk at the end of the sentence. It is the conditional that exists only to imply its opposite — *we both know you're not.* When you say to someone, "Go on then — *if you're so honest,*" you are not genuinely entertaining the possibility of their honesty. You are weaponizing the word.

Notice something deeper. They tied **truthfulness** to **timing.** "If you're truthful — tell us *when.*" They made the Prophet's *ṣidq,* his very integrity, hostage to a piece of information no human possesses. It is a rigged test. Demand the one thing that cannot be produced, then declare the failure to produce it a failure of honesty.

Stay with me, because the Quran's response is going to dismantle this trap so cleanly it will take your breath.

They said: *your truthfulness depends on your knowing the time.*

And the answer is going to say: **the time was never the point of truthfulness at all.** A truthful warner and a knower of the hour are two completely different things — and the disbelievers have deliberately confused them.

### Element Three: *Innamā* — the word that draws a line reality cannot cross

Now the reply. And this is the architectural center of the whole passage.

> **قُل إِنَّمَا ٱلْعِلْمُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَإِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ**
>
> *Qul innamā-l-ʿilmu ʿinda-llāhi wa innamā ana nadhīrun mubīn.*
>
> "Say: The knowledge is *only* with Allah, and I am *only* a clear warner."

I want to teach you one word before anything else: **إِنَّمَا** — *innamā.*

In Arabic, *innamā* is a particle of *ḥaṣr* — restriction, exclusivity. It does two things at once: it affirms one thing and it negates everything else. When you say *innamā X,* you are saying "X — and *only* X, nothing besides." It builds a fence. Everything inside the fence is true; everything outside it is false. English has to stretch to capture it — "only," "nothing but," "none other than."

Now watch — the answer uses *innamā* **twice,** and it uses it to build two fences that, between them, completely dissolve the disbelievers' trap.

**Fence one:** *innamā-l-ʿilmu ʿinda-llāh* — "The knowledge is *only* with Allah."

Look at where the knowledge is placed: **عِندَ ٱللَّهِ** — *ʿinda-llāh* — "with Allah," in His presence, in His keeping. The word *ʿinda* denotes a place of belonging, of custody. The knowledge of the hour is not lost, not undiscovered, not yet-to-be-calculated. It *exists* — perfectly, completely — but it sits in one custody only, and that custody is not human. This is exactly how al-Muyassar reads the line: the knowledge of *the time the Hour rises* is something Allah *ikhtaṣṣa bihi* — has **reserved to Himself alone,** made His own exclusive possession. The fence of *innamā* draws a perfect circle around Allah and leaves every created being, the Prophet ﷺ included, on the outside.

This is the trap springing harmlessly shut. They said, "If you're truthful, name the time." The answer says: *the time was never mine to possess in the first place.* You demanded I produce something that was never part of my role. Asking the warner for the hour is like demanding the lighthouse keeper tell you the exact minute the ship will hit the rocks. That is not his job. His job is the light.

**Fence two:** *wa innamā ana nadhīrun mubīn* — "and I am *only* a clear warner."

Here the second *innamā* fences in the Prophet's role. **نَذِيرٌ** — *nadhīr* — "warner." And note the form of this word: it is *faʿīl,* a form that in Arabic carries intensity and permanence — not "one who warned once" but a *warner* by constitution, a warning made flesh. And **مُّبِينٌ** — *mubīn,* from the root *B-Y-N,* to make clear, to make distinct — "a *clarifying* warner," one whose warning leaves no fog, no ambiguity, no excuse. Al-Muyassar unpacks the whole role in a single breath: *I am only a warner — I make you fear the consequence of your disbelief, and I clarify for you what Allah has commanded me to clarify* — and then he adds the telling phrase, *ghāyata-l-bayān,* "to the utmost limit of clarity." That is the entire content of the messenger's mandate: not the date, but the danger; not the schedule of the Hour, but its certainty, laid out so plainly that no one could later plead they were never told.

[PAUSE]

Do you see what these two fences do when you place them side by side?

> The knowledge of the hour → **only** with Allah.
> The role of the messenger → **only** to warn, clearly.

Between them there is no gap and no overlap. The disbelievers had jammed two things together — "knowing the time" and "being truthful" — and the double *innamā* surgically separates them. **Truthfulness lives entirely in the warning. The timing lives entirely with Allah.** A warner can be perfectly, totally truthful and not know the hour, because the hour was never the content of his truthfulness. The content of his truthfulness was: *it is coming, and you are not ready.*

And notice the mercy hiding in this. If the messenger *did* tell you the date — say, four hundred years from now — what would you do? You would relax. You would defer. You'd plan to repent in year three hundred and ninety-nine. The withholding of the date is not evasion. **It is the thing that keeps the warning alive.** A warner who hands you a date hands you permission to wait. A warner who says "it is near and the time is hidden" hands you the only thing that can move a heart: *now.*

---

### The bridge

So look at what the linguistic construction has shown us.

The *matā* — "when" — that conceded the "whether." The *hādhā* — "this" — that admitted the promise was already real and known. The *in kuntum ṣādiqīn* — the rigged test that bound truthfulness to timing. And then the double *innamā* that cut the rigged test in two and revealed that the warner's honesty never depended on the hour at all.

That is the architecture of the words. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about an argument in Makkah fourteen centuries ago — but about something happening inside every human chest, including yours and mine.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The "when" we use to avoid the "now"

Let me start not in Makkah but in your own life, because you already know this move. You have made it.

You know a thing is true and you know it requires something of you — and so you ask a question about its *timing* in order to delay its *claim.*

"I'll get serious about my prayer — when life calms down."
"I'll fix that relationship — when the time is right."
"I'll change how I'm living — when I'm older, when I've had my fun, when the moment comes."

Every one of those is *matā hādhā-l-waʿd.* "When is this thing?" — asked not to find out, but to push the thing past the horizon of *now.* We have a remarkable capacity to convert a present obligation into a future appointment, and to feel, in the act of scheduling it, as though we have already begun to honor it. The man who says "I'll repent later" has, in his own mind, already half-repented. He has not. He has only relocated the demand to a country called Later, which is the one country no one ever actually arrives in.

This is what the disbelievers were doing on a cosmic scale. The Day made a claim on them *today* — change how you live, who you worship, what you chase. And the question "*when* is this Day?" was the machine they built to keep that claim permanently in the future tense.

Now let the verse illuminate the experience.

The reply — *innamā-l-ʿilmu ʿinda-llāh* — refuses to play. It will not give them a date, because a date is exactly the drug they're seeking. The whole point of hiding the hour is to collapse the distance between *now* and *then.* If you do not know whether the Day is in four hundred years or four hours, you cannot defer to it. The only safe assumption is that it could be *now.* And a heart that lives as though the reckoning could be *now* is the only kind of heart that actually changes.

The Prophet ﷺ said it directly in another place — that among the signs of the Hour is that it comes upon people *suddenly,* while a man is mending his pool, or lifting food to his mouth. The suddenness is the mercy. The hidden date is not Allah withholding a kindness. **It is Allah withholding the one piece of information that would let you keep procrastinating.**

So here is what this changes about tomorrow. The question is never "*when* is the reckoning?" — that question is a trap you set for yourself. The question Allah hands you instead is: "*If it were today, where would I stand?*" And that question, unlike the first one, you can actually answer — and acting on the answer is the entire point.

[PAUSE]

What is the one thing you have moved to the country called Later — that you already know, in the quiet, belongs to Now?

### Theme Two: The question you fire at the sky comes back as a verdict

Now we arrive at the verse that should make the hair on your arms stand up. Verse 27.

Let me first bring you into the human experience underneath it. There is a particular horror in getting *exactly what you asked for.* The person who taunts "go on, do it then" — and it is done. The one who scoffs "I'd like to see you try" — and they do. The mockery you flung as a challenge, returning to you as an event. There is no shelter in that moment, because you cannot even claim you were ambushed. *You called for this.*

Now read what the Quran does.

> **فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهُ زُلْفَةً**
>
> *Fa-lammā raʾawhu zulfatan* — "But when they see **it** drawn near…"

Notice the pronoun. **رَأَوْهُ** — *raʾawhu* — "they saw **it**." Saw *what?* The verse doesn't restate the noun. It just says "it." And the "it" reaches all the way back across two verses to grab their own word: *hādhā-l-waʿd,* "**this** promise." The thing they pointed at and mocked — "when is *this*?" — is now the "it" they are *seeing.* The abstract, deferred, scoffed-at promise has become a thing in front of their eyes.

And the word **زُلْفَةً** — *zulfatan,* from the root *Z-L-F,* meaning nearness, proximity, drawing close. They asked "*when?*" — demanding a far-off date they were sure could never be produced. And the answer, when it finally comes, is not a date at all. It is a *distance:* **near.** Right here. Closed in. The thing they tried to push over the horizon is now pressed against their faces.

[PAUSE]

> **سِيٓـَٔتْ وُجُوهُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟**
>
> *Sīʾat wujūhu-lladhīna kafarū* — "the faces of those who disbelieved are grieved."

**سِيٓـَٔتْ** — *sīʾat* — from the root *S-W-Aʾ,* the root of evil, ugliness, of being stricken with distress. It is in the passive: the faces *are made* grieved, are *struck* with woe — something is done *to* them. And it lands on the **وُجُوه** — the *faces.* Of all the body, the face. Because the face is where a human being cannot lie. The face is the one place the inner state surfaces whether you will it or not. These are the same faces that wore the smirk in verse 25 — *in kuntum ṣādiqīn,* "if you're so truthful" — and now the smirk is gone, struck off, replaced by a dread that no composure can hold back. **The face that mocked is the face that falls.**

And then — the verdict. Spoken over them.

> **وَقِيلَ هَـٰذَا ٱلَّذِى كُنتُم بِهِۦ تَدَّعُونَ**
>
> *Wa qīla hādhā-lladhī kuntum bihī taddaʿūn* — "and it is said: **This** is that which you used to call for."

There it is. **هَـٰذَا** — *hādhā* — "**this.**" The very word they opened with. *Matā hādhā-l-waʿd* — "when is **this**?" And now the reply, at last, in the only moment that could answer it: *hādhā-lladhī* — "**this** is the one."

The question and its answer are the same word. They asked "when is *this*?" and the universe answers "*this* — this right here — is what you called for." The demonstrative they used to point mockingly at a far-off abstraction is turned around and used to point at the reality now standing in front of them. *This. The one you meant. Here it is.*

And the final verb: **تَدَّعُونَ** — *taddaʿūn,* from the root *D-ʿ-W,* to call. The classical commentators read it in more than one way, and each adds a layer rather than competing. Some read it as "you used to *call for* it" — to demand it, to hasten it, exactly as they did in verse 25: *bring it on, when is it?* Others read it from the sense of *claim* — "you used to *deny / make claims* against it." Hold both, because together they capture the whole posture: these were people who *both* demanded it sarcastically *and* denied it would ever come. And it came. The thing you dared us to produce, and swore we never could — here it is, near, and your faces have already confessed what your tongues won't.

[PAUSE]

Let me name the universal thing under this.

Every soul is, in some way, *calling* for the meeting with reality — by how it lives. The one who lives as though there is no reckoning is calling for the reckoning to expose that. The one who lives in heedless mockery of the Day is, with every mocking word, addressing the Day directly: *come then.* And the most sobering teaching of verse 27 is that the call is *heard.* Reality is not indifferent to how we speak to it. The "when is this?" flung at the sky does not vanish. It is logged. And on a Day appointed by the only One who knows the date, it is read back to the one who said it, word for word, as the caption beneath the thing they refused to believe.

The mercy — and there is always mercy, even here — is that you are reading this *before* that day, not during it. The verse is shown to us *in advance.* That is the entire purpose of a *nadhīr mubīn,* a clear warner: to let you hear the verdict while there is still time to make sure it is never spoken over you. To read the ending while you can still change which character you are in it.

So what does this change about how you live tomorrow? It is this: become deliberate about what you are *calling for* with your life. Not just your words — your habits, your defaults, your private hours. Every life is a sentence addressed to reality. Make sure that when reality finally answers — *hādhā-lladhī kuntum bihī…* — "this is the one you used to call for" — the thing it points to is something you would have wept with joy to see, and not something that strikes the face.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architectural reveal

Now stand back and look at all three verses at once, because the structure is the proof of everything we've said.

**Verse 25 — the question:**
*matā* **hādhā** *-l-waʿd … in kuntum ṣādiqīn*
"When is **this** promise … if you *are* (*kuntum*)?"

**Verse 27 — the answer:**
*raʾawhu zulfatan … qīla* **hādhā** *-lladhī* **kuntum** *bihī taddaʿūn*
"They saw **it** drawn near … it is said: **this** is the one you *used to* (*kuntum*) call for."

Look at the threads pulled taut across the passage:

- The mocking **هَـٰذَا** *hādhā* ("this") of the question returns as the **هَـٰذَا** *hādhā* ("this") of the verdict. Same finger, pointing first in mockery, then at the standing reality.
- The **كُنتُمْ** *kuntum* ("you are") of "if you *are* truthful" returns as the **كُنتُمْ** *kuntum* ("you used to") of "you *used to* call for." Their own being-verb, turned from a taunt into a record.
- The far-off "**when**" they demanded is answered not with a time but with a nearness — **زُلْفَة** *zulfah.* They asked for distance; they got proximity.

And in the center of it all, in verse 26, sits the double **إِنَّمَا** — the two fences — that explain *why* the question could never be answered on their terms and *why* it will be answered, devastatingly, on Allah's: because the knowledge was *only* with Him, and the messenger's role was *only* to warn — clearly, in time, in advance.

The passage is a closed loop. **The question is the answer, returned.** That is not a coincidence of wording. That is the design — a mercy and a warning folded into the same architecture, so that the one who reads it today can hear the loop close *now,* while the ending can still be changed.

### Bringing it together

The linguistic journey and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

The grammar showed us a question engineered to defer — *when,* not *whether* — and a reply engineered to collapse the deferral — *only* with Allah, the date forever hidden so that *now* is the only honest posture.

The themes showed us our own hearts doing the same thing — converting present claims into future appointments, calling out to reality with how we live — and reality, patient but not deaf, answering at last with our own words.

One says: stop asking *when.* The other says: start watching *what you call for.* They are the same instruction. **Live as though the answer is now, because the One who holds the date designed the not-knowing precisely so that you would.**

### Questions to carry

- What have I filed under "Later" that I already know belongs to "Now"?
- When I demand proof "on my terms" before I'll submit — am I seeking, or am I deferring?
- If the promise were drawn *near* — *zulfah,* pressed against my face today — would my face fall, or lift?
- What is my life, by its habits and defaults, *calling out* to reality? What caption am I writing for myself?
- Do I treat the hidden hour as Allah withholding a kindness — or as Him giving me the only thing that keeps my heart awake?

### One-sentence distillation

They asked *when,* to keep the Day forever in the future — so He hid the date forever, to keep it forever *now,* and handed their own mocking word back to them as the verdict, while there was still time to never deserve it.

### The closing invitation

Something can shift after this — not as a burden laid on you, but as a door opened. The door is small and it is simply this: stop scheduling the surrender. Stop asking the sky *when.* Take the one thing you have exiled to Later and bring it home to Now — one prayer prayed on time, one wrong made right, one habit turned — and in doing so, change which sentence reality will one day read back to you. You have been shown the ending in advance. Almost no one is given that. Use it.

### A brief du'a

O Allah, You who alone hold the knowledge of the hour — do not let us be among those whose faces fall when they see it drawn near. Let the thing we have spent our lives calling toward be Your pleasure and Your face, so that when it is said *"this is the one you used to call for,"* it is said as a homecoming and not as a sentence. Wake our hearts to *now,* while *now* is still ours to use.
