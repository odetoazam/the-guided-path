- [MODERATE] "The word albāb does not mean 'understanding.' It means kernels." — Stated too categorically. The kernel etymology is lexically attested (Mufradāt al-Rāghib is cited in the morphology note), but *ulū-l-albāb* functions in Quranic usage as the standard phrase for rational/intellectual perception. Al-Jalālayn glosses the *albāb* in this very ayah as those who "take heed through its indication of Allah's oneness and power" — no kernel metaphor foregrounded. The reflection's own translation correctly renders it "kernel of understanding," which walks the overstatement back. The contrast with *ḥuṭām* (husk vs. kernel) is a legitimate literary device, but framing it as "albāb does *not* mean understanding" misrepresents the lexical record; the word carries both senses simultaneously. This is rhetorical overreach in service of a sound interpretive point, not a factual error.
