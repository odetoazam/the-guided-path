# Is Not Allah Enough? — The Question That Dissolves Every Manufactured Fear

> أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُۥ ۖ وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ ۚ وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ
>
> *Alaysa-llāhu bikāfin ʿabdah? Wa yukhawwifūnaka billadhīna min dūnih. Wa man yuḍlili-llāhu famā lahu min hād.*
>
> **"Is not Allah sufficient for His servant? Yet they try to frighten you with those besides Him. And whoever Allah leaves to stray — for him there is no guide at all."**

---

## INTRODUCTION: The Room Where the Threat Was Made

Before we open a single word, I want you to stand inside the moment this ayah answered.

Makkah. A man stands more or less alone against the entire religious, commercial, and tribal establishment of his city. He has been telling them, plainly, that the stone gods they have circled for generations are nothing — that they hear nothing, hold nothing, decide nothing. And the people who run that city, who profit from those gods, who built their whole identity around them, have run out of patience.

So they come to him with a threat. And notice the *kind* of threat. They don't only say *we* will harm you. The classical commentators — al-Baghawi reports this among the narrations around this ayah — record that they warned him: *stop insulting our gods, or one of them will strike you. You'll be touched with harm. You'll lose your mind. The idols themselves will afflict you.*

[PAUSE]

Sit with how strange that is. They are threatening him **with the very things he just told them are powerless.** They are pointing at the stones and saying, *be afraid of these.*

And here is the question I want hanging in the air for the next hour: **how do you answer a threat like that?** Not with an argument. Not with a counter-threat. Allah answers it with a *question* — one short question — and that question does something an argument could never do. It doesn't refute the fear. It dissolves the ground the fear was standing on.

This is one of those ayahs that, once it enters you, quietly rearranges how you meet every intimidation for the rest of your life — every boss, every diagnosis, every market, every crowd, every "or else." Because the people of Makkah are long gone, but the *structure* of what they did to the Prophet ﷺ is done to you and me constantly. Someone, or something, is always pointing at a powerless thing and telling us to be afraid of it.

Let's find out what Allah said back.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One — The Question That Is Really an Answer: *Alaysa*

Let me teach you something about questions before we touch this one.

There is a kind of question we ask not because we don't know the answer, but because we want the *listener* to arrive at the answer themselves, out of their own mouth, so they can't escape it. When a mother asks her child, "Didn't I tell you not to touch that?" — she is not gathering information. She knows what she told him. The question is a *trap of clarity*. It forces the child to say "yes."

In Arabic grammar, this is the heart of the **rhetorical interrogative**, and there is a specific form of it that scholars call *istifhām taqrīrī* — a question asked **to compel affirmation.** When that question is built on a negation — *is it not...?* — the only honest answer the listener can give is the emphatic Arabic word **بَلَىٰ — balā — "yes, absolutely, it certainly is."**

Now look at how the ayah opens:

> **أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُ**
> *Alaysa-llāhu bikāfin ʿabdah*
> "Is not Allah sufficient for His servant?"

The first word, *alaysa*, is the negating verb *laysa* — "is not" — with the questioning hamza fused onto its front. **"Is not...?"**

Let's sit with why this is built as a question at all. Allah could have said, flatly, *Allahu kāfin ʿabdah* — "Allah is sufficient for His servant." A clean declarative statement. True, complete, beautiful.

But He didn't. He bent it into a question. And the question is doing something the statement could not.

A statement is something Allah tells *you*. You receive it. You can nod at it, file it away, even believe it abstractly while your stomach stays in knots. **But a question of this kind makes you answer.** It reaches into your chest, past your defenses, and pulls the *balā* — the "yes, of course He is" — out of *you*. You are no longer a person being told that Allah is enough. You have become a person who just *confessed* that Allah is enough.

[PAUSE]

Notice something else, and this is where it becomes almost unbearable in its tenderness. The Prophet ﷺ is being *frightened*. That's the situation. Fear is in the room. And Allah does not begin His response with "Do not be afraid." He does not begin with a command at all. He begins by inviting His servant to *remember something he already knows* — and to say it back.

Because the cure for a manufactured fear is rarely an order to stop feeling it. The cure is to be walked, gently, to the one fact that makes the fear absurd, and to be the one who *names* that fact. **Is not Allah enough?** And the heart, even a frightened heart, cannot help it: *balā. Yes. He is.*

That is the difference between a verse that informs you and a verse that *moves* you. This one was built, from its first syllable, to move you.

---

### Element Two — The "Extra" Letter and the Missing One: *bi-Kāfin*

Now we go closer, into the second and third words, and I have to teach you two small grammatical facts, because together they unlock the whole verse.

> **بِكَافٍ عَبْدَهُ** — *bikāfin ʿabdah* — "sufficient for His servant"

**The first fact: that little ب — that "bi" — at the front of *kāfin*.**

In Arabic, you would expect the sentence to read simply *Allahu kāfin ʿabdah* — "Allah is a sufficer of His servant." The word *kāfin* alone already means "one who suffices, one who is enough." So what is that extra *bi* doing glued to the front of it?

Grammarians call this a **harf zā'id** — an "added" or "extra" particle. But — and hold onto this — **"extra" never means "pointless" in the Quran.** A particle is called *zā'id* because it is not required to complete the *grammar*. It is added precisely to complete the *emphasis*. It is the linguistic equivalent of a hand pressed flat on the table while someone says, "He is *enough*." The grammar didn't need the hand. The *certainty* did.

So *bikāfin* is not merely "sufficient." It is something closer to **"truly, unquestionably, more-than-sufficient."** The *bi* is the weight of Allah's own emphasis laid onto the word, in the very sentence where His servant is being made to feel that Allah might *not* be enough — that he needs to fear the idols too. The intimidators are whispering *you are not safe.* And the structure of the divine reply doesn't just say *you are safe* — its grammar leans in and presses down on the word.

[PAUSE]

**The second fact — and this one is breathtaking — is the letter that is *missing*.**

Look at the word in the script: **كَافٍ**. The root is *k-f-y* (ك-ف-ي), the root of *kifāya*, sufficiency. The full active participle would be **كَافِي — kāfī** — ending in a *yā'*. But the *yā'* is gone. It has been dropped, leaving only the *tanwīn*, that little *-in* sound: *kāf-in*.

This is not a typo and it is not an accident — it is documented, and the scholars dwell on it. Al-Qurtubi, in his tafsir, stops directly on this word and explains: **the *yā'* was dropped** because of the meeting of its silence with the silence of the *tanwīn* that follows. He even notes that some of the Arabs would *restore* the *yā'* when pausing — "kāfī" — going back to the original. But the reading that came down, the script we recite, **drops it.**

Now — I want to be careful and honest with you here, because the methodology of grounded reflection demands it. The *dropping of the yā'* is a documented grammatical fact; al-Qurtubi confirms exactly why it happens. What I'm about to offer is not a claim *about the grammar* — the grammarians give a phonetic reason — but an invitation to *contemplate* the shape that results. Hold it as reflection, not as a rule.

And the shape is this: the word for Allah's sufficiency arrives in your mouth **lighter than its full form, almost weightless, ending on an open breath** — *kāf-in* — rather than the heavier, closed *kāfī*. There is no straining in it. The grammar tells you it is more-than-enough — *bikāfin* — and the very *sound* of it is effortless, unforced, exhaled rather than pushed. **Sufficiency, when it is real, does not strain.** Allah being enough is not a heavy thing He must carry. It is the lightest fact in existence.

Remember this dropped *yā'*. We are going to find it again at the very end of the verse, and when we do, the whole architecture will open.

---

### Element Three — The Manufactured Fear: *Yukhawwifūnaka*

Now the middle of the ayah, and the word at the center of the entire human drama:

> **وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِ**
> *wa yukhawwifūnaka billadhīna min dūnih*
> "Yet they try to frighten you with those besides Him."

The word is **يُخَوِّفُونَكَ — *yukhawwifūnaka*** — from the root *kh-w-f* (خ-و-ف), the root of *khawf*, fear. And I need you to see what *form* of the verb this is, because the form is the meaning.

Let me teach the concept first. In Arabic, the simplest form of this root is *khāfa* — "he was afraid." That's a thing that happens *inside* a person. But here the verb has been moved into **Form II** — *khawwafa* — which adds a doubling, a *shadda*, to the middle letter. And what Form II so often does is **make someone else do the thing.** *Khāfa* is "to be afraid." *Khawwafa* is **"to make afraid" — to instill fear, to manufacture it in another person.**

So *yukhawwifūnaka* does not mean "they are afraid." It means **"they are working to *produce* fear in you. They are *manufacturing* dread and aiming it at your heart."** Fear here is not a weather event that happened to the Prophet ﷺ. It is a *product* that other people are *building* and trying to install in him.

[PAUSE]

Now listen to the word itself. *Yu-khaw-wi-fū-na-ka.* That doubled middle — *khaww* — the way the sound thickens and drags in the throat before it can move on. There is something laborious in it, something that has to be worked at. And that is exactly right, because **manufacturing fear in someone else is labor.** It takes effort to convince a person to dread a powerless thing. The intimidators are *working.* You can hear them working in the very texture of the verb.

And then — read what they are working *with*:

> *billadhīna min dūnih* — "with those besides Him."

Stay with this. Earlier I told you to read absence as carefully as presence. Here is a presence and an absence side by side, and the contrast is the whole point.

On one side: **ٱللَّهُ** — *Allah.* Named. Singular. Definite. The proper name of the Real. And His relationship to His servant is *possessive, intimate*: **عَبْدَهُ — ʿabdah — His** servant. *My* one.

On the other side, the things they want him to fear: **ٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِ** — *"those who are besides Him."* No names. No faces. A vague, plural *those.* They are not even granted a noun of their own — they are defined entirely by what they are *not*: not Him, *below* Him, *other* than Him, in His shadow — *min dūnih.* The idols cannot be described by what they *are*, because they *are* nothing. They can only be described by their distance from the One who is.

[PAUSE]

Do you see what the grammar has done? It has staged the entire confrontation in the architecture of the sentence. **One named, intimate, sufficient God — versus a faceless, nameless, plural *those.*** And we are asked: *with which of these is your heart going to live?*

The intimidators are pouring all their labor — that thick, dragging *khaww* — into building a fear out of *nothing*, out of "those-besides-Him." And the ayah lets the emptiness of their raw material show right there in the words. **You cannot build a real fear out of nothing. But people will exhaust themselves trying, and they will try it on you.**

### The Bridge

So look at what these three pieces have given us. A *question* that pulls the answer out of your own chest — *is He not enough?* A word for sufficiency carrying Allah's own emphatic weight, arriving weightless on the tongue — *bikāfin.* And a manufactured, laborious fear built out of faceless nothings — *yukhawwifūnaka billadhīna min dūnih.*

The grammar has drawn the whole battlefield. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself, about *your* reality, tomorrow morning, when something points at a powerless thing and tells you to be afraid.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One — Who Is Manufacturing Your Fear?

Let me start where you actually live.

You woke up today inside an economy of fear. I mean that almost literally. There are entire industries whose business model is *yukhawwifūnaka* — to manufacture dread in you and aim it at your heart, because a frightened person clicks, buys, complies, scrolls, and stays. The headline is engineered to frighten you. The notification is engineered to frighten you. The comparison the algorithm serves you — someone richer, calmer, more married, more successful — is engineered to make you afraid that you are falling behind. Even the people around you, often without meaning to, pass their fears to you like a cold: *aren't you worried about money? aren't you worried about what they'll think? aren't you worried, aren't you worried, aren't you worried.*

Here is what the ayah lets you finally see clearly: **fear, very often, is not something that happens to you. It is something done to you.** It is a *product*. There is labor behind it — that dragging *khaww* — and there is usually someone, or some system, profiting from the install.

And the second thing the ayah lets you see is even more freeing. Look again at what the fear is always built *out of*: **al-ladhīna min dūnih** — those *besides Him.* Anything that is not Allah. A market. A crowd's opinion. A stone. A diagnosis read without *bismillāh.* A rival. Every single fear that is manufactured in you is, by definition, built out of something *other than* the only One with real power. **It is all "those-besides-Him."**

[PAUSE]

This does not mean the things you fear can do no harm in the world. The Prophet ﷺ could, in worldly terms, be hurt. You can lose the job. The diagnosis can be serious. The ayah is not telling you that causes don't exist. It is telling you something far deeper: **that not one of those causes operates outside of Him, beside Him, or against His sufficiency.** They are *min dūnih* — *below* Him — every one. The market moves under Him. The cells divide under Him. The crowd's tongue wags under Him.

So the question the intimidators were really asking the Prophet ﷺ — and the question your fear-economy is really asking you — is this: **will you relocate your sense of safety from the One who is, into the things that are besides Him?** Will you let your heart's center of gravity slide off of Allah and onto the *those*?

And Allah's reply is not "the *those* are not dangerous." His reply is a question that resets your center of gravity in five words. *Alaysa-llāhu bikāfin ʿabdah.* **Is not Allah enough?**

What does this change tomorrow? Here is the practice. The next time you feel that cold drop of dread — about money, about people, about your body, about the future — before you do anything else, *name the raw material.* Ask: *what is this fear actually built out of?* And watch how, every time, the honest answer comes back: *those-besides-Him.* A number. An opinion. A possibility. A "those." And then let the verse ask you its question, and let your chest give the answer it was made to give. **Balā. Yes. He is enough.**

### Theme Two — The Two Words That Bracket Everything: Enough, and Guided

Now I want to take you to the deepest layer, and to do it I have to bring back the dropped *yā'* I asked you to remember.

We saw it first in **كَافٍ — kāfin** — "sufficient." The full form *kāfī*, with its *yā'* quietly removed.

Now look at the final word of the entire ayah:

> **وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ**
> *wa man yuḍlili-llāhu famā lahu min hād*
> "And whoever Allah leaves to stray — for him there is no **guide** at all."

The last word is **هَادٍ — *hādin*** — "a guide." Its root is *h-d-y* (ه-د-ي), the root of *hudā*, guidance. And the full active participle would be **هَادِي — hādī** — ending, once again, in a *yā'*. And once again — **the *yā'* is dropped.** *Hād-in.* The exact same shape as *kāf-in.*

[PAUSE]

Do you feel the architecture closing? Stand back and look at the whole ayah as a single built thing, because now it reveals itself.

The verse **opens** with one active participle: **كَافٍ — kāfin — "Sufficer."** What Allah *is*, for His servant.

The verse **closes** with another active participle, in the identical grammatical shape, identical dropped *yā'*, identical indefinite *-in*: **هَادٍ — hādin — "guide."** What no one *can be*, when Allah withholds His own guidance.

And held *between* these two matching pillars — *kāfin* on the left, *hādin* on the right — sits the entire human drama: **the manufactured fear.** *Yukhawwifūnaka billadhīna min dūnih.* The intimidation. The threat. The trembling.

The structure is the argument. **Allah's sufficiency is the opening wall. The absence of any guide-but-Him is the closing wall. And every fear that human beings can manufacture is a small, anxious thing rattling around in the space between two facts that completely enclose it.**

[PAUSE]

Now let's understand the closing wall, because it is where the *real* fear — the only one worth having — finally appears.

The verb is **يُضْلِل — *yuḍlil*** — from the root *ḍ-l-l* (ض-ل-ل), "to go astray." It is in **Form IV**, which here carries a causative-transitive sense: not merely "to stray," but for Allah to *let* a person stray, to leave him to the misguidance he has chosen. I want to be precise and faithful here, the way the tradition is: the scholars of tafsir are careful that this divine *iḍlāl* is never arbitrary cruelty — al-Muyassar glosses it as Allah *forsaking* the one who turned away, leaving him to the path *he* insisted on. It is consequence, not caprice.

And then the verdict: **فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ** — "there is not for him *any* guide." Notice that little **مِنْ — min** before *hādin*. Like the *bi* on *kāfin*, it is an "added" particle — *zā'id* — and it exists to do one thing: **to make the negation total.** Not "he has no particular guide." **"There is not, for him, so much as a single guide. Not one. Not anywhere."**

[PAUSE]

Now bring the two pillars together and feel what the verse has actually done to your fear.

It has performed a complete *exchange*. The intimidators came to make the Prophet ﷺ afraid of *those-besides-Him* — afraid of stones, of harm, of losing his standing, his safety, his mind. And the ayah, with its two bracketing walls, quietly takes that fear out of his hands and replaces it with the *only* fear that is real:

**The thing actually worth fearing is not the idols. It is not the threat. It is not any "those." The only catastrophe in existence is to be among those whom Allah leaves without a guide. And the only refuge from *that* catastrophe is the very same Allah who, in the same breath, has just declared Himself *more than enough* for His servant.**

So the fear and the cure are the same Person. The One you should fear losing is the One who has guaranteed He will not let you be lost — *for His servant.* That is why the verse can afford to be so calm about the idols. When the only real loss is to be left without His guidance, and He has just declared Himself your sufficer, **every other "or else" the world can manufacture goes silent.**

[PAUSE]

What does this change tomorrow? It re-points your fear at its true target. Most of our suffering is *misdirected fear* — terror aimed at the powerless "those," while the one thing actually worth our trembling, our standing in His guidance, we treat casually. The practice is a daily redirection: take the anxiety you were about to spend on a "those" — the deadline, the person, the number — and spend even a fraction of it on the real stake. *Am I holding onto His guidance? Am I His ʿabd, His servant, the one He has promised to suffice?* Because the moment you are honestly *that*, the verse's opening wall is yours: **bikāfin ʿabdah.** And nothing rattling in the room between the walls can touch you.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture, Now Visible

Let me show you the whole building at once, now that we've walked through its rooms.

```
              ٚ                                (kāf-in — dropped yā', emphatic bi, weightless)
   وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِ   ← THE RATTLING SPACE: manufactured fear,
                                                built from faceless "those-besides-Him"
   وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ  ← PILLAR TWO: what no one CAN BE without Allah
                                                (hād-in — same dropped yā', total negation min)
```

Two matching pillars — *kāfin* and *hādin*, the same shape, the same silent *yā'* — and between them, dwarfed, the entire commotion of human intimidation. The verse doesn't *argue* that your fear is small. **It builds a structure in which your fear is visibly, architecturally small** — enclosed on both sides by Allah.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes turn out to be one insight seen from two directions.

From the **language**: a question engineered to pull *balā* from your chest; a sufficiency carrying divine emphasis yet arriving weightless; a fear that is laborious, manufactured, and built from nameless nothings; and two bracketing participles that enclose the whole.

From the **life**: you live in an economy that manufactures dread out of "those-besides-Him," and the only fear worth having — the loss of His guidance — is guarded by the very One who has declared Himself *more than enough for His servant.*

Same truth, two doors. **The grammar of the verse is the geometry of safety.**

### Questions to Carry

1. When I trace my fear back to its raw material, what is it actually built out of — and is any of it more than a "those-besides-Him"?
2. Who, or what, profits when I am afraid? Whose labor is the *khaww* in my chest?
3. Do I treat the loss of a job, a reputation, or a comfort as a greater catastrophe than the loss of His guidance — and what does that ranking reveal about where my heart's center of gravity has slid?
4. Can I let the verse ask me its question — *is He not enough?* — slowly, today, until my chest actually answers?
5. Am I living as *ʿabdah* — His servant, the specific one He has promised to suffice — or as my own, unguarded and unenclosed?

### One-Sentence Distillation

When Allah declares Himself *more than enough* for His servant, every manufactured fear collapses into noise — because the only loss worth dreading is to be left without His guidance, and the sole refuge from that loss is the very One declaring Himself enough.

### The Closing Invitation

Nothing here asks you to pretend the world is harmless. The deadlines are real, the diagnoses are real, the threats people make are sometimes real. The invitation is smaller and far more total than pretending: it is to *relocate the center of your safety* — out of the trembling "those-besides-Him," and back onto the One named in the first word, who pressed His own emphasis onto the word *enough* in the same breath He spoke about your fear. Let the next dread that arrives be met not with an argument, but with His question. And let your heart give the answer it was built to give.

### A Brief Du'a

*O Allah, You who are Sufficient — bikāfin — for Your servant: be enough for me, and make my heart know You are enough, so that no fear manufactured from those-besides-You can find a place to land. And You who alone can guide: do not leave me among those for whom there is no guide. Keep me Your ʿabd, the one You have promised to suffice. Āmīn.*
