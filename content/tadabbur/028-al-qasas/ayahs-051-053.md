# When the Word Arrives in Pieces

## Surah Al-Qasas, Ayahs 51–53

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The Ayah

**Arabic:**

> [ayah:28:51] ۞ وَلَقَدْ وَصَّلْنَا لَهُمُ ٱلْقَوْلَ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ
>
> [ayah:28:52] ٱلَّذِينَ ءَاتَيْنَـٰهُمُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ هُم بِهِۦ يُؤْمِنُونَ
>
> [ayah:28:53] وَإِذَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦٓ إِنَّهُ ٱلْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مُسْلِمِينَ

**Transliteration:**

> Wa laqad waṣṣalnā lahumu-l-qawla laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn.
>
> Alladhīna ātaynāhumu-l-kitāba min qablihi hum bihi yu'minūn.

And when it is recited to them, they say: "We have believed in it. Indeed, it is the truth from our Lord. Indeed, we were, even before it, Muslims — already submitted."

### Rebuilding the World

To hear these ayahs the way the first listener heard them, you have to walk into a Makkan courtyard sometime around the tenth year of the Prophet's ﷺ mission. You have to feel a particular kind of pressure.

The pressure was this: a small community — beaten, mocked, boycotted — was being told by its opponents that the Quran could not possibly be from God. And one of the reasons they gave was strangely formal. *Why is this Quran arriving in fragments? Why does it dribble out, ayah by ayah, situation by situation? Real scripture comes down all at once, like Musa's tablets. Real scripture is a finished book.*

You can find this objection just a few surahs back, in Surah Al-Furqan: **"Why was the Quran not sent down to him all at once?"** (25:32). It was a real argument being thrown at a real prophet, and it was being thrown precisely because the timing of revelation seemed, to a hostile observer, like the work of a man making it up as he went along.

And here is what's remarkable about where we are now in Surah Al-Qasas. The surah has just finished a sustained, almost overwhelming narrative of Musa — from the cradle in the river, through the years in Madyan, through the staff and the burning bush, through the confrontation with Pharaoh. The surah has been about how Allah brings someone up slowly, piece by piece, into the role they will eventually occupy.

Musa did not become Musa all at once. He became Musa over forty years.

And now, just as the long Musa story finishes, the surah pivots — and Allah addresses the objection. He answers it not with a defense but with a description. He describes what He has been doing. **"We have conveyed the Word to them — connected, piece after piece."** And He brings forward a quiet group of witnesses no one expected to hear from: the People of the Book — Jews and Christians who, when they encountered this gradual, piece-by-piece Quran, recognized it.

[PAUSE]

### The Hook

The opponents in Makkah said: *real revelation arrives all at once.*

Allah's reply does not deny the gradualness. It admits it. It *highlights* it. And then it does something stranger still — it presents the gradualness itself as a mercy. As a feature, not a flaw.

Sit with that for a moment. The very thing the critics held up as proof of fraud — *Allah holds up as proof of love.*

How does that work? How can the slow drip of revelation, the ayah-here-and-ayah-there, the answering-of-questions-as-they-arose — how can that be evidence of Allah's mercy rather than evidence of human authorship?

The answer lives in a single word: **waṣṣalnā**.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Waṣṣalnā* — The Word That Was Connected

Before we open the verb Allah uses here, I want you to think about a kind of object you have probably seen but never named.

Imagine a master craftsman building a long ornamental chain. He takes one link, forges it, polishes it, fits it into the previous link, hammers the join shut. Then the next link. Then the next. Each link is its own complete thing — its own circle of metal, its own integrity. But each link is also welded to what came before, so that when you hold up the finished chain, what you have is not a pile of rings. You have a single object, continuous, each piece carrying the weight of the next.

Arabic has a verb that describes exactly this kind of work. The root is **و-ص-ل** (*wa-ṣa-la*), which in its simplest form means *to arrive*, *to reach*, *to connect*. When two things were apart and become one, the verb *waṣala* describes the joining.

But the form Allah uses here is not the simple form. It is **Form II — waṣṣala**, with a doubled middle letter. This is one of the things classical Arabic does with extraordinary precision. The doubling, the *tashdīd*, is not decoration. In Arabic grammar, Form II most often carries the meaning of **intensification or repetition** — doing the action again and again, or doing it thoroughly, deliberately, over and over until it is fully accomplished.

So **waṣala** means *to connect*. But **waṣṣala** means *to connect again and again*, *to keep linking and linking and linking*. To do the work of joining repeatedly, like the chain-maker fitting one link after another, deliberately, patiently, until the chain is whole.

This is the verb Allah uses for what He did with the Quran.

[PAUSE]

Notice something. He could have said *anzalnā lahumu-l-qawl* — *We sent down the Word.* He uses that verb all over the Quran. He could have said *ātaynāhumu-l-qawl* — *We gave them the Word.* He uses that too. Both would have been grammatically clean. Both would have been theologically correct.

He did not say either of those things here. He said **waṣṣalnā** — *We made the Word arrive in connected pieces. We linked it together for them, piece by piece, situation by situation, until the chain was complete.*

Let's sit with this. The opponents in Makkah looked at the piece-by-piece arrival of revelation and saw fragmentation. They saw inconsistency. They saw a book that wasn't yet a book.

Allah looks at the same thing and uses a verb that means **link-by-link continuity**. He does not see fragmentation. He sees a chain. He sees a craftsman's work. The very gradualness the critics treated as a defect, Allah names as a method.

Here's what's remarkable. A book that arrives all at once is given. A book that arrives **waṣṣala-style** — joined and joined and joined over twenty-three years — is *companioned*. It walks alongside the community. It answers what the community is actually asking. It descends into the moment of grief, the moment of war, the moment of doubt, the moment of joy. It is not a monument. It is a hand held out, again and again, in real time.

That is the mercy hidden inside the verb form. Form II tells us this was not one act of sending. It was a thousand acts of sending. Allah did not deliver the Quran. **Allah accompanied them with the Quran**, link by link, for the length of a prophetic life.

The classical lexicographers — Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī in his *Mufradāt*, Ibn Manẓūr in *Lisān al-ʿArab* — note that *waṣṣala* in this verse carries this very nuance of *connection without break, succession without interruption, the joining of one thing to another in a continuous series.* Some classical commentators add a further dimension: that *waṣṣala* also points to how the Quran connects the stories and laws and reminders of all previous scriptures — joining what came before to what is here now, so that nothing of God's prior speech is left orphaned.

Both meanings belong. The Quran is a chain across time *(joining prior revelations)* and a chain across the moments of a single life *(joining one situation to the next)*. **Waṣṣalnā** does both.

### Element Two: *Laʿallahum Yatadhakkarūn* — So That They Might Remember

Now look at what follows. Allah tells us *why* He did it this way.

> لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ — *so that they might remember and take heed.*

There's a small word here — **laʿalla** — that we should pause on. In Arabic, *laʿalla* most often means *perhaps* or *so that, with the hope that*. When a human being says *laʿalla*, it expresses uncertainty — *I hope this works, but I'm not sure*.

But Allah, of course, is not uncertain about what will happen. The classical scholars therefore explain that when *laʿalla* appears in divine speech, it is not expressing Allah's uncertainty — it is **describing the purpose from our side, the human side, the side of effort and possibility.** It is the language of a door held open. As if Allah is saying: *I have laid this out so that the path of remembrance is open to them — whether they walk through it or not is their part.*

And then comes the verb: **yatadhakkarūn**.

The root is **ذ-ك-ر** (*dh-k-r*), which gives us the simple verb *dhakara* — to remember, to mention, to call to mind. From this root we get *dhikr*, the most basic word in the Quranic vocabulary for remembrance of God.

But again, Allah has not used the simple form. He has used **Form V — tafaʿʿala — yatadhakkarūn**. And Form V in Arabic typically carries the meaning of doing something **to oneself, taking on a state, internalizing a process.**

So *dhakara* means *to remember*. **Yatadhakkaru** means *to make oneself remember, to take the act of remembering into oneself, to do the inner work of recall.*

This is not the remembrance of a fact you happened to recall. This is the remembrance of a soul that *labors* to remember — that turns the reminder over in its hands, that lets the reminder reshape it.

Now connect the two halves of ayah 51 and watch what emerges.

Allah says: *We connected the Word for them, link by link* — **so that they might do the inner work of remembering.**

[PAUSE]

Here is the architecture. A book arriving all at once would *overwhelm*. It would land like a flood. The hearer would receive it, set it on a shelf, and call it scripture. There would be no room for slow recognition, for one verse to enter the heart in a moment of grief and another to enter in a moment of joy.

But a book arriving link by link — that book has room to be lived with. It allows for the kind of remembrance that does not just file information away but *re-knows the self*. The gradualness is not a delivery mechanism. **The gradualness is part of the message.** Form II of *waṣṣala* and Form V of *tadhakkara* are doing the same kind of work — both are about the *deepening of an act over time*, the patient repetition that turns surface knowledge into integrated knowledge.

The Word is joined in pieces so that the soul can be joined to it, piece by piece. *Waṣṣalnā* on Allah's side. *Yatadhakkarūn* on ours. The verb forms mirror each other. The slow joining of revelation produces the slow joining of the self.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: *Innā Kunnā Min Qablihi Muslimīn* — We Were, Even Before It, Already Submitted

The third element is in ayah 53 — the words spoken by the People of the Book when they hear the Quran recited.

Listen to what they say:

> قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦ إِنَّهُ ٱلْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مُسْلِمِينَ
>
> They said: "We have believed in it. Indeed, it is the truth from our Lord. Indeed, we were, even before it, Muslims — already submitted."

Three short clauses. Each one is doing precise work.

**First clause:** *āmannā bihi* — *we have believed in it.* The verb here is in the perfect tense in Arabic — *we have believed,* a completed act. They are not saying *we are starting to believe.* They are saying *the belief has happened.*

**Second clause:** *innahu-l-ḥaqqu min rabbinā* — *indeed, it is the truth from our Lord.* Notice the construction. They do not say *truth from a Lord* or *truth from the Lord of these people.* They say *from* ***our*** *Lord.* They are claiming this Quran's source as their own source. They are saying: *the God who sent this is the God we already worship.*

**Third clause** — and this is the one to sit with — *innā kunnā min qablihi muslimīn.* **Indeed, we were, even before it, Muslims — already submitted.**

Now, let's teach the grammar before we open the meaning.

There is a specific Arabic construction here: *kunnā* (we were) + the active participle *muslimīn* (those who submit). When you put the past-tense verb *kāna* together with a participle, you do not get a simple past tense. You get something that grammarians call **a continuous state in the past** — *we were in the state of being submitters, ongoingly, all that time.* Not *we became Muslims* and not *we converted to Islam* — but **we already were, all along, people of submission.**

And then there's the prepositional phrase wedged in the middle: *min qablihi* — *from before it,* meaning *before this Quran.* They are saying: *before this Quran ever reached us, we were already in this state.*

[PAUSE]

Here's what's stunning. The word **muslim** here is not the proper name of a religious community. It is the active participle of the verb *aslama* — *to submit, to surrender, to give oneself over.* A *muslim*, at the root level of the word, is *one who has handed himself over to God.*

So what these People of the Book are saying — these Jews and Christians who, the early Muslims would have known, came to the Prophet ﷺ and recognized what he was bringing — what they are saying is not *we have now become members of a new religion.* What they are saying is much deeper:

> *The submission that this Quran is calling us to is the submission we were already living. The God this Quran points to is the God we already knew. When we encounter this recitation, we are not converting — we are coming home.*

[PAUSE]

Stay with me. There is something humbling here for everyone listening.

The Quran does not say these believers from prior scripture became Muslims **because** they heard the recitation. It says the recitation **revealed to them that they had been Muslims all along.** The Quran did not give them a new identity. It gave them recognition of an identity they had been carrying without knowing what to call it.

This is the rarest kind of conversion. It is not a turning away from one thing toward another. It is a *coming into focus* of what was always there. A blurred image becoming sharp.

And now you see the conversation between the three ayahs.

- Ayah 51: Allah connects the Word, link by link, so the heart can do its slow work of remembrance.
- Ayah 52: Those given prior Scripture believe in it.
- Ayah 53: They say *we were already submitted — before this came, we were already His.*

The gradualness in Allah's revelation **matches** the gradualness in their own coming-into-recognition. They didn't suddenly become believers. They had been becoming believers their whole lives — through their own prophets, their own scriptures, their own struggle to find the One — and the Quran was the final link in their own chain of *waṣṣala*. The last link that closed the circle.

[PAUSE — major]

### Bridge from Words to Reality

What have we found? Three linguistic discoveries, each pointing the same way.

**Waṣṣalnā** — Allah's Word does not land. It arrives, link by link, in a continuous chain.

**Yatadhakkarūn** — The purpose is not delivery of information but the internalization of remembrance, a slow process that requires the slow arrival.

**Kunnā min qablihi muslimīn** — The recognition, when it comes, is not the start of submission but the unveiling of submission that was already there.

A pattern is emerging. Allah's way with hearts is *not all-at-once.* It is link-by-link, recognition-by-recognition, a slow joining of what He has placed in us to what He is now sending down to us.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: Why God's Love Looks Like Slowness

I want you to think about how a parent teaches a child to swim.

There are two ways to do it. One is to take the child to the deep end and drop them in. *Sink or swim. Figure it out.* The other is to spend hours, weeks, months, in the shallow end. The parent holds the child's stomach. The child paddles. The parent lets go for two seconds, then catches them. The child cries; the parent kisses the forehead. The next day, the parent lets go for three seconds. Then five. Then a minute. Then a whole length of the pool.

Both methods produce a swimmer. But one is *training* and the other is *companionship*. One says, *here is the skill, acquire it.* The other says, *I will be with you through every breath until you can breathe without me.*

The opponents in Makkah were asking, in effect, why Allah did not just drop the Quran in the deep end. Why not deliver it all at once, the way they imagined Musa received the Tablets? Why this twenty-three-year shallow-end, hand-on-the-stomach process?

And the answer Allah gives in *waṣṣalnā* is that **the slowness is the love.**

[PAUSE]

Think about what it means that revelation came down in response to actual situations. When the community grieved a death, an ayah came. When a slander was made against the Prophet's family, an ayah came. When a battle was lost, an ayah came. When someone asked about the new moon, an ayah came. When a marriage broke down, an ayah came.

A book delivered all at once cannot do this. A book delivered all at once is, by definition, *before* the situations that it addresses. It is generic. It is general. It is timeless in the cold sense of the word — *not in time at all.*

But *waṣṣala*-revelation is timeless in the warm sense. It is in time. It is *with* the community in time. Every link of the chain was forged in a real moment, with a real wound or a real question or a real victory, and that link still carries, for us, the warmth of that moment.

This is why a verse from Surah Al-Duha — *Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor is He displeased* — still meets people fourteen hundred years later in the middle of the night. Because it was forged in the middle of someone's night, at a moment when the Prophet ﷺ feared he had been abandoned. The link was made *there*. And we, when we are *there*, find ourselves linked to him, and through him to Allah.

[PAUSE]

So here is what Allah is teaching, just beneath the surface of one verb in Surah Al-Qasas:

**The pace of revelation is itself revelation.** The slow arrival of the Quran is teaching us how Allah deals with hearts in general. Not all at once. Not in floods. Not as a download. **Link by link. Situation by situation. Year by year.**

And if that is how He worked with the Prophet's ﷺ community, then that is how He works with us.

You are not behind schedule because your understanding of the deen has come to you slowly. You are not failing because the verse that was opaque to you at twenty makes sense to you at thirty-five. You are not deficient because there are still ayahs you have not yet metabolized.

*That is what* ***waṣṣalnā*** *looks like inside a single human life.* Allah is connecting you to His Word link by link. Some links will be hammered into place this year. Some next year. Some in a moment of loss you have not yet experienced. The chain is being built. The slowness is not a delay. It is the design.

**What this changes about tomorrow:** When you sit with the Quran tomorrow, do not measure your relationship with it by how much you have already understood. Measure it by which link is being forged right now. There is one verse, this week, that is being slowly fitted to your soul. Pay attention to that one. The chain is long. The chain-maker is patient. He has been doing this for a long time.

[PAUSE — major]

### Theme Two: The Quiet Witness — When Recognition Replaces Conversion

Now let's turn to the second theme, which lives most deeply in ayah 53.

I want you to think about a very specific kind of moment. You are reading a book, or listening to a teacher, or hearing a sermon, and the speaker says something — and you feel a strange jolt. Not the jolt of *learning something new.* The jolt of *hearing your own thought finally said out loud.*

That has happened to all of us. We thought something we could not articulate, and someone else articulated it, and the moment they did, we knew it was true — because we had been carrying it the whole time.

That moment is what ayah 53 is describing.

> "We have believed in it. Indeed, it is the truth from our Lord. **Indeed, we were, even before it, Muslims — already submitted.**"

When these People of the Book heard the Quran recited, they did not experience a *new* belief. They experienced a *recognition* of belief they had been carrying. Their own scriptures, their own prophets, their own monotheism, their own deep yearning for the truth — these had already shaped them into people who, when the final link of the chain arrived, simply said: *yes. This.*

[PAUSE]

There is something profoundly humble in their words, and something profoundly dignified.

Humble — because they are not announcing a triumph of new insight. They are not saying *we have figured this out.* They are saying *we have come home.*

Dignified — because they are not erasing where they came from. They are not saying *our prior life was darkness and this is light.* They are saying *we were submitters all along, and now we have been given the language of our submission.*

Notice that the Quran does not embarrass these people. It does not require them to renounce their entire history. It honors their journey by telling them: *the road you have been walking — this is where it was going. You have not been wrong. You have been on the way.*

This is staggeringly gentle. And it tells us something about how Allah sees the human story across history.

Allah does not see the People of the Book as enemies of revelation who had to be defeated. He sees the sincere ones among them as **fellow travelers on a long road, whose road had its own *waṣṣalnā* — its own link-by-link arrival of the truth — and who, when the final link came, recognized it because they had been forged on the same chain.**

[PAUSE]

Stay with this — because it does something for our own lives too.

There is a kind of believer who thinks the only valid spiritual experience is *being struck by lightning.* The dramatic conversion. The before-and-after. The moment when everything changed.

But this ayah opens a second door — a door for the people whose faith did not arrive with lightning. The people who feel they have *always* known. The people whose belief is not the result of one decisive moment but the result of a thousand small recognitions stretched across a life. The people who hear a verse and weep, and cannot quite say why, except that *it feels like something they already knew.*

The Quran says to those people: *you are not less. Your knowing is not less. The People of the Book in ayah 53 were like you. They did not bring the Quran into a vacuum. They brought it into a heart that was already, quietly, in submission. And Allah honored their quiet.*

[PAUSE]

And there is a final layer here, almost too tender to name.

Notice that the ayah does not say *they came to the Prophet ﷺ and declared their Islam.* It says *when it is recited to them, they say...* The whole transformation happens **in the moment of recitation.** Not in a creedal declaration. Not in a public act. In the hearing of the words.

The *tilāwah* — the recitation — is itself the meeting place. The verb **yutlā** (is recited) is in the **passive imperfect**, which in Arabic grammar suggests an ongoing, repeated, present-tense action. The recitation is not a one-time event. It keeps happening. And every time it happens, somewhere, a heart that was already on the chain finds its link click into place.

This is still happening. It happened this morning, somewhere in the world, when someone heard a Quranic recitation and felt their bones recognize it. It will happen tonight, somewhere else.

**What this changes about tomorrow:** When you recite the Quran tomorrow — or listen to it being recited — remember that *tilāwah* is not just a personal devotion. It is a sound in the world that is, even now, finding the people who were already submitted before they knew the name for it. Your recitation might be the link that closes someone's chain. And someone else's recitation is closing yours.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Look at how these three ayahs sit together once you see them whole.

**Ayah 51** describes Allah's side of the relationship — *We connected the Word for them, link by link, so they might do the inner work of remembrance.*

**Ayah 52** describes a particular group's response — *Those given prior Scripture believe in it.*

**Ayah 53** gives that group their own voice — *We were, even before it, already submitted.*

The structure moves from *Allah's act → human recognition → human voice.* From the chain-maker, to the chain being received, to the receiver speaking back. The whole passage is itself a *waṣṣala* — a small chain of three links showing us what the large chain produces.

And notice this: the verb form in ayah 51 (**Form II — intensive, gradual, deliberate**) on Allah's side, is mirrored by the verb form in ayah 51 (**Form V — reflexive, internalizing**) on our side. *Waṣṣalnā* meets *yatadhakkarūn*. The slow joining outside produces the slow joining inside. The grammar is enacting the theology.

### Bringing It Together

The opponents in Makkah said: *real scripture comes all at once.* The piece-by-piece arrival is proof against you.

Allah replies: *the piece-by-piece arrival is proof of Me. Because My way with hearts is never all-at-once. My way is to link and link and link, situation by situation, recognition by recognition, until the chain is whole. And here are the witnesses who will tell you so — the sincere ones from prior scripture, who, when they encountered this Word, did not experience a beginning. They experienced an arrival. Because their road was already going where this Word was already pointing.*

The linguistic discoveries and the thematic depths are not two things. They are one thing seen from two sides:

- From the linguistic side: Form II *waṣṣala* + Form V *tadhakkur* + continuous past *kunnā muslimīn* = the grammar of slow, mutual, recognitional faith.
- From the thematic side: Revelation as companionship, not delivery. Faith as recognition, not invention. Submission as a journey home, not a leap into the unknown.

### Questions to Carry

1. Which link of the Quran is being fitted to me right now? Not the verses I once understood — but the verse that, this week, is being hammered slowly into place?
2. Have I been demanding "all at once" from my own spiritual life — when Allah has been working *waṣṣala*-style all along?
3. Are there people in my life whose faith does not look dramatic, who have *always seemed* to know — and have I honored their quiet the way ayah 53 honors it?
4. When I recite or hear Quran, do I remember that the *tilāwah* is still doing its work in the world, still finding the hearts that were already on the chain?
5. What in me was *muslim min qablihi* — already submitted before I had words for it? And have I made peace with that part of my own story?

### One-Sentence Distillation

> Allah did not deliver the Quran — He **accompanied** people with it, link by link, until the slow recognition in the heart matched the slow descent of the Word, and the sincere ones, when they heard it, did not become believers but discovered they had been believers all along.

### The Closing Invitation

If anything shifts after this, let it be this: stop measuring your faith by speed and start measuring it by joining. *Waṣṣalnā* is the verb. Allah is in no hurry, and yet He does not pause. He keeps fitting one link to the next. Your job is not to assemble the whole chain by Friday. Your job is to recognize the link He is fitting today and to let it lock into place.

And when you encounter someone whose faith does not look like yours — whose journey to Islam was a slow homecoming rather than a thunderclap — honor what ayah 53 honors. Recognize that some people were *muslimīn min qablih.* The chain was already running through them. The Quran just gave their submission its name.
