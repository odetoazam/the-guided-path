# The Long Silence: When Generations Forget and Allah Still Speaks

## Surah Al-Qasas — Ayah 45

### Arabic

> وَلَـٰكِنَّآ أَنشَأْنَا قُرُونًۭا فَتَطَاوَلَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْعُمُرُ ۚ وَمَا كُنتَ ثَاوِيًۭا فِىٓ أَهْلِ مَدْيَنَ تَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَـٰتِنَا وَلَـٰكِنَّا كُنَّا مُرْسِلِينَ

### Transliteration

*Walākinnā anshaʾnā qurūnan fataṭāwala ʿalayhimu l-ʿumur. Wa mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli Madyana tatlū ʿalayhim āyātinā — walākinnā kunnā mursilīn.*

### Translation

*"But We brought into being generations, and ages stretched long upon them. Nor were you dwelling among the people of Madyan, reciting to them Our signs — but it is We who have been sending."*

## INTRODUCTION

### The world this ayah is answering

Let me put you somewhere first.

It is roughly the seventh year of revelation in Mecca. The Prophet ﷺ has been telling the story of Musa for years now — pieces of it, scenes of it. The cradle in the river. The killing of the Egyptian. The flight into the desert. The two girls at the well. The ten years in Madyan. The fire on the mountain. The court of Pharaoh.

And the Quraysh are listening. Some of them are stunned. Some of them are furious. And some of them are doing exactly what you would do.

They are checking.

They are sending caravans to scholars of the Jews in Yathrib and asking: *Is this true?* They are looking for the seam. They are looking for the place where the story breaks. Because if this man in front of them — this **unlettered man** who has never left the Hijaz, who has no library, no scribe, no teacher, no rabbi — if he is reciting to them the inner life of Musa son of ʿImrān fifteen centuries earlier with names and locations and emotional textures that match what the People of the Book have preserved in their scrolls...

Then where is he getting it from?

[PAUSE]

This is the question Surah Al-Qasas is built to answer. And it answers it not by argument. It answers it by **architecture**.

Three times in this passage — verses 44, 45, and 46 — Allah pauses the story of Musa and turns directly to His Prophet ﷺ, and says: *You were not there. You were not there. You were not there.*

> "And you were not on the western side when We decreed to Musa the command..." (28:44)
>
> "And you were not dwelling among the people of Madyan..." (28:45)
>
> "And you were not at the side of the mountain when We called..." (28:46)

Three negations. Three places. Three moments in Musa's life that the Prophet ﷺ has just described with the precision of an eyewitness. And Allah is saying — to the Quraysh, through His Prophet — *I know you are looking for the seam. There is no seam. He was not there. He could not have been there. I was there. And I am the One telling him.*

This is the surah's quiet thunder.

### Where this ayah sits

But our ayah — verse 45 — is the strangest of the three.

The other two name **places**: the western side of the valley, the side of the mountain. Geographic locations where prophetic events happened. You weren't at *that spot.*

This middle one is different. It does not just say *you were not in Madyan.* It says: **you were not dwelling among the people of Madyan, reciting to them Our signs.**

Allah is not naming a place the Prophet ﷺ wasn't. He is naming a **role** the Prophet ﷺ wasn't playing — at a time when **no Prophet was playing it**.

And before He even gets there, He opens with something even stranger. He does not begin with *but you were not.* He begins with: **but We brought into being generations, and ages stretched long upon them.**

[PAUSE]

What is that doing there?

Why does Allah, in the middle of defending His Prophet's truthfulness, suddenly speak about generations stretching across centuries of silence?

### The hook

I want you to feel the strangeness of this before we open it.

The Quraysh have just heard a story about a Prophet who lived in Madyan among Shuʿayb's people. And the obvious counter is: *Maybe Muhammad heard it from someone who heard it from someone who heard it from someone in Madyan.* A chain of human transmission. That is the natural objection.

Allah's answer is not to break the chain. It is to **show that there is no chain at all.**

Because between Shuʿayb's Madyan and Muhammad's Mecca there is a gap so vast — generations upon generations stretched so long — that whatever was in Madyan has been forgotten. The witnesses are dust. Their children are dust. Their children's children are dust. The signs that were recited in Madyan are not preserved in any oral tradition the Quraysh can access.

And yet here is a man in Mecca, reciting them.

How?

The ayah's answer is one of the most stunning sentences in the whole Quran. And it is hidden in a single, almost-missed word at the very end.

Let me show you.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Anshaʾnā qurūnan* — "We brought generations into being"

Before we open the word, I need to teach you a concept.

In Arabic, there are several verbs for *making*. There is **khalaqa** — to create, to design from nothing. There is **jaʿala** — to set, to place, to appoint. There is **ṣanaʿa** — to craft. And there is **anshaʾa**.

**Anshaʾa** is the rarest and the most specific of them. It comes from the root *n-sh-ʾ*, and its core meaning is *to cause to rise up, to grow up, to develop into something over time.* When Arabs used this root for a young man, they meant *he grew up* — not he was born, but he came into his height, his stature, his presence. When they used it for a cloud, they meant *it rose and gathered.* When they used it for a building, they meant *it was raised up, story by story.*

So **anshaʾnā** does not mean *We created.* It means: **We raised them up. We grew them. We caused them to develop, generation upon generation.**

Now hold that. And look at what it is the object of:

**Qurūnan** — *generations.* From the root *q-r-n*, which means *to join, to pair, to bind together.* A *qarn* is a generation in the sense of a cohort of people bound together in time — those who share a moment, who walk the earth together. Classical scholars debated the length of a *qarn* — some said forty years, some said eighty, some said a hundred and twenty — but the lexical core is not the *length.* The lexical core is the *binding.* A generation is a group of human beings tied together by the rope of a shared lifetime.

So put the two together: **anshaʾnā qurūnan.**

*We raised up — generation after generation after generation.*

[PAUSE]

Notice the verb. Allah is not saying *We let generations happen.* He is not saying *generations arose.* He is saying **We raised them.** Each one. The grandmother who lived in a village outside Madyan in 1100 BCE. Her son. Her son's son. Her son's son's son. The whole bound chain of them, stretching from Shuʿayb's time to Muhammad's time — **Allah raised every single one.**

He is claiming authorship of the silence.

Stay with that. The Quraysh's whole objection rests on the idea that there is a gap — a human-shaped gap — between Shuʿayb and Muhammad, and someone must have filled it with rumor and hearsay. Allah's first move is to say: *that gap is not empty. That gap is full of generations. And I raised every one of them.*

The silence between the prophets is not a gap in the record. It is a record I kept.

### Element Two: *Fataṭāwala ʿalayhimu l-ʿumur* — "and the lifetime stretched long upon them"

Now we come to one of the most aurally beautiful and theologically heavy phrases in the surah. And we are going to slow down for it.

Let me teach you another concept first.

In Arabic, verbs have *forms.* The same root — three letters — can be put into different patterns, and each pattern changes the meaning. The basic form of *ṭ-w-l* (to be long) is **ṭāla** — *it was long, it became long.* That is Form I.

But our word is not **ṭāla.** It is **taṭāwala** — Form VI.

Form VI in Arabic carries the meaning of *gradual development over time, often with an element of strain or extension beyond what is natural.* It is the form you use when something stretches and keeps stretching, when an action draws itself out, when time itself seems to elongate.

So **ṭāla** would just mean *the lifetime was long.* But **taṭāwala** means: *the lifetime kept stretching itself out, drawing itself longer and longer, extending across more and more time until the extension itself became the point.*

And listen to the sound. *Ta-ṭā-wa-la.* The syllables themselves elongate. The word *sounds* like what it means. Your mouth has to physically draw out the *ṭā* and the *wa* — your tongue stretches, your breath extends. It is one of the Quran's perfect onomatopoeic moments. Allah is describing time stretching, and He is doing it with a word that stretches when you say it.

[PAUSE]

Now: *what* stretched?

**Al-ʿumur** — *the lifetime.* The root *ʿ-m-r* gives us *ʿumr* (lifespan), *ʿamara* (to inhabit, to populate, to make flourish), *iʿtamara* (to perform ʿumrah), and *al-ʿumrān* (civilization). The core meaning is *the inhabiting of time by life.*

So **fataṭāwala ʿalayhimu l-ʿumur** — *and the inhabited time stretched itself long upon them.*

Notice the preposition: **ʿalayhim** — *upon them.* Not *for them.* Not *to them.* **Upon them.** The lifetime stretched out *over* the generations like a long shadow, like a covering blanket of years, like sediment settling.

Here is what this is doing.

The Quraysh might say: *Maybe someone preserved the Madyan story.* Allah says: *Look how long the silence has been. Look how the centuries stretched. Look how generations were raised and lived and died and were forgotten — and the inhabited time itself stretched out so long over them that whatever was once known has been buried under sheer accumulation of years.*

This is not just *time passed.* This is *time piled up. Time deepened. Time became its own forgetfulness.*

[PAUSE]

And now look at what Allah has just done.

He has shown you the gap. He has named the silence. He has stretched out the centuries between Shuʿayb's Madyan and Muhammad's Mecca in a single Arabic verb whose syllables elongate as you speak them. He has made the listener *feel* the impossibility of bridging that gap by any human means.

Only then does He turn to His Prophet ﷺ and say: *And you were not dwelling among the people of Madyan.*

The gap has been established. Now the contrast can land.

### Element Three: *Thāwiyan* — "dwelling" — and the word that should not be there

This is the strangest word in the ayah. And it is where the door opens.

The root is *th-w-y*. It means *to settle in a place, to take up residence, to dwell in a way that implies belonging.* A **thāwī** is not a traveler passing through. He is not a guest staying for a season. A **thāwī** is someone who has *settled* somewhere — someone who has put his roots into the soil of a place and lives there as one of its people.

Lexicographers note that *thāwiyan* carries an even more specific shade: it can imply *dwelling among people in the role of a teacher, a guide, a settled presence who is known to that community.* This is not the word for a foreigner. This is the word for someone who *belongs.*

Now look at what the ayah says:

**Wa mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli Madyana tatlū ʿalayhim āyātinā.**

*And you were not a settled-dweller among the people of Madyan, reciting to them Our signs.*

[PAUSE]

I want you to notice something. Something the classical mufassirūn paused at and stayed with.

The other two negations — verse 44 and verse 46 — say *you were not at the western side* and *you were not at the side of the mountain.* These are places where Musa was. The Prophet ﷺ wasn't at the places where the events of Musa's life unfolded.

But this middle verse does not say *you were not in Madyan* — meaning *you weren't there when Musa was there.* It says: *you were not **dwelling** among the people of Madyan **reciting Our signs to them.***

Whose role is that?

That is not Musa's role in Madyan. Musa, when he was in Madyan, was not yet a Prophet. He was a fugitive working for Shuʿayb's family. He was not reciting signs.

That is **Shuʿayb's** role. Shuʿayb was the one who dwelt among the people of Madyan reciting to them the signs of Allah.

[PAUSE]

Stay with this.

Allah is not saying *you weren't there when Musa was in Madyan.* He is saying something much deeper. He is saying: **you were not the Prophet of Madyan. You were not dwelling there as their settled teacher. You did not stand in Shuʿayb's place reciting the signs.**

Why does this matter?

Because the Quraysh's objection has a deeper layer. They might say: *Well, maybe he heard the Madyan story from the descendants of Madyan's people.* And Allah is answering: *No. He was not their resident teacher. He has no relationship of belonging to that community. The chain you are imagining — settled communities preserving prophetic memory and passing it to him — that chain does not exist. He was not* thāwiyan *anywhere except Mecca, among a people who knew nothing of Madyan.*

The word **thāwiyan** is doing surgical work. It is closing one specific door that an objector might try to open.

### Element Four: The asymmetry that opens the whole ayah

Now here is the moment.

I want you to look at the very end of the ayah:

**Walākinnā kunnā mursilīn.**

*But it is We who have been sending.*

In Arabic, when you contrast two things with **walākin** (*but*), the second clause is supposed to mirror or invert the first. So if the first clause was *you were not dwelling among the people of Madyan reciting Our signs* — the natural mirror would be:

*But We were teaching you. But We informed you. But We told you what happened there.*

Look at what Allah actually says.

**Walākinnā kunnā mursilīn.** — *But it is We who have been sending [messengers].*

[PAUSE]

Do you see it?

The clause does not mirror. It pivots. The natural mirror would have been about *how the Prophet ﷺ knows* the story. The actual mirror is about *Allah's pattern of sending messengers.*

The structure is also worth noticing: **kunnā mursilīn** — using **kunnā** (the past continuous *we were/we have been*) with **mursilīn** (the active participle *sending ones*). This is not *We sent.* It is *We have been, all along, the Senders.* It is a statement of continuous, ongoing, pattern-establishing action. Sending messengers is not what Allah did once. It is what Allah does. It is His ongoing posture toward humanity.

And the word **mursilīn** is plural and unspecified. Allah does not say *but We sent you* (singular). He says *but We have been Senders* — of how many? To how many places? Across how many of those stretched-out generations?

[PAUSE]

This is the answer to the puzzle, hidden in plain sight.

The Quraysh asked: *How does a man in Mecca know what happened in Madyan?* And the surface answer is: *Allah told him.* But the deep answer is the one Allah actually gives: **Because I am the One who has been sending messengers all along — to Madyan, and to every other people, and now to you. The same Hand that authored the original event is the Hand that is authoring the recitation of it. There is no chain of human transmission to attack. There is only Me, sending then, and Me, sending now.**

The ayah's logic, restated:

*You think the story came from Madyan to here. It didn't. There has been a long silence between Madyan and here — I raised all those generations, and the lifetime stretched long upon them. You are not* thāwiyan *in Madyan. You have no membership there. But you don't need one. Because the same Sender who spoke to Madyan is the Sender who is speaking to you. The story is not coming horizontally, person to person, across the centuries. It is coming vertically, from Me, down — twice. Once to them, once to you.*

[major PAUSE]

### Bridge to the themes

We have stayed inside the words long enough to see something stunning.

This ayah is not really about Madyan. Madyan is the case study. The ayah is about **how revelation works** — and about **the silence between prophets** as itself a designed silence, raised and stretched by Allah, that proves rather than disproves what He is doing now.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about how Allah moves through history, and what that means for someone listening in 2026.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The silence between revelations is not absence — it is authorship

Let me start somewhere very different.

Think about a long-distance relationship. Or a parent and a college-age child. Or two friends who used to be close and now go months without speaking.

There is a particular kind of pain that lives in those gaps. When you don't hear from someone, the silence does not stay neutral. It starts to *mean* something. It starts to feel like absence. Like they have forgotten you. Like the relationship has thinned out and gone quiet not because nothing is happening — but because nothing is happening on their end. The longer the silence stretches, the more it accumulates as evidence that something is missing.

This is exactly the experience the Quraysh were sitting in, theologically.

Centuries of silence. No major Prophet to the Arabs in living memory. The descendants of Ibrāhīm in the Hijaz had not received a settled, recited revelation for a very long time. And the natural reading of that long silence — the *human* reading of it — is: *Allah has not been speaking. Allah is not active here. Whatever happened in the past in Madyan, in Egypt, in the Holy Land — that was then. Now is silence.*

And then this verse comes.

[PAUSE]

**Walākinnā anshaʾnā qurūnan fataṭāwala ʿalayhimu l-ʿumur.**

*But We — We are the One who raised those generations. We are the One whose lifetime stretched long upon them.*

Notice what Allah is doing. He is not apologizing for the silence. He is not explaining it as a regrettable gap. He is **claiming it.** He is saying: *that long stretch of generations between Shuʿayb and Muhammad — every breath of every one of them was raised by Me. The silence was not absence. The silence was a chapter I wrote.*

This is the first move toward a totally different theology of God's hiddenness.

In the modern world, we have inherited a habit of reading God's silence as evidence against Him. *Where was He during the famine? Where was He during the war? Where was He during my mother's death? Where was He in the long years when I prayed and heard nothing?* The silence becomes the case for the prosecution.

The Quran's answer, hidden in this ayah, is that **the silence is part of the speech.** The stretches between revelations, between answered prayers, between visible mercies — these are not gaps in Allah's authorship. They are passages in it. Allah did not stop being active when revelation paused between Shuʿayb and Muhammad. He was raising every generation in that gap. He was extending the lifetime upon them. The silence was His.

[PAUSE]

Sit with what this does to your own life.

You have stretches in your life where nothing seems to be happening. Where du'a goes up and nothing comes back down. Where you cannot feel Allah's nearness. Where the years stretch long upon you — **fataṭāwala ʿalayka l-ʿumur** — and you wonder if you are still in the story.

The ayah's first answer to you is: **you are still in the story. The silence is still authored.** The generations stretched long upon them too, and Allah was there in every one of them, raising them up. The fact that no Prophet came in that century did not mean Allah was elsewhere. The fact that no answer came to your du'a this year does not mean Allah is elsewhere.

The silence between speeches is not the speaker's absence. It is the speaker's pacing.

This is not a sentimental consolation. It is a structural claim about how Allah moves. He is **kunnā mursilīn** — the One who has been Sending. Continuously. Across the silences. The pattern of His action is not the pattern of His visibility. He acts in the gaps as much as in the speeches.

**Practical application for tomorrow:** Look at the silence in your own life — the prayer that has not been answered, the relationship that has not healed, the direction that has not become clear. Stop reading that silence as Allah's absence. Read it as a chapter He is writing whose meaning will only be visible from later in the book. Your job in the silence is not to demand a speech. Your job is to remain inhabited by the silence — *fataṭāwala ʿalayka l-ʿumur* — and to trust that the One who raised every generation through their stretched centuries is raising you through yours.

[major PAUSE]

### Theme Two: You do not need to belong to the story to receive it

Let me bring you to something else.

Modern psychology has a concept called *epistemic belonging.* It describes how human beings tend to feel they can only legitimately *know* things that come to them through their own communities, their own lineages, their own lived experience. If you didn't grow up in it, you can't really speak about it. If your family wasn't from there, you can't really understand it. If you weren't in the room, you don't really have standing.

There is something true in this. Lived experience is real. Community memory is real. But it has a shadow side. The shadow side is that people start to feel that **truth itself is a possession of insiders.** That if you weren't born into something, you can never really receive it. That access requires lineage.

This is exactly the framework the Quraysh are using against the Prophet ﷺ.

*You're not from Madyan. You don't have a chain of teachers going back to Shuʿayb. You weren't dwelling there as a* thāwī. *You have no insider's claim to this story. How dare you recite it as if you know.*

And listen to Allah's answer.

**Wa mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli Madyana... walākinnā kunnā mursilīn.**

*You were not a settled-dweller among the people of Madyan... but it is We who have been sending.*

[PAUSE]

Notice what Allah does *not* do. He does not give the Prophet ﷺ a counterfeit lineage. He does not say *but secretly your great-great-grandfather was from Madyan.* He does not say *but you were taught by someone whose chain goes back to Shuʿayb.* He does not manufacture epistemic belonging.

He **bypasses it.**

He says: *the access to this story does not run through your membership in any community at all. It runs through Me. I am the Sender. The story comes to you the same way it came to them — from above. You don't need to be a* thāwī *anywhere on earth to receive it, because the source is not on earth.*

This is a radical statement.

It means that the Prophet's authority does not rest on a chain of human transmission. It rests on a *vertical* relationship to the Sender. And here is the thing — that same vertical access is *the structure of revelation itself.* It is how every Prophet got the message. Musa did not get his message because he had a Madyan lineage. He got it because Allah called him at the side of the mountain. Allah is the Sender. Always. Across all those stretched generations. To everyone He chose.

Now bring this back to you.

You probably do not feel that you belong fully to the chain of religious knowledge. Maybe you didn't grow up in a scholar's household. Maybe your Arabic is broken. Maybe you came to Islam later in life. Maybe you came back to it after a long period away. Maybe you sit in a Friday khutbah and feel like you are watching a tradition that other people own and you are eavesdropping on.

[PAUSE]

The ayah is for you.

The Prophet ﷺ himself — *the Prophet ﷺ* — was not a *thāwī* in the lineage of revelation. He had no inherited religious office. No human chain of teachers. No membership in the community of the previous Prophets. And Allah said: *that does not matter. The Sender is not bound by your lineage. I send to whom I will, where I will, when I will. The access is vertical. From Me. Down.*

This is the structure of the relationship Allah is offering you. Not a horizontal membership card that you have to prove you earned. A **vertical openness** that is offered to everyone who turns toward the Sender.

You do not need to belong to the story to receive it. You only need to face the One who is telling it.

**Practical application for tomorrow:** Stop measuring your access to Allah by your credentials. Stop asking whether you are scholarly enough, Arabic enough, lineage enough, mosque-trained enough. Those are horizontal questions. They matter for some things — they do not gate your access. Open the Quran tomorrow and read it not as an outsider trying to qualify for entry. Read it as a person under an open sky, with the same Sender above you who sent to Madyan and to Egypt and to Mecca. He is **kunnā mursilīn** — still Sending. The vertical is still open.

[major PAUSE]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture revealed

Now look at the ayah whole.

The ayah has three movements:

1. **The silence is established.** *We raised generations, and the lifetime stretched long upon them.*

2. **The horizontal possibility is closed.** *You were not a settled-dweller among the people of Madyan, reciting to them Our signs.*

3. **The vertical reality is opened.** *But it is We who have been sending.*

Do you see what this is? It is a perfect proof structure. Allah does not argue for revelation. He **performs** revelation. The very ayah that defends the Prophet ﷺ against the charge of having no source *is itself* a moment of having only one source. The Prophet is reciting words about why he must have received the words from Allah. The form is the content. The recitation is the proof.

And the architecture is symmetrical: the long silence (vertical absence on the human side) is mirrored by the long sending (vertical presence on the divine side). The same gap that disqualifies any human chain qualifies Allah's chain. The silence is the proof.

### Bringing it together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are one insight from two directions.

The linguistic insight: Allah uses **anshaʾnā** to claim authorship of generations, **taṭāwala** to stretch the silence into something He owns, **thāwiyan** to close the door of horizontal access, and **kunnā mursilīn** to open the door of vertical access — all in one breath.

The thematic insight: the silences in our lives are authored, and we do not need horizontal belonging to receive vertical revelation.

These are the same truth. Allah is the Sender across silences. He is not bound by the chains of human transmission. He is not absent in the gaps. He is the One who raised the gaps, stretched them, and continues to send through them.

### Questions to carry

1. Where in your life are you reading silence as Allah's absence — when it might be a chapter He is writing whose meaning is not yet visible?

2. Whose voice in your head tells you that you do not have enough lineage, credentials, or community standing to receive directly from the Quran? Where did that voice come from, and what would change if you ignored it?

3. If Allah is **kunnā mursilīn** — has-been-and-still-is Sending — what is He sending you right now, today, that you have been treating as background noise?

4. The Prophet ﷺ was given a story he never witnessed. Where in your life have you been given knowledge you did not earn — and have you noticed it as a sign of the Sender, or just accepted it as luck?

5. The long stretch of generations between Shuʿayb and Muhammad was raised by Allah. What if the long stretch of your own forgotten ancestors — the ones whose names you do not know — was also raised by Him, deliberately, as part of His authorship of you?

### One-sentence distillation

*The silences between Allah's speeches are not His absence — they are His authorship, and the One who raised every generation in those silences is still the Sender today, reaching anyone who turns toward Him, regardless of lineage.*

### The closing invitation

What should shift after this is not your information. It is your reading of silence.

The next time the years stretch long upon you — *fataṭāwala ʿalayka l-ʿumur* — and Allah feels far, do not read the silence as His departure. Read it as Surah Al-Qasas read it: as a chapter He is raising, a long passage He is authoring, a stretch of time that is His before it is yours.

And the next time you wonder if you have enough belonging to receive directly from this Book — remember that the Prophet ﷺ himself had no human chain to Madyan, and the Sender bypassed that question entirely. The vertical is open. It has always been open. **Kunnā mursilīn.**

### Du'a

> Yā Allāh, You who raised every generation in the long silence between Your speeches — raise me in mine. You who sent Your signs to people who were dust before my grandfathers were born — send Your signs to me now, in this stretch of years You are authoring upon me. Let me stop reading silence as absence. Let me stop measuring my access to You by lineages I do not have. You are **kunnā mursilīn** — the One who has been Sending — and I am turning my face up. Reach me.
