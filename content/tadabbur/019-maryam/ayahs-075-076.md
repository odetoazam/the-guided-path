# Extension and Increase: The Two Lengthenings of Ar-Rahman

## Introduction: The Question Behind the Question

> قُلْ مَن كَانَ فِى ٱلضَّلَـٰلَةِ فَلْيَمْدُدْ لَهُ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ مَدًّا ۚ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا رَأَوْا۟ مَا يُوعَدُونَ إِمَّا ٱلْعَذَابَ وَإِمَّا ٱلسَّاعَةَ فَسَيَعْلَمُونَ مَنْ هُوَ شَرٌّ مَّكَانًا وَأَضْعَفُ جُندًا
>
> وَيَزِيدُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ هُدًى ۗ وَٱلْبَـٰقِيَـٰتُ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتُ خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا

> *Say: Whoever is in misguidance — let the Most Merciful extend for him an extension. Until, when they see what they are promised — either the punishment or the Hour — they will come to know who is worse in position and weaker in army. And Allah increases those who are guided in guidance. And the enduring good works are better in your Lord's sight in reward, and better in return.*

### Rebuilding the Moment

Sit with what has just happened in this surah.

For seventy ayahs, you have been walking through the most intimate biographies in the Quran. Zakariyya weeping in private for a son. Maryam seized by birth pains under a palm tree, alone. The infant Isa speaking from the cradle. Ibrahim breaking with his father over an idol. Musa called from the side of a mountain. Idris taken to a high place.

Notice what every one of these stories has in common: **none of them had the social standing the world recognizes.** Maryam was an unmarried woman accused of fornication. Ibrahim was a son disowned by his father. Musa was a fugitive who killed a man. Zakariyya was an old man with no progeny — and in his society, that meant nothing.

These are the people Allah chose to mention by name, in His own Book, until the end of time.

Then come ayahs 73 and 74. The Quraysh — wealthy, propertied, "established" in Mecca — turn to the believers (mostly slaves, freedmen, the poor) and ask: *"Which of our two parties is best in position (maqāman) and best in society (nadiyyā)?"*

It is the oldest argument in the world. Look at our houses. Look at our gatherings. Look at our furniture. Look at how we *appear*. Doesn't your God notice? If He loved you, wouldn't you have what we have?

### The Hook

The surah does not respond by defending the believers' material standing. It does not say "you'll get yours one day." Instead, it does something far stranger.

It tells the Prophet ﷺ to **declare** — with divine authority, directed at those who have taunted — that the misguided will indeed receive their extension. To announce: *let* them be given more. *Let* Ar-Rahman extend it for them.

And then, in the very next breath, it says something about the believers — but it uses **a different verb entirely.**

Two groups. Two lengthenings. One word for each.

If you understand the difference between those two words, you will understand why the worst thing in this world is to ask Allah for *more time* without asking for more *guidance*.

## Part One: The Linguistic Journey

### Element One: Two Verbs That Look Like Synonyms (مدد vs زيد)

Before we open these ayahs, let me ask you about something everyday.

You know the difference between **extending** something and **adding to** something, even though in casual English we might use them interchangeably.

If your phone bill is due, and the company "extends your due date by thirty days" — what just happened? You owe the same money. The amount didn't change. The pressure has just been *stretched* over more time. The debt is still there, only now it sits longer, and often grows quietly through interest.

But if your bank account "added thirty dollars" — something genuinely new entered. The total grew. There is more there than before.

Both involve "more time" in some sense. Both *look like* the situation has improved. But one is a stretching of what already is. The other is a multiplication of what already is.

Hold that distinction in your mind. Arabic preserves it perfectly.

#### The word *madd* (مدّ)

The root م-د-د in classical Arabic carries the core sense of **stretching, extending, drawing out across distance or time.** From this root come words for stretching a rope, extending the hand, prolonging a vowel, drawing out the tide. *Madda al-bahr* — the sea stretched. *Madda yadahu* — he extended his hand.

What unifies these meanings? Something already exists, and it gets *pulled longer.* No new substance enters. The thing is simply *extended.*

In ayah 75, Allah says: **فَلْيَمْدُدْ لَهُ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ مَدًّا** — "let the Most Merciful extend for him an extension."

Notice: this is form I of the verb (yamdud) followed by the noun *maddan* — the same root, repeated as a cognate object. In Arabic this is called *al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq*, the absolute object. Its function is **to intensify the verb.** Not just "extend" — but "extend an extending." A real, full, generous stretch.

Allah is saying: whatever extension they are asking for, by their lifestyle, by their delay, by their sense that the punishment will never come — *let them have it.* Generously. Fully. Stretched out as long as they want.

And here is what the classical commentators add that the word alone does not show you: the extension is not neutral. It does not simply preserve the misguided person in their current state. Rather, as classical commentary on this passage observes, the respite granted to the misguided only deepens them further in that very misguidance. The rope is let out, but while it is being let out, the person is sinking further — because to remain in rejection for another day is not to hold a position but to deepen one. More time in falsehood adds more falsehood to the account. The debt does not merely sit; it compounds.

al-Tabari, recording the interpretation of the early commentators, pairs the word *madd* with *imlāʾ* — not just prolonging but "giving full rope," from the root meaning to let something out to its full length: *"fa-l-yutawwal lahu fī ḍalālatihi wa-l-yumlā lahu fīhā imlāʾan."* Let him be prolonged in his misguidance and let him be given full rope in it — a giving of full rope. This is the technical vocabulary of *istidrāj*: the incremental drawing-down, where each extension silently enlarges the account that will one day be settled.

#### The word *ziyādah* (زيادة)

The root ز-ي-د is something else entirely. Its core sense is **increase, addition, accumulation.** *Zāda al-māʾu* — the water increased (a new quantity entered). *Yazīdu fī ʿumrihi* — he increases in his lifespan (something is added). The number grew.

This root does not mean "stretching what is already there." It means "bringing in something new."

In ayah 76, Allah says: **وَيَزِيدُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱهْتَدَوْا۟ هُدًى** — "And Allah increases those who are guided in guidance."

The verb is *yazīd*. The thing increased is *hudā* — guidance itself. The guided don't just get *more time*. They get more *of the substance they already love.* The guidance they have multiplies into more guidance. Light produces light. Insight produces insight. The heart that turned a little, turns more. The eye that saw a little, sees more.

And the mechanism of this increase, according to al-Tabari, is specific: it comes through what is *newly renewed* in the believer's life — each obligation accepted, each farīḍah submitted to, each fresh act of obedience. The increase is not automatic or merely dispositional. It accrues through practice: "through what is renewed for him of faith in the obligations that are newly prescribed for him, which he affirms and acts upon." The believer who prays today is more guided than the believer who prayed only yesterday, because today's act has added to the stock. Every new farīḍah accepted is a new increment deposited.

[PAUSE]

Stay with this contrast.

The misguided ask, in effect, for **more of the world.** Allah grants them an extension — but what they receive is more *of the same condition they were already in*, and it deepens as it stretches. The rope is pulled longer while the sinking continues.

The guided receive **more of the substance.** Their guidance is not stretched; it is *added to.* Each act of turning draws a new deposit.

This is why a person who turns to Allah for one year often feels in that year more growth than ten years of their old life put together. It is not that the year was longer. It is that the verb changed. They moved from being in a *stretched extension* to being in a *true increase.*

#### The two divine names

And now look at something stunning. Look at *who* extends and *who* increases.

The verb *yamdud* in ayah 75 is attributed to **Ar-Rahmān** — The Most Merciful. *Let Ar-Rahmān extend.*

The verb *yazīd* in ayah 76 is attributed to **Allāh** — the proper name, the Lord of all.

Why is the *extension* of the misguided attributed to Allah's mercy-name?

Because the extension *is itself a mercy.* Every breath the misguided one draws is mercy. Every sunrise he wakes to is mercy. Every meal, every moment of health, every postponement of the reckoning — these are all coming from Ar-Rahmān. The fact that he was not seized at the moment he chose disbelief is itself a stretching of mercy.

But notice: mercy without response *becomes weight.* The longer the extension, the heavier the bill at the end. Because every moment of mercy he received *while turning away* is a moment he will be asked about.

This is what the classical scholars called *istidrāj* — the gradual drawing-down. The hadith says: when you see Allah giving a person what he wants while he disobeys, **that itself is the punishment beginning** — slowly, beautifully, mercifully — beginning.

The mercy is real. But the mercy is being held by hands that refuse to close around it. So it pours through and pools at his feet, and one day he will be asked: *what did you do with all of that?*

This is what makes the verb *yamdud* so terrifying when you hear it correctly. It is the verb of a rope being slowly let out. And every meter is mercy. And every meter is going to be counted.

### Element Two: The Cognate Object That Hides a Whole Argument

Look at the structure of ayah 75 again, very precisely.

**فَلْيَمْدُدْ لَهُ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ مَدًّا** — "let Ar-Rahman extend for him an extension."

We already noticed that *maddan* is a cognate object — the same root as the verb, repeated for intensification.

But there is something else here. Something almost uncomfortable.

This verb — *fa-l-yamdud* — is grammatically a command form. The *lām al-amr*, the command-lām, is attached. In Arabic, it is the structure used when you order something to happen.

But who is being commanded? Allah is the one doing the extending. So who is commanding whom?

The grammarians have a name for this construction: it is the **lām of permission** or **lām of consequence** — *li-l-amr* with the meaning of *li-l-tahdīd* or *li-l-takhliyah*. It is a command in form, but a *concessive surrender* in meaning. It is Allah saying, in essence: *fine. Let it be so. So be it. The mercy will be granted.*

This is one of the most quietly devastating constructions in the Quran. The disbelievers are demanding standing, wealth, time. And Allah's response is not refusal. It is something far more unsettling. It is **proclamation** — a sovereign declaration so calm it is more devastating than any rebuff.

*Fine. Let Ar-Rahman extend it. Let him have it. Generously. Fully. Until the moment of seeing.*

There is a kind of refusal that is itself a mercy — Allah refusing your request because the request would harm you. And there is a kind of granting that is itself a punishment — Allah granting your request because you insisted on something He knew would weigh against you.

Have you ever in your life *gotten exactly what you asked for* and discovered, in retrospect, that the giving was its own form of being abandoned?

Stay with that.

That is what the lām of *fa-l-yamdud* sounds like, to someone with ears for Arabic. It is the sound of a generous "yes" that contains within it everything you should have feared.

### Element Three: "Worse in Position" — A Word Returned

Here is something the surface translation cannot show you.

In ayah 73, the disbelievers had taunted: *"ayyu al-farīqayni khayrun **maqāman** wa aḥsanu nadiyyā"* — "which of the two parties is better in **position** and finer in society?"

The word they used was **maqāman** — position, standing, station. They chose this word deliberately. They were boasting about *where they stood*, in the social and material sense.

Now look at how Allah closes ayah 75: *"fa-saya'lamūna man huwa sharrun **makānan** wa aḍ'afu jundā"* — "they will come to know who is worse in **position** and weaker in army."

Allah uses **makān** — the same root, almost the same word. Position. Standing. Where one stands.

He has taken their own boast-word and **handed it back to them**, fully intact, only with the comparative inverted. They asked who was *better in position*. Allah says: you will find out, with absolute certainty, *who is worse in position.*

This is not Allah using His own preferred categories to refute them. This is Allah taking the very category they chose — the one they were proudest of, the one they *defined the contest by* — and saying: *fine. Let us judge by your category. Let us see who has the better position when seeing arrives.*

Notice the pairing again: **sharrun makānan** (worse in position) and **aḍ'afu jundā** (weaker in army). The disbelievers had said *aḥsanu nadiyyā* — finer in *gathering, society, assembly.* What is a *nadī* in pre-Islamic Arabia? It is the assembly of men, the council, the gathering of force and influence. It is the human muscle around you.

Allah replaces *nadī* with *jund* — army, troops, soldiers. Why? Because on the Day they will see what they are promised, the *gathering* will not be a council of equals. It will be revealed as what it always was: an *army* — the muscle they thought protected them. And it will be **weaker** than they imagined.

The friends who would have died for you. The clan that vouched for your name. The wealth that opened doors. The networks that absorbed your mistakes. All of it is being reframed — gently, by a single word substitution — from *finest of societies* to *weakest of armies.*

[PAUSE]

Notice also what al-Muyassar adds when it explains the seeing: *ḥattā idhā raʾā yaqīnan* — "until he sees with certainty." The entire dispute of the Quraysh was about appearances — who *looks* more favored, who *appears* to have divine approval. The word *yaqīnan* lands as the final answer: all appearance will dissolve, and only certainty will remain. There will be no reasserting the old boast. They will know with the knowledge that admits no argument.

And classical commentators note further that "either the punishment or the Hour" opens two timelines, not one. The first option — *al-ʿadhāb* — was understood to include punishment in this world: the hastened reckoning, before the Last Day, whether by defeat, calamity, or the collapse of everything the extension had propped up. The promise is not deferred entirely to the Hereafter. The extension can end here, in a worldly settling, before the final one arrives.

### Element Four: *Al-Bāqiyāt al-Ṣāliḥāt* — The Feminine Plural That Endures

Now move to ayah 76, and notice what has been placed against the disbelievers' "position" and "army."

**وَٱلْبَـٰقِيَـٰتُ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتُ خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا**

*And the enduring righteous things are better in your Lord's sight in reward, and better in return.*

Look at the form: **al-bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt.** Feminine plural.

In Arabic, the feminine plural is frequently used for non-human categories — for things, abstractions, and classes rather than named persons. But when it is used here, it is doing something specific. It is describing a *category* of deeds rather than a *list* of named ones. The Quran does not say "the prayers" or "the charities." It says *the enduring righteous things.* The category is left deliberately open.

The root of **bāqiyāt** is ب-ق-ي: to remain, to persist, to survive after everything else has passed. *Baqiya al-māʾu* — the water remained, after the rest was used. *Baqiya al-waladāni* — two of the children survived, when others did not. The *bāqī* is what the passage of time cannot erode. What death cannot cancel. What circumstance cannot take.

This is the direct answer to *makān* and *jund*. The disbelievers boasted of position and force — two things built entirely from the materials of this world. Position is social; it depends on who recognizes you. Force is collective; it depends on who stands with you. Both require *other people* to exist. And other people, of course, are temporary.

The *bāqiyāt* are what remain **after the others are gone.** After the council has dispersed. After the army has scattered. After every supporter has turned to face their own account. What will be left, sitting in the register, is only what you did — and specifically, what *endured* into the weighing.

The root of **ṣāliḥāt** is ص-ل-ح: soundness, fitness, rightness, suitability. A *ṣāliḥ* thing is not merely good in some abstract sense; it is *fitting* — the right thing in the right proportion, doing what it was supposed to do. *Ṣaluḥa al-māʾu lil-shurb* — the water was fit to drink. *Ṣaluḥa al-ʿamal* — the deed was sound, valid, acceptable. The *ṣāliḥāt* are deeds that, when weighed, will be found *valid.* Structurally sound. Accepted.

Now ask: what are these deeds, specifically?

The classical commentators differed here, and their ikhtilāf is itself illuminating. Some held that *al-bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt* refers primarily to the five daily prayers — the most consistent temporal structure of worship, the deeds most literally "remaining" across each day's hours. Others identified them with the foundational phrases of dhikr; hadith traditions name them as *subḥānAllāh, al-ḥamdu lillāh, lā ilāha illā Allāh, Allāhu Akbar, wa lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh* — the five words of remembrance that persist without requiring wealth, gathering, or standing. Others, following a broader reading, held that any sincere and accepted deed falls within the category — any act of worship that *persists* in its record past the moment of performance.

This disagreement is not a problem. It is an answer to the Quraysh's narrow question. They defined greatness by a specific list — *that* house, *that* gathering, *that* name. The Quran answers with a category so wide it cannot be captured in a list: any righteous thing that endures. The humblest dhikr whispered in a dark room. The prayer performed alone with no one watching. The charity given where no social credit accrues. These are the *bāqiyāt* — the things no council of enemies can strip from you, because they were never *in* the social register to begin with. They were deposited elsewhere.

And then, finally: **خَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا** — "better in return."

The word **mardad/maraddā** derives from the root ر-د-د, meaning "the place or act of returning" — the outcome, the destination, where the road leads back. What returns to you. The *mardā* of the disbelievers' position: stripped, reversed, the word *makān* handed back to them upside down on the day of seeing. The *mardā* of the believers' enduring deeds: something better that comes back.

The Quran places *thawābā* (reward) and *mardā* (return) together deliberately. Reward is what is given. Return is where the journey ends. The disbelievers' journey ends at *seeing* — at the moment of certainty they cannot dispute. The believers' journey ends at a *good return.* Two trajectories. Two destinations. The same compass, pointing in opposite directions.

## Part Two: The World Running on Two Clocks

Here is what these two ayahs ask you to hold simultaneously.

Right now — at this exact moment, as you read — both of these operations are running in the world. Some people are being extended. Others are being increased. These are not future events. They are present realities, invisible to every metric we use to evaluate who is doing well.

The person driving the expensive car — extended or increased? The person praying Fajr in the cold before anyone else is awake — extended or increased? The person whose business is growing faster than their conscience — extended or increased? The person whose schedule is being slowly stripped of everything except what matters — extended or increased?

You cannot tell from the outside. This is precisely the point. The Quraysh *thought* they could tell. They looked at the material display and read it as evidence of favor. The Quran says: *you are reading the evidence backward.* What looks like favor might be the rope being let out. What looks like difficulty might be the account being settled, cleared, renewed — guidance being added to guidance.

The classical commentators, when moving from ayah 75 to 76, note the deliberate pairing: after Allah mentions the extended time allowed to those in misguidance — which serves only to increase them in that very misguidance — He then announces the increase in guidance granted to the rightly guided. The same divine operation, running in two directions. The same mercy reaching everyone. In those who receive it with turning, it increases their guidance. In those who turn away, it deepens their turning away, and Ar-Rahmān, in His mercy, extends the time before the account is demanded.

This symmetry has a Quranic mirror that Ibn Kathir draws our attention to: ayah 9:124, where the Quran itself is described as operating on a divided audience. When a surah is revealed, those who believe are increased in faith — the same word, *zāda* — and they rejoice. But those with diseased hearts are increased in filth upon their filth. The same revelation. The same word *zāda.* Two opposite results, determined entirely by the orientation of the heart that receives it.

The extension and the increase are therefore not two separate tracks for two separate people. They are *the same divine mercy, experienced differently* — according to whether the heart is closing around what it receives or letting it pour through.

[PAUSE]

This should terrify you, a little, and settle you, a little — depending on where you find yourself.

If you are afraid, that fear is useful. The fear that says: *what if what looks like my blessing is actually my extension?* That is not paranoia. That is the question the Companions used to ask themselves. They were afraid of exactly this — of mistaking prosperity for approval, of being extended through the world's pleasures until the day of seeing arrived and the account was opened.

But if you are turning — if you are genuinely, even imperfectly, turning — then the verb over you is *yazīdu.* And that verb does not require completion. It does not require you to have achieved something visible. It requires only that you be in motion toward the guidance that is being added. The obligation you accepted yesterday added to you. The prayer you made this morning, however dry it felt, added to you. The farīḍah you submitted to this week, without fully understanding it — that was addition, not extension. The moment you turned your face back after turning away: that was a new deposit in the account.

The debt of the extended one grows. The register of the increasing one fills. And both are invisible to the eyes that only read the surface.

## Part Three: The Question That Does Not Leave

There is a question embedded in these two ayahs that I want to leave with you, because it is the kind of question that, once heard, does not go quiet.

**What is being lengthened in my life — and what is being added?**

These are not the same question. The first asks about time. The second asks about substance.

You might have been given more time this year than you expected. More opportunity, more health, more income, more room to breathe. The ayah asks: what is growing *in* that time? Is there more light in the house — more understanding, more turning, more submission, more peace in the places where pride used to live? Or is there simply more room — more house, more gathering, more appearance of standing — and underneath it, exactly the same person, only with a slightly longer extension?

The disbelievers did not experience their prosperity as a slow punishment. They experienced it as success. The proof of divine favor. This is the terror of *istidrāj* — it is invisible from the inside. You cannot feel a rope being let out if you are not looking for it.

What does "looking for it" mean, in practice?

It means measuring your life by the *bāqiyāt*, not by the *makān*. It means asking not "what do I have?" but "what endures of what I have done?" The five prayers — are they happening? The small dhikr — is it in the morning and evening, or has it drifted? The act of charity — not the large one that gets mentioned, but the small regular one that no one saw? The relationship repaired that required humility? The word of truth spoken when silence would have been safer?

These are the *bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt.* Not the monuments. Not the *maqām.* The enduring, sound, valid, quiet things — the ones that will be sitting in the register when the gathering disperses and every supporter has turned to their own account.

And here is what the ayah promises — not warns, but *promises* — about those deeds: they are **better in your Lord's sight in reward, and better in return.** The word is *khayrun.* Better. Not different. Not compensatory. Not "you lose in this world but gain in the next." *Better.* What they produce is genuinely, by the actual standard of goodness, more than anything the disbelievers were boasting of.

The council the Quraysh were proud of will scatter. The alliances will dissolve. The names that opened doors will be forgotten within a generation or two. But the *bāqiyāt* — the small, sound, enduring deeds — will sit in the register, gathering, until the Day when the account is opened.

[PAUSE]

The surah has been walking you through prophets and righteous ones for seventy-four ayahs. None of them had *makān.* None of them had the assembly the Quraysh were proud of. What they had was this: the ongoing, quiet, relentless accumulation of righteous deeds, done in the sight of the One whose sight alone determines the outcome.

The return to which they returned was a good return.

**خَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا.**

## Closing

اللهم إنا نسألك أن تزيدنا هدى ولا تمدنا في ضلالتنا

*O Allah, we ask You to increase us in guidance, and do not extend us in our misguidance.*

O Allah — You who are Ar-Rahmān, whose mercy is the very extension You offer — let what You extend in our lives be the rope of guidance, not the rope of heedlessness. Let what You add to us be substance, not appearance. Let the verb over us be *yazīdu*, not *yamdud.*

Let our enduring deeds be genuinely enduring. Let the five prayers find their times, day after day, adding increment upon increment. Let the small dhikr run underneath the noise of the day. Let the charity go out without needing to be seen. Let the farāiḍ be met not as burdens but as deposits — each one a new addition, a new increase, a new layer of light settling into what was already there.

And when the Day arrives when what was promised is seen with certainty — when all position is stripped to its actual weight and all armies dissolve and every gathering turns to face its own account — let us be among those whose register has been filling, quietly, with the *bāqiyāt.* The things no gathering could strip, because they were never the gathering's to give.

**خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ مَّرَدًّا**

*Better in your Lord's sight in reward, and better in return.*

Āmīn.
