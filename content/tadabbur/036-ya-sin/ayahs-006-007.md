# The Heedless Were Never Warned

## Introduction: Standing at the Mouth of the Surah

Let me read these two verses to you first. Sit with the sound of them before we touch a single meaning.

> **لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا مَّآ أُنذِرَ ءَابَآؤُهُمْ فَهُمْ غَٰفِلُونَ**
> *Li-tundhira qawman mā undhira ābā'uhum fa-hum ghāfilūn.*
> **"That you may warn a people whose forefathers were not warned — so they are heedless."**

> **لَقَدْ حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَىٰٓ أَكْثَرِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ**
> *Laqad ḥaqqa l-qawlu ʿalā aktharihim fa-hum lā yu'minūn.*
> **"Already the Word has come due upon most of them — so they will not believe."**

[PAUSE]

To understand where these verses land, you have to feel where they sit.

Ya-Sin does not open quietly. It opens with two letters — *Yā Sīn* — and then an oath. Allah swears: **wa-l-Qur'āni l-ḥakīm** — *by the Wise Qur'an.* He swears by His own speech that the one receiving it, Muhammad ﷺ, is **truly among the messengers**, **upon a straight path.** A *tanzīl* — a sending-down — from the Mighty, the Merciful.

And then — immediately — comes the *why.* Why was this revelation sent down? What is it *for?*

That is where our verse begins. With a single letter of purpose: **li-** — *so that.* "**So that** you may warn."

Here is the world you have to step into to feel the weight of this.

Picture the Arabian Peninsula in the early seventh century. For generations — long generations — no prophet had walked among these people. The scholars call this period the **fatra**: the gap, the interval, the silence between messengers. Other nations had their books, their prophets, their living memory of revelation. The Children of Israel had Moses and the long line after him. But these tribes — the Arabs of Makkah, the people of Quraysh — al-Baghawī tells us plainly: **no prophet had come to them before Muhammad ﷺ.**

Imagine inheriting silence. Imagine a people who did not *reject* guidance so much as they had simply never been *handed* it. Their fathers died without a warner. Their grandfathers. A whole lineage of souls walking through life with the divine channel gone quiet.

What does a heart become after generations of that silence?

[PAUSE]

That is the question these two verses answer. And the answer is one of the most psychologically precise words in the entire Qur'an — a word that describes not the loud sinner, not the defiant rebel, but the *sleeping* soul.

**Ghāfilūn.** The heedless.

Here is the hook. Hold it as we walk.

The verse does not say these people *refused* the warning. It says something far stranger and far more merciful: **their forefathers were not warned.** There is an almost gentle diagnosis here — *they couldn't have known.* And yet the very next breath, verse 7, drops a verdict like a gavel: **the Word has come due upon most of them — they will not believe.**

How do we move, in the space of one verse to the next, from *"they were never warned"* — which sounds like an excuse — to *"the Word has come due"* — which sounds like a sentence?

What changed between verse 6 and verse 7?

Something did. And when you see it, you will understand the most important thing about your own life right now.

Let us go slowly.

## PART ONE: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Letter That Means Two Opposite Things — مَّا

Before we touch the verse, let me teach you something about Arabic that English readers almost never get told.

In Arabic there is a tiny word — just two letters, *mīm* and *alif* — **mā**. And this little word is a shapeshifter. Depending on context, it can be a word of **negation** — "not." Or it can be a **relative pronoun** — "that which," "what." Same letters. Same sound. Opposite grammatical jobs.

Think of it like a single English word "left." *"He left."* He departed. *"On the left."* A direction. *"What's left."* A remainder. The reader's mind chooses the meaning from the surrounding road. Arabic does this constantly, and the Qur'an — the most deliberate text in the language — sometimes places **mā** at exactly the spot where *both* readings are alive at once.

Now look at our verse:

> لِتُنذِرَ قَوْمًا **مَّآ** أُنذِرَ ءَابَآؤُهُمْ
> "That you may warn a people — **mā** — were warned their forefathers."

[PAUSE]

Stay with this. The classical commentators stood right here and split into two roads — and I want you to walk both, because the Qur'an built both into the verse on purpose.

**The first road — mā as negation.** This is the reading of Qatāda and, al-Qurṭubī tells us, *the majority of the people of tafsīr.* On this reading **mā** means **"not":**

> "That you may warn a people whose forefathers **were not warned.**"

A people in the *fatra.* A people of inherited silence. No warner came to their fathers, so the heedlessness is almost... understandable. This reading carries an undertone of **mercy and urgency:** *Go to them — they never had a chance, their fathers never heard, the silence has gone on too long. Break it.*

**The second road — mā as a relative pronoun.** This is reported from Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrima. On this reading **mā** means **"that which":**

> "That you may warn a people **with the very warning that** their forefathers were warned with."

Now the meaning flips toward **continuity.** The message you bring, O Muhammad, is *the same message* every messenger ever brought. Nūḥ warned with it. Ibrāhīm warned with it. You are not bringing a novelty — you are bringing the **one eternal warning,** delivered again to a people who let it lapse.

[PAUSE]

Now — here is what I want you to feel.

A shallow reading panics at this and asks: *"But which one is correct? Which did Allah mean?"*

Tadabbur does the opposite. It asks: *"What if the ambiguity is the point? What if both are true, and the single word holds them together like a key cut to open two doors?"*

Look what happens when you let both live:

- Their fathers **were not warned** (the silence was real — the *fatra* was real).
- And the warning you bring them **is the same warning** that, in principle, every father of every nation was owed.

Both at once: *these specific people inherited silence* **and** *the message itself is the eternal, unchanging summons.* The mercy of "they never heard" and the majesty of "this is the one timeless call" — folded into two letters.

**This is what a single mā can carry when Allah is the one who places it.** Not vagueness. Not the author being unsure. A density so high that two complete theologies fit inside one syllable.

[PAUSE — let it settle]

And notice the deeper thing the second reading exposes, the thing that becomes the hinge of verse 7: if this is **the same warning their forefathers were owed,** then the moment it arrives, the era of excuse is *over.* The silence has been broken. Hold that. We are coming back to it.

### Element Two: The Sound of a Sleeping Soul — غَٰفِلُونَ

Let me teach you one more thing about Arabic before we open the next word — this time about **sound.**

In a revelation meant to be *heard* before it was ever read, the *texture* of a word is not decoration. Harsh meanings often ride harsh sounds. Heavy realities sit on heavy consonants. The mouth is made to *enact* the meaning.

Now feel this word in your own mouth. Don't just read it — *say* it:

> **غَٰفِلُونَ** — *ghāfilūn.*

That opening sound — **gh**, the *ghayn* — is one of the thickest, most guttural sounds in Arabic. It is produced at the very back of the throat, a low rumble. It is not a sharp sound, not a bright sound. It is **muffled.** Clouded. It is, quite literally, the sound of something thick.

And what does the word mean? From the root **gh-f-l** — to be unaware, to be inattentive, to let something **slip past** you unnoticed. **Ghafla** is heedlessness. Not denial. Not rebellion. *Inattention.* The lexicographers connect it to a thing being neglected, untended, left out of account — like a field left **ghufl,** unmarked, unattended.

[PAUSE]

Here is what I need you to notice, and it is the whole key to these people.

Allah did not call them **kāfirūn** here — *deniers.* He did not call them **mukadhdhibūn** — *those who cry lies.* In verse 6, He calls them **ghāfilūn** — the *heedless.*

This is a precise diagnosis. Denial is loud. Denial *knows* there is something to push against; it has heard the claim and clenched its fist around "no." But **ghafla** is silent. The heedless person is not arguing with God. **He has simply forgotten that the question exists.** He is not saying no. He is saying nothing — because he does not notice anything is being asked.

[PAUSE]

And now look at the grammar, because the grammar deepens the wound.

**Ghāfilūn** is not a verb. It is an **ism fāʿil** — an active participle. And here is the difference that matters: a verb names an *action,* something that happens and passes. A participle names a **state** — a condition that *clings,* an ongoing identity. Arabic could have said *"they grew heedless"* (a verb, an event). Instead it says **fa-hum ghāfilūn** — *"so they are the heedless ones."* It is not something they *did.* It is something they *are.* A standing condition. A settled fog.

And notice the small word welded to the front: **fa-** — *"so."* The *fā' of consequence.* It draws a line of cause: *their fathers were not warned* — **so** — *they are, by inheritance, in the fog.* The heedlessness is presented almost as a result of a history they did not choose. The silence rolled downhill, generation to generation, and pooled in them as **ghafla.**

**That is the state these two verses begin with: not a crime, but a condition. Not a fist, but a fog.**

### Element Three: The Word That Comes Due — حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ

And now — verse 7. Everything turns here.

> **لَقَدْ حَقَّ ٱلْقَوْلُ عَلَىٰٓ أَكْثَرِهِمْ**
> "Already — **the Word has come due** — upon most of them."

Let me open three pieces of this, because together they do something the fog of verse 6 did not prepare you for.

**First: لَقَدْ — laqad.** This is the *lām* of emphasis fused to **qad** before a past-tense verb. In Arabic, this construction is a hammer. It means: *this is certain, this is realized, this is finished, take it as done.* It is the difference between "it may have happened" and "**it has, definitively, happened.**" The verse opens verse 7 not with a possibility but with a *verdict already entered.*

**Second: حَقَّ — ḥaqqa.** From the root **ḥ-q-q** — and this root is one of the great roots of the Qur'an. It is the root of **ḥaqq:** truth, right, reality, what is *due.* The verb **ḥaqqa** means a thing **became true, became realized, became established** — and, crucially, **became due,** the way a debt *comes due.* When the word **ḥaqqa** is used, something that was always *true in principle* now becomes *operative in fact.* The bill that was always owed has now landed on the table.

**Third: ٱلْقَوْلُ — al-qawl.** "The Word." The decree. The saying. The judgment that Allah long ago spoke — the standing decree that those who, having been reached by the truth, turn away from it, **earn** the consequence of that turning.

Put them together:

> **laqad ḥaqqa l-qawl** — *"The Word has, definitively, come due."*

[PAUSE]

Now feel the seismic shift between the two verses, because *this* is the answer to the question I left hanging at the start.

In verse 6, these people are **ghāfilūn** — the heedless, the foggy, the ones whose fathers were never warned. There is *softness* in that diagnosis. Excuse, almost. *They never heard.*

But between verse 6 and verse 7, **something arrives.** The warner. *You,* O Muhammad. **Li-tundhira** — *that you may warn.* The whole reason the revelation came down was to break the silence — and now it *is* broken. The warning has landed in their ears. The *fatra* is over. The channel is open. They have *heard.*

And the instant they have heard and turned away — **ḥaqqa l-qawl.** The Word comes due.

[PAUSE]

Do you see it? **Heedlessness is innocent only as long as it is genuinely uninformed.** The fog is excused while it is truly *fog.* But the moment the light is brought directly to the eyes and the eyes choose to stay shut — the fog stops being weather and becomes a *choice.* And a choice, sustained, becomes a *destiny.* **ḥaqqa l-qawl.**

This is why the order of the verses is the entire teaching. Verse 6 is the soul *before* the warning reaches it. Verse 7 is the soul *after.* The warner standing between them is the hinge of every accountable life.

---

### Element Four: The Mercy You Could Miss — أَكْثَرِهِمْ

One more word, and it is easy to read straight past it. But read past it and you miss an act of divine mercy hidden inside a verse of judgment.

The Word came due upon — **aktharihim.** From the root **k-th-r:** abundance, the many, the majority.

**"Most of them."**

[PAUSE]

Not **all** of them. **Most.**

Sit with how easily Allah could have closed that door. He could have said *the Word has come due upon them* — full stop, the whole people, sealed. The grammar was right there. Instead, into the very sentence of judgment, He sets a limit on the judgment: **aktharihim — most.**

Which means: **some are not in it.** Some, even among this foggy, turning-away people, are not sealed. The door is left visibly ajar inside the room of the verdict.

This is a pattern you will find again and again once your eye is trained for it: when Allah pronounces a hard reality over a people, He so often refuses to make it total. He leaves the *aktharihim.* He leaves the remainder. He leaves the crack of light under the door — because the whole *point* of the warner standing between verse 6 and verse 7 is that **the verdict is not yet sealed for you.** "Most" is a word addressed, quietly, to the one who refuses to be the most.

[PAUSE]

And the verse closes: **fa-hum lā yu'minūn** — *"so they will not believe."* That same **fa-** of consequence again. And **lā yu'minūn** — the negation *lā* with the *imperfect* verb, which in Arabic carries the *ongoing,* the *present-and-continuing.* Not "they did not believe" (a past event) but "**they will not** / **they do not** believe" — a refusal that *keeps* refusing, a door they *keep* pulling shut.

### Bridge: From the Letters to the Reality

Step back and see what these four words have built.

A shapeshifting **mā** that holds mercy and majesty in two letters. A guttural **ghāfilūn** whose very sound is the muffled fog it names — a *state,* not an act. A **ḥaqqa l-qawl** that turns, between two verses, the excused fog into a debt come due — because a warner arrived. And an **aktharihim** that, even in the verdict, refuses to seal the door for everyone.

The architecture is already whispering its secret, and we will name it fully at the end. But the words have done their work. They have shown us a *progression* — from silence, to fog, to the breaking of the silence, to the hardening of the fog into a destiny — with mercy braided through every stage.

Now let us ask what this reveals not just about an ancient people — **but about your own soul, this week, in this life.**

## PART TWO: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: Ghafla — The Most Dangerous State Because It Doesn't Feel Like One

Let me start not with the verse but with where you already live.

Have you ever driven somewhere familiar — the route to work, the road home — and arrived with **no memory of the drive?** You know you stopped at the lights. You know you turned. But the fifteen minutes are simply… gone. You were awake. Your eyes were open. Your hands steered. And yet *you weren't there.* Some autopilot drove the car while the "you" that notices, that experiences, that *attends* — was somewhere else entirely.

That blank stretch of road is **ghafla.** And here is the terror of it: **while you were in it, it felt like nothing at all.** It felt like normal life. Ghafla does not announce itself. It has no symptom you can feel from the inside. That is precisely what makes it the most dangerous state a soul can be in.

[PAUSE]

Let me give you the analogy that I think carries the whole weight of this verse.

**Carbon monoxide.** It is the deadliest gas in the home, and it kills more quietly than any other, for one reason: **it is odorless.** You cannot smell it. You cannot taste it. It does not sting your eyes or catch in your throat. As it fills the room, you do not feel alarm — you feel *drowsy.* Comfortable. Sleepy. The very thing that is killing you makes you feel *more* at ease, not less. People die of carbon monoxide in their sleep having felt, in their final conscious moments, *perfectly fine.*

**That is ghafla.** It is the carbon monoxide of the soul. The heart does not feel itself going numb. The numbness *is* the feeling. The person sliding away from God does not experience alarm — he experiences *normalcy.* "I'm fine. Things are fine. What's the fuss?" The absence of distress is not evidence of safety. With this particular danger, **the absence of distress is the symptom itself.**

[PAUSE]

Now — what is the warner? **The warner is the carbon monoxide detector.**

The detector exists for exactly one reason: *because you cannot feel the gas.* If you could smell it, you would not need the alarm. The whole function of the warning is to make audible a danger that your own senses are structurally unable to register. This is why **li-tundhira** — "that you may warn" — is framed as the entire *purpose* of the revelation. Not to inform you of something you could have figured out. To make a *sound* in a room you didn't know was filling with gas.

And now you understand why these people are **ghāfilūn** and not **kāfirūn.** The denier has heard the alarm and ripped the battery out. The heedless one *never had a detector in the house.* Their fathers had no detector. The silence of the *fatra* meant generations breathing the odorless gas of a life without warning, feeling perfectly fine the entire time.

[PAUSE]

Let me bring this all the way home, because the original listeners of Ya-Sin lived in the *fatra* — and you, my friend, do not. You live in something stranger.

You live in an age that has perfected the *manufacture* of ghafla. The Arabs inherited silence. We have engineered a *noise* so total it does the same work silence did — it keeps the soul from ever noticing the question. Every glowing rectangle is tuned to fill your attention so completely that there is no quiet left in which a warning could land. Notification, scroll, autoplay, the next thing, the next thing, the next thing. You can go an entire day — an entire *year* — moving, working, laughing, achieving, and arrive at the end of it like that blank stretch of highway: *Where was I? Was I anywhere?*

**This is the most spiritually precise danger of our time, and it has a Qur'anic name. Ghafla.** Not atheism. Not rebellion. Not a clenched fist against God. Just a fog so comfortable, so well-furnished, so *normal,* that you never once feel the gas filling the room.

[PAUSE]

So what does this change about tomorrow?

It means the most important spiritual act available to you is not, first, to *believe harder* or *worship more* — it is, first, **to wake up.** To install the detector. To deliberately puncture the fog with something that makes a *sound* in the room. The five prayers were built for exactly this — five alarms across the day, dragging you out of the autopilot, forcing the foggy "you" back into the driver's seat: *Where am I? Before whom am I standing?* The Qur'an recited slowly enough to *hear* is a detector. A single conscious breath of *"yā Allāh"* in the middle of the scroll is a detector.

Ask yourself tonight, honestly: **What is filling my room that I cannot smell?** Not the loud sins you'd notice. The *odorless* ones. The hours that vanish. The drift you've stopped feeling because it has become your normal. That — not the obvious failing — is the ghafla these verses came to break.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: The Door Marked "Most" — and the Terror and Mercy of "The Word Comes Due"

Let me take you to the most uncomfortable phrase in these two verses, and then show you the mercy folded inside it.

> **laqad ḥaqqa l-qawlu ʿalā aktharihim** — "The Word has come due upon most of them."

Here is where many hearts flinch. *If the Word has already "come due," if it's already decreed that they won't believe — then was it ever a fair fight? Did they have a chance? Isn't this fatalism — God deciding in advance who's lost?*

Let me sit inside that discomfort with you, because the verse, read precisely, says something far more just and far more frightening than fatalism.

Begin with where you already live. **A doctor reading a scan.** A patient has felt fine for years — *ghāfil,* you might say, of what's growing inside. Then the scan is taken, and the doctor, looking at it, says quietly: *"This is now terminal."* Now — did the doctor's *words* cause the illness? Did saying "terminal" make it terminal? Of course not. The doctor is not *authoring* the outcome. The doctor is *recognizing* a reality that the patient's own choices and the disease's own course had already built. The diagnosis **comes true** — it *ḥaqqa* — because it was *always accurate.* The words name what *is.*

[PAUSE]

That is the grammar of **ḥaqqa l-qawl.** Allah's "Word" coming due is not Allah *forcing* a heart shut. It is Allah's perfect, timeless knowledge **recognizing** a heart that has — through its own sustained turning-away — *built itself* shut. Remember the sequence we uncovered: the warning *arrives* (verse 6 to verse 7), it is *heard,* and it is *refused.* The "coming due" follows the refusing. The Word does not reach down and pry the eyes closed. It *certifies* eyes that have chosen, and kept choosing, to stay closed. **fa-hum lā yu'minūn** — that ongoing, present-tense refusal we saw in the grammar — *they keep not-believing.* The decree describes the refusal; it does not manufacture it.

And *this* is why the order matters so much. As long as a soul is merely **ghāfil** — genuinely foggy, never truly reached — the Word has *not* come due. There is time. There is mercy. There is the warner still on his way. The verdict of verse 7 falls only *after* the warning of verse 6 has landed and been pushed away. **Heedlessness is the waiting room. The Word coming due is the sentence. And the warner is the judge entering — which means as long as you can still *hear* the warning, the sentence has not yet been passed on you.**

[PAUSE]

Now — the door.

I told you to hold onto a single word: **aktharihim.** *Most.* Not all.

Feel what that word is doing in a verse like this. Allah is describing a people so deep in turning-away that the Word has come due upon them — and *even here,* in the darkest sentence in these two verses, He will not say "all." He carves out a remainder. **Some of these very people are not sealed.**

Why? Why leave the door visibly open inside the room of judgment?

Because the verse is not, finally, a *report* about *them.* **It is a warning to *you.*** That is the whole point of *indhār.* If verse 7 said "all of them," it would be a closed history — a thing that happened to a dead people, nothing to do with your Tuesday. But "**most**" reaches out of the verse, grabs you by the collar, and asks: *Which are you? The most — or the remainder? The door is still open. Which side of it will you stand on?*

[PAUSE]

And here is the mercy that should make you exhale.

**You are reading this.** A warning is, *right now,* making a sound in your room. By the simple fact that these words have reached your attention and you have *noticed* them — you are, in this moment, **not in the fog.** The carbon monoxide detector is going off. The very discomfort you may feel reading verse 7 is *itself the evidence* that the Word has *not* come due on you — because the heedless feel no discomfort at all. **The fog has no alarm in it. You are hearing an alarm. Therefore you are awake.** Right now. Use it.

So what does this change about tomorrow?

It means **do not waste the alarm.** The most dangerous thing you can do with a moment of spiritual wakefulness — a verse that stung, a death that shook you, a Ramadan that softened you — is to *admire* the feeling and then roll back over into the fog. The whole tragedy of these verses is people who *heard* and let the hearing pass. Every moment of noticing is a hand on the still-open door. The door marked **"most"** does not stay open by itself. It stays open as long as you keep walking toward it.

Ask yourself: **The last time a warning truly reached me — what did I do with the next hour?** Did I act while the alarm was still sounding? Or did I let the fog roll back in and call it peace?

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now let me show you the structure the words were building all along — the thing I asked you to hold from the very beginning.

Look at how the two verses *end.* Listen to them side by side:

> Verse 6: …**fa-hum ghāfilūn** — *"so they are heedless."*
> Verse 7: …**fa-hum lā yu'minūn** — *"so they will not believe."*

[PAUSE]

Do you hear it? **The same cadence. The same opening — fa-hum, "so they." The same shape — a consequence, drawn by that fā', landing on a plural state of lack.**

Allah built the two verses as a matched pair, twin pillars with twin capitals — and the matching is the message. Because the *only* thing that changes between the two endings is the **stage of the soul:**

- **fa-hum ghāfilūn** — the soul *before* the warning. The fog. The waiting room. The excused silence.
- **fa-hum lā yu'minūn** — the soul *after* the warning, having refused it. The fog now *frozen.* The choice now *settled.*

The parallel structure *is* the doctrine. **Heedlessness is the soil; disbelief is the fruit that grows in it once the warning has been refused.** The same "so they" runs through both — one continuous line from fog to refusal — with only the warner, standing in the gap between the verses, marking where excuse ends and accountability begins. The form of the verses *enacts* the truth they teach. That is the proof, sitting in plain sound, that no human author arranged.

### Bringing It Together

See now how the linguistic discoveries and the lived themes were the same insight approached from two directions.

The shapeshifting **mā** taught us that the warning is *both* a mercy to a people who never heard *and* the one eternal summons — and that is exactly the two-sided truth of the *fatra* and the alarm in the carbon-monoxide room: *you couldn't have known* and *now you can.* The guttural **ghāfilūn,** a *state* and not an act, was the odorless gas that feels like normal life. The **ḥaqqa l-qawl,** the Word coming *due* like a debt, was the doctor's scan certifying — not causing — a reality the soul had built. And the merciful **aktharihim,** "most, not all," was the door left visibly open inside the verdict — the word that turns an ancient report into a question pointed straight at your chest.

Linguistics and life met in the middle and said the same thing.

### Questions to Carry

Take these with you. They are not rhetorical. Leave them genuinely open and let them work on you over the coming days.

1. **What is filling my room that I cannot smell?** Not the loud sins — the *odorless* drift I've stopped noticing because it became my normal.
2. When was the last time a warning genuinely *reached* me — and what did I do in the hour after? Did I act while the alarm sounded, or admire the feeling and roll back into the fog?
3. The verse says "**most**," not "all." If I am brutally honest — which side of that word is my week building toward?
4. What are the *detectors* in my life — the things that make a sound and break the fog — and have I been quietly unplugging them?
5. If the difference between **ghāfilūn** and **lā yu'minūn** is what a soul does *after* it hears — what am I doing, right now, having heard?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**Heedlessness is innocent only until the warning arrives — and the moment it arrives and is refused, the fog the soul was excused for becomes the destiny the soul has chosen; yet even then Allah says "most," not "all," leaving the door open for the one who is still awake enough to hear.**

### The Closing Invitation

You did not arrive at these words by accident. A warning is making a sound in your room right now — and the proof is that you noticed it, because the heedless notice nothing.

So here is the invitation, not the obligation. Do not let this be the blank stretch of highway. Do not arrive at the end of this reflection and remember none of the drive. Let one thing change — small, real, today. One prayer prayed *awake.* One puncture in the fog. One step toward the door marked "most."

The whole of Ya-Sin opens with Allah swearing by His own wise speech that a warner has come *so that* you might be warned. The warning has reached you. **What you do in the next hour is the entire difference between verse 6 and verse 7.**

### A Brief Du'a

O Allah, You sent down Your speech *that we might be warned* — so do not let us be among the **ghāfilūn,** breathing the odorless fog and calling it peace. Keep the alarm sounding in our hearts; keep us awake enough to hear it. And by Your mercy, place us among the remainder — not the *most* upon whom the Word has come due, but the few who heard, and turned, and walked through the door while it was still open.

Āmīn.
