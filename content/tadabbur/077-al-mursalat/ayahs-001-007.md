# By the Things You Cannot See

## Five Oaths for One Certainty — Surah Al-Mursalat 77:1–7

---

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the words first. In Arabic, in sound, and then in a meaning you can hold.

> [ayah:77:1] وَٱلْمُرْسَلَـٰتِ عُرْفًا
> [ayah:77:2] فَٱلْعَـٰصِفَـٰتِ عَصْفًا
> [ayah:77:3] وَٱلنَّـٰشِرَٰتِ نَشْرًا
> [ayah:77:4] فَٱلْفَـٰرِقَـٰتِ فَرْقًا
> [ayah:77:5] فَٱلْمُلْقِيَـٰتِ ذِكْرًا
> [ayah:77:6] عُذْرًا أَوْ نُذْرًا
> [ayah:77:7] إِنَّمَا تُوعَدُونَ لَوَٰقِعٌ

*Wal-mursalāti ʿurfā. Fal-ʿāṣifāti ʿaṣfā. Wan-nāshirāti nashrā. Fal-fāriqāti farqā. Fal-mulqiyāti dhikrā. ʿUdhran aw nudhrā. Innamā tūʿadūna lawāqiʿ.*

And in English, as close as English will carry it:

*By those sent forth one after another — and the storms that blow with violence — and those that scatter and spread wide — and those that part the true from the false — and those that cast down a reminder — as a clearing of excuse, or as a warning — indeed, what you are promised is going to fall.*

[PAUSE]

Now. Before we open a single word, I want you to feel the room this was first spoken into.

### Rebuild the world

Picture Makkah. Not the city of lights and millions you know today — a hard town of stone and trade, ringed by mountains that hold the heat. And in that town a man is saying something the leaders find not just offensive but *absurd*. He is telling them that one day the sky will split, the mountains will move like wool, and every human being who ever lived will stand and answer for what they did.

And the response — you can almost hear it — is a kind of laugh. *Resurrection? After we are dust? After the bones are scattered and the wind has taken what's left of us?* To them this was the one genuinely unbelievable claim. Not God — they believed in a god. Not angels — they spoke of them. But *the Day*. The reckoning. The idea that this whole running world was heading somewhere and would be brought to account. That, they could not swallow.

So watch what Allah does. He does not begin this surah with an argument. He does not open a debate. He raises His hand and He **swears an oath**. And then another. And another. Five of them, hammered one after the next, fast, rhythmic, rising — by forces in violent motion, forces these very deniers lived inside of every single day and *never once doubted*.

And only after the fifth oath does the sentence land:

*Indeed, what you are promised is going to fall.*

And here is a detail the earliest Muslims kept about how these very words reached us. Al-Bukhārī preserved it from Ibn Masʿūd — who would become one of the great readers of the Qurʾān. He was beside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in a cave at Minā when this surah came down, catching it, he said, straight from the Prophet's mouth, *his mouth still moist with it* — when a snake darted out at them and the moment broke. Sit with the scene for a breath: the surah of the *sent ones*, of the unseen forces in motion, descending in a cave, received mouth to mouth, while the world outside laughed at the very Day it had come to announce.

### The hook

Here is the question I want you to carry through everything that follows. Hold it like a stone in your hand.

**Why five?**

Why does Allah — who could establish anything with a single word, *kun* — stack five separate oaths to prove one short promise? When does the truth need that much swearing? And what exactly are these five things He swears by — things so undeniable that He uses them to prove the one thing His listeners refused to believe?

[PAUSE]

Stay with me. Because the *what* of these oaths, and the *why* of their number, turn out to be the same answer arriving from two directions.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The names with no namer

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we touch the ayah — something that will change what you see.

In ordinary speech, when you describe an action, you usually name who is doing it. *The soldiers marched. The horses ran. The messengers rode out.* Subject, then verb. We are wired to ask *who*.

But Arabic has a particular kind of word called the **active participle** — *ism al-fāʿil* — and its sister, the **passive participle**. These are words that name a doer *by the doing*. Not "the person who sends," but "the sender." Not "the one who was sent," but simply — **the sent**. The action becomes the name. The identity collapses into the act.

In English we have faint echoes of it. "The runners." "The fallen." "The believers." You hear it: the word points to people, but it shows you only what they *do*, never who they *are*.

Now open the ayah.

Every single one of these five oaths is built on exactly that kind of word. Five of them in a row:

- **ٱلْمُرْسَلَـٰتِ** *al-mursalāt* — "the ones sent forth" (root *r-s-l*, the root of *rasūl*, messenger — a passive participle: things that have been *sent*)
- **ٱلْعَـٰصِفَـٰتِ** *al-ʿāṣifāt* — "the ones blowing violently"
- **ٱلنَّـٰشِرَٰتِ** *al-nāshirāt* — "the ones scattering, spreading wide"
- **ٱلْفَـٰرِقَـٰتِ** *al-fāriqāt* — "the ones that separate, that part"
- **ٱلْمُلْقِيَـٰتِ** *al-mulqiyāt* — "the ones casting down"

[PAUSE]

Now notice what is *missing*.

Notice it carefully, because this is the whole architecture of the opening.

**Allah names what they do. He never names what they are.**

Five names — and not one of them is a noun you could point to. *The sent ones.* Sent — but what is sent? *The blowers.* Blowing — but who blows? *The scatterers, the separators, the casters.* The grammar describes pure motion, pure function, pure purpose — and leaves the actor in shadow.

In the science of the Qur'an this deliberate veiling has a name: *al-mubham* — the left-unspecified. And it is not an accident or a gap the commentators must fill. It is a door Allah is holding open on purpose.

Because watch what happens when the scholars walk through it.

### The composite sketch

The classical commentators did not agree on what these five things are — and when you read them together, you realize they were never meant to.

Al-Ṭabarī, the great early cataloguer of the dispute, opens his commentary on these lines with the very words that should frame everything: *ikhtalafa ahlu-l-taʾwīl* — "the people of interpretation differed." And he lays the views out side by side. The first and dominant one: these are **the winds**. *Al-mursalāt ʿurfan* — the winds sent in succession, one following hard on the heels of the last. And — this is worth holding precisely — al-Ṭabarī traces that very reading back to **Ibn Masʿūd himself**: he records Abū al-ʿUbaydayn putting the question to Ibn Masʿūd, who answers that *al-mursalāt* are the winds.

Al-Jalālayn says the same: *the winds, following one another like the mane of a horse.*

But that is only the first room. Other classical commentators — Mujāhid among them — read these same un-named forces as **the angels**: *al-mursalāt*, the angels sent with Allah's command; *al-mulqiyāt dhikran*, the angels casting down the revelation onto the hearts of the prophets.

And al-Muyassar, reaching for the whole picture, reads the chain as **both, in turn**, stage by stage — beginning with the winds sent blowing one after another, moving through *al-ʿāṣifāt* as the violent, **destroying** winds, then the angels charged with the clouds, driving them — arriving at the angels who carry down the revelation that *parts the true from the false*. Notice that even within the "winds" reading there is a shadow already falling: these sent forces are not only the bearers of rain and mercy; *al-ʿāṣifāt* are the winds that level and destroy. The same dispatched power that waters a land can flatten it. The unseen-in-motion is never merely gentle.

Now — here is what I want you to refuse to do. Do not treat this as a multiple-choice question where you must circle one. The *mubham*, the un-naming, is precisely what lets all of these be true at once. Allah did not say "the winds" and He did not say "the angels." He said **the sent ones**. And the winds are sent. And the angels are sent. And the revelation that the angel carries is sent. They are three layers of a single reality:

**Unseen forces, dispatched by a hidden command, moving through the world with purpose, arriving to separate truth from falsehood and to deliver a warning.**

The wind you feel on your face. The angel you cannot see driving the storm. The revelation descending onto a human heart. Three rungs of one ladder — and the un-named word holds all three rungs at once.

[PAUSE]

This is what the absence was *for*. A noun would have closed the door to one room. The participle keeps the whole house open.

What this single grammatical choice reveals: **Allah is swearing by the unseen-in-motion** — by exactly the category of thing His listeners could feel but never see, could never deny yet never grasp. Hold that. We will need it.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Two: The horse's mane — *ʿurfā*

Now I want to slow all the way down on one word. The very first one after the oath. Because it is doing more than it looks like it's doing.

*Wal-mursalāti* — **ʿurfā**.

The root is *ʿ-r-f*. And it is a rich root. From it comes *maʿrūf* — what is known, what is recognized, what is good and customary. From it comes *ʿarafa* — to know. And the commentators read *ʿurfan* here along two lines that, again, do not cancel each other but layer.

The first — al-Jalālayn says it plainly, and al-Baghawī with him: *ʿurf* is **the mane of a horse**. *Kaʿurfi-l-faras* — like the mane of the horse, where the strands of hair lie one against the next, in order, each following the one before it, flowing in a single unbroken line down the neck.

Sit with that image, because it is breathtakingly precise.

When the wind is sent — *al-mursalāt ʿurfan* — it does not arrive as one chaotic slap. It comes **in succession**. Gust following gust. Wave of air after wave of air. Ordered. Continuous. Each strand of wind laid against the last like the hairs of a mane streaming in the gallop.

And here is what that picture quietly tells you: **these forces are not random.** A mane is not a tangle. It falls in order. The winds Allah sends, the angels Allah dispatches, the revelations Allah casts down — they come in *arrangement*, in sequence, one prepared and following from the one before. There is a hand behind the order.

Now the second reading — Mujāhid, Qatāda, in al-Baghawī: *ʿurfan* meaning **in abundance, in a continuous stream**, from the Arab expression *al-nāsu ilā fulānin ʿurfun wāḥid* — "the people came to so-and-so in one unbroken stream," crowd upon crowd, when they pour toward someone without stopping.

Do you see how the two readings are the same shape from two angles? The mane is order in succession. The crowd is succession in abundance. Both say: **continuous, sequential, unstopping, arranged.** Not a single gust but a sustained sending. Not one messenger but a stream of them across the centuries. Not one verse but a Book descending over twenty-three years, *ʿurfan* — strand after strand after strand.

[PAUSE]

And feel the sound of the word itself for a moment. *ʿurfā.* It opens in the throat — that deep *ʿayn* — and rolls. The whole opening rolls: *ʿurfā… ʿaṣfā… nashrā… farqā… dhikrā… nudhrā.* Every oath ends in that same falling *-ā*, like gust after gust, like the strands of the mane themselves, one sound laid against the next. The *form* of the ayah is doing what the *meaning* of the ayah says. Succession you can *hear*.

What this one word reveals: the forces Allah swears by are **ordered and unceasing**. Behind what looks to us like the loose weather of the world, there is sequence. There is a mane. There is a hand on the reins.

[PAUSE — major]

### Element Three: *wāw* and *fā* — the grammar of unfolding

One more feature, and it is the most easily missed, because it hides in the two smallest letters in the passage.

Teach yourself this first, in plain life. In Arabic there are two little connectors that English flattens into one word, "and."

There is **wāw** (وَ) — pure "and." It joins two things without telling you the order between them. *I bought bread and milk.* Maybe milk first. It doesn't say.

And there is **fā** (فَ) — "and then," "and so," "whereupon." It carries **sequence and consequence**. *He stumbled and so he fell.* The *fā* tells you the second thing came out of the first. *Then.* As a result.

Now watch the connective spine of these five oaths. I'll mark each one:

1. **وَ**ٱلْمُرْسَلَـٰتِ — *wa*, the sent ones
2. **فَ**ٱلْعَـٰصِفَـٰتِ — *fa*, **and then** the violent blowers
3. **وَ**ٱلنَّـٰشِرَٰتِ — *wa*, the scatterers
4. **فَ**ٱلْفَـٰرِقَـٰتِ — *fa*, **and then** the separators
5. **فَ**ٱلْمُلْقِيَـٰتِ — *fa*, **and then** the casters of the reminder

Do you see the rhythm hidden in the letters? *wāw… fā… wāw… fā… fā.*

The *wāw* opens. The *fā* drives forward. And what the *fā* is telling you, three times, is: **this is not a list. This is a process.** One stage becoming the next.

The sending *becomes* the violent storm. *Fa.* The scattering *becomes* the separating. *Fa.* The separating *becomes* the casting down of the reminder. *Fa.*

Read it as the life of a wind: it is **sent** — *fa* — it rises to a **gale** — and then it is **spread** wide across the sky — *fa* — it **parts** the clouds — *fa* — and out of that parted sky comes the rain, the mercy, the thing that was the point all along.

Or read it as the life of revelation, which the commentators heard underneath: the command is **sent** — *fa* — it descends with **overwhelming force** onto the heart of the Prophet — it is **spread** out into the world — *fa* — it **separates** truth from falsehood, *al-fāriqāt farqan* — *fa* — and it is **cast down as a reminder**, *al-mulqiyāt dhikran*, onto the hearts of human beings.

[PAUSE]

The *fā* is the engine. It refuses to let these five things sit side by side as a catalogue. It makes them *move*. It makes them *arrive somewhere*. The whole opening is a current flowing toward a single destination — and the *fā* is the flow.

And where does it arrive? At the fifth oath: *al-mulqiyāt **dhikran*** — those casting down a **reminder.** Everything before it — the sending, the storm, the scattering, the separating — was building to *this*: that a reminder would be delivered to you. The motion of the entire cosmos, in these lines, is bending toward one purpose: **that you would be warned in time.**

What this reveals: the forces of the unseen are not idling. They are *en route*. They are a sequence with a terminus. And the terminus is *your* heart, receiving a reminder, before it is too late.

### The bridge

Step back and see what these three features have shown us together.

The participles told us Allah swears by **unseen things, named only by their motion** — winds, angels, revelation, all at once.

The word *ʿurfā* told us those things come **in ordered, unceasing succession** — a mane, a stream, a hand on the reins.

And the *wāw–fā* spine told us they are **a process flowing toward a purpose** — toward a reminder cast down into the human heart.

Now let us ask what all of this reveals — not just about words, but about reality itself, and about the thing you are standing inside of right now without seeing it.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: You have never seen the wind

Let me begin where you actually live.

You have never, in your entire life, seen the wind.

Stop and check that. It sounds wrong. But it's true. You have never once laid eyes on the wind itself. You have seen *leaves* turning over. You have seen a flag snapping, a tree bending, dust lifting off a road, a woman's scarf pulling sideways. You have felt cold air move across the back of your hand. You have *heard* it press against a window at night.

But the wind? The thing itself? It is completely invisible. It has no color. It has no edge. You could not point to it in a photograph. Air in motion — and motion you can only ever see by what it *moves*.

And yet.

There is not a single human being on earth who is an atheist about the wind. No one stands in a gale and says, "I will not believe in what I cannot see." You know it absolutely. You'd stake your life on it. You build houses to resist it and ships to ride it. You have organized whole civilizations around a thing **you have never once seen with your eyes.**

[PAUSE]

Now feel what Allah has done in these seven lines.

He swears — *by the very thing you cannot see but cannot deny.* By the sent ones. By the violent blowers. By the unseen-in-motion that rules your world from behind a curtain.

And He swears by it to prove **the one thing the deniers said they couldn't see**: the Day. The reckoning. *Innamā tūʿadūna lawāqiʿ* — what you are promised is going to fall.

Do you feel the trap closing, gently, with perfect justice?

The Makkans said: *we won't believe in a Day we cannot see.* And Allah answers, in effect: *Then you must disbelieve in the wind. For you have never seen it either. You have only ever known it by its effects — by what it sends, what it scatters, what it parts. And you would never dream of denying it.*

This is the whole logic the Qur'an returns to again and again — *al-istidlāl bil-maḥsūs*, proving the unseen by the felt. You already live, every waking second, surrounded by powers you cannot see and would never deny — gravity holding you to your chair, the air filling your lungs, the wind moving the world. Your entire life is an act of trust in invisible forces. **The unseen is not the exception in your life. It is the ground of it.**

And so the Day is not asking you to believe in some new and alien category. It is asking you to be *consistent*. The same God who sends the wind you cannot see has promised a Day you cannot see — and He keeps the appointment of the wind every single morning. *He is not in the habit of breaking the rhythm.*

[PAUSE]

What does this change about tomorrow?

Here is the practical edge. We tend to grant reality only to what we can put our hands on — the bill, the deadline, the body, the thing on the screen. And we quietly file the Day, the meeting with Allah, the weight of our deeds, under "abstract." Someday. Unreal. Not now.

These oaths are a correction. The next time the wind moves something in front of you — a curtain, a branch, your own coat — let it be a *dhikr*, a reminder, exactly as the ayah says: *al-mulqiyāt dhikran.* Here is an invisible thing you do not doubt. Treat the Day with the same seriousness you already, without thinking, grant the wind. Live as though the unseen is the *more* real, not the less — because the One who sends the wind has told you which way the current is flowing.

### Theme Two: When the truth needs five oaths

Now back to the question I asked you to carry. *Why five?*

Let me start, again, inside something human.

Think about how conviction actually forms in a person — not the conviction of the mind, but the conviction of the *gut*. The kind you would act on.

A stranger tells you something once. You nod. You file it under "maybe." Now a second person, unconnected, tells you the same thing. You straighten a little. A third. A fourth. By the time five independent voices, from five directions, have told you the same thing — something shifts that is *below* argument. You don't reason your way there. The repetition itself does the work. The truth stops being a claim you are evaluating and becomes a floor you are standing on.

This is not a flaw in you. It is how the heart is built. The mind can be convinced by one clean proof. But the **heart** is convinced by *accumulation* — by being surrounded, by hearing it from every side until there is nowhere left to stand that isn't inside the truth.

[PAUSE]

Now look at what Allah is doing.

He has one thing to tell these people. *Innamā tūʿadūna lawāqiʿ* — the promise is going to fall. One short sentence. He could have said it bare.

Instead, He surrounds them. Five oaths, from five directions, each one a force they already believe in absolutely. *By the sent ones. By the storms. By the scatterers. By the separators. By the casters of the reminder.* Wind from the east, wind from the west, the angel, the storm, the revelation — five witnesses to the same single fact, closing in, until by the time the seventh line arrives, there is no longer anywhere to stand that is outside the truth of it.

He is not arguing with the mind. He is **convincing the heart the way the heart is actually convinced** — by surrounding it. The five oaths are not five proofs that the Day will come. They are five hands placed on the listener's shoulders, turning him slowly, until he is facing the only direction left.

And notice — this is *mercy* in its method. Allah did not have to do this. The decree is the decree whether you're convinced or not. The Day will fall on the persuaded and the unpersuaded alike. That He takes the trouble to surround your reluctant heart with witnesses it cannot reject — that is not the act of a judge eager to condemn. It is the act of One who *wants you ready.*

[PAUSE]

And the sixth line tells you so, out loud.

*ʿUdhran aw nudhrā* — **as a clearing of excuse, or as a warning.**

This is the stated purpose of the whole descending reminder. *Nudhr* — a warning, for the one who can still turn. And *ʿudhr* — from the root of *ʿudhr*, excuse — meaning the removal of all excuse, so that on that Day no soul can stand and say, *no one told me. I was never warned. I never had the chance.*

Al-Muyassar names it precisely: the reminder is sent down *iʿdhāran wa-inḏāran* — as a clearing of excuse and as a warning — *li-allā yakūna lahum ḥujja* — **so that they would have no argument left** on the Day they stand before Him.

Feel the weight of that. The warning comes *first*. Before the fall. The reminder is cast down — *al-mulqiyāt dhikran* — into your living, breathing days, while there is still time to move. The God who promises the Day also, in the same breath, sends the warning of it ahead of itself, so that the Day, when it falls, falls on no one who was kept in the dark.

[PAUSE]

What does this change about tomorrow?

It reframes what a reminder *is*. We tend to feel the reminder — the verse that pricks us, the death that sobers us, the fear that visits at 3 a.m. — as an intrusion. An unwelcome heaviness. Something interrupting our peace.

These lines tell you to read it the opposite way. The reminder reaching you is *al-mulqiyāt dhikran* — it is the final, merciful stage of that whole cosmic current, the sending and the storm and the scattering all bending toward this: **that the warning would reach you in time.** A reminder that lands on your heart is not the Day arriving. It is the Day's *advance mercy* — the *ʿudhr*, the chance, the warning before the warning is too late to use.

So tomorrow, when something reminds you — do not flinch from it as if it were the fall itself. Recognize it for what the ayah says it is: a thing *cast down to you* so that you would not be caught unprepared. The discomfort of being reminded is the kindest thing that can happen to a person before the Day. It means you are still inside the *ʿudhr*. You are still in time.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture revealed

Now stand back and see the whole shape, because it has been building under your feet the entire time.

Six lines of oath. One line of answer.

The six lines are pure **motion** — sent, blowing, scattering, separating, casting, all carried forward by that driving *fā*, a current with no rest in it, surging toward a single end. And then the seventh line is pure **stillness**:

*Innamā tūʿadūna lawāqiʿ.* — Indeed, what you are promised **is going to fall.**

And look at that last word — *wāqiʿ*, from *w-q-ʿ*, **to fall, to land, to come down and stay down.** After six lines of things flying, blowing, scattering across the sky — everything finally *lands*. The motion of the entire opening resolves into one thing coming down to earth and not moving again. The whole storm of the surah was always falling toward the ground of that one certainty.

The six restless oaths and the one settled answer are the same truth from two sides: **everything in the unseen world is in purposeful motion toward an appointed landing.** The wind is going somewhere. The angel is going somewhere. The revelation is going somewhere. And so are you. *And the place it all lands is the Day.*

### Bringing it together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes were one insight all along, arriving from two directions.

The grammar showed us **unseen forces, named only by motion, ordered like a mane, flowing by the engine of *fā* toward a reminder.** The themes showed us **a God who proves the unseen Day by the unseen wind, and who surrounds the reluctant heart with five witnesses out of mercy, so the warning lands before the fall does.**

Same truth. The form *is* the meaning. Allah swears by invisible-yet-undeniable things in motion — to prove an invisible-yet-undeniable Day in motion — and stacks the oaths five-high not to win an argument but to ready a heart. The *what* and the *why* of those five oaths turned out, as I promised, to be the same answer.

### Questions to carry

Sit with these. They are not rhetorical. Leave them open.

1. What invisible things do I trust completely without ever having seen them — and why do I grant the Day less reality than I grant the wind?
2. When a reminder reaches me and stings — do I flinch from it, or do I recognize it as a mercy sent *ahead* of the fall, while there is still time?
3. If the warning comes precisely so that I will have no excuse on that Day — what excuse am I quietly still rehearsing?
4. The whole cosmos in these lines is in purposeful motion toward a landing. Is my life moving with that current — or am I the one thing pretending to stand still?

### One-sentence distillation

Allah swears by the unseen forces you feel every day but have never seen — wind, angel, revelation — to prove that the Day you cannot see is as certain as the storm you cannot see, and He swears it five times over not to defeat your mind but to ready your heart before the warning is too late to use.

### The closing invitation

Nothing here asks you to believe something foreign. It asks you to be *consistent* — to extend to the Day the same plain trust you already, without thinking, extend to the wind. And it offers you a gift: that every reminder reaching your heart is the *ʿudhr*, the merciful warning sent ahead of the fall, proof that you are still in time. Let the next breeze across your face be a *dhikr*. And let the next reminder that stings be received not as an intrusion, but as the kindest thing that can reach a person who has not yet stood before their Lord.

### Du'a

O Allah, You who send the winds we cannot see and keep the appointment of the wind every morning — make us among those who trust the appointment of the Day. You cast down the reminder as a mercy before the fall; do not let us be of those who turned from the warning until the warning came too late. Let every reminder that reaches our hearts find us still in time, and let us stand on that Day with no excuse — because we needed none. *Āmīn.*
