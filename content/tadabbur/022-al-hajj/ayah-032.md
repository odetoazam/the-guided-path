# The Heart Is the Only Currency

## *Sūrat al-Ḥajj, ayah 32*

> [ayah:22:32] ذَٰلِكَ وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ شَعَـٰٓئِرَ ٱللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى ٱلْقُلُوبِ
>
> *Dhālika wa man yuʿaẓẓim shaʿāʾira Llāhi fa-innahā min taqwā al-qulūb.*
>
> *"That [is so]. And whoever magnifies the symbols of Allah — indeed, this is from the taqwā of the hearts."*

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The world this ayah enters

Surah Al-Hajj is unusual. It is the only surah in the Qur'an named after a ritual — not a story, not a prophet, not a quality, but a journey. A journey toward a House that Allah established before any other house on earth.

The surah is also strange in its movement. It begins with the convulsion of the Last Day. Then it descends into the human womb, walks us through embryology, plants seed in dry earth and brings it back to life. It travels through the destruction of past nations, through the permission of armed self-defense, through the slaughter of sacrificial cattle. By the time we arrive at this ayah, the listener has been through cosmic upheaval and biological miracle and historical ruin and ritual choreography.

And then everything stops.

The procession of laws and rites pauses. The voice softens. Allah does not give us another rule. He gives us **the secret behind every rule He has just given.**

### What was happening when these words landed

Picture the early Muslims hearing this in Madinah. Many of them had grown up with the Kaʿbah — they knew its stones, they knew the seasons of pilgrimage, they had watched their fathers and grandfathers tie cattle and lead them to the sanctuary. The motions were familiar. Some of them had seen those motions emptied of meaning — turned into tribal display, into spectacle, into a way to be seen by other tribes.

And now, here, Allah is preparing them to do these same motions again — but as something they had never been before.

What separates the empty motion from the transformative one?

That is the question this ayah answers.

If the surah is a body, this ayah is the heartbeat. If the surah is a key ring, this ayah is the cut that fits the lock. If the surah is the sky, this ayah is the single star you can navigate by.

### The hook

There is a reason some people return from Hajj transformed and some return only tired. There is a reason two people can pray the same prayer side by side and only one of them comes out of the prayer changed. There is a reason a single dhikr from a sincere mouth weighs more in the unseen ledger than a thousand from a distracted one.

Allah is about to tell us why — in a single sentence so quiet you might miss it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

We have three words to walk through. Each one alone would justify a lifetime of reflection. Together they say something the surrounding rules cannot say.

---

### Element One: **shaʿāʾir** — the markers placed in the world

#### A concept first

Before we open the word, sit with this idea.

Imagine you are walking through a forest you have never entered. The trail is unmarked. You don't know if you are going forward or in circles. Someone who knows the forest comes with you, and at intervals along the way they tie a small ribbon to a branch — not a sign, not an arrow, just a ribbon. To anyone who didn't know what the ribbons meant, they would be invisible. But to you, walking with the guide, every ribbon registers. *I am still on the path. I am being shown the way.*

Now imagine the ribbons were placed in the forest before you ever entered it. Imagine the guide says: *I tied these long before you were born. They have been waiting for you.*

This is the world Allah is asking us to live in.

He has placed **markers**. Not just instructions. Markers. Things in the world whose entire purpose is to register on a heart that is alive enough to feel them.

#### The word

> **شَعَائِرَ** — *shaʿāʾir*

The plural of *shaʿīrah*. Its root — ش-ع-ر — is one of the most quietly powerful roots in the Arabic language.

From this root come:
- *shaʿara* — to know with the senses, to perceive, to feel
- *shuʿūr* — feeling, awareness, consciousness
- *shaʿr* — hair (because it is what registers the lightest touch on the skin)
- *shiʿr* — poetry (because poetry is the speech that *registers*, that is felt before it is understood)
- *shaʿīrah* — a marker, a sign by which a thing is known

Notice what this root does. It does not mean "to know" in the cold, intellectual sense. It means **to know by sensing**. To register. To feel something land.

A *shaʿīrah*, then, is not just a sign. It is a thing placed in the world that is supposed to **be felt**. A station of perception.

#### Stay with this

When Allah calls the rites of Hajj His *shaʿāʾir*, He is not saying: *these are My laws.* He is saying: *these are My markers in the fabric of the world. They are places where, if your heart is alive, you will feel something.*

The Kaʿbah is a *shaʿīrah*. The standing at ʿArafah is a *shaʿīrah*. The walking between Ṣafā and Marwah is a *shaʿīrah*. The shaving of the head, the donning of the iḥrām, the cattle led to slaughter — these are not arbitrary obligations. They are tied ribbons in the forest. They are points where Allah has marked the world for the heart that can perceive.

Notice something now. A blind man can walk past a road sign and not know it is there. A deaf man can stand inside a symphony and not hear it. The sign is real. The symphony is real. But the faculty to register them is absent.

What Allah is preparing us for in this ayah is exactly that question: *Is your faculty of perception alive enough to register what I have placed in the world?*

[PAUSE]

#### One layer deeper

The classical lexicons tell us something else. The plural form *shaʿāʾir* — this particular pattern (faʿāʾil) — often carries the sense of things that are scattered, distributed, multiple. The *shaʿāʾir* are not one marker. They are markers throughout the journey. Throughout the year. Throughout the life.

Hajj is not the only place where Allah has tied ribbons. The five daily prayers are markers. The fast of Ramadan is a marker. The greeting of *salām* is a marker. The mosque is a marker. The Qur'an itself, recited aloud in the night, is a marker.

Allah has saturated the believer's life with **stations of perception**. Places where the heart, if it is awake, will feel something.

The question this ayah is about to ask is: *what kind of heart is awake enough to feel them?*

---

### Element Two: **yuʿaẓẓim** — the labor of making great

#### A concept first

In Arabic, verbs come in patterns. The same three-letter root can be conjugated in different "forms" — Form I, Form II, Form III, and so on — and each form changes the meaning in a precise way.

Form II, the form called *tafʿīl*, is the form of **intensification and deliberateness**. It takes a simple action and makes it sustained, doubled, deliberate. Form II often turns *to be X* into *to make X* — or *to do X once* into *to do X with intensity, with repetition, with effort*.

A simple example. The root ع-ل-م means *to know*. Form I is *ʿalima* — he knew. Form II is *ʿallama* — he taught (he made another know, repeatedly, deliberately, until the knowing was complete).

The shape of Form II is heavier than Form I. There is a doubling — a *shaddah* — on the middle letter. The mouth has to work harder to say it. The pronunciation itself enacts the meaning.

#### The word

> **يُعَظِّمْ** — *yuʿaẓẓim*

The root is ع-ظ-م — meaning *to be great, to be vast, to be weighty*.

Form I, *ʿaẓuma*, would mean *he became great*.

But Allah does not use Form I.

He uses **Form II**, *ʿaẓẓama / yuʿaẓẓim* — with the doubled middle letter, the doubled effort, the doubled deliberateness. *To make great. To magnify. To labor at exalting.*

Hear it on your tongue. *Yu-ʿAẒ-ẒIM.* The doubling sits in the middle of the word like a weight you have to lift over.

#### Why this matters

Allah did not say: *whoever sees the symbols of Allah as great* — that would be the passive recognition of greatness, the simple Form I.

He said: *whoever yuʿaẓẓim* — **whoever does the work of making them great in his life.**

This is critical. *Yuʿaẓẓim* is not an opinion. It is not a feeling that visits you on a holy day. It is a posture sustained over time. It is what you do when the alarm goes off for fajr and you are tired. It is how you carry your body when you enter the mosque. It is how you treat the muṣḥaf when it is sitting on the table. It is whether you allow the call to prayer to interrupt your sentence, or whether you finish your sentence first.

To *yuʿaẓẓim* a *shaʿīrah* of Allah is to **enact its weight in your life**. To let it cost you something. To let it shape your schedule, your money, your attention, your body.

Form II is the verb of effort. The shape of the word itself contains the effort: the doubling at its center is a physical labor of pronunciation.

Allah is saying: *the heart that recognizes the markers is not enough. The heart must move. The body must follow. The life must be rearranged.*

[PAUSE]

#### Notice the asymmetry

Here is something worth pausing on. Allah did not say *whoever knows the symbols of Allah are great*. He did not say *whoever announces that the symbols of Allah are great*. He did not say *whoever defends the symbols of Allah*.

He said *whoever does the labor of magnifying them*.

This is not the verb of theology. It is the verb of practice.

A man can have correct theology about the *shaʿāʾir* and never *yuʿaẓẓim* them. He can know the Kaʿbah is the House of Allah and walk past it on his phone. He can know the prayer is the pillar of religion and pray it like he is filling out a form. He can know Ramadan is sacred and let the month slip through his hands like water.

The intellect's recognition is not the same as the body's enactment. Allah is naming the second.

### Element Three: **fa-innahā min taqwā al-qulūb** — the partitive *min* and the surprising specification of hearts

This is the part that, when you see it, changes everything.

#### A concept first

In Arabic, the preposition *min* has many uses. One of the most important is the **partitive** *min* — the *min* that means *from, of, a portion of*.

If I say *kultu min al-khubz* — I ate from the bread — I am not saying I ate the bread in its entirety. I am saying I ate a portion of it. Some of it. The bread is larger than what I consumed. There is more bread.

This kind of *min* opens a horizon. It tells you: *this thing you are looking at is part of something bigger.*

#### The phrase

> **فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى الْقُلُوبِ** — *fa-innahā min taqwā al-qulūb*

*Indeed, it is **of** the taqwā of the hearts.*

Notice what Allah did *not* say.

He did not say *fa-innahā taqwā al-qulūb* — "indeed, it IS the taqwā of the hearts." That would have made the act and the inward state identical. The act would equal taqwā.

He said *fa-innahā **min** taqwā al-qulūb* — "indeed, it is **from / a portion of** the taqwā of the hearts."

The act of magnifying the markers is one expression of a larger inward reality. The visible enactment is a piece — one piece — of a hidden whole.

This is breathtaking when you sit with it.

#### Stay with this

The act is a window. Not the room.

When you see someone *yuʿaẓẓim* the *shaʿāʾir* — when you see them slow their walk as they enter the mosque, when you see them hush their voice when the muṣḥaf is opened, when you see their face change at the call to prayer — what you are seeing is **a piece of something interior**.

You are seeing one ribbon of a much larger garment. The garment is *taqwā*. The garment is in the heart. What is visible to you is only the edge of it.

And — this is what stuns me — Allah is telling you that **the act is not the whole story even of itself**. The act is a *partial* expression. There is more in the heart than is visible in the act. The heart is larger than the gesture.

This means that the believer's life is not a performance scaled to the audience. It is a life scaled to a heart only Allah can see. The visible acts are the spillover of an interior reality. They are the steam rising off a hot pot. The fire is somewhere else.

[PAUSE]

#### And then — the hearts

Now look at the second half: *taqwā al-qulūb* — the taqwā **of the hearts**.

Here is the question that should arrest you.

Why did Allah specify *the hearts*?

There is no other taqwā.

You cannot have taqwā of the elbow. You cannot have taqwā of the tongue separated from the heart. Taqwā has only one possible location.

So why did Allah pin the location explicitly? Why not just say *min al-taqwā* — "from taqwā"?

Classical commentators sat with this. Their answer is severe and clarifying.

Allah specified the hearts **because the alternative was so possible**. Because so many of us perform an outward taqwā that has never reached the heart. Because so much of religious life can be theater. Because the person standing in the front row of the prayer can be further from Allah than the person sleeping in the back, and the person whose tongue runs constant *dhikr* can have a heart no breath of remembrance has ever entered.

When Allah says *taqwā al-qulūb*, He is not being redundant. He is being surgical. He is cutting away every kind of taqwā that lives only on the surface — the taqwā of the body without the heart, the taqwā of the tongue without the heart, the taqwā of the reputation without the heart — and naming, with terrible clarity, **the only kind that counts**.

The taqwā of the hearts.

The kind that is hidden.
The kind that nobody but Allah can verify.
The kind you cannot fake even to yourself for very long.

[PAUSE — major pause]

#### The composite picture

Now hold the three words together.

*Shaʿāʾir* — markers Allah has placed in the world.
*Yuʿaẓẓim* — the sustained labor of enacting their weight in your life.
*Min taqwā al-qulūb* — and that whole enactment is one partial expression of a hidden heart-reality that only Allah can see.

What Allah has just described is **the architecture of every meaningful act of worship**:

There is a marker in the world.
You do the work of making it weighty.
And the work itself is the surface of an inward state that is larger, deeper, and invisible.

The act is the visible peak. The heart is the mountain underneath. And the marker in the world is the place where the mountain breaks the surface.

### Bridge

We have walked through three words. We have seen the markers placed in the world. We have seen the labor of magnifying them. We have seen that the labor is only the partial spillover of an inward state located in a place only Allah can see.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The heart is the only currency

#### Begin where we already live

Think about the people in your life who love you.

Not the ones who say they love you. The ones who actually do.

If you compare what they do to what someone else might do, the actions can look identical. A husband bringing his wife tea. A mother packing her child's lunch. A friend showing up on the day you needed someone.

But you know — without anyone telling you — when the gesture is hollow and when it is full. You can feel it. The same cup of tea, from a heart that resents you, lands as an obligation. From a heart that loves you, it lands as a small grace.

You did not learn to read the difference in school. You read it because the heart of another person is something a human being can almost-see. We sense it. We feel it. The gesture and the heart behind the gesture are not the same thing, and we know it.

Now extend that.

If a human being can almost-see the heart of another human being — what do you think Allah sees?

#### The ayah illuminates this

Allah is telling us, in this single sentence, that **what He sees in any act of worship is not the act**.

He sees the heart that brought the act.

The same prayer, performed identically, weighs differently in His sight depending on what is in the heart that performed it. The same sacrifice, the same ḥajj, the same charity, the same fast — they are not the same to Him, because the hearts are not the same.

This is why elsewhere, in another surah, Allah says about the sacrificial animals: *"It is not their flesh that reaches Allah, nor their blood; rather, what reaches Him is the taqwā from you"* (22:37). Same surah. Same logic. **The flesh does not arrive. The heart arrives.**

The currency of the unseen ledger is not actions. It is **hearts that produced actions**.

This means the most consequential thing about your worship is something you cannot photograph. You cannot describe it in a status update. You cannot quantify it on an app. The most consequential thing about your worship is **the state of the heart from which it issued** — and that state is known only to you and to Allah.

[PAUSE]

#### Sit with the implication

This is liberation, and it is also terror.

Liberation, because nobody else's eyes count. The neighbor who watches you. The relative who measures you. The community that ranks you. The stranger who judges your hijab or your beard or your prayer or your fast. **None of their seeing matters.** Allah is not consulting them about your account. He is looking at one place — the place inside your chest — and that place is not visible to any of them.

Terror, because nothing you can perform can compensate for an absent heart. You can build the life of an outwardly perfect Muslim and have nothing in the heart to make it count. The acts are receipts. The heart is the payment. And if there is no payment, the receipts are paper.

Both are true. The ayah holds them both.

#### The movement deeper

Here is what this means for the way you live tomorrow.

When you perform any act of worship — *any* act — there is one question that decides whether the act has weight:

*Is my heart in this?*

Not: *did I do it correctly?* Not: *did I do enough of it?* Not: *did I do it in the right place?*

*Is my heart in this?*

If you find that the answer is no — that the act is happening but the heart is somewhere else — the work is not to abandon the act. The work is **to bring the heart back**.

The Companions used to weep when they prayed. Not because they had been told to weep. Because something in their heart was so awake to the markers Allah had placed in their lives that the markers landed every time. The call to prayer was not a notification. It was a summons that registered.

For most of us, the markers no longer register. We have walked past them so many times that the ribbons in the forest have become invisible.

The work of this ayah is to make them visible again.

#### The practical application

Pick one *shaʿīrah* — one marker — that has gone numb in your life.

For most of us it is the prayer.

Tomorrow, before the next prayer, pause for thirty seconds before *takbīr*. Do not rush into it. Stand at the entrance of the prayer the way you would stand at the entrance of a king's palace if you actually believed the king was inside.

Ask your heart: *what is in you right now? Are you here? Are you elsewhere? Bring yourself.*

Then begin.

You will not do this perfectly. You will not even do it consistently. But every time you do it, you are doing the work of *yuʿaẓẓim* — you are making the marker weigh again. You are letting the ribbon become visible. You are bringing the heart back to the place where the heart is supposed to be.

That thirty seconds of pause — that act nobody sees, that act that does not appear in the count of your prayers — **that is the act this ayah was written for**.

### Theme Two: The world as marked text — and the eye that can read it

#### Begin where we already live

Think about the difference between hearing a song you love and hearing it for the first time as a stranger.

The first time, the song is pleasant. Maybe.

But after the song has lived in your life — after it has played at a wedding, after it carried you through a hard week, after you sang it with someone you loved who is gone now — the same song lands completely differently. The notes have not changed. The lyrics have not changed. *You* have changed. The song now arrives bearing weight that was not in the notes.

Or think of a place. A street. A house. To a tourist it is geography. To someone who grew up there, every corner is loaded. The street is not just a street. It is a marker. A station of memory.

This is how perception works. The world is not made of objects. The world is made of objects that **register** — or fail to — depending on the state of the one perceiving.

#### The ayah illuminates this

Allah has placed markers in the world.

Some of them are universal — the Kaʿbah, the prayers, the months, the Qur'an itself. Some of them are personal — the moment the call to prayer reaches your ears in a city you do not know, the smell of the place where someone first taught you a verse, the cloth your grandmother used to spread when she sat down to read.

The world is **dense with markers**. They are everywhere. The believer walks through a forest of ribbons.

But here is the line — and this is the line the ayah draws:

**The markers cannot mark you if your heart is not awake to them.**

Two people can stand in front of the Kaʿbah. One of them is shattered by what he sees. The other is checking his phone. The Kaʿbah is the same. The eye is the same. The difference is in the heart that is, or is not, equipped to perceive.

The root of *shaʿāʾir* — ش-ع-ر — was, you remember, the root of perception. *To feel. To know by sensing.* A *shaʿīrah* is a thing meant to **register on a heart that can sense**.

A heart without taqwā cannot sense the markers Allah has placed. The forest looks empty. The ribbons are invisible.

This is why two people can pray the same prayer and only one of them comes out of the prayer changed. The prayer is full of markers — the *takbīr* is a marker, the *fātiḥah* is a marker, the *sajdah* is a marker. But the markers only mark a heart that is alive enough to register them.

#### The movement deeper

Here is the staggering thought.

Your job in this life is not just to perform the *shaʿāʾir*. Your job is to **become a heart capable of perceiving them**.

This is a different project entirely.

The first project — performing the markers — can be done with discipline. You can be a punctual Muslim with no heart. You can fast every Ramadan, pray every prayer, give every zakāt, perform Hajj, and remain a heart that never registered any of it.

The second project — becoming a heart that can perceive — cannot be done with discipline alone. It requires something else. It requires the deliberate cultivation of inward life. It requires returning attention to the chest. It requires asking, again and again, *what is happening in here?*

This is what Allah, in this ayah, has just hinted at. *Min taqwā al-qulūb* — from the taqwā of the hearts. The thing that allows the markers to register is taqwā located inside. Without it, the world is mute. With it, the world begins to speak.

#### Sit with the paradox

Here is a paradox to hold without rushing to resolve.

The act of *yuʿaẓẓim* — the labor of magnifying the markers — is what builds the taqwā that allows the markers to register.

In other words: the practice precedes the perception, and the perception deepens the practice.

When you first stand in prayer with effort — even when your heart is not in it, even when you are forcing the body to be present while the mind drifts — you are doing the labor of *yuʿaẓẓim*. The labor is real even when the perception is not yet ready. And over time, the labor itself **builds the heart that begins to perceive**.

This means that even on the days when the prayer is empty, the act of doing it with effort is not wasted. The body is teaching the heart. The repetition is shaping the perception. One day — perhaps not soon — you will stand in *takbīr* and feel something land. The marker will register. The ribbon will become visible. And on that day you will know that all the empty prayers were not empty after all. They were soil.

#### The practical application

Walk through your day tomorrow with one question.

*What markers has Allah placed in this day that I am walking past without seeing?*

The call to prayer, when it cuts through your meeting. The first sip of water after a hot afternoon. The face of a parent who is older this year than they were last year. The Qur'an in the car you have not opened in months. The hand of a person who needs help. The empty stomach of a fast that you broke without saying *bismillāh*.

Each of these is a *shaʿīrah*. Each of them is a tied ribbon. They are all there. The question is whether your heart is awake enough to register them.

Begin with one. Just one. Let one marker, today, actually mark you.

That single registered moment — that one ribbon you actually saw — is the beginning of the heart that this ayah is calling forward.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architectural reveal

Look back at the ayah now.

> *Dhālika wa man yuʿaẓẓim shaʿāʾira Llāhi fa-innahā min taqwā al-qulūb.*

It begins with *dhālika* — *that*. A single demonstrative pointing back to everything Allah has just described. The Hajj rules. The cattle. The garments. The avoidance of falsehood. *That.*

Then: *and whoever does the labor of magnifying the markers of Allah*.

Then: *that labor is one piece of the taqwā of the hearts.*

The architecture is a descent. From the visible (the rituals: *dhālika*) — to the active (the labor: *yuʿaẓẓim*) — to the inward (the heart: *al-qulūb*).

The ayah moves us from outside to inside. From what can be seen to what cannot be seen. From what can be counted to what can only be witnessed by Allah.

And the partitive *min* tells us that even the inward labor we just named is only **part** of what is in the heart. The heart is bigger than the act. The heart is bigger than what we can describe. The heart is the mountain, and the act is only where the mountain breaks the surface.

### Bring it together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

The linguistic side told us:
- *Shaʿāʾir* are markers placed in the world.
- *Yuʿaẓẓim* is the sustained labor of enacting their weight.
- *Min taqwā al-qulūb* locates the meaning of the labor inside the heart.

The thematic side told us:
- The heart is the only currency Allah is counting.
- The world is a forest of markers, and only the heart with taqwā can see them.

Both sides converge on a single sentence:

**The world is full of signs from Allah, but only the heart can read them — and only the heart can be read by Him.**

### Questions to carry

Carry these home with you. Do not answer them quickly.

1. **Which *shaʿīrah* in my life has gone numb?** Which marker have I been performing for so long that I no longer feel it land? What would it take to feel it again?

2. **If Allah is reading my heart and not my actions, what is He reading right now?** Not what would I want Him to read. What is actually there.

3. **Where in my worship is the ratio inverted — where is the visible large and the inward small?** Where am I performing more than I am praying?

4. **Whose seeing am I living for?** When I do the *shaʿāʾir*, am I doing them in the line of sight of other people, or in the line of sight of Allah?

5. **What labor of *yuʿaẓẓim* is Allah inviting me to begin?** Not what I should have been doing. What He is asking of me, in this season of my life, right now.

### One-sentence distillation

The visible markers Allah has placed in the world only become weighty when a heart with taqwā does the labor of making them weighty — and that labor itself is only the surface of an inward state larger than any act can reveal.

### The closing invitation

You do not have to overhaul your life tonight.

You only have to change one thing.

The next time you stand in prayer — the next time you reach for the muṣḥaf — the next time the call to prayer reaches your ears — pause for one breath. Bring your heart to the doorway of the act. Ask: *am I here?*

Then enter.

That single breath of attention — that small, invisible, unimpressive pause — is the beginning of the *taqwā of the hearts*.

It is what Allah is reading.

It is what counts.

Make our worship the surface of an inward life only You can see. And place in our chests the taqwā that turns ritual into encounter, before the day comes when we are summoned to a House where only the heart will be weighed.*

> *Āmīn.*
