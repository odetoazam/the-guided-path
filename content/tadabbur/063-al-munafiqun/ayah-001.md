# The True Sentence of a Lying Tongue

> [ayah:63:1] إِذَا جَآءَكَ ٱلْمُنَـٰفِقُونَ قَالُوا۟ نَشْهَدُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱللَّهُ يَشْهَدُ إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَـٰفِقِينَ لَكَـٰذِبُونَ

*Idhā jāʾaka l-munāfiqūna qālū nashhadu innaka la-rasūlu llāh, wa-llāhu yaʿlamu innaka la-rasūluh, wa-llāhu yashhadu inna l-munāfiqīna la-kādhibūn.*

**"When the hypocrites come to you, they say: 'We bear witness that you are indeed the Messenger of Allah.' And Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars."**

## INTRODUCTION: A Sentence That Should Have Been Praised

Let me set the scene before we touch a single word.

Madinah. The community is no longer a handful of believers hiding in Makkah. It is a city, a society, a body politic. And in any society that begins to hold power, a new kind of person appears — a person who would never have bothered to fake belief when belief was dangerous, but who now finds it *useful* to be counted among the believers.

These are the people the Quran calls *al-munāfiqūn*. Not the open enemy outside the walls. The smiling face inside the gathering. The one who sits in the front row of the prayer and votes against you in the dark.

And here is the moment this surah opens onto. A delegation of them comes to the Prophet ﷺ. They look him in the eye. And they say something so correct, so theologically flawless, that if *you* had said it, it would have been counted among your acts of worship:

*"We bear witness that you are the Messenger of Allah."*

[PAUSE]

Stop and feel the strangeness of that.

This is the **shahāda**. This is the second half of the testimony of faith. *Muhammadun rasūlullāh.* These are the very words a person says to *enter* Islam. These are words the angels record as light. A believer says them and the heavens open.

And the hypocrites say them — word for word, perfectly — and in the very next breath Allah calls them **liars**.

Not "they were wrong." Not "they were mistaken about the Prophet." Allah does not contest the *content* of their sentence at all. In fact, He immediately *confirms* it: *yes, you are indeed His Messenger.* The sentence is true. The sentence is gloriously, eternally true.

And the people who spoke it are liars.

[PAUSE]

How? How can a man speak the truth — the highest truth there is — and have the All-Knowing turn to him and say *you are lying*?

That question is the whole surah. Hold onto it. Because the answer is going to reach into your chest and ask whether the words *you* say — the "I'm fine," the "I'm so grateful," the "of course I forgive you," even the *Allāhu akbar* you may say a thousand times — whether those words are true on your tongue or true in your heart.

Today we are going to learn that **the lie is not always in the sentence. Sometimes the lie is in the act of speaking it.**

Let's begin.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Word That Comes From a Tunnel — *al-Munāfiqūn*

Before we look at what they said, let's look at what they are *called*. Because the name itself is a diagnosis.

#### First, a small lesson in how Arabic builds a word

In Arabic, almost every word grows from a three-letter root, like a plant from a seed. The root carries a core image, and every word built on it inherits something of that image. When you know the root, you don't just know the definition of a word — you know its *ancestry*. You know the picture buried underneath it.

So when the Quran calls these people *munāfiqūn*, the obvious thing is to translate it "hypocrites" and move on. But the Arab who first heard this word did not hear an abstract moral label. He heard a *picture*. And the picture is extraordinary.

#### Opening the word

The root is **ن-ف-ق** (*n-f-q*). And the classical lexicons — Lisān al-ʿArab, al-Rāghib's Mufradāt — trace the word *munāfiq* back to a specific animal: the **jerboa**, a small desert rodent. And specifically to its burrow.

Here is what the lexicographers describe. The jerboa digs its burrow with great cunning. It makes the main entrance — the one you can see — and it tunnels through. But it does not stop there. It digs a *second* passage all the way up to just beneath the surface on the far side, and leaves the top crust intact, hidden, thin. This hidden second exit is called the **nāfiqāʾ**.

So when a predator comes digging down the visible entrance, the jerboa runs to the secret end, bursts up through the thin crust where no one was watching — and is gone.

[PAUSE]

Sit with that image. Because Allah did not call them "the deceivers" or "the two-faced." He reached for a word whose root means: **a creature with a hidden exit it has prepared in advance.**

The hypocrite is not someone who got caught in a lie. The hypocrite is someone who, *while showing you the front door, has already dug the back tunnel.* The allegiance is the visible entrance. The escape hatch is built before they ever speak to you. They come to the Prophet ﷺ saying "we bear witness" — and the whole time, beneath the surface, the tunnel out is already dug and waiting.

Notice something. The jerboa's deception is not improvised. It is *architecture*. It is labor done in advance, in the dark, so that the betrayal is ready before it's ever needed. That is the difference between a moment of weakness and *nifāq*. One is a stumble. The other is a structure.

#### Going deeper — the form of the verb

The corpus tells us this word comes from **Form III** of the verb — *nāfaqa*. In Arabic, Form III (the *fāʿala* pattern) characteristically carries the sense of *directing an action at someone*, of engagement, of a relationship enacted *toward* a target. This is the form of *qātala* (to fight someone), *sābaqa* (to race against someone).

So *nifāq* in this form is not a private internal flaw sitting quietly in a person. It is a thing *done at you*. It is an action aimed across the space between two people. The hypocrite is not merely inwardly divided — he is inwardly divided *and pointing the false face directly at you.* The grammar itself tells you: this is performance, and it has an audience.

**What this single word reveals:** the hypocrite is not a confused believer. He is an architect of a concealed exit, performing allegiance *at* the very person he has already planned to abandon.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: The Three Witnesses — A Courtroom in One Sentence

Now we come to the heart of it. And to see it, you have to notice that one root appears *three times* in this ayah, and each time it changes everything.

#### The lesson first: what it means "to witness"

The root **ش-ه-د** (*sh-h-d*) is one of the weightiest in the Quran. *Shahida* means to witness — but not in the weak sense of "I saw something happen." To give *shahāda* is to *testify*. It is a courtroom word. When you say *ashhadu*, you are not reporting a fact; you are *staking yourself on it*. You are saying: I have seen this with such certainty that I will stand before a judge and put my own credibility on the line for it.

That is why the declaration of faith is called the *shahāda*. To say *ashhadu an lā ilāha illā llāh* is to testify — to take the witness stand for the existence and oneness of God and stake your eternity on the testimony.

Now watch what Allah does with this one root in a single verse.

#### The first witness: *nashhadu* — "we bear witness"

The hypocrites say: *nashhadu innaka la-rasūlu llāh.* "**We bear witness** that you are indeed the Messenger of Allah."

They didn't have to use this word. They could have said "we believe," "we know," "we affirm." But they reached for the *strongest* possible verb. They climbed onto the witness stand. They didn't just claim an opinion about the Prophet ﷺ — they claimed an *inner certainty*, a seen-with-the-heart conviction they were willing to testify to.

And — this is crucial — look at the emphasis they pile on. *Inna*-ka (an emphatic particle). And then the *lām* — *la*-rasūl. In Arabic this is the *lām al-muzaḥlaqa*, the "emphatic lām" that slides into the predicate of *inna* to intensify it further. So the sentence is not flat: "you are the messenger." It is armored with emphasis on emphasis: *"you are most surely, most certainly, the Messenger of Allah."*

They are not under-claiming. They are *over*-claiming. They are protesting a little too much.

[PAUSE]

#### The second witness: *wa-llāhu yaʿlamu* — "And Allah knows"

Now Allah responds. And here is the first surprise: He does not deny what they said. He *confirms* it.

*Wa-llāhu yaʿlamu innaka la-rasūluh* — "And Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger."

Notice — Allah uses the *same* emphatic structure they used. *Inna*-ka, *la*-rasūl. He matches their emphasis, word for word. As if to say: you want to talk about certainty? Here is certainty. The thing you are *performing* certainty about is, in fact, certainly true.

So the content of their sentence is vindicated by God Himself. The Prophet ﷺ *is* the Messenger. That was never in question.

Then why are they liars?

Because look closely at what Allah affirms and what He withholds. He affirms the *fact*: "you are His Messenger." He does **not** affirm their *witness*. He does not say "and they bear witness truthfully." He shifts the verb. They said *nashhadu* — "we witness." Allah says *yaʿlamu* — "He **knows**." 

Do you feel the gap opening? They claimed to *witness* a truth from the heart. Allah quietly relocates the real witnessing away from them and onto Himself. *You* didn't witness this. *I* know it. Your stand in the witness box was empty.

#### The third witness: *wa-llāhu yashhadu* — "And Allah bears witness"

And now the root returns a third time — and this is the verdict.

*Wa-llāhu yashhadu inna l-munāfiqīna la-kādhibūn* — "And **Allah bears witness** that the hypocrites are surely liars."

Stop. Feel the architecture.

They said *nashhadu* — "**we** bear witness."
Allah says *wa-llāhu yashhadu* — "**Allah** bears witness."

The exact same verb. They took the witness stand. Now the only Witness whose testimony is absolute takes the stand *against* them — using their own verb. They claimed to testify. Allah testifies — *about* their testimony — that it is a lie.

And here is the full courtroom, all in seventeen words:
- The **false witnesses** give their statement (*nashhadu*).
- The **all-Knowing** establishes the actual facts (*yaʿlamu*).
- The **true Witness** — for *ash-Shahīd*, the Witness, is one of Allah's own names — delivers the verdict (*yashhadu*).

[PAUSE]

#### Now — the question from the introduction, answered

So how can their sentence be *true* and they still be *liars*?

Here it is. Allah did not call them liars about **Muhammad ﷺ**. He called them liars about **themselves**.

The lie was never in "you are the Messenger of Allah." That part is true; Allah Himself confirmed it. The lie was in the word *nashhadu* — "**we** bear witness." They claimed to possess in their hearts a testimony they did not possess. The sentence reported on the Prophet — but it *also* made a hidden claim about *their own inner state*: namely, that they had seen and affirmed this truth in the depths of themselves.

**And about that inner state — they lied.**

This is the whole anatomy of *nifāq* laid bare. The hypocrite's sentences are often *true*. That's what makes them so hard to catch. He says "I love you." He says "Allah is great." He says "the Prophet is the Messenger of Allah." Every word checks out. The lie is not in the *words* — it is in the implied claim that *the heart is saying the same thing as the tongue.*

**What this threefold witness reveals:** you can speak a perfect truth and stand condemned as a liar — because every testimony makes two claims at once. One about the world, and one about your own heart. Allah judges the second.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: One Pronoun That Changes the Temperature — *Rasūl Allāh* vs. *Rasūluh*

There is one more thing hidden here, and it is so small you could read this ayah a hundred times and miss it. But once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

Look at how the *same idea* is said twice — once by them, once by Allah.

They say: *innaka la-rasūlu **llāh*** — "you are the Messenger **of Allah**." They use the noun. The name. *Allah.*

Allah says: *innaka la-rasūlu**h*** — "you are **His** Messenger." He uses the pronoun. *His.*

#### Why does this matter?

In Arabic, as in any language, there is a difference in warmth between naming someone formally and referring to them by an intimate pronoun. "This is the property of Mr. Johnson" is not the same temperature as Mr. Johnson saying "this is *mine*."

When the hypocrites speak, they say "Messenger **of Allah**" — correct, formal, at arm's length. It is the language of a man reciting a credential he does not feel. He names *Allah* the way you'd name an institution you're trying to get a loan from. Accurate. Distant. Transactional.

When Allah speaks, He does not repeat the formal name. He draws the Prophet ﷺ in close with a single attached letter: *rasūlu-**hu*** — "**My** Messenger," *His* Messenger. The possessive pronoun is an embrace. It says: *this man belongs to Me.*

[PAUSE]

Feel what just happened. The hypocrites used the louder, longer, more *emphatic* phrase — "Messenger of *Allah*" — and it came out cold. Allah used the shorter, quieter, possessive form — and it came out *warm*. 

This is the signature of *nifāq* exposed at the level of a single pronoun: **the hypocrite is always louder than he is close.** He compensates for the missing intimacy with extra volume. He says "Allah" three times where the believer's heart would simply whisper "*hu*" — *Him* — because the believer doesn't need to prove the relationship. He's *in* it.

**What this pronoun reveals:** the distance between a heart that performs belonging and a heart that *belongs* can be measured in the difference between a name said loudly and a pronoun said tenderly.

### Bridge: From Words to Reality

Look at what these three things have shown us.

The *name* showed us a creature with a hidden exit dug in advance.
The *threefold witness* showed us a true sentence spoken by a false witness — and a God who judges the heart behind the words, not the words.
The *pronoun* showed us that the louder the claim, the more we should ask what closeness it is covering for.

Now let's ask what all this reveals not just about the hypocrites of Madinah — but about the human heart. About *your* heart. Because the surah did not preserve this verse so we could feel superior to ʿAbdullah ibn Ubayy. It preserved it so we would feel *examined*.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Lie That Passes Every Fact-Check

Let me start where you actually live.

You know how to say the right thing. We all do. It is one of the first social skills we ever learn. Someone asks "how are you?" and you say "good, you?" — and you may be quietly falling apart. A relative dies and you write "she was a beautiful soul" about a person you privately could not stand. You post "so grateful, so blessed" under a photo, and you feel nothing but the small anxiety of waiting for the likes.

None of those sentences is *factually* false. "Good." "Beautiful soul." "So blessed." A lie detector wired to your words would find nothing. The grammar is clean. The content is defensible.

And yet you know — *you know* — that something in those moments was not true. The untruth wasn't in the sentence. It was in the gap between the sentence and the heart that sent it.

[PAUSE]

We have built an entire civilization on this gap. We call it being "professional," being "appropriate," "managing your brand." A whole economy now runs on saying true-sounding things with a hollow center. The influencer who "is *obsessed* with this product." The condolence that is copy-pasted. The apology that contains the words "I'm sorry you feel that way" — technically an apology, actually an accusation.

Now bring the ayah back.

Allah looked at the most perfect sentence a human tongue can form — *you are the Messenger of Allah* — and He did not grade it on its content. He graded it on its source. And finding the heart empty where the tongue was full, He called it a *lie*. The highest possible truth, spoken from a hollow place, was registered in the heavens as falsehood.

[PAUSE]

Do you understand how serious this is? It means **the accuracy of your words will not save you.** On the Day of Judgment there will be people who never said a wrong thing in their lives — every statement orthodox, every phrase pious — and the verdict will be *kādhibūn*. Liars. Because Allah was never auditing their grammar. He was auditing the distance between their tongue and their heart.

And it cuts the other way too — and this is the mercy in it. The believer who stammers, who can't find the eloquent phrase, whose *Allāhu akbar* comes out clumsy and whose duʿāʾ is just "*yā Rabb*… *yā Rabb*…" with tears and no vocabulary — that one is telling the *truth*. Because for once the tongue and the heart are saying the same thing.

**What this changes about tomorrow:** Before you say the beautiful thing, the correct thing, the expected pious thing — pause for half a second and ask: *is my heart in the room for this sentence, or am I sending it alone?* You don't have to stop saying good things. You have to stop sending them without you. Even once a day, let one sentence — one *subḥānallāh*, one "I love you," one "I forgive you" — be a sentence where the heart actually shows up behind the words. That single sentence, spoken with you fully present, weighs more than a thousand correct ones spoken from the doorway.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: The Terror and the Mercy of Being Fully Known

Now the second depth. And it is the most personal place this ayah goes.

There is a phrase sitting in the middle of this verse, almost as an aside, and it is one of the most quietly devastating phrases in the Quran:

*wa-llāhu yaʿlamu* — **"And Allah knows."**

#### Start with what you already feel

There is a particular exhaustion that modern people carry, and most of us can't name it. It's the exhaustion of *being seen but not known.* You have a curated self — the photos, the captions, the version of you that shows up at work, the version that shows up at the masjid, the version your parents think you are. You maintain all of them. It is enormous, invisible labor. And underneath all of them is a self that *no one* has fully met. Sometimes not even you.

And here is the strange thing about that hidden self: we are terrified of it being seen, *and* we are starving to be seen. Both at once. We build the mask precisely *because* we believe that if anyone saw the real thing, they would leave. So we are alone inside the performance, exhausted by it, and convinced that being truly known would be the end of being loved.

[PAUSE]

Now hear the ayah again. *Wa-llāhu yaʿlamu.* And Allah knows.

To the hypocrite, this phrase is a **terror.** It is the back tunnel collapsing. He built the whole architecture of concealment — the front face, the loud testimony, the hidden exit — and Allah says, in four syllables, *I see the tunnel. I watched you dig it.* Every word of his perfect sentence is reflected back to him with its hollow center exposed. There is no exit that Allah did not see him dig.

This is the horror of *nifāq*: you can fool the entire community. The Prophet ﷺ himself, by Allah's wisdom, was not always shown who the hypocrites were. ʿAbdullah ibn Ubayy walked among the believers, prayed in their rows, was buried by their hands. The mask *worked* — on everyone except the One it was hiding from. And in the end, the only audience that mattered had seen through it from the first day.

[PAUSE]

#### But now turn it over

Here is the thing the hypocrite can never receive, and the believer can. *Wa-llāhu yaʿlamu* is not only the most frightening sentence in the world. For the sincere heart, it is the most **comforting** sentence in the world.

Because remember the exhaustion — being seen but not known. The performance no one sees behind. The loneliness inside the mask.

To the believer, *Allah knows* means: **there is One who needs no mask.** There is One before whom the performance can finally stop. You don't have to construct anything for Him. You don't have to find the eloquent phrase or arrange your face. He already knows the real thing — the doubt you don't admit, the wound you've never told anyone, the prayer your tongue mangles, the love for Him you can't prove to anyone because it lives in a place no camera reaches.

*Allah knows.* The hypocrite hears: *I am caught.* The believer hears: *I am finally, fully, met.*

[PAUSE]

And do you see the mercy hidden in the structure of the verse? When the believer's heart is sincere but his words come out poor — Allah's *yaʿlamu* sees the rich heart behind the poor words and credits the heart. The same divine knowing that *condemns* the hypocrite — whose words were rich and whose heart was poor — *rescues* the believer whose heart is rich and whose words are poor. It is the *same attribute*. The same searchlight. It damns the one and saves the other, depending entirely on which way the gap runs: tongue fuller than heart, or heart fuller than tongue.

**What this changes about tomorrow:** Stop performing for Allah. He is the one audience you never have to perform for, and the tragedy is that we treat our prayer like a performance for everyone *but* Him — worrying how we look in the row, how our recitation sounds, what others think of our piety. Tonight, in your next ṣalāh, let it land that there is no one to fool and no one to impress. The mask is not required here. Bring Him the real self — the tired one, the doubting one, the one with the ugly thoughts you'd never say aloud — because *He already knows*, and He invited that one to stand before Him anyway. That is not exposure. That is the only place in your life where you are safe to be fully known and still kept.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture, Revealed

Now stand back and look at the whole verse as one built structure, because its shape *is* its argument.

Watch the verb of witnessing move:

1. **They** witness — *nashhadu* — loud, emphatic, armored with *inna* and the *lām*. The front door of the tunnel.
2. **Allah knows** — *yaʿlamu* — the quiet middle. The searchlight that sees the hidden exit.
3. **Allah** witnesses — *yashhadu* — the verdict, delivered with the *same verb they used*, now turned against them.

The verse opens with a human claiming to witness and closes with God witnessing that the human's witness was a lie. The hypocrites' testimony is *surrounded* on every side by Allah's knowing and Allah's witnessing. They are enclosed. Boxed in by divine awareness on the left and the right. They thought they were standing in front of the Prophet ﷺ delivering a statement. They were standing in a courtroom, encircled, with the Judge as the only true Witness.

And notice the final word of the verse: *la-kādhibūn* — "**surely liars**" — carries the *exact same emphatic lām* they used on *la-rasūl*. They emphasized their truth-claim; Allah emphasizes their lie with the identical grammatical weight. Their own emphasis is turned back on them. The armor they put on the lie becomes the armor on the verdict.

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic journey and the themes are one insight seen from two sides.

The *name* — the creature with the hidden tunnel — is the **structure** of the gap between tongue and heart.
The *threefold witness* is the **judgment** of that gap.
The *pronoun* — *Allah* said loudly versus *hu* said tenderly — is the **felt texture** of that gap.

And the two themes are simply that same gap, lived: the **lie that passes every fact-check**, and the **God from whom no gap can hide.** It all comes down to one fault line running through the human heart — the distance between what the tongue says and what the heart holds — and one Witness who measures it exactly.

### Questions to Carry

1. Which of my most *correct* sentences are the most hollow? Where do I say the right thing precisely *because* I don't feel it?
2. If Allah judged me today not on the accuracy of my words but on the presence of my heart behind them — how much of my speech would survive?
3. Who am I performing for in my worship? And what would change if I truly believed, in my body, that He already knows?
4. Where, in my life, have I dug a "back tunnel" — a hidden exit prepared in advance, while showing someone the front door of loyalty?
5. Is there one place where my heart is *fuller* than my words — a sincere love or grief or faith I've never managed to say? Have I let it comfort me that *Allah knows* it anyway?

### One-Sentence Distillation

You can speak the highest truth with your tongue and be recorded a liar in the heavens — because every word you say testifies twice, once about the world and once about your heart, and Allah, the only true Witness, judges the second.

### The Closing Invitation

Don't leave this reflection resolving to *say better things*. The hypocrites said the best thing there is. Leave it resolving to **close the gap** — to slowly, daily, drag your heart up to meet your tongue, until the two are saying the same thing. That is the lifelong work the word *nifāq* warns us away from and the word *ṣidq* — truthfulness — calls us toward. Not a more eloquent mouth. A more present heart.

And when you fail — when you catch your tongue running ahead of your heart again — remember that the same *wa-llāhu yaʿlamu* that terrifies the hypocrite is the mercy that holds you. He knows you're trying. He knows the real thing underneath the clumsy words. Bring Him that. He has been waiting for the real you the whole time.

### A Brief Du'a

*O Allah, You who know what the tongue hides from the world — close the distance in me between what I say and what I am. Let my **nashhadu** be true witness and not empty sound, and when my heart is fuller than my words can carry, accept it, for You know what is in it. Make me of the truthful, and never of those whose perfect sentences hide a hollow place. Āmīn.*
