ISSUES:

- [MODERATE] "aḥsana … It is the **superlative** shape: 'the *best*, the *most* beautiful.' … Let me make sure you feel the strangeness of this" — The reflection builds its entire central theological argument on the strict superlative reading of أَحْسَنَ (God accepts the *best* deeds from a mixed record). However, Al-Jalalayn explicitly states **"(أحسن) بمعنى حسن"** — أحسن here carries the sense of simply "good," not the comparative-superlative — and Al-Baghawi confirms **"وكلها حسن، والأحسن بمعنى الحسن"** ("all their deeds are good; aḥsan here means ḥasan"). Both sources are saying that because the deeds in view are already righteous (ṣāliḥāt), the elative form does not function as a strict superlative selecting some tier over others. Al-Muyassar does preserve the superlative sense ("أحسن ما عملوا من صالحات الأعمال"), so the position is not baseless, but the reflection presents one side of a genuine classical disagreement as self-evident without acknowledgment.

- [MODERATE] Morphology comment claims **"نَتَقَبَّلُ Form V (qbl)"** — this is correct. But the reflection's prose extends Form V's semantic content beyond what morphology alone establishes: "the grammar itself leans in," "God reaches toward," "a deliberate, gracious step forward." The form تَقَبَّلَ does carry a nuance of gracious or favoring acceptance (attested in usage), but the specific directional imagery ("bends toward you") is interpretive elaboration, not a morphological property. This is permissible spiritual tafsīr but should be framed as interpretive rather than grammatically derived. Not a factual error; flagged for register.
