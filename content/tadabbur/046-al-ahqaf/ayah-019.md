# Every Deed a Step

### Surah al-Ahqaf, Ayah 19

> [ayah:46:19] وَلِكُلٍّ دَرَجَـٰتٌ مِّمَّا عَمِلُوا۟ ۖ وَلِيُوَفِّيَهُمْ أَعْمَـٰلَهُمْ وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ
>
> *Wa li-kullin darajātun mimmā ʿamilū, wa li-yuwaffiyahum aʿmālahum wa hum lā yuẓlamūn.*
>
> "And for each one there are degrees according to what they did — and so that He may pay them their deeds in full, and they will not be wronged."

---

## INTRODUCTION: The World Before the Verdict

Let me bring you into the room where this ayah arrives.

Surah al-Ahqaf is a Makkan surah, revealed in the years when the message was small, mocked, and surrounded. And just before our ayah, the Quran does something quietly devastating. It places two human beings side by side.

In ayah 15, there is a person who reaches forty — *ḥattā idhā balagha ashuddahu wa balagha arbaʿīna sanah* — a person who reaches the fullness of strength, reaches forty years, and turns to his Lord and says: *Rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* — "My Lord, enable me to be grateful for the favor You gave me and my parents, and to do righteousness that pleases You." A whole life of small choices has matured into this one upward-facing prayer.

And then, in ayah 17, there is another person. One who says to his own parents — the very people who carried him, who were anxious over his coming into the world — *uffin lakumā*, a sound of contempt, "ugh, enough of you both." One who hears the promise of being raised again and says: *mā hādhā illā asāṭīru-l-awwalīn* — "this is nothing but the legends of the ancients."

Two people. Two trajectories. One climbing toward gratitude, one curdling into scorn.

Now — here is the question the original listener would have been holding, the question the entire passage has been building toward: **What happens to these two? How does the Owner of the Day of Judgment sort a lifetime of difference?**

You could imagine a crude answer. Two bins. Saved and damned. Pass and fail. A wall down the middle of creation with the good on one side and the bad on the other.

[PAUSE]

That is not what Allah says.

What He says in ayah 19 is far stranger, far more precise, and — if you let it land — far more terrifying *and* more comforting than two bins could ever be. He says there are **degrees**. Not two. Degrees. A gradation so fine that no two souls stand on exactly the same step.

Stay with me. Because hidden inside nine short words is an entire theory of justice — one that answers a fear most of us carry without ever naming it.

## PART ONE: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One — وَلِكُلٍّ — "And for each one"

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we touch the word.

In Arabic, there is a small grammatical event called *tanwīn* — the "nunation," that little doubled vowel sound at the end of a word, the *-un, -in, -an* that you hear. Usually it just marks a word as indefinite. But there is a special use of it the grammarians call *tanwīn al-ʿiwaḍ* — the "tanwīn of compensation." It is a sound that *stands in for a missing word*. The tanwīn is there precisely because something has been deliberately left out, and the little sound carries the weight of the absence.

Think of how, in English, you can say "I have some, and she has some too" — and the word *some* trails off into nothing, but you both know exactly what fills the blank. The blank is doing work.

Now look at the very first word of our ayah: **وَلِكُلٍّ** — *wa li-kullin*. "And for each... ."

For each *what*?

[PAUSE]

The Arabic does not say. *Kull* means "each, every, all" — but it is a word that almost always leans on a noun. Each *person*. Each *one of them*. Each *group*. And here Allah severs it. He puts the tanwīn on it — *kullin* — and lets the noun fall away.

Notice something. This is not a slip of brevity. This is design. The classical commentators — al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi, and others — fill in the blank in two complementary ways, and I want you to hold both at once, because together they sketch the full picture.

The first reading: *li-kulli wāḥidin min al-farīqayn* — "for each one of the two groups." The believer and the disbeliever. The grateful one of ayah 15 and the scornful one of ayah 17. Each group has its degrees.

The second reading, narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās: it reaches down to the individual. He said this ayah means that even *the one who raced toward Islam by a single hour* is more excellent than the one who lagged behind him — *wa law bi-sāʿah*, even by an hour. So fine is the gradation that an hour of precedence registers.

Do you feel what the missing word does? By refusing to name the thing, Allah lets the verse expand to its widest possible reach. **For each.** For each group. For each person. For each hour. The grammatical absence is not a gap in meaning — it is the meaning. The blank is as wide as the whole of creation, and you are somewhere inside it.

That is what this single word reveals: there is no one outside the accounting. No category broad enough to hide in. *For each.*

[PAUSE]

### Element Two — دَرَجَاتٌ — "Degrees"

Now the word that the whole ayah turns on.

Let me give you its root first, because the root is a picture. **دَرَجَاتٌ — darajāt** — comes from the root *د ر ج (d-r-j)*. And this root, across the Arabic language, draws one consistent image: **gradual movement, step by step.**

From this root comes *daraj* — a flight of **stairs**. From it comes *darrajahu* — to lead someone forward little by little, by stages. From it comes *tadarruj* — gradualness, progression by increments. And from it comes a quieter meaning the lexicons preserve: *daraja* can mean to **fold** something up, gradually, fold over fold.

So before it is ever a theological term, *daraja* is a **staircase**. Hold that image. A staircase is the opposite of a leap. A staircase is a thousand small risings, each one distinct, each one a precise height above the one below it.

Now hear the ayah again: *wa li-kullin **darajātun** mimmā ʿamilū* — "and for each, **degrees** — *steps* — according to what they did."

Here is what's remarkable, and you might miss it if you read quickly. We tend to imagine the next life in two flat plains: the Garden up there, the Fire down there. Two destinations. But Allah does not describe two plains. He describes a **staircase** — an endless gradation where every soul stands on the exact step its deeds have earned. Not the saved and the damned. **The infinitely sorted.**

Let's sit with this, because the commentators saw something here that sharpens it even further. The word Allah chose is *darajāt* — *degrees*, and in Arabic *daraja* carries the flavor of *rising*. (There is a sister word, *darakāt*, that the Quran uses elsewhere for the *descending* levels of the Fire.) Yet here Allah uses the *upward* word — *darajāt* — for **everyone**, both groups together.

How? Ibn Zayd, narrated in both al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi, resolves it beautifully: *the steps of the people of the Fire go downward, and the steps of the people of the Garden go upward.* One staircase, read in two directions. For the people of gratitude it climbs without ceiling. For the people of scorn it descends without floor. The same architecture of precision governs both.

[PAUSE]

Think about what this does to the idea of a "passing grade." There is no such thing here. You do not clear a bar and join an undifferentiated crowd of the saved. Every prayer prayed in difficulty, every word swallowed for the sake of Allah, every hour you *raced ahead by* — to use Ibn ʿAbbās's image — sets you on your own step. And every act of turning away sets its own descent.

The staircase is the stepping stone. The unseen reality it points to is this: **your life is not being graded in bins. It is being measured in millimeters.** Nothing you did was too small to register a step.

That should frighten us a little. And — we will see — it should also be the most consoling thing we have ever heard.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three — وَلِيُوَفِّيَهُمْ — "So that He may pay them in full"

We come to the verb, and to the most precise word in the verse.

First the grammar, taught plainly. Arabic verbs come in **forms** — patterns that bend a root's basic meaning. **Form I** is the bare action. **Form II** doubles the middle letter of the root, and one of its core functions is **intensification** — to do the action *thoroughly, completely, to the utmost*. Form I *kasara* is "he broke." Form II *kassara* is "he smashed to pieces." The doubled letter is the sound of completeness.

Now the root here is **و ف ي (w-f-y)**. Its basic meaning is **fulfillment, completion, faithfulness**. From it comes *wafā'* — loyalty, keeping your word fully. From it comes *al-wafī* — the one who pays back every last bit of what he owes.

Allah does not use the simple form. He uses **Form II**: **يُوَفِّي — yuwaffī** — *wa li-**yuwaffiya**hum aʿmālahum*. The intensive. And so the meaning is not merely "He will recompense them." It is: **He will pay them their deeds in FULL — complete measure, the last atom, nothing rounded off, nothing held back.**

The function of this Form II here is *intensive* — completeness of measure — not causative. Allah is not making someone else pay. He Himself renders the full due, exhaustively.

Let me show you how deep this root runs. From the very same root *w-f-y* comes the word the Quran uses for **death** — *tawaffā* — "to take the soul." Why would "taking a soul" come from the root of "paying in full"? Because death, in the Quranic imagination, is Allah **collecting in full** what He gave — taking back the complete measure of the life He lent. The soul is rendered up entire, not a fragment left behind.

So the same root that names your death names your reward. He takes you *in full*. He pays you *in full*. There is a terrible symmetry in that, and a beautiful one.

[PAUSE]

And notice the small letter that opens the word — the **لِ** (*li*), the *lām* of purpose. *Wa **li**-yuwaffiyahum* — "**so that** He may pay them in full." This is not just a description of what will happen. It is a statement of *why creation runs the way it does*. The degrees, the accounting, the raising of the dead the scornful man laughed at in ayah 17 — all of it exists **in order that** full payment can occur. The resurrection is not theater. It is the settling of accounts to the last atom. *Li-yuwaffiyahum.* That is the purpose clause of the cosmos.

One precise note for honesty: the early reciters differed slightly here. As al-Qurtubi records, Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb and others read it with a *yā'* — *li-yuwaffiyahum*, "**He** may pay them in full" — referring back to Allah, mentioned just before. Others read it with a *nūn* — "**We** may pay them in full." The meaning converges either way: the Payer is Allah, and the payment is complete.

What this single verb reveals: with Allah there is **no approximation**. No rounding. No "close enough." Full measure, every atom, by the One whose very name in this root means *the Faithful Payer of every due*.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Bridge

See what these three words have built together.

**Wa li-kullin** — no one is outside the accounting.
**Darajātun** — and the accounting is not in bins but in steps, infinitely fine.
**Wa li-yuwaffiyahum** — and every step is paid in full, to the atom.

The construction has shown us a *machinery of perfect precision*. Now let us ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the deepest ache and the deepest hope a human being carries.

## PART TWO: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One — The Terror of Being Averaged

Let me start where you actually live.

You have been, at some point, a number. A student in a class of forty whose effort dissolved into a curve. An employee whose year of quiet, unseen labor got summarized in a single sentence by someone who wasn't watching. A patient who was a chart. A customer who was a "segment." A name that became a statistic.

There is a specific grief in this — modern psychology sometimes calls it the wound of *not being seen* — the sense that the most particular, costly, private things you did were swept into an average and lost. You stayed late while others left; the report said "the team did well." You forgave when no one knew there was anything to forgive. You held a tongue that wanted to cut. And none of it *registered* anywhere.

We are, all of us, terrified of being **rounded off**.

[PAUSE]

Now read the ayah into that wound.

*Wa li-kullin darajātun mimmā ʿamilū.* For each one — *each* — degrees, according to **what they did**. Not according to what the group did. Not according to the average. Not according to what was visible to others. *Mimmā ʿamilū* — flowing precisely *from what they themselves did.*

The staircase we uncovered is the answer to the curve. On a curve, your effort is relative to everyone else's; you can do everything right and still be averaged down by the room. On the staircase, **there is no room.** There is only you and the exact height your own deeds raised you to. No one else's performance moves your step. No one else's failure drags it. No one else's brilliance shrinks it.

And then — *wa li-yuwaffiyahum aʿmālahum* — paid **in full**. The thing you thought no one saw was not only seen; it was *recorded to the atom and will be paid complete*. The Quran says elsewhere, with the same logic, *fa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin khayran yarah* — "whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it" (99:7). An atom. The smallest thing you can imagine, and even *it* is not averaged away.

Here is the movement I want you to feel. The same precision that should frighten us — *every deed registers a step* — is, from the other side, the most consoling sentence ever spoken to a human being: **nothing you did for Allah was ever lost in the crowd.** The kindness no one thanked you for. The night prayer no one knew about. The hour you raced ahead by. It is all on your step. It is all coming, in full.

[PAUSE]

What does this change about tomorrow?

It means you can finally stop performing for the average. So much of our exhaustion comes from needing the *room* to see — needing the credit, the acknowledgment, the visible win, because we secretly fear that what is unseen is unrecorded. The ayah cuts that fear at the root. The Witness is not the room. The Witness is the One who pays in full. So you are free — free to do the small, invisible, uncurved good, knowing it goes straight to your step and nowhere else.

Do one thing tomorrow that *no one will ever know about*. And let it be enough that it is written.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two — "And They Will Not Be Wronged"

Now the final three words, and the oldest grievance of the human heart.

*Wa hum lā yuẓlamūn.* "And they will not be wronged."

Let me teach you the root before the consolation. The verb is from **ظ ل م (ẓ-l-m)**, and the literal, primary meaning the lexicons give for *ẓulm* is **putting something where it does not belong** — and from there, *darkness*. The same root gives *ẓulmah* — darkness, the night. Injustice, in Arabic, is *a thing out of its place*, and it is *dark*. To wrong someone is to take what is theirs and put it where it should not be — and to do it in the shadows.

Now notice the grammar, because the grammar is the consolation. The verb is **passive**: *lā yuẓlamūn* — "they will not be wronged." And in Arabic, the passive voice deliberately **drops the doer**. The sentence does not say "Allah will not wrong them," nor "no one will wrong them." It removes the agent entirely and simply seals the outcome: *the wronging will not happen.* From any direction. By anyone. Including — the silence shouts it — from the Judge Himself.

[PAUSE]

Read absence carefully here. The missing agent is not vagueness. It is *totality*. Name one source of injustice and you imply others might exist. Remove the agent and you close every door at once. No deed under-counted. No oppressor's theft left standing. No good erased by someone else's lie about you. The whole accumulated darkness of human history — every *ẓulm*, every thing placed where it did not belong — pulled back into its proper place. The darkness undone.

This is why the scornful man's mockery in ayah 17 matters so much. He laughed at the resurrection: *"tales of the ancients."* But strip away the resurrection and you strip away *this* — *lā yuẓlamūn*. You leave the murdered unavenged, the exploited unpaid, the quiet faithful unrewarded forever. To deny the raising is not just to deny a doctrine. **It is to consign creation to permanent injustice.** The grateful man of ayah 15 and the scornful man of ayah 17 are not just two beliefs about the future. They are two answers to the question: *will the books ever be balanced?*

Allah's answer is *li-yuwaffiyahum… wa hum lā yuẓlamūn.* Paid in full. Wronged by none.

Let's sit with how rare this is. Every human system of justice approximates. Courts settle for "reasonable." Histories are written by the winners. The genuinely fair outcome — every atom of good rewarded, every atom of wrong addressed, no one shortchanged, no one over-charged — has *never once* occurred on this earth. Not once. The ayah is not describing an improvement on human justice. It is describing something that has no precedent anywhere in your experience: **flawless equity.**

And here is the tenderness inside the terror. *Lā yuẓlamūn* protects you *from* the Judge as much as it promises the Judge will act. You will not be over-charged either. No deed punished twice. No sincere repentance ignored. No mercy withheld that was promised. The al-Muyassar commentary glosses it exactly this way: *not by an increase in their sins, nor by a decrease in their good deeds.* The scale tips neither against you unfairly nor — and feel the mercy — does it round your good *down*.

[PAUSE]

What does this change about tomorrow?

It means you can lay down the exhausting work of being your own avenger. So much bitterness is the soul refusing to wait for a justice it doesn't believe is coming. *Lā yuẓlamūn* lets you release the grievance — not because it didn't matter, but because *it matters so much that it has been guaranteed a perfect hearing.* You are not letting it go into nothing. You are handing it to the One who pays in full.

Is there an injustice you are carrying — something taken from you, some good of yours that was buried? Tomorrow, practice handing it, in your own words, to the One who promised *lā yuẓlamūn*. Not forgetting it. Entrusting it.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at how the linguistic discoveries and the human themes turn out to be the same insight, approached from two sides.

The grammar gave us a **machinery**: *kullin* with its missing noun — no one outside the count. *Darajāt* with its staircase root — measured in steps, not bins. *Yuwaffī* in its intensive Form II — paid in full, every atom. *Lā yuẓlamūn* in its agentless passive — wronged by none.

The themes gave us the **ache that machinery answers**: the terror of being averaged, and the grievance of being wronged.

They are one truth. The reason your unseen good is safe (Theme One) and the reason every injustice will be undone (Theme Two) is the *same* reason: **Allah accounts not in categories but in atoms, and pays not approximately but in full.** The precision that consoles the overlooked is the precision that vindicates the oppressed. One staircase. Read upward for the grateful. Read downward for the scornful. Wronged — in either direction — by no one.

And notice the shape of the whole verse, now that the work is done: it opens with *what you did* (*mimmā ʿamilū*) and closes with *what is done to you* (*lā yuẓlamūn*). Your action at one end, His justice at the other, and the full payment — *yuwaffiyahum* — bridging them in the center. The architecture of the ayah *is* the architecture of the Day: your deeds in, His full and faultless measure out.

### Questions to Carry

1. Where in my life am I exhausting myself performing for the *room* — for the average, the credit, the visible win — when the only Witness who matters records in atoms?

2. What good have I done that "no one saw" — and can I believe, really believe, that it is already on my step?

3. What injustice am I carrying as my own unpaid debt to collect — and what would it feel like to entrust it to the One who promised *lā yuẓlamūn*?

4. The scornful man called the reckoning "tales of the ancients." Where do I quietly live *as if* there will be no full payment — cutting corners no one sees, swallowing grievances I think will vanish?

5. If even *an hour* of racing ahead registers a degree (Ibn ʿAbbās), what would I do *today* that I have been postponing?

---

### One-Sentence Distillation

Not one atom of what you did will be rounded off, lumped in, or overlooked — every deed sets you on its own precise step, and you will be paid in full, wronged by no one.

---

### The Closing Invitation

You do not have to be seen by the room anymore. You do not have to avenge yourself. The accounting you feared is the accounting you have been longing for your whole life — a justice so fine it misses nothing, so faithful it shortchanges no one, so complete that it has no precedent on this earth. Let the staircase reframe your week: every small, unseen, uncurved good is not lost in an average. It is your step. Climb it.
