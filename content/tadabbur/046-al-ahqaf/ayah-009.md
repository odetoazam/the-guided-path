# Not New, Not Knowing

## Surah Al-Ahqaf, Ayah 9

---

### The Words Before Us

> [ayah:46:9] قُلْ مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُمْ ۖ إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ وَمَآ أَنَا۠ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ

*Qul mā kuntu bidʿan mina-r-rusuli wa mā adrī mā yufʿalu bī wa lā bikum. In attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayya wa mā ana illā nadhīrun mubīn.*

> **"Say: I am no novelty among the messengers, nor do I know what will be done with me or with you. I only follow what is revealed to me, and I am only a clear warner."**

---

### Rebuilding the World

I want you to imagine the marketplace of Makkah.

A man stands before his own people — people who know his face, his lineage, his honesty, who called him *al-Amīn*, the trustworthy, before any of this began. And now they are circling him with a question that is really an accusation. *Who do you think you are?* What is this thing you have brought? We have never heard of it from our fathers. It is **new**. It is strange. It is unheard of. And if you are really a messenger from God — tell us what comes next. Tell us your fate. Tell us ours. Prove it.

Notice the climate of that moment. There is mockery in the air, but underneath the mockery there is a demand for spectacle. *Show us you are special. Show us you are unprecedented. Show us you know the future.* The whole pressure of that crowd is pushing the Prophet ﷺ toward one response — toward claiming grandeur, toward asserting himself, toward saying *yes, I am extraordinary, I am unlike anything that came before.*

[PAUSE]

And then Allah hands him the script. And the script does the opposite of everything the crowd is demanding.

The script begins with *qul* — **Say.** Not "feel this." Not "believe this privately." *Say it out loud. Announce it. Stand in front of them and define yourself.*

And what is the self-definition Allah gives to the most beloved of all His creation, the seal of the prophets, the one for whom — in one reading — the universe itself was brought into being?

Four negations. *I am not. I do not. Only. Only.*

Here is what should stop us before we go a single step further. When God Himself writes the introduction card for His greatest Messenger — when the Creator gets to choose the words by which His most beloved will present himself to the world — He builds that introduction almost entirely out of the word **"not."**

Why would the highest dignity be expressed through the language of negation? What is being protected here? And what does it mean that the secret of the Messenger's greatness is hidden inside the things he refuses to claim?

Stay with that question. We are going to earn the answer.

## PART ONE — THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

---

### Element One: *Bidʿan* — The Word That Belongs to God Alone

Let me teach you something before we open the word.

In every language, there are words that have a *home*. A word can live comfortably in one place and feel strange, even trespassing, in another. The English word "create," for example — when a chef says "I created a new dish," we understand it loosely. But there is a deeper sense of "create" that means *to bring into being from nothing* — and that sense doesn't really belong to chefs or artists at all. They only ever rearrange what already exists. The deep sense of "create" has a home, and the home is God.

Arabic is exquisitely precise about this. And our ayah contains one of these "homed" words, used in a way that is doing enormous theological work — quietly, almost invisibly.

The word is **بِدْعًا** — *bidʿan.*

The root is *bāʾ–dāl–ʿayn* (ب د ع). And I need you to hear what this root actually means, because it is more than "new." To *abdaʿa* something is to originate it **with no prior model, no precedent, no template, no example that came before.** It is to bring forth something *first of its kind.* The classical lexicons are united on this: *al-badʿ* is "the first." *Al-badīʿ* is that which has no precedent whatsoever.

Now — where does this root *live*? What is its home?

[PAUSE]

It is one of the names of Allah. **Al-Badīʿ.** *Badīʿu-s-samāwāti wal-arḍ* — the Originator of the heavens and the earth (2:117). The One who brought the cosmos into existence with no blueprint, no precedent, no prior universe to copy. To be *al-Badīʿ* is to be utterly, absolutely first. That is a divine station. It belongs to God and to no one else.

So now listen again to what the Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say:

**مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ** — *I am not a* bidʿ *among the messengers.*

Do you feel what just happened?

The exact root that names Allah as the Unprecedented Originator — the Prophet is commanded to **negate it of himself.** "I am not unprecedented. I am not first-of-my-kind. I am not without precedent." Ibn ʿAbbās and the classical commentators read it plainly: *I am not the first messenger to be sent; many came before me.*

Let's sit with the precision of this. The crowd is demanding that he be a *bidʿ* — something brand new, something never-before-seen, a spectacle. And the answer pushes the word *bidʿ* away from himself entirely. As if to say: **novelty without precedent is not my category. That category has an Owner, and I am His servant, not His rival.**

[PAUSE]

Here is what is remarkable. We tend to assume that the way to honor someone is to call them *unique, one of a kind, unprecedented.* That is our instinct. And the Quran takes the most honored human being who ever lived and has him say: *I am precisely not that.* My dignity is not in being first. My dignity is in being **part of a line.** *Mina-r-rusul* — among, *from within,* the messengers. He locates himself inside a procession that stretches back through Mūsā, ʿĪsā, Ibrāhīm, Nūḥ, back to Ādam. He is a link in a chain, not a bolt from the blue.

And notice the absence here — read it carefully. He does not say "I am the greatest of the messengers," though he is. He does not say "I am the last," though he is. In *this* moment, under *this* pressure, the only thing he affirms is **continuity.** What he withholds is every claim to standalone exceptionalism. The grammar of the sentence empties him of the very thing the crowd wanted him to grab.

What does this one word reveal? That the deepest form of greatness is not to stand alone as something unprecedented — that is God's alone — but to take your place faithfully within what God has been doing all along.

### Element Two: *Yufʿalu* — The Sentence With a Hidden Doer

Let me teach you a grammatical feature before we use it, because once you can hear it, you will hear it all over the Quran, and it will change how you read.

In English we have the active voice and the passive voice. Active: *"I will do something."* Passive: *"Something will be done."* The difference is not decorative. In the active voice, the doer is front and center — *I, the actor, the one in control.* In the passive voice, the doer disappears from the sentence. *"Something will be done"* — done by whom? The passive voice can hide the actor, or, in a sacred text, it can point to an Actor so obvious, so overwhelming, that to name Him would almost diminish Him. Scholars of the Quran call this the **divine passive** — the construction where the unnamed Doer is unmistakably Allah.

Now look at the second movement of our ayah:

**وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُمْ** — *and I do not know what* **will be done** *with me, nor with you.*

*Yufʿalu* — "will be done." Passive. The verb does not say *what I will do,* or even *what God will do* in active, named terms. It says **what will be done.** The doer is removed from the surface of the sentence.

Let's sit with this, because there are two extraordinary absences stacked on top of each other here, and each one is a teaching.

The first absence is the **doer.** Who is the one who will "do" with the Prophet and with his people? Of course it is Allah. Everyone knows it is Allah. But the Prophet, in his speech, does not seize the divine name and put it in the position of an object he is commenting on. He keeps himself in the position of the one *to whom things are done.* Notice the posture of the soul this creates. He is not the actor. He is the acted-upon. *Yufʿalu bī* — "it will be done *with* me." He places himself grammatically in the receiving position before God — the position of total servanthood. This is *adab*, the exquisite courtesy of the servant who will not even put himself in the same clause as a verb he claims to control.

[PAUSE]

The second absence is the **object** — the *what.* *Mā yufʿalu* — **"whatever** will be done." He does not specify it. He does not fill it in. The future is left as a blank that only Allah can write. The most knowledgeable human being who ever lived, the one who received revelation from the heavens, says in front of his enemies: *I do not know what comes next. Not for me. Not for you.*

Now — the classical scholars discussed exactly *what* is being not-known here, and there is a genuine *ikhtilāf*, a real difference of reading, that we should honor honestly rather than flatten.

One major line of reading — reflected in al-Jalālayn and in al-Tafsīr al-Muyassar — understands it of **this world**: *I do not know what will happen to me or to you here — whether I will be driven out of my city, or killed as prophets before me were killed, or whether you will be struck down as the deniers before you were struck down.* The particular outworking of events in the *dunyā* is hidden.

Al-Baghawī and al-Qurtubī preserve that scholars **differed** over the verse's meaning — al-Baghawī writes explicitly, *"the scholars differed concerning the meaning of this āyah."* One reported tradition takes it of the Hereafter — that the specific, detailed outcome is left in God's hands — and al-Baghawī narrates that when the verse came down, some of the *mushrikūn* seized on it gleefully, as though it leveled the Prophet down to themselves; a tradition holds that this is part of what a later revelation, *"that Allah may forgive you what came before and what will come after"* (48:2), came to clarify.

I am not going to pretend one reading erases the other, because the sources themselves don't. What every angle agrees on is the heart of the matter: **the particular, detailed unfolding of the future is not the Messenger's to know.** The categories of guidance — that the believer's end is mercy, that the denier's end is loss — those are revealed and certain. But the precise script of what happens, day by day, to *this* man and *these* people, is sealed with God. As another āyah puts it with devastating breadth: *with Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them but He* (6:59).

[PAUSE]

Think about what it cost to say this *in that moment.* The crowd demanded a fortune-teller, a man who would dazzle them with knowledge of the unseen as proof of his office. And Allah's script has him say the opposite: *I am not your soothsayer. I do not trade in predictions. I do not know my own fate.* He surrenders the very thing that would have most impressed them — and in surrendering it, he tells the truth about what a prophet actually is. **A prophet is not someone who knows everything. A prophet is someone who is given something — and faithfully passes it on.**

What does *yufʿalu* reveal? That true servanthood speaks in the passive before God — *whatever will be done* — and that the honesty of not-knowing is itself a form of worship.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: *In … Illā* — The Architecture of the Emptied Self

Now I want to show you the bones of the whole ayah, because the structure itself is the message.

First, a small but beautiful piece of grammar. The second half of the verse contains a construction Arabic uses for one specific purpose: **restriction** — what the grammarians call *qaṣr* or *ḥaṣr.* It is built by surrounding a statement with a negation and an exception: *not … except.* In English we do something similar with "only." "I do *nothing but* follow." "I am *nothing but* a warner."

Look:

**إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ** — *I follow* **nothing except** *what is revealed to me.*

That little *in* at the start is not "if" here — it is *in* the negating particle: "not." *In attabiʿu illā* — "I do not follow *anything except*…" The negation clears the entire field, and the exception lets exactly one thing back in. The structure first **empties** — *I follow nothing* — and then **admits one thing only** — *except what is revealed to me.*

And notice the verb he chooses: *attabiʿu*, "I follow." This is Form VIII of the root *tabiʿa* — the form of *deliberate, active following,* of treading in someone's footsteps. He does not say "I invent" or "I author" or "I originate." Remember *bidʿ* from the opening? The verse refuses originality a second time, now in the realm of teaching: *I am not the source of this. I follow it.* The content is not his. He is a transmitter walking a path that was laid for him.

And what is the one thing the restriction lets back in? *Mā yūḥā ilayya* — "what is revealed to me." *Yūḥā* — again the **divine passive.** Not "what I receive," not "what I produce" — what *is revealed,* by the unnamed Revealer who is unmistakably Allah. The root is *waḥy* (و ح ي), in Form IV, the form that here carries the act of conveying, of causing inspiration to reach its recipient. The Prophet is again the one *to whom* something is done — *ilayya,* "to me." Revelation arrives at him. He does not reach up and seize it.

Then the second restriction:

**وَمَآ أَنَا۠ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ** — *and I am* **nothing except** *a clear warner.*

Same architecture. *Mā ana illā* — negate the whole self, then admit one role: *nadhīr,* a warner. And here is a final touch of lexical richness. *Mubīn* is a Form IV participle from the root *bāna / abāna* (ب ي ن). The classical scholars read it in two complementary ways at once — and both are true. *Mubīn* can mean **"one who makes things clear"** — his warning clarifies, it removes excuse, it lights up the path. And it can mean **"one who is himself clear, manifest"** — there is nothing hidden or ambiguous about what he is. A *clear* warner: plain in message, plain in office. No mystique. No concealment. No grandiose secret. What you see is what he is.

[PAUSE]

Now stand back and look at the whole shape of the ayah:

- *I am **not** a novelty.* (not the Originator)
- *I do **not** know the future.* (not the all-knowing)
- *I follow **nothing except** what is revealed.* (not the author)
- *I am **nothing except** a clear warner.* (not the controller of outcomes)

Four times the self is emptied. Negation, negation, restriction, restriction. Every false grandeur the crowd wanted to project onto him — *be unprecedented, be a knower of the unseen, be the source, be the power* — is stripped away. And what is left standing when all the false claims have been cleared?

Two things, and only two: **I follow what is revealed.** **I warn, clearly.**

That is the whole of him, by his own God-given account. Not a self inflated with claims — a self *emptied* of every claim that belongs to God, until all that remains is pure relationship: *receiving* from God, and *delivering* to people.

[PAUSE]

This is the architecture, and now we can ask what it reveals not just about words — but about reality itself. Because the structure of this sentence is the structure of a soul. And it turns out to be the exact opposite of the structure our age teaches us to build.

## PART TWO — THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

---

### Theme One: The Cult of the Unprecedented, and the Dignity of the Chain

Let me start where you already live.

We are perhaps the first generation in human history to be told, relentlessly, from childhood, that our highest aspiration is to be **original.** *Be unique. Stand out. Be a disruptor. Be the first. Build something nobody has ever seen. Don't follow — lead. Don't copy — innovate.* The word "derivative" is one of the worst insults you can give to a piece of work. The word "unprecedented" is one of the highest compliments. We have built entire economies, entire identities, entire anxieties around the demand to be *new.*

And it is exhausting. Have you felt it? The quiet panic that whatever you are, it isn't *special* enough. That to merely continue what came before — to be a good son in a line of good sons, a teacher like the teachers before you, a believer in the long chain of believers — is somehow a failure of ambition. We are taught that being a *link* is small, and being a *spark from nowhere* is great.

[PAUSE]

Now bring that anxiety to our ayah and watch what happens.

The greatest human being who ever lived, at the peak moment of pressure to assert his uniqueness, says: **مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ** — *I am no novelty. I am one of the messengers. I am in the line.*

And it is not a confession of weakness. It is the source of his authority. Think about it: *why* should the Makkans believe him? His answer is — *because I am not new.* Because what I bring is what Nūḥ brought, what Ibrāhīm brought, what Mūsā and ʿĪsā brought. The very thing they used as an accusation — *this is strange, this is unprecedented* — he turns into his credential by **denying** it. *No. This is not strange. This is the oldest message in the world. La ilāha illā Allāh has been spoken on every prophet's tongue. I am simply the latest to carry the most ancient truth.*

[PAUSE]

Do you see the liberation in this?

The cult of the unprecedented is a prison, because *bidʿ* — origination from nothing — is a divine attribute, and when a human being reaches for a divine attribute, he is crushed by a weight he was never built to carry. You were not made to be the source. You were made to be a *carrier.* And there is profound rest in that.

Here is the spiritual movement underneath: the impulse to be *unprecedented* is, at root, the impulse to be **self-caused** — to owe nothing to anyone, to stand alone, to be the origin of your own meaning. And that is precisely the posture of *Iblīs,* who refused to take his place in the order and insisted on his own exceptional case. Whereas the posture of every prophet, and supremely of *this* prophet, is: *I take my place. I am in the line. I receive, and I pass on.*

When you pray tonight, you are not inventing anything. You are doing what Ibrāhīm did, what Maryam did, what the Prophet ﷺ did, what your grandmother did. You are a link. And the ayah tells you: **that is not your smallness. That is your dignity.** The chain is not a cage. The chain is what connects your small, brief life to something that began with the first human and will not end until the last.

What does this change about tomorrow? It means you can stop auditioning to be exceptional. You can pour your energy into being *faithful* instead of being *novel.* Tomorrow, do one ordinary act of obedience that millions before you have done — pray on time, give a little, hold your tongue — and feel it not as routine, but as *taking your place in the line.* The peace you've been chasing through originality was waiting for you in continuity all along.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: The Station of Not-Knowing

Again, let me start where you live.

There is a particular kind of suffering that defines modern life, and it has a name even when we don't name it: the suffering of **uncertainty.** We cannot bear not knowing. We refresh the screen. We check the forecast, the diagnosis, the test result, the market, the message that hasn't been answered. We run the scenarios in our heads at three in the morning — *what if this, what if that.* We have more information than any people in history and *less* peace, because every piece of information only sharpens the one thing we cannot get: certainty about what comes next.

And underneath the refreshing and the scenario-running is a hidden belief — that if we could just *know,* we would finally be safe. That knowledge of the future is the thing standing between us and peace.

[PAUSE]

Now hear the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, commanded by his Lord to say, in public, to his enemies:

**وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُمْ** — *I do not know what will be done with me, nor with you.*

Let that land. The single most spiritually elevated human being who ever drew breath — the one who saw Jibrīl, who was taken through the heavens, who received the words of God Himself — *does not know his own future.* And he is commanded to *announce it.* Not to hide it as a weakness. To declare it as a truth.

Stay with me, because there is something here that quietly dismantles our entire relationship with uncertainty.

We assume that the people closest to God are the people who *know the most.* That nearness to God means access to information — what's coming, how it ends, what to do. And here is the most beloved of all of them saying: *I don't know what tomorrow holds for me.* Which means **nearness to God is not measured in certainty about the future.** It never was. The Prophet's peace did not come from knowing the outcome. His peace came from knowing the *One* who holds the outcome.

[PAUSE]

This is the turn. Let me say it as plainly as I can.

You have been trying to solve your anxiety by acquiring *knowledge of the future.* But the future is, by divine design, sealed — *with Him are the keys of the unseen* (6:59); *no soul knows what it will earn tomorrow, and no soul knows in what land it will die* (31:34). That door is locked, and it is locked *on purpose,* and even the Prophet ﷺ stood on the outside of it. So as long as your peace depends on opening that door, you will never have peace, because that door is not for human hands.

But look at what the ayah does *immediately* after admitting the not-knowing. It does not collapse into despair. It does not say *and therefore all is chaos.* It says: **إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ** — *I follow nothing except what is revealed to me.*

Do you see the substitution? Where there *could* be knowledge of the future, there is instead **fidelity to what has been revealed.** The unknown future is replaced not with anxiety, but with *obedience to the known command.* He doesn't know what tomorrow does to him — *but he knows what he is supposed to do today.* And that is enough. That is more than enough. That is, in fact, the whole of the spiritual life.

[PAUSE]

This is what *tawakkul* actually is. We mistranslate it as "trust that things will work out the way I want." That is not trust; that is bargaining. Real *tawakkul* is the posture of this entire ayah: *I do not know what will be done with me — and I will follow what is revealed to me anyway.* The doer is unnamed because the doer is God, and I have placed myself, grammatically and spiritually, in the position of the one to whom things are done — *yufʿalu bī* — receiving His decree the way the verb itself receives its action: openly, without resistance, without the illusion of control.

There is a strange and enormous relief in this. The reason you are exhausted is that you have been carrying a job that was never yours — the job of *knowing and securing the outcome.* Put it down. It belongs to *al-Badīʿ,* the One who originates and decrees. Your job — the only job the Prophet himself claimed — is much smaller and infinitely lighter: *follow what is revealed; warn clearly; do the next right thing that has been made known to you.* The outcome is not your department. It never was.

What does this change about tomorrow? The next time you catch yourself spiraling into *what if* — the diagnosis, the message, the result, the future you cannot see — try this exact movement: name the not-knowing honestly (*I do not know what will be done*), and then immediately ask the Prophet's question instead of the anxious one. Not *"what will happen?"* but *"what has been revealed for me to do right now?"* Pray the prayer. Make the call. Keep the promise. Speak the truth. Let the locked door stay locked, and walk through the open one. That is the station of not-knowing — and it is not a lower place than certainty. It is a higher one.

[PAUSE — major pause]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

---

### The Architecture, Revealed

Now that we have walked through the words, look at what this ayah actually *is.* It is a self-portrait built entirely out of subtraction. Watch the four strokes:

1. **مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا** — *I am not the Originator.* (That is *al-Badīʿ.*)
2. **وَمَآ أَدْرِى** — *I am not the All-Knowing.* (The keys of the unseen are His.)
3. **إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰ** — *I am not the Author.* (I follow; I do not invent.)
4. **وَمَآ أَنَا۠ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ** — *I am not the Controller of outcomes.* (I warn; I do not decree.)

Four divine prerogatives — origination, knowledge of the unseen, authorship of the message, command over destiny — and one by one, the Prophet ﷺ hands each of them back to its rightful Owner. *Tawḥīd* is usually taught as a set of affirmations. Here it is taught as a set of *renunciations.* The Messenger declares God's oneness not by listing what God is, but by clearing away everything *he himself* is not.

And what remains when the clearing is done? A pure conduit. *Receiving* (what is revealed to me) and *delivering* (a clear warner). That is the entire shape of a prophet — and, in smaller measure, the entire shape of a faithful soul.

---

### Bringing It Together

Our two themes were the same insight seen from two directions.

The **cult of the unprecedented** is the ego reaching for *bidʿ* — the desire to be self-originated, to owe nothing, to be the source. And the **anxiety of the unknown future** is the ego reaching for *ʿilm al-ghayb* — the desire to know what only God knows, to control what only God controls. Both are the same disease: the soul trying to occupy a throne built only for God. And the ayah is the cure for both, administered in the same breath: *I am no novelty* dissolves the first; *I do not know what will be done* dissolves the second. What is left is the emptied, peaceful self that simply *follows what is revealed* and *warns clearly* — and finds, in that smallness, a freedom the kings of the earth never tasted.

---

### Questions to Carry

1. Where in my life am I exhausting myself trying to be *unprecedented* — when I was made to be a faithful *link in a chain*?
2. What "locked door" of the future am I standing outside of, refreshing, demanding to know — and what *open door* of present obedience am I ignoring while I stand there?
3. The Prophet ﷺ defined himself by what he handed *back* to God. If I made that list honestly — the things that are not mine, the outcomes I do not control — what would I be free to put down today?
4. When I introduce myself — to others, to my own mind — do I reach for claims, or could I rest in *"I follow what is revealed; I try to warn clearly, starting with myself"*?
5. Is my *tawakkul* really trust in God — or is it a quiet bargain that things go the way I have already decided they should?

---

### One Sentence

The greatest of the messengers was commanded to introduce himself almost entirely in the language of "not" — because the secret of his greatness, and the cure for our restlessness, is the emptying of every claim that belongs to God until all that remains is *receiving from Him* and *passing it on.*

---

### The Closing Invitation

You do not have to be new. You do not have to know the future. These two burdens — the burden of being exceptional and the burden of being certain — have been on your shoulders so long you may have forgotten they were ever optional. The Prophet ﷺ, by divine command, set them both down in a single sentence, in front of the very people pressuring him to pick them up. The invitation is simply to set them down too: to take your honored place in the long line of those who believed, and to trade the question *"what will happen to me?"* for the question *"what has been revealed for me to do?"* That trade is not a loss of control. It is the beginning of peace.

---

### A Closing Du'a

*O Allah, You are al-Badīʿ — the Originator who needs no precedent — and we are nothing but the ones to whom things are given. Teach us the dignity of taking our place in the line of those who loved You before us. Free us from the exhaustion of needing to be unprecedented and the anguish of needing to know what only You know. Let it be enough for us, as it was enough for Your beloved ﷺ, to follow what You have revealed and to warn — beginning with our own souls — clearly.* Āmīn.
