As if the Fire is the buyer and they are the goods laid out." — Rhetorically vivid, but the classical tafsirs read عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ as *presented to the Fire for punishment* (Jalalayn: بأن يعذبوا بها; Muyassar: للعذاب), not the Fire-as-examiner framing. The preposition supports the reading linguistically, but the inspector/buyer imagery is an extension beyond what the sources attest. Acceptable as tadabbur rhetoric if not presented as the tafsir meaning itself — and in the text it is framed as reflection ("As if"), so this is borderline rather than a clear violation.
