# The Footsteps and the Impossible Purification

## Surah An-Nur, Ayah 21

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ ۚ وَمَن يَتَّبِعْ خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَإِنَّهُ يَأْمُرُ بِالْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ ۚ وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ مَا زَكَىٰ مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ أَبَدًا وَلَـٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يُزَكِّي مَن يَشَاءُ ۗ وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

> *"O you who have believed, do not follow the footsteps of Shaytan. And whoever follows the footsteps of Shaytan — indeed, he commands what is shameful and rejected. And were it not for the bounty of Allah upon you and His mercy, not one of you would ever have been purified — but Allah purifies whom He wills. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."*

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The World This Ayah Lands In

Picture Madinah in the months after the slander.

A rumor — a false, devastating rumor about the Prophet's wife ﷺ — has been moving through the believing community for nearly a month. Some of the best people in Madinah, people whose names you would recognize, have repeated it. Not with malice. Just… casually. A whispered version here. A "did you hear?" there. And by the time Revelation comes down to clear the air, an entire community is sitting with the weight of having become a vehicle for something they would have sworn they would never carry.

This is the world this ayah enters.

The early ayahs of Surah An-Nur are still warm with the specifics of that crisis. Allah has just exposed what the community did, named the sin, prescribed the punishments, traced the social pathways the rumor traveled. And then, right here, at ayah 21 — He pauses the legislation. He stops giving rules. And He says something that doesn't sound, at first, like it belongs in a chapter about social discipline.

He says: *Don't follow the footsteps of Shaytan. And by the way — none of you would ever, in all of eternity, be capable of purifying yourselves.*

Read that again. Slowly.

This is one of the most theologically loaded sentences in the entire Quran. And it is sitting in the middle of what looks like a surah about community ethics. That is not an accident. That is the point. [PAUSE]

### The Hook

We are about to discover that the person who repeated the rumor and the person who didn't repeat the rumor — both of them — are standing on exactly the same ground. Allah is going to say something that should terrify the self-righteous and liberate the broken. And He will say it with a single Arabic word that means *forever*.

Stay with me. This ayah is going to dismantle something in us that needs to be dismantled.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Khuṭuwāt* — The Footsteps, Not the Person

Before we open the word, let me ask you something.

If a stranger walked up to a person you love and said: *"Come with me. I'm going to ruin your marriage, get you fired, and destroy your reputation"* — how successful would that stranger be?

He would fail instantly. No one chooses ruin when ruin is named.

But what if, instead of saying that, he simply said: *"You look tired. Let me carry that for you."* And then, three months later: *"Have you considered telling them how you really feel?"* And then, three months after that: *"You deserve to be happy."* Each step alone is harmless. Each step alone is even reasonable. **It is only when you turn around and look at the road behind you that you realize where you are.**

That is *khuṭuwāt*.

Allah does not say *lā tattabiʿū ash-shayṭān* — "do not follow Shaytan." He says *lā tattabiʿū **khuṭuwāti** ash-shayṭān* — "do not follow the **footsteps** of Shaytan."

Notice something. The word is plural. *Khuṭuwāt*. Not one step. Many. A trail.

The root is **kh-ṭ-w**, which in classical Arabic gives us the verb *khaṭā* — to take a single step, to plant one foot in front of the other. *Khuṭwah* is the singular: one footprint, one stride. The plural in this ayah is doing structural work. **Shaytan does not appear in front of you. He appears behind you, in the prints he has already left in the sand, asking you to follow them one at a time.**

Let's sit with this.

If Shaytan appeared as Shaytan, no believer would follow him. So he doesn't. He appears as a step. Just one step. A small adjustment. A reasonable accommodation. *"Just open the message." "Just listen, you don't have to repeat it." "Just say it once, to one person."* Each *khuṭwah* is small enough that refusing it feels disproportionate. And by the time you have followed enough of them, the destination — *fahshāʾ* and *munkar*, shamefulness and rejection — was never something you decided to walk toward. It was simply where the steps led.

The community of Madinah did not wake up one morning and decide to slander the Mother of the Believers. They took steps. *"I heard something."* Step. *"I'm only telling you because you should know."* Step. *"It's probably not true, but…"* Step. By the time the rumor was on every tongue in the city, no single person had decided to do what was, collectively, being done.

This is why Allah, in this surah of community purification, names the mechanism by name. Not the destination. The **method**. Because if you only watch for destinations, you will never see Shaytan coming. You will only ever see his footprints — and by then you will already be standing in them. [PAUSE]

There is a second feature here, easy to miss. Look at the pivot in the sentence:

*lā **tattabiʿū** khuṭuwāti ash-shayṭān* — "do not follow…" (plural — addressing all of you)

*wa **man yattabiʿ** khuṭuwāti ash-shayṭān* — "and whoever follows…" (singular — addressing one of you)

Did you catch it? Allah begins by addressing the whole community. Then He shifts to the individual. **You cannot hide in the crowd.** A communal sin is committed by individuals one at a time. The slander was a community-scale catastrophe — but it was caused by single people taking single steps. The grammar is enforcing this. *Don't follow* (all of you), *and whoever follows* (you in particular).

Here's what this single word — *khuṭuwāt* — has revealed: **Shaytan does not destroy you. He invites you, footstep by footstep, to walk yourself there.**

### Element Two: *Yaʾmuru* — He *Commands*

Now look at what comes next, because the verb is going to disturb you.

*fa-innahu **yaʾmuru** bil-fahshāʾi wal-munkar* — "indeed, he **commands** what is shameful and rejected."

Stay with that verb. *Yaʾmuru.* From the root **a-m-r**, which in Arabic specifically means *to issue an authoritative command* — the kind a master gives a servant, a king gives a subject, an officer gives a soldier. It is not *yadʿū* (he calls), not *yuwaswisu* (he whispers), not *yuzayyinu* (he beautifies). It is *yaʾmuru*. **He commands.**

This should bother you. Because in nearly every other passage about Shaytan in the Quran, he is described with much softer verbs. He **whispers** (*yuwaswisu*). He **suggests** (*yuzayyinu*). He **promises** (*yaʿiduhum*). He **calls** (*yadʿū*). The Quran is exquisitely careful to preserve human moral agency. Shaytan does not have the authority to command anyone.

So why, in this single ayah, does Allah suddenly say *yaʾmuru* — *he commands*?

Look at what it is conditioned on. The full sentence: *wa man yattabiʿ khuṭuwāti ash-shayṭān fa-innahu yaʾmuru bil-fahshāʾi wal-munkar*. **And whoever follows the footsteps of Shaytan — then he commands them with shamefulness and rejection.**

The command only activates *after* you have followed the footsteps.

Let's deepen this. At the beginning of the road, Shaytan whispers. He does not have the authority to command, and he knows it, and the believer knows it, and so he speaks the language of suggestion: *"Maybe just listen…" "It wouldn't hurt to glance…" "Perhaps just this once…"* You hold all the cards. You can refuse. The relationship is one of seduction, not authority.

But every footstep you take is a transfer of authority. **By the time you have followed enough of his footsteps, he is no longer suggesting to you. He is commanding you.** And the most disturbing thing is: by then, you don't notice the shift. You think you are still choosing. You think you are still making decisions. But the verb has changed. The relationship has changed. *Yuwaswisu* has become *yaʾmuru*.

Here's what this invites us to consider — and this is interpretive application beyond the bare grammar. There is a moment in every soul's slow drift where the language of *"I'm just curious"* becomes the language of *"I can't stop."* The grammar of the soul shifts from suggestion to compulsion. The Arabic of this ayah may be marking exactly that hinge. The man who says *"I'll stop scrolling whenever I want"* is being whispered to. The man who says *"I tried to stop and I couldn't"* is being commanded.

Notice the two words he commands with: *al-fahshāʾ* and *al-munkar*.

*Al-fahshāʾ* is from **f-ḥ-sh**, which in classical Arabic means something so excessive, so beyond the bounds of decency, that even fallen human nature recoils from it. It is the **inward shamefulness** — the things you would not want to be seen doing.

*Al-munkar* is from **n-k-r** — *to deny, to reject, to find foreign*. The *munkar* is what is rejected by the collective conscience — what the community, when it sees it, instinctively says *"no, not this."* It is the **outward shamefulness** — what the community would not accept.

So Shaytan commands what your private self is ashamed of *and* what your community would reject. Both at once. This is why his work is so effective in surahs like An-Nur. **He gets you to do what you yourself find shameful, in a way your community will eventually have to confront.** The slander was both: privately, those who repeated it knew, somewhere, that they shouldn't have. Publicly, when the truth came out, the entire community had to face *munkar* in itself.

Two themes will return to this point. Hold it for now.

### Element Three: *Mā Zakā Minkum Min Aḥadin Abadan* — The Sentence That Should Stop You

Now we come to the sentence that should genuinely stop you. Read it slowly:

*wa **lawlā** faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa raḥmatuhu **mā zakā minkum min aḥadin abadan***

*"And **were it not for** the bounty of Allah upon you and His mercy — **not one of you, ever, would have been purified.**"*

Let me unpack the words layer by layer, because almost every part of this sentence is performing a kind of grammatical violence on us.

**First: *lawlā*.** This is what grammarians call *ḥarf imtināʿ li-wujūd* — "a particle of impossibility-because-of-existence." It introduces a counterfactual. It means: *because X exists, Y did not happen. But if X had not existed, Y would have happened.* The structure forces you to imagine a universe where Allah's bounty and mercy are absent — and to see what that universe looks like.

**Second: *mā zakā*.** The verb *zakā* in form I (the basic form) means *to become pure, to grow, to be cleansed*. It is the **intransitive** form — the one where purity is a property the subject acquires by itself. *Mā zakā* — none of you would have *become pure*.

**Third — and this is where the sentence escalates — *minkum min aḥadin abadan*.** Three layers of emphatic negation stacked on top of each other.

- *minkum* — "from among you"
- *min aḥadin* — "any single one." The *min* here is *zāʾidah*, an emphatic preposition that classical grammarians call the *min al-istighrāqiyyah* — the *min* of total inclusion. It does not just mean "any." It means *not even one, no exceptions.*
- *abadan* — "ever, for all eternity." *Abad* in Arabic does not mean "for a long time." It means *time without end*.

Stack them. *Not one of you. Not a single one. Not ever. Not in the entire span of eternity.*

Read that sentence one more time, with all three weights felt at once:

*"Were it not for Allah's bounty and mercy, not a single one of you, ever, in all of eternity, would have become pure."*

[PAUSE]

This is the sentence I told you would dismantle something in us.

Because look who Allah is addressing. *Yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* — "O you who have believed." This is not addressed to the kuffar. Not to the hypocrites. Not to the people who repeated the rumor. **It is addressed to the believers — including the ones who refused to repeat the rumor. Including the ones who defended Aisha. Including the ones who recognized the slander for what it was and held their tongues.**

Even those people — Allah is saying — would not have become pure on their own.

Let's deepen this. There is a temptation, after a community-wide moral failure, for those who didn't fail to feel… clean. *I didn't repeat it. I told them to stop. I am not part of this problem.* Allah is reaching directly into that temptation, the moment it begins to form, and He is saying: **the only reason you didn't fall is My bounty. Not your strength. Not your intelligence. Not your taqwa. Mine.**

If you were left to yourself — *abadan*. Forever and ever and ever. Not one of you would have been purified. Not even once. Not in any timeline. Not in any version of you.

Now look at the contrast in the very next clause:

*wa **lākinna Allāha yuzakkī man yashāʾ*** — "but Allah purifies whom He wills."

Watch the verb shift. The first verb was *zakā* — form I — intransitive — *to become pure on one's own*. The second verb is *yuzakkī* — **form II** — *to purify*. **Form II in Arabic carries causation and intensification: someone is doing the purifying TO someone else.** The grammatical move from *zakā* to *yuzakkī* is the entire theology of this ayah in two letter changes.

You do not *become pure*. You are *being purified*. The purification has a Subject, and the Subject is not you.

This is the architecture of *tazkiyat an-nafs* in the Quran. Not "purify yourself" as a self-help project. **Allow yourself to be purified by the One who alone has the verb.**

### Bridge

So what have these three linguistic moves shown us?

The first — *khuṭuwāt* — revealed that moral collapse is a road, not a cliff. You are walked there, footprint by footprint.

The second — *yaʾmuru* — revealed that the further you walk that road, the less your "choices" are choices. The verb of your relationship with Shaytan upgrades from suggestion to command without you noticing.

The third — *mā zakā… abadan* — revealed that even the believer who *didn't* walk that road did not save himself. Allah saved him.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Geometry of Drift

Modern psychology has a name for what *khuṭuwāt* describes. It calls it *gradualism* — the phenomenon where moral standards erode in increments small enough that the person doesn't notice the slope. The classic experimental demonstration: people will not flip a switch they believe administers a fatal shock. But they will flip a switch that administers a tiny shock. And another. And another. And by the 30th switch, they are administering shocks they would have refused, in horror, to administer at the start.

The starting position is not the dangerous one. **The angle of descent is.**

We live in a culture engineered to exploit this. Every algorithm, every notification, every infinite scroll, every recommendation engine — these are *khuṭuwāt* manufacturing systems. The platforms have learned, with billions of dollars of optimization, that no one can be sold the destination. Everyone has to be walked there. The first video is innocent. The second is interesting. The third pushes the boundary by 5%. The thirty-first video would have been unthinkable to the version of you who started watching. But the version of you who has now watched thirty videos is a different person, and that person has different thresholds.

Allah is naming this fourteen centuries before Silicon Valley.

But notice what the ayah does that secular gradualism research does not. Secular research describes the *mechanism*. The Quran identifies the *engineer*. There is a will behind the slope. There is a strategist authoring the footprints. He is real, He has a name, and His method is documented.

This changes how you fight it.

If you think you are fighting *yourself* — your weak willpower, your bad habits — you will exhaust yourself. Because you will be fighting the steps after you have already taken them. You will try to leap from where you are, halfway down the slope, back to the top, by sheer force of repentance. Sometimes you will succeed for a week. Then the slope reasserts itself. Because **the slope was never about willpower. The slope was about footsteps.**

The Quranic intervention is upstream. Not "be better when you reach the bottom." But: **don't follow the steps**. Refuse the small step. The one that doesn't seem to matter. The one whose refusal seems disproportionate. *That* is the step that kept you out of the destination.

Here is the practical shape of this in your life. The next time you feel the inner murmur — *just open it, just look, just listen, just say it once* — recognize the murmur for what it is. **It is not a thought. It is a footstep.** You are not being asked to consider an idea. You are being asked to plant your foot somewhere. And the only useful question, in that moment, is not *what's the harm in this one step?* The useful question is: *whose footprints am I about to plant my foot inside of?*

The community of Madinah lost a month of its dignity to a rumor because individuals, one at a time, did not ask that question. The slander was not a single act. It was a million footsteps.

### Theme Two: The Liberation of *Mā Zakā Minkum Min Aḥadin Abadan*

Now let's stay with the second sentence. The one that should have stopped you.

*Were it not for Allah's bounty and mercy, not a single one of you, ever, in all of eternity, would have been purified.*

I want you to feel the two opposite reactions this sentence generates, depending on who is reading it.

**The self-righteous person reads this and is offended.** Wait — what about my struggle? What about the years I spent disciplining my nafs? What about the prayers I never missed, the hijabs I wore, the temptations I refused? Allah is saying *none of that purified me*? Allah is saying that without His bounty, I would still be unpurified — *abadan*?

Yes. That is exactly what He is saying.

**The broken person reads this and weeps.** Wait — even me? Even after everything I've done? Even the parts of myself I cannot stand? Allah is saying that purification is *His* verb, not mine? That I am not the one in charge of becoming clean?

Yes. That, too, is exactly what He is saying.

This single sentence is the great equalizer. **It crushes pride and lifts despair, with the same words, in the same breath.** Because it is locating purification outside the human entirely — and that location is good news for the broken and disorienting news for the proud.

Let's deepen this.

There is a particular kind of religious person — and we have all been one, at various points — who experiences the religion as a vending machine for purity. *I put in good deeds. I get out clean soul.* This person has a moral ledger. He knows where he stands. He compares himself, quietly, to others. He notices who isn't praying, who isn't dressing modestly, who repeated the rumor. And in noticing, he is — he believes — getting cleaner.

Allah says: *abadan*. Not in eternity. Not ever. Without My bounty.

Read that as a knife.

**The very mechanism of self-purification — the comparison, the ledger, the noticing of others' falls — is itself a form of impurity that no amount of self-effort can clean.** You cannot scrub yourself clean of self-righteousness using the soap of self-righteousness. The cleaning would re-dirty you in real time. The only way out is to stop being the agent of your own cleaning.

This is the breathtaking theology of *yuzakkī* — Form II — Allah is the One who purifies. You receive purification the way the earth receives rain. Not by demanding it. Not by deserving it. By being open to it.

And now look back at the surah. This ayah is sitting in the middle of a chapter full of rules about modesty, gaze-lowering, marriage, slander. Rules upon rules. And right in the middle of all those rules, Allah pauses and says: *None of these rules will purify you. I purify you.*

The rules are not the engine. They are the **invitation**. The form. The shape. They are how you make yourself available to being purified. But they are not the purification itself. **A person who keeps every rule and does not realize that purification is Allah's act will be a Pharisee. A person who keeps no rules and waits for purification will be lost. The believer keeps the rules with the constant awareness that the rules are not what is purifying him.**

Here is the practical shape of this. Tonight, before you sleep, do this one thing. Go through your day. Find the moment you felt cleanest. Find the moment you were most certain you were on the right side. Find the moment you compared yourself, even silently, even just in a flicker, to someone who was failing.

And in that moment of remembered righteousness, say: *Yā Allāh, were it not for Your bounty, I would not be standing here. Not in any version of me. Not for one moment. Not abadan.*

If you can mean that — really mean it — you will feel something shift in your chest. Because what shifts is not your behavior. **What shifts is who you think is doing the work.**

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at how this ayah moves.

It opens with a warning to the community: *don't follow the footsteps*. It narrows to the individual: *and whoever follows*. It exposes the mechanism: *he commands shamefulness and rejection*. And then — at the exact moment you might expect the ayah to continue with rules, instructions, preventive measures — it does the opposite. It pulls the camera back so far that the entire human moral project collapses into a single observation: **none of you would ever have been purified without Me.**

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are saying the same thing from two directions.

The footsteps revealed that you cannot save yourself from the road, because the road is engineered to deceive you about itself. *Mā zakā minkum min aḥadin abadan* revealed that even when you successfully navigate the road, the success was not yours. Both insights converge on the same point: **the human project of self-purification is not viable. It was never viable. It is not a question of effort. It is a question of agency.**

And this is why the ayah ends with two of Allah's names: *Samīʿun ʿAlīm* — All-Hearing, All-Knowing. He hears every footstep you considered. He knows every moment you were saved without realizing you needed saving. He is the audience to a drama you do not even know is being staged.

### Questions to Carry

Carry these with you.

1. Where, in your life right now, are you taking small steps you would refuse to take all at once? What is the destination those steps are walking you toward?

2. When was the last time you noticed someone else's moral failure and felt, even faintly, cleaner by contrast? What would it mean to read *mā zakā minkum min aḥadin abadan* as having been written specifically about that moment?

3. If purification is Allah's verb and not yours, how does that change the way you ask Him for it? How does it change the way you experience your own struggle?

4. The verb shifted from *yuwaswisu* (he whispers) elsewhere in the Quran to *yaʾmuru* (he commands) in this ayah. Where in your life is Shaytan no longer suggesting — but commanding? What footsteps got you there?

5. The community of Madinah did not collectively decide to slander. Each person took one step. What collective sins is your community currently constructing, one footstep at a time, without anyone deciding to construct them?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**Shaytan does not destroy you — he walks you, footprint by footprint, to a destination you would have refused at the start; and the only reason any believer is not standing in that destination right now is that Allah, by His sheer bounty, has been purifying souls that could never, in all eternity, have purified themselves.**

### The Closing Invitation

After this ayah, two things should shift. First — when you feel the small pull toward something you would not consciously choose, name it as *khuṭwah*. Footstep. The naming itself breaks the spell, because Shaytan's whole strategy depends on you not seeing the road as a road. Second — when you find yourself feeling clean, righteous, on the right side — let *abadan* enter that moment. Not as a punishment. As a liberation. The moment you stop being the agent of your own purity is the moment Allah can begin His work in you. Stop trying to clean yourself. Become available.

### Du'a

*Yā Allāh, You have shown us that without Your bounty, we would not be purified — not one of us, not ever, not in all of eternity. Save us from following the footsteps that we cannot see are footsteps. Make us hear the murmur for what it is before we plant our foot inside it. And purify us, yā Muzakkī, the way only You purify — for we have no verb, and You have every verb, and we are nothing without the bounty You have already, mercifully, been spending on us all along.*

*Āmīn.*
