# The Cosmic Liturgy You Cannot Hear

## Surah An-Nur, Ayah 41

> [ayah:24:41] أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُۥ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلطَّيْرُ صَـٰٓفَّـٰتٍ ۖ كُلٌّ قَدْ عَلِمَ صَلَاتَهُۥ وَتَسْبِيحَهُۥ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌۢ بِمَا يَفْعَلُونَ
>
> *Alam tara anna Allāha yusabbiḥu lahu man fī s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi wa-ṭ-ṭayru ṣāffāt. Kullun qad ʿalima ṣalātahu wa-tasbīḥah. Wa-Llāhu ʿalīmun bimā yafʿalūn.*
>
> "Have you not seen that Allah — to Him glorifies whatever is in the heavens and the earth, and the birds with wings outspread? Of each one He knows its salah and its tasbih. And Allah is fully Knowing of all that they do."

## Introduction: The World This Ayah Walks Into

Stay with where we are in this surah for a moment.

A few ayahs ago, Allah spoke the most luminous parabola in the Quran — the verse of Light. He compared His own light to a niche, a lamp, a glass like a brilliant star, lit from a blessed olive tree neither of east nor west. Then He described the houses He permitted to be raised — houses where men whom *neither trade nor commerce distracts* glorify Him morning and evening.

Then the contrast: those who reject the light. Their works become a mirage in a desert — the thirsty traveler runs to it, finds nothing. Or layered darknesses: the dark of the deep sea, dark waves above dark waves above dark clouds, "darknesses one above another. When he stretches out his hand, he can hardly see it."

So Surah An-Nur has placed two human possibilities in front of us: the lit niche, or the dark sea.

And now — *now* — Allah does something extraordinary. He widens the camera. He lifts our heads. He says: while you were busy being either luminous or lost, **let me show you what the rest of the universe was doing the entire time.**

[PAUSE]

Here is the hook I want you to carry into this reflection:

There is a liturgy happening right now — at this very second — that involves every single thing in existence except, possibly, you. The bird at your window has been in it longer than you've been awake. The mountain outside the city has not stopped. The fish in the deep dark have not paused. And Allah is going to tell us something almost unbearable about what that bird and that mountain and that fish *already know* — and what we, the ones given the Book, somehow do not.

Let's open the ayah.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Yusabbiḥu* — A Verb in the Wrong Tense, in the Right Tense

Before we go to the word, let me teach you something about how Arabic builds intensity.

In Arabic, verbs come in patterns called *forms*. Form I is the basic action — to know, to write, to break. But when Arabic wants to say "to do this *intensively*, *repeatedly*, *deliberately*," it uses Form II — the *tafʿīl* pattern. Same root, doubled middle letter, transformed meaning.

So *kataba* (Form I) means "he wrote." But *kattaba* (Form II) means "he made write, he caused to be written, he wrote and wrote." Form II takes a quiet verb and turns up the volume.

Now the word.

The root **س-ب-ح** (*s-b-ḥ*) — at its physical base, in classical Arabic — means **to swim, to float, to glide far away through water**. The fish *yasbaḥu*. The classical lexicons document it as motion through a medium, away, far out.

When Allah uses Form II of this root — *yusabbiḥu* — He transforms the physical image into a theological act. To make *tasbīḥ* of Allah is to **move Him, in your declaration, far away from any imperfection**. To send Him, in language, *out beyond* any defect that could be ascribed to Him. To declare Him utterly transcendent.

Now hold the form in your hand.

Allah says: *yusabbiḥu lahu man fī s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ.*

He uses the **imperfect tense** — the present-continuous in Arabic. Not "glorified" (past, finished). Not "will glorify" (future, pending). But **glorifies** — happening now, ongoing, unbroken. And the form is **Form II** — the intensified, repeated, sustained act.

[PAUSE]

So what Allah is actually saying — if we render the morphology with full weight — is:

> *Whoever is in the heavens and the earth is, right now, and continuously, and intensively, sending Allah far above and beyond every conceivable imperfection.*

Not a moment of worship. Not a daily prayer. **A constant, never-paused, sustained launching of Allah's purity beyond every limit, by every created thing in the cosmos.**

The fish in the dark trench is doing this. The wind moving through the alley you walked through this morning was doing this. The dust on a leaf in a forest no human will ever visit is doing this. Right now.

Notice something: the verb's root meaning — *to swim far away* — was not chosen by accident. The whole cosmos is *swimming Allah outward*. Pushing His purity past every horizon. Like a fish moves through water without resistance, every created thing moves Allah's transcendence outward without strain. **It is what they do because it is what they are.**

Listen also to the *sound* of the word. *Yu-sab-bi-ḥu.* Soft sibilants. Air moving. Almost the sound of breath. The Arabic itself sounds like the act it names — a quiet, continuous breathing-out of Allah's praise.

[PAUSE]

So far we have one verb. And it has already opened this:

The universe is not silent. The universe is in continuous, intensive prayer.

Move with me to the next element.

---

### Element Two: *Ṣāffāt* — The Wings That Tell You Everything

Allah gives us a category — "whoever is in the heavens and the earth" — and then He singles out one specific group:

**وَٱلطَّيْرُ صَـٰٓفَّـٰتٍ** — *wa-ṭ-ṭayru ṣāffāt* — "and the birds, [in a state of being] arrayed-in-rows / with-wings-outspread."

Why birds? And why this strange word *ṣāffāt*?

Let me teach the grammar first, because it carries the meaning.

In Arabic, when you describe the *state* a thing is in while doing something, you use a construction called the *ḥāl*. It's a circumstantial accusative. It tells you: this is *how* the action is happening, the posture, the condition. So if I say in Arabic, "Zayd came riding," the word "riding" is in the ḥāl — accusative — telling you the *state* he was in *while coming*.

So *ṣāffāt* is in the accusative because it's a ḥāl. It is telling you: **this is the state the birds are in while they glorify**.

Now the root. ص-ف-ف (*ṣ-f-f*) means **to arrange in a row, a rank, a line, a flat extension.** A *ṣaff* is a row. *Ṣafḥ* is a flat surface. The active participle *ṣāffah* (singular feminine) — *ṣāffāt* (plural) — describes something that is *spread out flat, ranked, arrayed*.

Applied to a bird in flight, the classical lexicons explain: **wings outspread, held flat, gliding without flapping** — and *also* the meaning of birds flying *in formation, in rows*, the way migrating birds do.

[PAUSE]

Now stay with me. Look up a moment from this page. Picture a bird gliding — wings flat, motionless, sustained. It is *not flapping*. It is held by something. The air. The wind. A current it cannot see.

That is the image Allah has placed in your hand. **A creature in a state of complete extension, complete openness, completely surrendered to a current that holds it up.**

And in *that very state* — *ṣāffāt* — the bird is also engaged in tasbīḥ. The wings outspread *are* the tasbīḥ. The posture *is* the prayer.

Here's what's remarkable. There is another surah named after this exact word — Sūrat **aṣ-Ṣāffāt** (37) — where Allah swears by *aṣ-ṣāffāti ṣaffā* — "those arranged in rows, in ranks." The classical scholars (Ṭabarī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī) understand that ayah primarily as the angels in their cosmic ranks of worship. The same morphology. The same posture. **A bird gliding with outspread wings and an angel in its rank of glorification share the same Arabic word.**

Allah is showing us something through that overlap. The bird with wings outspread is doing, in its own register, what the angel in formation is doing in its register. The cosmos has the same shape at every level. **Surrender. Extension. Held by an unseen current. Arranged.**

Stay with me on the absence here. Allah did not say *aṭ-ṭayru tuṣallī* (the birds pray) or *aṭ-ṭayru tasbaḥu fī s-samāʾ* (the birds swim through the sky). He chose the participle *ṣāffāt*. **He froze the bird in mid-glide and called that frozen posture itself the act of worship.**

The wings *are* the salah. The extension *is* the tasbīḥ.

[PAUSE]

What does this invite us to consider?

That worship in the cosmos is not always *added on top of* what a thing does. For most of creation, **what they are doing is already what they are saying.** The bird in flight is not flying *and also* worshipping. The flight, in its very geometry, is the worship.

This is the inverse of how we live. We do our work, *and then* we pray. We separate the two. The cosmos does not separate. The cosmos's motion is its salah.

Hold this — we'll come back to it.

### Element Three: *Kullun Qad ʿAlima Ṣalātahu wa-Tasbīḥah* — The Past Tense That Indicts the Living

This is the line that, once you see it, changes the whole ayah.

> **كُلٌّ قَدْ عَلِمَ صَلَاتَهُۥ وَتَسْبِيحَهُۥ**
>
> *Kullun qad ʿalima ṣalātahu wa-tasbīḥah.*
>
> "Of each one — already — its salah and its tasbīḥ has been known." (On who the knower is, see the third point below.)

Let me unpack three things here, slowly.

**First: *qad ʿalima* — "has *already* known."**

In Arabic, *qad* placed before a perfect-tense verb intensifies its already-completedness. *ʿAlima* alone would mean "knew." *Qad ʿalima* means "has already known, has indeed known, the knowing is done and settled." It is **knowledge that is not in process**.

The bird is not in flight school. The mountain is not learning its tasbīḥ. The fish is not catching up. **Each thing's worship was already settled and already known before this moment, before you noticed, before the question was even asked.**

[PAUSE]

**Second: *ṣalātahu* — "*its own* salah."**

Read that again. Allah uses the word **salah** — the same word He uses for the worship He prescribes to humans — and He attaches it to every created thing with a possessive pronoun. *Its salah*. The mountain's salah. The bird's salah. The drop of rain's salah.

The classical scholars stop here. They have to. Because Arabic has many words for prayer — *duʿāʾ*, *tasbīḥ*, *ḥamd*, *taḥmīd*, *dhikr* — and Allah chose *ṣalāh*. The most specific. The most ritually loaded. The one we associate with the five prayers, with formal worship, with prescribed motion and word.

What does it mean for a tree to have a salah? For a stone to have one? For a dust mote?

This is one of those places where the classical scholars give us not contradicting answers but *layered* answers — different angles on a single reality. Some, like Ibn ʿAbbās (as transmitted), read it as **literal**: every created thing has a real, specific worship known to itself, in its own modality, that we cannot perceive. Others read it as the *natural functioning* of the thing being its salah — the tree's salah is to be a tree exactly as Allah designed it, the river's salah is to flow as designed. Others, like al-Rāzī, hold both — the conscious and the structural — at once.

Don't pick one. Hold them all. Because the deeper claim is the same in every reading: **whatever the creature's mode of worship is, it was guided into it, it performs it, and Allah knows and counts it.**

[PAUSE]

**Third: who knows it?**

Here is the single most discussed point in this ayah. *Kullun qad ʿalima ṣalātahu*. "Each — has known — its salah."

Who is the one who has known? The classical commentators answer this with striking consistency: **Allah**. Al-Jalālayn does not even leave it to inference — the tafsīr inserts the subject directly into the text, glossing *kullun qad ʿalima* as *qad ʿalima **Allāh*** — "Allah has known its salah and its tasbīḥ." Ibn Kathīr's rendering carries the same sense: *of each one, **He** knows its salah and its glorification*. This is the primary reading, and it is the one the ayah's own closing line confirms — *wa-Llāhu ʿalīmun bimā yafʿalūn*, *and Allah is fully Knowing of what they do*. The sentence begins and ends with the same Knower.

So the picture the ayah paints is this: the cosmos is worshipping in modes we cannot hear or recognize, and **not one of those modes escapes Allah's knowledge**. Nothing in that vast liturgy is unwitnessed. The bird's prayer is not lost in the noise.

A minority reading takes *kullun* itself as the subject — each creature knows its own salah — since *kullun* stands first in the clause. It is grammatically available, and some later commentators mention it. But it is not what the major tafāsīr settle on, and nothing in this reflection should be built on it alone. Hold it as a possibility at the edge, not as the ayah's plain sense.

What the commentators *do* affirm about the creature's side comes from a different angle. Al-Muyassar puts it directly: *kullu makhlūqin qad arshadahu Llāhu kayfa yuṣallī lahu wa-yusabbiḥuh* — **every creature, Allah has guided it in how to pray to Him and glorify Him.** The knowledge is real and it is in the creature. But it was *put* there. It is issued knowledge, not self-generated knowledge. That distinction will matter in a moment.

[PAUSE]

Now stay with me. Here is what this single sentence does to me.

Every created thing was **issued** its salah — *arshadahu Llāh*, Allah guided it — and it has never had to be reminded since. The bird hatched into knowledge of its salah. The fish swam into knowledge of its tasbīḥ. The mountain stood up out of the earth already guided.

And we — the ones to whom Allah sent a Messenger, sent down a Book over twenty-three years, sent angels with revelation, taught explicitly *qul* (say), *iqraʾ* (recite), *aqim aṣ-ṣalāh* (establish the prayer) — **we are the only creature who has to be *taught*. The only one who can *forget*. The only one who can know exactly what salah is and not perform it.**

The bird needs no Book. The bird already knew.

Allah did not give the bird a Quran because the bird did not need one. He gave it to *us* because we are the species whose default state is *not knowing*. Whose default state is forgetting. Whose default state is needing reminders, every day, five times.

And what does the ayah say at the end? *Wa-Llāhu ʿalīmun bimā yafʿalūn.* *And Allah is fully Knowing of what they do.* Not just what they say. What they *do*. He knows the bird's deeds. He knows the mountain's. He knows yours.

[PAUSE — major pause]

Bridge: We have walked through three linguistic doors. *Yusabbiḥu* — the continuous, intensive sending of Allah's purity outward by every existing thing. *Ṣāffāt* — the bird with wings outspread, where the posture is itself the prayer. *Kullun qad ʿalima* — every creature's salah is already known to Allah, and every creature was already guided into it, and the species we belong to is the only one that can be guided and still fail to act.

Now let's leave the words and ask what they reveal about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Hum You Stopped Hearing

You have a refrigerator in your kitchen. It is humming right now. It has been humming since you woke up. It will hum for the rest of the day.

Did you hear it before I just mentioned it?

Almost certainly not. The brain has a feature — habituation — where a constant, unchanging stimulus drops below the threshold of conscious attention. The hum is *real*. Your ears are *receiving* it. Your auditory cortex is *processing* it. But your conscious mind has filed it away under *background*, and you live as though it isn't there.

This is what humans do. With everything. We habituate.

Now imagine for a moment that the hum of your refrigerator was not refrigerator hum. Imagine it was the sound of every created thing in existence engaged in continuous tasbīḥ. The rocks under the building. The pipes in the walls. The dust on the windowsill. The bird outside. The clouds above. The atoms in your own bones.

Allah is telling you, in this ayah, **that is exactly what the hum is.**

You are living inside a cosmic sound — a sustained, intensive, never-paused chorus of every existing thing pushing Allah's purity out beyond every horizon — and you have stopped hearing it.

[PAUSE]

The Quran says elsewhere — Surah al-Isrāʾ 17:44:

> *Wa-in min shayʾin illā yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi wa-lākin lā tafqahūna tasbīḥahum.*
>
> "There is not a single thing except that it glorifies with His praise — but you do not understand their tasbīḥ."

That last clause is devastating. *Lākin lā tafqahūna tasbīḥahum.* You do not *understand* it. You are *in it*. It is *all around you*. But you cannot *parse* it.

Here is what this changes about how you live.

You are not bringing worship into a silent universe. You are joining a worship that is already in progress.

Think about what that does to the salah you'll pray today. The Maghrib you'll pray as the sun sets. You are not initiating an act. You are not lifting a flag in an empty field. **You are catching up to a liturgy that has been in continuous performance since the first thing was created — and adding your voice to it.**

The bird is already in salah when you start. The mountain is already in salah. The trees outside the masjid are already in salah. You walking in to pray is the *human* finally arriving — late, distracted, needing reminders, having forgotten — to a service that has been running flawlessly without you.

[PAUSE]

What changes tomorrow?

When you stand for fajr, before you say *Allāhu akbar*, listen for one breath. Not literally — you won't hear the cosmic tasbīḥ with your ears. But internally, *acknowledge*: I am about to join something already happening. I am not starting this. I am the latecomer.

That single shift — from "I am performing prayer" to "I am joining prayer" — is one of the most powerful psychological reorientations the Quran offers. It moves you from *originator* to *participant*. From *audience-of-one* to *member-of-the-cosmos*. From the lonely, self-conscious posture of "is my salah good enough" to the relieved posture of "I am joining the angels and the birds and the mountains and the rain — they are all praying, and now I am, too."

The pressure comes off. The loneliness comes off. **You are no longer praying alone in a silent room. You never were.**

### Theme Two: Knowledge Before Action — and the Indictment of the Knowledgeable

There is a quiet, almost unbearable theme buried in the words *qad ʿalima ṣalātahu*.

Let me be precise about where it comes from, because we just did the careful work above. The theme does *not* rest on reading the creature as the knower — that was the minority reading, and we set it aside. It rests on what al-Muyassar states plainly: *every creature, Allah has guided it in how to pray to Him and glorify Him.* The knowledge of worship was **placed** in every created thing. And here is the thing to notice: every creature except one performs what was placed in it.

Let me bring you in through a human door.

You probably know someone who has, on paper, every reason to be excellent at something — and isn't. The person who took piano lessons for ten years and cannot play. The person who has read fifty books on health and cannot maintain a habit. The person who graduated top of his class and produces nothing. There is a specific human grief in watching someone *who has been given the knowledge* fail to act on it. The grief is sharper than watching someone fail who never had the knowledge. Because the knowledge was *given*. It was *already there*. And still — nothing.

Now read the ayah again with that human grief in your hand.

*Kullun qad ʿalima ṣalātahu wa-tasbīḥah.*

**The knowing is already settled.**

The bird, with no prophet sent to it, has its worship. The fish, with no Book revealed to it, has its tasbīḥ. The mountain, with no prayer-time announced to it, keeps its own. They were *given* the knowledge of their worship as the very condition of their existence — and Allah knows every last one of those acts.

[PAUSE]

And then — *we*.

We were given more. We were given a Messenger. A Book. A community. A *qiblah*. A schedule. A language. Imams. Scholars. A thousand years of preserved transmission. Our own copy of the Quran on our bookshelf. We have been given knowledge of our salah in such overwhelming, redundant, catalogued, transmitted form that no human in history has ever had more access to the knowledge of how to worship Allah than the human alive today.

And yet.

The bird, with none of that, has its salah. We, with all of it, often do not.

This is not a guilt-trip. It is something heavier and quieter than guilt. It is a *recalibration of where the failure actually lives*. The failure is not in our knowledge. The failure is not in our access. The failure is not in our information. **The failure is in the gap between our knowledge and our action — and that gap is something only the human species has the capacity to produce.**

The bird has no gap. Its knowing *is* its doing. It does not know its salah and then decline to perform it. Its knowledge of its worship is identical to its performance of its worship. There is no daylight between them.

We have all the daylight in the world.

[PAUSE]

Stay with this paradox without rushing to resolve it.

We were given freedom — the capacity to choose. The bird was not. So in one sense, the bird's seamless worship is *not* a moral achievement. It is a *constraint*. The bird could not refuse if it wanted to.

But in another sense — and this is the sense the Quran wants us to feel — **the human's freedom was supposed to make our worship *more* significant, not *less* present.** Our salah was supposed to be richer than the bird's because it was chosen. Instead, for many of us, our freedom has been used to introduce the gap. To know and not do. To learn and not act.

The Prophet ﷺ said something that lives in the same neighborhood as this ayah: *Ṭalab al-ʿilmi farīḍatun ʿalā kulli muslim* — seeking knowledge is an obligation on every Muslim. But the scholars across centuries have warned: knowledge that is not acted on becomes a *ḥujjah* against you, not for you. It becomes evidence on the Day of Judgment that you *knew* and still did not. The bird will have no such evidence brought against it. It performed exactly what it knew.

[PAUSE]

What changes tomorrow?

This week, identify one thing you know to be a salah upon you — in the wide sense, not just the five prayers. A specific obligation. A specific recommended act. A specific avoidance you know is required of you. Something where there is *no information gap*. You know it. The knowledge is settled. *Qad ʿalimta.*

Close the gap on that one thing this week. Not for everything. One thing.

Because the cosmos is not asking you to know more. The cosmos is showing you that knowledge alone was never the goal. **The goal was, and is, the seamlessness of the bird** — the closing of the daylight between *qad ʿalima* and *yusabbiḥu*. To know your salah and to perform it as the very next breath.

That is the lesson the bird, with its outspread wings, is trying to deliver to every human who has ever stopped to watch it glide.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at what we discovered from two directions.

From the linguistic side: a verb in Form II, in the imperfect tense — sustained, intensive, ongoing. A bird with outspread wings whose *posture is the prayer*. A perfect tense verb — *qad ʿalima* — telling us the knowledge was settled before the question was even asked.

From the thematic side: a cosmic liturgy already in progress that we are joining, not starting. A species — ours — that uniquely has the capacity to know its salah and not perform it, while every other created thing performs its salah as the seamless extension of knowing it.

These are the same insight from two angles. **The cosmos is in continuous, intensive worship because each created thing's knowledge of its worship is identical to its performance of its worship.** The two are one. The verb's continuity (*yusabbiḥu*) and the verb's settled completion (*qad ʿalima*) are not in tension — they describe the same seamlessness. Knowledge already settled, action continuously ongoing.

The human is the one creature in whom that single act has been split into two. And the Quran, the Messenger, the salah, the dhikr, the daily reminders — all of it — exists to help us *re-fuse* what has been split. To bring our knowing and our doing back into the seamless geometry of the bird in flight.

### Questions to Carry

1. What is the hum you have stopped hearing? What act of worship is happening right now in your own body, your own breath, your own bones, that you have not paid attention to in a long time?

2. The bird's salah and its existence are the same act. What in your life have you separated that the cosmos shows you should not be separated — your work, your eating, your speaking, your raising of children?

3. *Qad ʿalima.* Already known. What do you already know about your worship that you have not yet acted on? Name it specifically. What is one thing you would do this week if you closed that gap?

4. When you next stand for salah, can you stand with the awareness that you are *joining* a service already in progress, not initiating one in a silent room?

5. If Allah is *ʿalīmun bimā yafʿalūn* — fully Knowing of what every created thing does — what does it mean that He is also fully Knowing of what *you* do, including what you know and decline to do?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**The entire cosmos is already in continuous, intensive prayer because every created thing's knowing of its salah and its performing of its salah are the same act — and the human is the one species in whom those two have come apart, and to whom Allah sent the Quran to put them back together.**

### The Closing Invitation

This is not asking you to feel guilty. This is asking you to feel *accompanied*.

You are not alone in your salah. You never were. The bird at your window prayed before you woke. The trees outside the masjid prayed while you walked in. The mountains prayed while you slept. They are still praying. They will pray when you sleep tonight.

Tomorrow, when you stand for fajr, do one thing. Before you say *Allāhu akbar* — pause for one breath. Internally, acknowledge: *I am joining what is already happening.* Then begin.

That single internal posture — joining, not initiating — is enough to start to close the gap the human creature has carried since the beginning. It is the human moving, slowly, toward the seamlessness of the bird with wings outspread.

### Du'a

> *Allāhumma — You who taught the bird its salah without sending it a Book, teach me my salah, even though You sent me a Book and I have so often not opened it. Close the gap in me between what I already know and what I do. Let my knowing be my doing, the way the bird's flight is its prayer. And join my voice, however small, to the chorus of every created thing that has not stopped glorifying You for a single moment since You said* Be. *Āmīn.*
