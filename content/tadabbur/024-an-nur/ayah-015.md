# When the Tongue Becomes the Ear

## Surah An-Nur, Ayah 15

> إِذْ تَلَقَّوْنَهُۥ بِأَلْسِنَتِكُمْ وَتَقُولُونَ بِأَفْوَاهِكُم مَّا لَيْسَ لَكُم بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ وَتَحْسَبُونَهُۥ هَيِّنًا وَهُوَ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ عَظِيمٌ

> *Idh talaqqawnahu bi-alsinatikum wa-taqūlūna bi-afwāhikum mā laysa lakum bihi ʿilm — wa-taḥsabūnahu hayyinan wa-huwa ʿinda-llāhi ʿaẓīm.*

> "When you were receiving it with your tongues, and saying with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, and you reckoned it a light thing — while with Allah it was tremendous."

---

## INTRODUCTION

### The Month That Did Not End

To enter this ayah, you have to enter a month.

A specific month, in a specific household, in the city of Madinah. The Prophet ﷺ has returned from an expedition with a wife who, by the time the caravan reached the city, had become the object of a rumor. The rumor took root and spread. It was not whispered by enemies of the community alone — it passed through the lips of believers. Some of them respected. Some of them well-known. Some of them in the inner circles.

For nearly a month, Allah did not speak.

Think about that silence. The Prophet ﷺ — who received revelation regularly, who could ordinarily turn to the heavens for clarification — was left in that silence. ʿĀʾisha (raḍiya-llāhu ʿanhā) was in that silence. The community was in that silence. And the rumor kept moving.

When revelation finally came, it did not come as a single line of vindication. It came as a sustained, devastating analysis of *how this had happened* — how a community of believers had, almost without noticing, become a delivery system for a lie. Ayahs 11 through 20 of Surat an-Nūr are not just an exoneration. They are a forensic dissection of a moral failure that the believers had been treating as conversation.

This ayah is the central piece of that forensic work. In just eighteen words of Arabic, Allah names the *exact* mechanism by which the slander circulated. Not the malice. Not the ill intent. The *mechanism*.

### The Hook

In this short ayah, Allah will say something grammatically impossible. He will describe people receiving information with the wrong organ. He will then describe them speaking with the right organ — but mention the organ in a way that should be redundant. And then He will end with a contrast so devastating that, if you sit with it, it rearranges what you think the moral weight of your daily speech actually is.

By the end of these eighteen words, you should understand something the Companions had to learn the hard way: that there is a way of being a "good Muslim" — praying, fasting, fighting alongside the Prophet ﷺ — and *still* be participating in something Allah calls *ʿaẓīm*. Tremendous. Of crushing weight.

Let's walk in slowly.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Tongue That Receives

#### A small grammatical note before we open the ayah

In Arabic, as in English, there is a grammar of *channels*. Some organs receive. Some organs produce. The eye receives — light comes in. The ear receives — sound comes in. The tongue produces — speech goes out. The hand can do either. This is so basic in human experience that no language has to teach it.

When a writer breaks this grammar — assigns reception to a producing organ, or production to a receiving organ — it is not a mistake. It is a sentence asking you to stop.

Now look at the first half of the ayah.

#### Opening the word

> *إِذْ تَلَقَّوْنَهُۥ بِأَلْسِنَتِكُمْ*
> *"When you were receiving it with your tongues..."*

The verb is **تَلَقَّوْنَ** — *talaqqawna*. Its root is **ل ق ي** — *to meet, to encounter, to receive what comes toward you.* In Form V — the *tafaʿʿala* pattern that Allah uses here — the verb intensifies. It is not passive receiving. It is *active*, *eager*, *engaged* receiving. The same form is used positively elsewhere — when Adam *talaqqā* words from his Lord (2:37), when believers *yatalaqqā* the angels of mercy. It is the receiving of someone leaning forward, hands open, ready.

So far, so good. They were *eagerly receiving* something.

But what follows breaks the grammar.

> *بِأَلْسِنَتِكُمْ* — *with your tongues.*

Stay with this. They were not receiving with their *ears*. The tongue is not a receiving organ. The tongue is what you use to *give* speech, not to *take* it in.

[PAUSE]

What is Allah doing?

Notice something: in normal human conversation, information enters through the ear, gets weighed in the mind and the heart, *and only then* — if it is found worth passing along — does it travel out through the tongue. The ear is the gate of intake. The mind is the room of consideration. The tongue is the door of release.

What Allah is describing is a community that *skipped* the middle two stages. The information did not enter the ear, get weighed, and then leave the tongue. It entered *directly into the tongue*. The tongue became both the gate of intake *and* the door of release. There was no chamber of judgment in between.

Here's what's remarkable: Allah is not saying *"you heard it and then you said it."* He is saying something more devastating — *the hearing and the saying were the same act.* The information never paused inside you long enough to be examined. It arrived already on its way out.

This is what gossip does. Gossip is not a category of speech. It is a *bypass* of the entire human apparatus of judgment. The tongue replaces the ear because the tongue is faster, and being first to pass it on is the dopamine. The thing you are receiving is not really being *learned*. It is being *transmitted*.

There is a reason Allah uses this exact phrase about this exact community. They were not lying. They were not even, in most cases, intending harm. They were simply *passing on* — receiving and releasing in a single motion, like a relay runner who never breaks stride to look at the baton.

A documented grammatical fact: the Form V *tafaʿʿala* of *l-q-y* implies eager, willing reception. What this invites us to consider is that gossip is not an *accident* of the tongue. It is the tongue *eagerly leaning in* — but only to take and release, never to hold.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: The Mouth That Did Not Need to Be Mentioned

#### A note on Quranic redundancy that is never redundant

When the Quran mentions an organ in connection with an action that organ already performs by definition, it is not filling space. *Speech happens in the mouth* — that is a tautology. Saying "they spoke with their mouths" is, on the surface, like saying "they walked with their feet." Information you do not need.

So when Allah says it, look closer.

#### Opening the word

> *وَتَقُولُونَ بِأَفْوَاهِكُم مَّا لَيْسَ لَكُم بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ*
> *"...and saying with your mouths what you had no knowledge of."*

**أَفْوَاه** — *afwāh* — is the plural of **فَم** — *fam*, mouth. The root **ف و ه** evokes the *opening*, the orifice itself. It is not a word about *speech*. It is a word about the *physical aperture* through which speech leaves.

And here is the key. In Arabic, when you want to talk about *meaningful speech*, you say *qāla* — *he said* — and stop there. The mouth is implied. But when you want to emphasize that the speech is *only mouth-deep* — that it is sound emerging from an aperture but not from any inner conviction — you say "*qāla bi-fīhi*" — *he spoke with his mouth.*

Lisān al-ʿArab and the classical balāghah tradition note this exact distinction. Speech that comes only from the *fam* — the opening — is speech that has not been authored by the heart. It is air pushed across vocal cords. It is *sound in the shape of words*, but the words have not been ratified by the inner self.

So now read it again:

> *وَتَقُولُونَ بِأَفْوَاهِكُم مَّا لَيْسَ لَكُم بِهِۦ عِلْمٌ*

*"And you were saying — with your mouths — that of which you had no knowledge."*

Allah is drawing a precise line. The speech was *real*. Sound was made. Sentences were formed. But the *interior* of the speech was empty. There was no *ʿilm* — no actual knowledge — backing what the mouth was producing.

[PAUSE]

Stay with this. The first half of the ayah said: *the tongue replaced the ear*. Now the second half adds: *and the mouth replaced the heart.*

The whole human machinery of careful speech — *I take in through my ear, I weigh in my mind, I ratify in my heart, I release through my tongue* — has collapsed into a single organ doing all four jobs poorly. The mouth alone, doing what the entire system was designed to do together.

And here's what makes this so cutting: notice Allah did not say "and you spoke *lies*." He said you spoke *what you did not know*. The category Allah is naming is not falsehood. It is *speech without grounding*. You may have been right about it, by accident. You may have been wrong. *That is not the issue.* The issue is that you released sound into the world that had no foundation in your knowing.

Every time, in any community, in any age — when we repeat what we have not verified, we are not first being dishonest. We are first being *speakers without knowers.* Mouths without hearts. Apertures without authors.

A documented grammatical fact: *qāla bi-l-fam* in classical Arabic is used to mark speech that is mouth-deep only. What this invites us to consider is that there is a category of religious failure that is not lying and not malice — it is simply releasing sound that no part of you actually authored.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: The Two Weights

#### A note on adjectives that sound like what they mean

Some Arabic words are *sonically transparent* — their sound enacts their sense. Allah uses this throughout the Quran. *Hamasa* — to whisper — sounds like a whisper. *Saʿiqa* — to be struck by lightning — has the sharp crack of the strike. The *Mufradāt* of al-Rāghib repeatedly draws attention to this.

In the closing of this ayah, two adjectives sit across from each other. Listen to them.

**هَيِّنًا** — *hayyinan*. Try saying it. The sound is genuinely *light*. The doubled *yāʾ* glides. The ending dissolves into a soft *nūn*. It is a word that *floats*.

**عَظِيمًا** — *ʿaẓīman*. Try saying this one. The opening *ʿayn* is throat-deep. The *ẓāʾ* is heavy, weighted, almost a stumble in the mouth. The word *lands*.

Now read the closing:

> *وَتَحْسَبُونَهُۥ هَيِّنًا وَهُوَ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ عَظِيمٌ*
> *"And you reckoned it light — while with Allah it was tremendous."*

#### Opening the contrast

The verb is **تَحْسَبُونَ** — *taḥsabūna*, from **ح س ب** — *to reckon, to calculate, to judge by accounting*. This is not casual perception. This is *appraisal*. The believers had *weighed* the rumor and assigned it a weight. Their scale read: *light*.

The root **ه و ن** — the root of *hayyin* — carries the sense of *easiness, gentleness, low cost*. To call something *hayyin* is to say *it is not a big deal*. It is the exact phrase a person uses today when they pass on something cutting and then say, *"I was just kidding"* or *"It was a small thing"* or *"Why are you making this such a big deal?"*

The root **ع ظ م** — the root of *ʿaẓīm* — carries *bone-deep weight*. *ʿAẓm* in Arabic literally means *bone* — the structural, load-bearing, foundational part of the body. *ʿAẓīm* is what is heavy at the core. Allah uses *ʿaẓīm* to describe Himself (*al-ʿAẓīm*), to describe the Throne, to describe the great events of the Hereafter.

Now hold both adjectives in your hands at the same time. The believers had picked up the rumor, weighed it, and said *hayyin*. Allah, in the same moment, was weighing the same rumor, and the scale on His side read *ʿaẓīm*.

[PAUSE]

This is not a difference of *opinion*. This is a difference of *scales*.

Stay with that. Allah is not saying, *"you thought it was small but actually it was a little bigger than you thought."* He is saying the gap between human estimation and divine estimation, in this case, was *the entire spectrum* — from *hayyin* to *ʿaẓīm*. From the lightest end of the scale to the heaviest end.

And the construction is doing something else, too. *ʿinda-llāhi* — *with Allah*. The phrase places the second weight in Allah's possession. As if to say: there is a scale that human beings carry around, and there is a separate scale, kept *with* Allah, and these two scales are not calibrated the same way. We do not get to set the weights. The weight of an act is not what we *experience* of it. The weight of an act is what *Allah registers* of it.

A documented linguistic fact: *hayyin* and *ʿaẓīm* are not synonyms inverted — they sit at opposite poles of weight in classical Arabic, and their phonetic textures enact this opposition. What this invites us to consider is that the moral universe has *two* scales, and the gap between them — for any given act — may be much larger than we suspect.

[PAUSE]

### Bridge

What the linguistic construction has shown us is this: Allah has named, with surgical precision, three separate failures inside one social act. The tongue replaced the ear. The mouth replaced the heart. And the human scale registered the weight of the act as the *opposite* of what Allah's scale registered.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: When the Tongue Eats the Ear

You already know this experience.

You are scrolling. A headline appears. Before you have read the article, before you have even read the second sentence of the headline, your finger has already moved toward the share button. Some part of you has already decided: *this is going out*. Your friends need to see this. The group chat needs to see this. Your followers need to see this.

You did not *learn* the thing. You *received* it for the purpose of *transmitting* it. The tongue replaced the ear before the ear had a chance to do its job.

Now multiply that by every WhatsApp forward you have ever sent without reading. Every retweet of a quote you didn't verify. Every "did you hear about..." that opened a conversation in which you knew, in your honest moments, that you had not actually heard *about* anything — you had heard *the words* of someone who had heard *the words* of someone who had heard *the words*. There was no original. There was only circulation.

This is what the ayah is naming. The tongue eating the ear is not a 21st-century invention. It is an ancient human pattern that Allah identified in Madinah, in a community of *Companions*, and named with such precision that fourteen centuries later, when we read it, we recognize ourselves in it without translation.

Notice what this means.

The Ifk did not happen because the people of Madinah were uniquely cruel. It happened because they were *human*, and humans, when given a piece of charged information, tend to circulate it before they verify it. The dopamine of being the first to tell is older than the printing press, older than gunpowder, older than the alphabet. We are wired for it.

So if Allah is correcting it in the ayah, He is not correcting an accident. He is correcting *our default*. He is saying: the easiest, most natural, most pleasurable thing you can do with a piece of information is to pass it on without checking it. And that easiest, most natural, most pleasurable thing — *that is what you are accountable for stopping*.

The Prophet ﷺ said, "It is enough lying for a person to narrate everything he hears." (Muslim) Notice he did not say *enough lying to narrate something false*. He said *enough lying to narrate everything you hear*. Because the act of indiscriminate transmission *is itself* a form of falsehood, even when each individual item is true. You have stopped being a discerner and become a conduit.

[PAUSE]

There is a paradox here worth sitting with. The harder thing is *not the silence*. The harder thing is *the pause*.

The silence — refusing to speak at all — is something monks and ascetics have practiced. It has its own difficulty, but it is a *clean* difficulty. You either speak or you don't.

The pause is messier. The pause is: *you receive something, and you have to hold it, and you have to weigh it, and only then do you decide what to do with it.* The pause is what creates the chamber between the ear and the tongue. The pause is what allows the heart to ratify the speech before it leaves.

What Allah is describing in this ayah is a community that had *lost the pause*. And what He is asking the believers to recover is not silence. It is the chamber. It is the space between intake and release where you ask: *Do I actually know this? Does saying this serve anything? What is the cost to the person whose name is in this sentence?*

That chamber is where being a Muslim happens, in this domain. Not in the speaking. Not in the staying silent. In the *holding*.

#### What this changes about tomorrow

Before you forward, share, or retell — *one breath*. One single deliberate breath in which you ask yourself two questions: *Do I have ʿilm of this?* and *If the person were standing in front of me, would I say it?* If the answer to either is no, the chamber is telling you to let it die in your mouth.

That is not silence. That is the recovery of the apparatus Allah built into you.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: The Two Scales

Here is what the closing of the ayah will do to you, if you let it.

You have, at every moment of your life, a *felt sense* of how much weight your actions carry. You know — or think you know — what counts as a "big deal" and what counts as a "small thing." A missed prayer feels big. A sharp word to your spouse feels small. Lying about a small matter at work feels small. Helping a stranger across the street feels small. Backbiting in the car after seeing a relative — *very small*. Almost not even a thing.

This felt sense is your inner scale.

Allah is telling you, in this ayah, that there is *another scale*. It is not next to yours. It is not a little different from yours. It is *kept with Him*, in a calibration *He sets*, and on certain matters — perhaps many matters — the readings on the two scales are not slightly different. They are *opposite ends of the spectrum*.

This is, I think, one of the most disorienting truths in the Quran.

Because here is the implication: the things you have been treating as *hayyin* in your life — light, easy, no big deal, why-are-you-making-this-such-a-thing — some of them, on Allah's scale, are *ʿaẓīm*. Bone-weight. Tremendous.

And — this is the part that should sit with us — Allah did not say *"you*'re wrong about*"* the weight. He said *you reckoned it.* You did the math. You weighed it. The verb is *taḥsabūna* — calculation. So the people Allah is correcting are not people who *failed to think*. They are people who *thought, weighed, concluded* — *and were wrong*. Their scale was off, but they did not know their scale was off, because they had no other scale to compare it to.

Where do you go from here?

You do not get a copy of Allah's scale. Allah does not hand us, in this dunya, a perfect register of which acts are *ʿaẓīm* in His sight and which are *hayyin*. We do not get that. What we get is something different and, in a way, more demanding.

We get the *suspicion*. We get the planted seed of doubt that says: *the thing I am about to call light may not be light. The thing my friends call light may not be light. The thing the culture calls light may not be light.*

[PAUSE]

This is the real work of the muʾmin in this ayah. Not the achievement of perfect calibration — that is impossible in this life. But the *humility* of knowing that your scale and the real scale may diverge, and the *practice* of treating things as heavier than they feel, *because* you suspect your scale is light.

Notice: this is the opposite of what makes us *feel* religious. Feeling religious is feeling *moral weight*. We feel weighty about the obvious sins, the public ones, the dramatic ones. What this ayah is teaching is that the scariest sins are the ones that feel *hayyin* — light — *to us*. The ones that pass through our day and we don't even register them as having happened.

The slander of ʿĀʾisha (raḍiya-llāhu ʿanhā) felt, to those participating, like *conversation*. Like passing on news. Like normal social processing of an interesting story. It did not feel like sin. It felt *hayyin*. That is exactly why Allah had to descend with eighteen words to tell them: *the thing you are doing right now, that you cannot even feel as a moral act — it is ʿaẓīm.*

The terror of the ayah is not that you might commit a great sin. The terror is that you might commit it without ever feeling like you did anything at all.

#### What this changes about tomorrow

A daily practice: at the end of each day, ask not *"what big sins did I commit?"* but *"what did I treat as hayyin today that may, with Allah, have been ʿaẓīm?"* The conversation in the car. The forwarded message. The dismissive comment about a relative. The mocking thought about a stranger. Treat the *unfelt* with more suspicion than the *felt*. Because the felt sins are already on your radar. The dangerous sins are the ones that have not yet shown up on your scale.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture

Look at what Allah has done in eighteen words.

He has named *three* organ-failures and *one* scale-failure, in perfect order:

1. **The tongue replaced the ear** — *receiving with tongues*
2. **The mouth replaced the heart** — *speaking with mouths what you did not know*
3. **Your scale read light**
4. **His scale read tremendous**

The first two failures are *mechanical* — a breakdown of the human apparatus of speech. The third and fourth are *moral* — a misreading of weight. And the structure tells us how the two are connected: *because* you let the tongue eat the ear and the mouth replace the heart, your scale stopped working. You can no longer tell what is *hayyin* and what is *ʿaẓīm*, because the chamber where weighing happens has collapsed.

The diagnosis and the prescription are the same sentence. The way you recover the right scale is by *recovering the chamber*. Restore the pause. Restore the holding. Restore the place where the heart ratifies what the mouth releases. *Then* — and only then — does your scale begin to recalibrate toward Allah's.

### Linguistic and Thematic Together

The two halves of this reflection are the same insight from two directions.

The linguistic discovery — that Allah broke the grammar of organs in this ayah — is the diagnostic image. The thematic discovery — that we go through life with a miscalibrated scale — is the lived reality of that diagnostic image. The tongue eating the ear *is* the scale going off. They are not two failures. They are one failure, described from inside the body and from inside the soul.

### Questions to Carry

1. When was the last time information passed through me without ever pausing in the chamber? What did I do with it?

2. Of all the things in my recent life that felt *hayyin* — light, no big deal, just conversation — which of them might Allah's scale be reading differently?

3. Am I more careful about the sins I *feel* than the sins I *don't feel*? Should it be the other way around?

4. In what specific situations does my tongue habitually eat my ear? What would installing a single deliberate breath cost me?

5. If, tomorrow, I had to give an account to Allah of every piece of information that left my mouth today, would the version I would give Him match the version I gave the world?

### The Distillation

> The same act may sit on two scales — one that you carry and one that Allah keeps — and the deepest danger of the tongue is not what it says, but the chamber it skips on the way to saying it.

### The Closing Invitation

There is a kind of tadabbur that ends in feeling bad about ourselves. That is not what this is. The point of this ayah is not to make you afraid to speak. It is to make you *honored* by the fact that Allah has installed in you a chamber — between intake and release, between hearing and saying — and to recognize that *that chamber is where so much of your relationship with Him happens*. Not in your prayer alone. Not in your fasting alone. In the small, daily, unobserved act of holding a piece of information for one extra second before deciding what to do with it.

You do not need to become silent. You need to become *the holder*. The one in whom information rests for long enough to be weighed, ratified, and only then — if it survives — released.

That is the recovery this ayah is asking for. Not a vow of silence. The recovery of the chamber.

### A Du'a from These Words

> *O Allah, install in us the chamber. Slow our tongues until they remember the ear. Teach our mouths to wait for our hearts. And recalibrate our scales toward Yours, so that what is ʿaẓīm with You does not stay hayyin with us. Forgive what we passed on without weighing, and let us not stand before You with mouths fuller than our knowing. Āmīn.*
