# The Disease of Loving That It Spread

## When Indecency Becomes Entertainment

---

## INTRODUCTION

Listen to the ayah:

[ayah:24:19] إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يُحِبُّونَ أَن تَشِيعَ ٱلْفَـٰحِشَةُ فِى ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

*Inna alladhīna yuḥibbūna an tashī'al-fāḥishatu fī alladhīna āmanū lahum 'adhābun alīmun fī ad-dunyā wal-ākhirah, wallāhu ya'lamu wa antum lā ta'lamūn.*

"Indeed, those who love that indecency should spread among those who have believed will have a painful punishment in this world and the Hereafter. And Allah knows and you do not know."

[PAUSE]

### Rebuilding the World

To feel this ayah, you have to feel what was happening in Madinah when it descended.

A rumor had been started. A whisper about ʿA'ishah, the wife of the Prophet ﷺ. The whisper traveled — through alleyways, into homes, into the marketplace. Some people repeated it. Some people debated it. Some people defended her. But there was a fourth group, and they are the most invisible group.

There was a group of people who never repeated the rumor out loud. Who never wrote anything. Who never publicly declared a position. But who, when they heard the rumor was traveling, **felt something move inside them**. A small, unnamed pleasure. A leaning-in. An *interest* in where the story would go next.

That fourth group is who this ayah is about.

[PAUSE]

We tend to read this surah and think the warning is for the slanderer. The one who started it. The one who repeated it. We picture villains with loud mouths. But Allah, in this verse, sees a different villain — one with a closed mouth and an open heart. A heart that **welcomes** the spread, even when the lips never participate.

### The Hook

There is a verb in this ayah that does not belong to gossip. We expect a verb of speaking, or repeating, or carrying. Allah uses a verb of **affection**. He doesn't say "those who spread." He doesn't say "those who repeat." He says — **those who love that it spread**.

And once you see that verb, the entire psychology of social shame opens up. Because Allah is not legislating speech here. He is diagnosing a sickness inside the chest that has nothing to do with what comes out of the mouth.

Stay with me. This is going to take us somewhere uncomfortable — and necessary.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Yuḥibbūna* — They Love It

Before we touch this verb, let's stop and notice what category of word it is.

In Arabic, there is a verb for nearly every social action. There is *qāla* (he said). There is *naqala* (he transmitted). There is *adhā'a* (he broadcast). There is *takallama* (he spoke). There is *rawā* (he narrated). The Quran uses every one of these verbs in different places when it talks about the movement of words.

Allah does not use any of them here.

He uses **yuḥibbūna** — from the root *ḥ-b-b*, the same root as *ḥubb* (love), *maḥabbah* (affection), *ḥabīb* (beloved). This is not a verb of action. **This is a verb of the heart.**

[PAUSE]

Notice something. *Yuḥibbūna* is in the imperfect tense — *they love*, ongoing, present, continuous. It's not a one-time feeling that visited them. It is a steady state. A disposition. The way someone might continuously love a sport, or a city, or a person. That is the kind of love being described here. **A standing affection toward the spread of disgrace.**

Let's sit with this for a moment, because the implication is staggering.

You can be the kind of person Allah is naming here without ever opening your mouth. You can never repeat a rumor. You can shake your head when others repeat it. You can even say *astaghfirullah*. But if, when you hear that someone fell, **something inside you leans forward to listen** — if you find yourself searching for the next chapter, returning to the post, refreshing the thread, opening the side conversation — then this ayah is talking to you.

The mouth is innocent. The heart is the criminal.

[PAUSE]

Here is what's remarkable. In classical Arabic rhetoric, when you want to maximize warning, you attach the warning not to the act but to the *desire* for the act. Because the act is occasional. The desire is **continuous**. The act is visible. The desire is **hidden**. Allah is going after the place no human authority can reach — the place where you privately enjoy something you would publicly condemn.

What does *yuḥibbūna* reveal? It reveals that Allah's law of speech reaches deeper than speech. It reaches into the **silent applause** of a heart that smiles when someone else falls.

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Tashī'a* — That It Spread

Now look at what they love. They love **an tashī'al-fāḥishah** — that the indecency *spread*.

The verb is *tashī'a*, root *sh-y-'*. Before we open it, let's understand what this root does in Arabic.

*Sh-y-'* describes a particular kind of motion. It is not throwing. It is not pushing. It is not transmitting from one point to another. It is **diffusion**. The way perfume fills a room. The way smoke seeps under a door. The way a color bleeds through fabric. Something that was contained becomes ambient — present everywhere, attributable to no one in particular, impossible to contain again.

The Arabs used this root for things that became *common*, *widespread*, *part of the air*. From this same root comes the word *shā'i'* — meaning "common" or "widespread" — describing news, rumors, customs, anything that has saturated the social atmosphere. *Mushā'* — held in common, undivided, ambient.

So when Allah says *an tashī'a*, He is not describing a rumor that was passed from one specific mouth to one specific ear. He is describing something atmospheric. **A miasma.** A cloud that has filled the community air.

[PAUSE]

And here is what is surgical about Allah's diagnosis: a single rumor only has so many tellers. Maybe ten people repeated it. Maybe fifty. But the *atmosphere* of a rumor — the ambient knowing, the way it sits in the social air, the way everyone has heard it without being able to say where they heard it — that atmosphere is created not by the tellers but by the **listeners who wanted it to keep traveling**.

Diffusion requires a medium. Smoke needs air to spread. A rumor needs hearts that welcome it. **The hearts that welcome it are what give the words their atmosphere.**

This is the genius of *tashī'a*. The verb itself names a phenomenon that cannot exist without an audience that wants the diffusion. You cannot make perfume fill a room with no air in the room. You cannot make a rumor become atmospheric in a community whose hearts refuse the diffusion. The community air **is the hearts of the believers**. Allah, in choosing this verb, is naming the listeners as the medium.

Pause on that. The believer is being told: **you are the air.** What you allow to diffuse through you, diffuses. What you refuse to be a medium for, dissipates.

[PAUSE]

### Element Three: *Fī Alladhīna Āmanū* — Inside the Believers

Now look at where this indecency is spreading. The Arabic says *fī alladhīna āmanū* — *fī* means **in/inside/among**.

Stop. Why is this remarkable?

If you wanted to say that someone was speaking *about* the believers, you would use *'an* (about). If you wanted to say *against* the believers, you would use *'alā*. If you wanted to say *to* the believers, you would use *ilā*. Allah uses **fī** — *inside*.

[PAUSE]

The image being drawn is not of people standing outside the believing community throwing rumors at it. The image is of indecency that has been **placed inside the body** of the believing community. Like a disease placed inside an organism. *Fī* makes the believing community a *container* — a body that has something foreign moving through its bloodstream.

This is why the diagnosis matters. Allah is not warning the believers about external attack. He is warning them about **internal contamination**. The indecency is inside. It is moving through the body. The question is not how to defend the walls. The question is how to clean the bloodstream.

And what cleans the bloodstream? Not law alone. Not punishment alone. **Hearts that refuse to be the medium of diffusion.**

[PAUSE]

Notice also: *alladhīna āmanū*. Allah does not say *fī al-mu'minīn* (in the believers, as a noun-class), which would be the simpler way to say it. He uses *alladhīna āmanū* — *those who believed*. The relative clause makes it a description of action rather than a category of identity. It says: *the people who did the act of believing*. The very people whose hearts said *yes* to Allah are now the soil this indecency is trying to grow in.

The contrast is razor-sharp. Their hearts said *yes* to Allah — *āmanū*. And now another *yes* is being asked of those same hearts — *yuḥibbūna an tashī'a* — *yes* to the spread of disgrace. **The two affections cannot coexist in the same chest.** You cannot say *yes* to belief and *yes* to the diffusion of indecency among the believers. The medium of one cannot be the medium of the other.

This is what makes the warning so heavy. The hearts being warned are believing hearts. Not enemies. Not hypocrites. **Believing hearts that have developed a taste for what believing hearts should reject.**

[PAUSE]

### Bridge: From Words to Reality

We've looked at three things. *Yuḥibbūna* — the verb of love attached to the spread. *Tashī'a* — the verb of atmospheric diffusion. *Fī alladhīna āmanū* — the geography of the disease placed inside the body of the believing community.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the architecture of social shame itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Pleasure Economy of Exposure

Before we open the spiritual side of this, let me describe something you already know in your bones.

Imagine you're scrolling. Your thumb is moving. You pass a recipe. You pass a sermon clip. You pass a meme. And then — a screenshot. A controversy. A name you recognize. Someone has fallen. Someone has been *exposed*.

Watch what your thumb does.

It stops.

It doesn't matter whether you "approve" of the exposure. It doesn't matter whether you tell yourself this is bad. **Your thumb already paid attention.** Your eyes already tracked the post. Some quiet part of your nervous system already leaned in.

That moment — the moment before you decided what you "thought" about it — is the moment Allah is naming.

[PAUSE]

There is a German word: *Schadenfreude*. Pleasure at another's misfortune. English doesn't have one word for it because admitting it requires more honesty than English-speaking culture wants to admit. Arabic, however, has had a word for the disease for fourteen centuries: *yuḥibbūna an tashī'al-fāḥishah*. Not a single word — a phrase. Because the disease is not just pleasure at misfortune. It is pleasure that **the misfortune travels**. Pleasure that the fall is becoming public. Pleasure that more people will see what you have seen.

That is a more specific and more sinister condition than schadenfreude. It is not enough that they fell. The pleasure requires that the falling **be witnessed by many**.

Why does the heart want this? Stay with me — this is the real diagnosis.

When someone falls publicly, their fall does something for the watcher. **It lifts the watcher up by lowering the watched down.** It says, without saying: *whatever I am, I am at least not that*. The diffusion of someone else's indecency creates an atmosphere in which the watcher's own hidden indecencies feel smaller, safer, less dangerous. The crowd has found its sinner. The crowd is watching that sinner. The crowd is not watching me.

This is why the heart loves diffusion. Diffusion is **camouflage**. As long as the air is full of someone else's smoke, my own fire stays hidden.

[PAUSE]

Now hear the ayah again. *Those who love that indecency should spread among those who have believed.* Allah is naming a heart that has discovered a self-soothing mechanism. A heart that uses other people's exposure as a way to feel less afraid of its own. A heart that doesn't actually want the community cleansed — it wants the community **distracted**. As long as the cameras are on someone else, the watcher's own corners stay dark.

This is why the warning is so severe. Because this heart is not just guilty of one act of cruelty toward one person. **This heart has built its entire spiritual hygiene on the public humiliation of others.** Every new scandal is a small act of self-cleansing — *I'm not them, I'm not them, I'm not them.* The fall of the brother becomes the soap of the sister. The exposure of the sister becomes the soap of the brother.

The whole community becomes a system of shame-laundering. Each member secretly relieved when another member falls. Each member quietly hoping the diffusion continues, because diffusion is what keeps them safe.

[PAUSE]

What does this change in how I live tomorrow?

It changes the moment when the screenshot appears. The moment when the gossip starts in the room. The moment when someone says, *did you hear about so-and-so?* — and your body leans forward.

The lean is the test. Not the words. **The lean.**

Practical application: Before you click. Before you ask the next question. Before you let the conversation continue — pause for one breath and ask: *what is the part of me that wants to know more?* If the answer is "concern," concern doesn't need details. If the answer is "responsibility," responsibility doesn't enjoy the discovery. If the answer is anything that *enjoys* — that is the *yuḥibbūna* moment. That is the moment of the ayah.

And in that moment, you have a choice the ayah is offering you. **You can refuse to be the air the rumor needs to travel through.**

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: Punishment in Both Worlds — Why This One

Now look at the punishment. *Lahum 'adhābun alīmun fī ad-dunyā wal-ākhirah.* "They will have a painful punishment in this world and the Hereafter."

Stop and notice something. Most punishments described in the Quran are framed in the next world. *Wa lahum 'adhābun 'aẓīm. Wa lahum 'adhābun alīm. Lahum nār jahannam.* The default time-horizon of Quranic punishment is the akhirah, because that is where final justice lives.

But here, Allah specifies **both**. *Fī ad-dunyā wal-ākhirah.* In this world, *and* the next.

Why does this particular sin get both?

[PAUSE]

Think about what indecency-spreading does. When a rumor diffuses through a believing community, what happens?

Trust between believers collapses. *That family — can they be trusted?* Marriages fracture. Friendships split. Mosques become gossip warehouses. Young people watching this dynamic decide they don't want to belong to a community that turns on its own. The community's ability to do anything together — to raise children together, to defend the weak together, to face hardship together — **erodes from the inside.**

This is the *dunyā* punishment. It is not lightning from the sky. It is the slow rot of the community that loved diffusion. The collapse is **structural**, not theatrical. The community that allowed every fall to become atmospheric finds, eventually, that no fall can be prevented anymore — because nobody trusts anybody enough to lean on each other when temptation comes. Everyone is too afraid of being the next subject of the diffusion.

So the *dunyā* punishment is the loss of the believing community itself. The thing the believers said *yes* to — the *ummah* — slowly becomes uninhabitable for them, because they made it a place of public exposure rather than private repair.

[PAUSE]

Then comes the akhirah punishment. Why both?

Because in this case, the sin has two victims. The person whose indecency was spread suffered a worldly harm — their reputation, their relationships, their psychological safety. So the punishment must include a worldly dimension to balance what was destroyed in the world.

But the spreader also damaged something cosmic. They took a community that Allah was building as a vessel for His remembrance — *fī alladhīna āmanū* — and they used that vessel as a stage for shame. They turned the household of belief into a theater of disgrace. **That damage reaches the akhirah** because what was being damaged was a thing whose final purpose belonged to the akhirah.

Both worlds were damaged. So both worlds receive the answer.

[PAUSE]

There is a tenderness even in this severity. Allah is essentially saying: *I see what you did to the dunyā of these people. I see what you did to the akhirah of My community. I will not let you escape into one and pay only with the other. The crime touched both worlds. The justice will touch both worlds.*

This is what divine justice looks like. It is **proportional in dimension**, not just in weight. A two-world crime gets a two-world response. Nothing is unaccounted for.

[PAUSE]

What does this change in how I live tomorrow?

It changes how I think about the *cost* of one small click. The click that participates in diffusion is not a small thing. It is participating in a sin that Allah has elevated to the rare category of two-world punishment. There is a reason the warning is this heavy. **Because the damage is this real.**

Practical application: When you find yourself about to forward the screenshot, repost the rant, share the exposure post — ask one question. *Am I about to be the air that this needs to travel?* If yes, you are choosing to make yourself a medium for something Allah has named with two-world consequences.

Refuse to be the air. Let the diffusion die in your hands. Let it stop with you.

[PAUSE]

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### Bringing It Together

Look at what we found.

The verb of the ayah is *yuḥibbūna* — a heart-verb, not a mouth-verb. The diagnosis is internal.

The action is *tashī'a* — atmospheric diffusion, which requires a medium. The medium is the listening community.

The location is *fī alladhīna āmanū* — inside the body of the believers. The disease is internal to the organism.

The punishment is *fī ad-dunyā wal-ākhirah* — both worlds. Because the damage was to both worlds.

Put it all together: Allah is describing **a community-level autoimmune disease**. The believing body produces antibodies that attack its own members. Not because external enemies have invaded — but because internal hearts have developed a taste for the failure of their own siblings. And because every member becomes both potential victim and potential medium, the disease cannot be solved by any single member acting in isolation. It can only be solved by **a critical mass of hearts that refuse to be the air**.

The ayah, then, is not just a warning. **It is a recruitment call.** It is asking: will you be the air the disease travels through, or will you be the place where it dies?

[PAUSE]

### The Final Phrase: *Wallāhu ya'lamu wa antum lā ta'lamūn*

Now look at how the ayah closes. *And Allah knows, and you do not know.*

Notice what is missing. There is no object. *Allah knows...* — knows what? *You do not know...* — do not know what?

The Arabic leaves it unspecified. And the unspecified-ness is the meaning.

[PAUSE]

It can mean: *Allah knows the truth of the rumor that is being spread, and you do not.* You don't know if the person you are smiling at is actually guilty. You are participating in atmospheric disgrace built on something only Allah can verify.

It can mean: *Allah knows the consequences of what diffusion does to a community, and you do not.* You don't see the slow rot, the structural collapse, the children walking away from belief because the household of belief became a household of exposure.

It can mean: *Allah knows what is in your heart when you lean forward to hear more, and you do not.* You have not even examined the reflex. You don't know why your thumb stopped. You don't know what the lean is doing inside you. **Allah does.**

It can mean: *Allah knows what punishment is being prepared, and you do not.* The two-world punishment is not theatrical yet. It is being assembled. You will see it. You don't see it now.

The unspecified object holds all four meanings simultaneously. **The point is that everything you would need to know to be the medium of a rumor — you do not have.** Allah does. So let the silence in this final phrase be a silence in your own throat. The thing you don't know is large enough that your speech, your forwarding, your leaning — all of it should stop.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Questions to Carry

1. What is the moment in my day when my thumb stops on someone's exposure? What happens in my body in that pause?

2. When I hear that a person has fallen, am I more interested in their repair or in their *story*? What does the answer reveal about which heart Allah is describing in this ayah?

3. Who in my life knows things about me that, if they loved diffusion, would harm me? Am I being to others what I would want others to be to me?

4. When I forward, repost, or share something exposing about a believer — am I being the *medium* the ayah is naming?

5. The ayah says *Allah knows, and you do not know.* What is the specific thing about the next rumor I'll hear that I do not know — and that should make my mouth and my fingers stop?

### One-Sentence Distillation

To enjoy that another believer's shame is becoming public is a sickness Allah names with the weight of two worlds, because the believing community was meant to be the air that *holds* its members — not the medium that *exposes* them.

### The Closing Invitation

This ayah is not asking you to police others. It is not asking you to confront the spreaders. It is asking you, very personally, to examine the **lean** in your own chest.

The next time exposure-content reaches you — the screenshot, the call-out, the thread, the WhatsApp message — pause for one breath. Notice what your heart does in that breath. If you find affection there — affection for the diffusion, affection for the discovery, affection for the spread — that is the moment the ayah is naming. And in that moment, you have a choice. You can be the air. Or you can be the place where it dies.

Be the place where it dies.

### Du'a

O Allah, You know what we do not know. Cleanse our hearts of any quiet pleasure in the exposure of those who said *yes* to belief alongside us. Do not let us be the air that disgrace travels through. Let our community be a place where each fall is met with cover, not with cameras — and let our chests be the place where every rumor we receive comes to die.

Āmīn.
