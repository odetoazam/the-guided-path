# The God Who Needs Nothing Asks for Your Help

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the verse first — in Arabic, then in sound, then in a plain English that we will spend the next hour taking apart and putting back together.

> يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ كُونُوٓا۟ أَنصَارَ ٱللَّهِ كَمَا قَالَ عِيسَى ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ لِلْحَوَارِيِّۦنَ مَنْ أَنصَارِىٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ ۖ قَالَ ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ نَحْنُ أَنصَارُ ٱللَّهِ

*Yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū kūnū anṣāra-llāhi kamā qāla ʿĪsā-bnu Maryama lil-ḥawāriyyīna man anṣārī ilā-llāh, qāla l-ḥawāriyyūna naḥnu anṣāru-llāh...*

"O you who believe — be helpers of Allah. As Isa, son of Maryam, said to the disciples: 'Who are my helpers toward Allah?' The disciples said: 'We are the helpers of Allah.' Then a group of the Children of Israel believed and a group disbelieved. So We supported those who believed against their enemy, and they became the ones who prevailed."

### Rebuilding the world

To feel this verse, you have to feel the whole surah leaning into it.

Surah As-Saff is short and it is sharp. It is named for a single word — *ṣaff*, the **row**, the battle line, the rank of bodies standing shoulder to shoulder. And from its opening, it is doing something uncomfortable. It turns to the believers — to *us*, the ones who have already said "we believe" — and it asks a question that stings:

**"O you who believe, why do you say what you do not do?"** *(61:2)*

And then, in case we missed it: **"Greatly hateful in the sight of Allah is that you say what you do not do."** *(61:3)*

Picture the original audience. Madinah. A young community that has been declaring its faith loudly, its loyalty, its readiness to give everything — and then, when the moment of real cost arrives, some of those voices grow quiet. The surah names the disease precisely: the distance between the tongue and the hand. Between the word and the weight behind it.

Then the surah does something interesting. It doesn't just scold. It paints a picture of what the cure looks like: **Allah loves those who fight in His path in a row, as though they were a solid structure** — *bunyān marṣūṣ*, a wall whose bricks are poured so tight no gap remains *(61:4)*. It reminds us of Musa, whose own people wounded him even as they knew he was sent to them. It reminds us of Isa, who came with a clear sign and the glad tiding of a messenger to come named Ahmad. And then it offers something astonishing — a **trade**, a *tijārah*, a deal that saves you from a painful punishment: you give your selves and your wealth, and what you receive back is forgiveness and a home in gardens.

And after all of that build-up — the rebuke, the solid row, the prophets, the trade — the surah arrives at its final word. Its last instruction. The thing all of it was driving toward.

This verse.

### The hook

Read the command again, and read it slowly, because something is wrong with it. Something that should stop you.

**"Be helpers of Allah."**

*Helpers.* Of *Allah.*

The same Allah whom the surah **opened** by telling us that everything in the heavens and everything in the earth is glorifying — the Mighty, the Wise, who lacks nothing, who is *al-Ghaniyy*, the Free of all need. That Allah. The One who, with a single word *kun*, brings universes into being.

And He is asking... to be **helped**?

[PAUSE]

By you. By a creature who cannot guarantee its own next heartbeat. What could you possibly add? What hole in the infinite are you being recruited to fill?

Hold that question. Don't answer it yet. Because the entire verse is the answer, and if I hand it to you now, it will mean nothing. Let's earn it.

## PART ONE: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Kūnū* — "Be," not merely "do"

Let me teach you something small about Arabic before we open the word, because it will change what you see.

In any language, there's a difference between a verb of *doing* and a verb of *being*. "Run." "Give." "Fight." Those are actions — things you perform and then stop. But "Be kind." "Be brave." Those are different. They don't ask for an action; they ask for a **state**. They ask you to become a certain kind of thing, so that the actions flow out of you on their own, without anyone commanding each one.

Now, when Allah wants to mobilize this community at the climax of the surah, He has a choice. He could say *unṣurū-llāh* — "Help Allah." A verb. An action. Do the deed of helping.

He does not.

He says: **كُونُوٓا۟** — *kūnū.* From the root *kāf-wāw-nūn*, the root of *being itself*, the root of *kun* — "Be," the very word by which He creates. It is the imperative of existence. And He attaches it not to an action but to an **identity**:

**كُونُوٓا۟ أَنصَارَ ٱللَّهِ** — *kūnū anṣāra-llāh* — "**Be** helpers of Allah."

Let's sit with this. He is not saying "perform some helpful acts for the religion." He is saying: let your very *being* become this. Let "helper of Allah" not be something you do on weekends and at fundraisers, but the category of creature you **are**.

Notice what this quietly answers. Remember the disease the surah diagnosed — saying what you do not do. The gap between tongue and hand. Where does that gap come from? It comes from treating faith as a thing you *say* and a few things you occasionally *do*. The cure is not to add more sayings or more doings. The cure is to change what you **are**, so the saying and the doing stop being separate from you and start being expressions of you.

You don't have to remind a fire to be hot. It doesn't *do* heat as an effortful task. It *is* heat. **Kūnū** is asking for that kind of integration. Be the fire, and the warmth takes care of itself.

[PAUSE]

And here, classical recitation opens a second window. The companions and the early reciters preserved this phrase in two readings — both authentic, both carried across the generations. Some read **أَنصَارَ ٱللَّهِ** — *anṣāra-llāh*, in a possessive construction: "the **helpers of Allah**," His helpers, belonging to His cause. And others — Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Nāfiʿ, as the commentators record — read **أَنصَارًا لِلَّهِ** — *anṣāran lillāh*: "helpers **for** Allah," helpers for His sake.

Don't treat these as competitors where you must pick one. They are two camera angles on one reality. The first reading tells you **whose** you are — you belong to His cause, you are stamped with His name. The second reading tells you **why** you move — everything you do is *for* Him, oriented to Him, not for your name or your tribe or your applause. Put them together and you have the whole of it: *belong to His cause, and act purely for His sake.* The reading the heart needs is both at once.

What does this single word *kūnū* reveal? That Allah is not recruiting your tasks. **He is recruiting your identity.**

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Anṣāra-llāh* — helping the One who needs no help

Now we walk straight into the thing that stopped us at the beginning.

**أَنصَار** — *anṣār.* Root *nūn-ṣād-rā*, *naṣr*: help, aid, the kind of support you give someone in a struggle, someone facing an enemy, someone who could otherwise be overcome. *Nāṣir* is the one who comes to your side when you are pressed. *Anṣār* is the plural — the band of helpers who rally.

And the word that follows is **ٱللَّهِ**. Allah.

Stay with the strangeness. *Naṣr* — the language of aiding someone who might lose — applied to the One who cannot lose. The Self-Sufficient. The One the surah just finished telling us is glorified by everything that exists, who owns the heavens and the earth, who needs no army, no treasury, no allies. Elsewhere He says it flatly: *"If you do not help him, Allah has already helped him"* — and the entire history turned anyway *(9:40)*. And: *"Allah will surely help those who help Him"* — and then, lest anyone misunderstand the direction of the need — *"indeed, Allah is Powerful, Mighty"* *(22:40)*.

So when the King who owns everything turns to the servant who owns nothing and says, *be My helper* — what is actually happening?

Let me give you a picture, because the concept will slip through your fingers without one.

Imagine a father — and let's make him not just wealthy but the founder of everything the family has, every building, every account, every name on every door. He could complete any task in his empire with a phone call. He needs no one. And one morning he finds his small child watching him work, and he kneels down, and he puts a single light box in the child's arms, and he says: "Help me carry this in."

Does the father need the help? The box is nothing to him. The child can barely manage it. If anything, the "help" *slows him down.*

So why does he ask?

[PAUSE]

Because the asking is **the gift.** The child is not being used. The child is being *included.* In that one sentence — "help me carry this" — the father transfers something no inheritance check could ever transfer: *belonging.* A stake. The unspeakable dignity of being told, by the one who needs nothing, *you are part of this with me.* The child walks beside the father now, not behind him. The need was not real. The **need was manufactured out of love**, so that there would be a doorway through which the child could be brought *in.*

That is *anṣāra-llāh.*

Allah does not need your hands. Your help adds nothing to an infinity. The cause does not hang on you — He tells you plainly that if *you* turn away, He will simply bring a people other than you, and they will not be like you *(47:38; 5:54)*. The cause is secured. **The invitation is for your sake, not His.** When He says "be My helper," He is kneeling down — if I may speak that way of the Majestic — and placing the light box in your arms, and giving you, a momentary creature, a *share in the eternal.* He is letting you carry something of the cause that the heavens and the earth glorify. The "need" is a door He built so that you could be brought inside.

Here is what undoes me about it. The Self-Sufficient *did not have to dignify us at all.* He could have run His cause through angels who never tire, through forces of nature, through a word. Instead He framed the whole thing as *needing us* — knowing full well it does not — purely so that we would have the honor of saying yes. The fiction of His need is the most generous thing in the verse.

And notice the word **drums**. Across this verse, *anṣār* appears three times — *anṣāra-llāh*, *anṣārī*, *anṣāru-llāh.* Help, help, help. The Arabic ear cannot escape it. And — let this land — the Muslims of Madinah who first heard this, the ones who had taken in the Prophet ﷺ and his companions, were known by exactly this name: **al-Anṣār**, *the Helpers.* This was not an abstraction to them. It was their own title being traced back to its source — back through Isa's disciples — and revealed to be not a tribal honorific but a **cosmic vocation** that the best of every age had answered.

What does *anṣāra-llāh* reveal? That the most self-sufficient Being in existence invented a way to *need* you — so that He could honor you with belonging.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Element Three: *Ilā* and *ẓāhirīn* — the climbing preposition and the surfacing of truth

Now look at *how* Isa asks, and watch one tiny word.

He says: **مَنْ أَنصَارِىٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ** — *man anṣārī **ilā**-llāh* — "Who are my helpers **toward** Allah?"

*Ilā.* It's a preposition of **direction** — "to," "toward," motion aimed at a destination. Isa does not say "who are my helpers *with* me" or "who will help *me.*" He says: who will be my helpers, *in the direction of Allah.* Who is moving the same way I am moving — toward Him. The question is not "who is on my side?" It is "who is facing where I face?"

And watch the disciples' answer, because they do something beautiful with it. They are asked "who are my helpers *toward* Allah?" — and they could have answered, simply, "we are *your* helpers, O Isa." That would have been natural. Loyal. Personal.

They don't. They answer: **نَحْنُ أَنصَارُ ٱللَّهِ** — *naḥnu anṣāru-llāh* — "We are the **helpers of Allah.**"

[PAUSE]

Do you see what they did? They climbed past the messenger. Isa asked "who helps me toward Allah," and they replied, in effect, *we don't help you reach Allah — we are Allah's own.* They refused to let their devotion terminate on the prophet standing in front of them. They looked through Isa, the way you look through a clear window, straight to the One the window faces. The preposition *climbed* — from helping-toward, to belonging-to. This is the sincerity that gives them their name.

And what *is* their name? **ٱلْحَوَارِيُّونَ** — *al-ḥawāriyyūn.* From the root *ḥā-wāw-rā*, which the lexicographers tie to *ḥawar* — pure, brilliant **whiteness**, the unclouded white of a clear eye. The early commentators preserve two pictures of why they were called this, and both are luminous. One: they were the **pure ones**, Isa's sincere intimates, cleansed and clarified in their devotion. The other, which al-Jalālayn records: they were *fullers* — those whose trade was to **whiten cloth**, to wash the grey and the stain out of fabric until it came back radiant.

Hold both pictures together and you have a definition of what a helper of Allah *is*: someone made pure, and someone who **purifies** — who takes what is stained and works it back toward white. The helpers of Allah are not merely loyal. They are *clean*, and they are in the business of making things clean.

Now follow the verse to its end, because it shows you what becomes of such people. After the division — *a group believed, a group disbelieved* — Allah says:

**فَأَيَّدْنَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ عَلَىٰ عَدُوِّهِمْ فَأَصْبَحُوا۟ ظَٰهِرِينَ**

"So We **supported** those who believed against their enemy, and they became **ẓāhirīn**."

That last word. **ظَٰهِرِينَ** — *ẓāhirīn.* Root *ẓā-hā-rā.* And here is the physical image Allah hands us. *Ẓāhir* is what is **on the surface, visible, on top, manifest** — the opposite of *bāṭin*, what is hidden underneath. A thing that is *ẓāhir* has risen into view, come up, prevailed to the top.

Don't analyze the image — ask what unseen reality it points to. Think about where these believers *started.* A small band. A *ṭāʾifah*, a faction. Outnumbered. The truth they carried was, for a long time, the *bāṭin* thing — buried, pressed down, hidden beneath the weight of the disbelieving majority and the enmity of their foes. And Allah's word for what He did to them is not merely "We made them win." It is: **they became ẓāhirīn** — *they came to the surface.*

[PAUSE]

This is the unseen law dressed in a physical picture. Truth has **buoyancy.** Hold a cork at the bottom of a deep barrel of water and let go — it does not crawl up. It *springs.* It surfaces. Not because the cork is strong, but because of a force in the medium itself that cannot tolerate it staying down. Truth is like that in the medium of reality. It can be held under — for a year, for a generation, for centuries — but the moment the hand of suppression tires, it *surfaces*, because Allah has built the cosmos so that the *bāṭin* truth becomes *ẓāhir.* "Rather, We hurl the truth against falsehood and it crushes its skull, and at once falsehood vanishes" *(21:18)*.

And the commentators tell us *when* this surfacing was completed for the true followers of Isa: it came to its fullness, as al-Muyassar notes, **with the sending of Muhammad ﷺ** — the one Isa himself had foretold in this very surah *(61:6)*. The little believing faction, pressed under for centuries, was finally vindicated and lifted to the surface when the final messenger came and confirmed who, all along, had been telling the truth about the son of Maryam.

What do *ilā* and *ẓāhirīn* reveal together? That those who climb past every intermediary to belong purely to Allah may start buried and outnumbered — and Allah has written into reality itself that, in the end, **they surface.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

### The bridge

Look at what these three words have built. *Kūnū* — He wants your *being*, not your tasks. *Anṣāra-llāh* — He invented a need He does not have, to honor you with belonging. *Ilā* and *ẓāhirīn* — those who answer with pure devotion are written, by the structure of the cosmos, to rise to the surface.

That is the grammar. Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about the two oldest aches in the human chest: *the need to matter*, and *the gap between who we say we are and who we actually are.*

## PART TWO: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The dignity of being asked

Let me begin where you already live.

There is a hunger underneath almost everything a person does, and it is not the hunger for pleasure, and it is not even, finally, the hunger for safety. It is the hunger to **matter.** To be *needed.* To know that if you vanished tomorrow, there would be a hole shaped like you — that your existence is load-bearing somewhere, for someone.

Watch how it drives people. A man stays late at a job that drains him, not for the money, but because there, at least, he is *needed* — they call his name when something breaks. A person posts into the void and refreshes for the little hearts because each one is a tiny vote that says *you registered, you exist, you count.* We are terrified, all of us, of the suspicion that we are **interchangeable** — that the world would close over the space we leave without a ripple. The deepest modern sickness is not sadness. It is the creeping sense of one's own *insignificance.*

And the world's answer to that hunger is always the same, and always a trap. It says: *earn your significance.* Achieve enough, accumulate enough, become impressive enough, and you will finally matter. So people climb, and at the top of every ladder they find the same emptiness, because significance you *earn* is significance you can *lose*, and a worth that depends on your output is a worth that evaporates the day your output does. Ask anyone who has retired, or fallen ill, or simply aged out of usefulness, how solid that kind of mattering turns out to be.

Now hear the verse again, into that exact wound.

**"Be helpers of Allah."**

[PAUSE]

The One who needs *nothing* — who is the only being in existence to whom you could genuinely add nothing — turns to you and frames His cause as though it *needs you.* Sit in the staggering kindness of that. The world makes you audition endlessly for a significance it then makes you pay rent on. Allah, who could run the whole of creation without a single one of us and never feel the absence, **chooses** to extend the invitation: *be part of this with Me.*

And here is why this significance is the only kind that holds. The significance the world offers is real *need* — they need your labor, and the day they don't, you're discarded. The significance Allah offers is the opposite: He does *not* need you, and so His invitation can **never** be withdrawn for lack of usefulness. Think about that carefully. *Because* His asking was never about His need, your place in His cause does not depend on your performance, your health, your productivity, your age. The father didn't hand the child the box because the box needed carrying. He handed it because he wanted the child *beside* him. That doesn't expire.

So the mattering Allah gives you is unconditional in the one way that counts: it was never transactional to begin with. You are not significant because the cause would collapse without you. You are significant because the Owner of the cause *invited* you into it — and an invitation from Him is a permanent change in your status, not a temporary contract pending review.

Connect this to the surface and the depth from before. The world's significance keeps you *bāṭin* — hidden, anxious, always one failure from being forgotten. The significance of being Allah's *anṣār* makes you *ẓāhir* — it lifts you into a permanence that no obscurity here can finally bury, because the One who counts you has already, in front of the angels, written your name into His cause.

**What changes tomorrow?** Tomorrow, when the old ache rises — when you scroll, or compare, or lie awake wondering whether your life adds up to anything — try meeting it with this one sentence instead of with another achievement: *The God who needs nothing asked me to help Him.* Then do one small, unwitnessed thing *for His cause* — a sincere prayer, a quiet act of service, a truth defended when it cost you — and let that act, seen by no one, be your answer to His invitation. Significance located there cannot be taken from you, because it was never about your usefulness in the first place.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Theme Two: From saying to being

Now let me take you to the second ache, and it is the ache this entire surah was built around.

We live in the age of the **declaration.** It has never been easier to *announce* who you are. You can broadcast your values to thousands before breakfast — what you stand for, what you oppose, what kind of person you are. The bio, the repost, the statement, the badge. And there is a quiet trap in all of it, because the human heart is easily fooled into thinking that the *announcement is the thing.* That having *said* you are generous is somehow adjacent to being generous. That having *declared* your faith loudly is somehow a deposit in the same account as living it.

It is not. And the most uncomfortable truth about it is that the declaration can actually *substitute* for the substance — the saying can become a *release valve* that drains off the very energy that should have gone into the doing. You said it, you felt the warm glow of having said it, and the pressure to actually *do* it quietly dissipated.

This is precisely the disease Surah As-Saff opened by naming: **"Why do you say what you do not do? Greatly hateful in the sight of Allah is that you say what you do not do."** And now watch how this final verse *cures* it — not with another rebuke, but with a precedent and a sorting.

First, the cure is built into the grammar we already opened: *kūnū*, **be**, not *qūlū*, say. The whole surah diagnosed a sickness of *saying*, and its final prescription is a verb of *being.* Stop trying to *say* yourself into faith. **Be** it, and let the words become true by being outrun by the deeds.

But then look at what the verse does next, because it is brutally honest about how this actually plays out. The disciples *said* the beautiful thing — "We are the helpers of Allah." A perfect declaration. Flawless. And what immediately follows?

**فَـَٔامَنَت طَّآئِفَةٌ مِّنۢ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ وَكَفَرَت طَّآئِفَةٌ**

"Then a **group** believed, and a **group** disbelieved."

[PAUSE]

A *sorting.* A *ṭāʾifah* and a *ṭāʾifah* — the word means a faction, a band, and its root carries the sense of a group that *moves*, that goes its own way. Two groups, and the verse sets them side by side with a stark symmetry: one *believed*, one *disbelieved.* The declaration was made — and then **reality came and sorted everyone who had been standing in the same crowd.**

This is the deepest mercy hidden in the verse's realism. The saying does not save you, and Allah does not pretend it does. After every grand profession comes the *sorting* — the moment of actual cost, actual difficulty, actual following-through, where the word is weighed against the deed and the two *ṭāʾifahs* separate from what had looked like one undifferentiated mass of "believers." Everyone *said* it. Not everyone *was* it. And it was the doing — the *being* — not the saying, that decided which group you ended up in.

Notice mercifully what the verse does *not* do. It does not say "and they all lived up to it." It tells you the truth: even among those gathered around a true prophet, even after a perfect declaration, a faction fell away. If you have ever declared your faith loudly and then watched yourself fail to live it, this verse is not condemning you from a distance — it is describing the oldest human pattern, the one that ran right through the disciples of Isa, and it is showing you that **the sorting is not the end — it is the mechanism.** It is *how* the real helpers get separated out and lifted up.

Because look where it lands: **"So We supported those who believed... and they became ẓāhirīn."** The reward did not go to those who *said.* It went to *alladhīna āmanū* — those who **believed**, and whose belief was real enough to survive the sorting. And what they received was not just survival but *taʾyīd* — **فَأَيَّدْنَا** — *We supported them*, an intensive backing, the strong hand of Allah placed beneath the faction that had been pressed underneath. The ones who let their being catch up to their saying got *divine reinforcement* poured into the gap where everyone else had only words.

**What changes tomorrow?** Pick one thing you have *said* about yourself — to others or to Allah — that has run ahead of what you actually *do.* One declaration your deeds have not yet caught up to. *I am someone who prays on time. I am patient. I forgive. I am a helper of Allah's cause.* And tomorrow, instead of saying it again, do the smallest concrete version of *being* it — once, quietly, at some real cost. Let the deed take one step toward the word. That single step is you moving, deliberately, into the *ṭāʾifah* that gets supported — and out of the one that only spoke.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture

Now stand back and see what this surah was doing all along, because the structure is the proof.

It **opened** with *tasbīḥ* — everything in the heavens and the earth glorifying Allah *(61:1)* — establishing, before anything else, that He is *al-Ghaniyy*, complete, in need of no one. Then it diagnosed our disease: *saying without doing.* Then it painted the cure: the **solid row**, bricks poured so tight there is no gap — the physical image of a community whose being and doing have fused. Then it walked through the prophets and offered the **saving trade.** And then, as its final word, it gave this verse.

Do you see how the whole surah is one motion? It begins with *Allah needs nothing* and ends with *be His helpers* — and the only way both can be true at once is the truth we uncovered: **the need is manufactured out of love, the invitation is the gift.** The surah opened by securing His self-sufficiency precisely so that, when it closed by asking for our help, we could not possibly mistake it for His need. He set up the first verse so the last verse could only be read as honor.

And inside this final verse, the same architecture in miniature: a **command** to us now (*be helpers of Allah*) — mirrored by a **precedent** from the past (Isa's question, the disciples' answer) — resolved by an **outcome** in history (the sorting, the support, the surfacing). The command is not asking us to invent something new. It is asking us to step into a row that stretches back through the *ḥawāriyyūn* — to take the same name they took, give the same answer they gave, and inherit the same end they were given.

### Bringing it together

The linguistic journey and the two themes are one insight seen from two sides.

*Kūnū* and *anṣāra-llāh* taught us that He wants our **being**, and that His "need" is a doorway built to honor us — and that is **Theme One**: the only significance that holds is the one He invented out of His not-needing us. The climbing *ilā* and the surfacing *ẓāhirīn* taught us that pure devotion, sorted by reality, rises — and that is **Theme Two**: the saying gets sorted, and only the *being* gets lifted.

One sentence binds both: **Allah does not need your help — and that is exactly why being asked to give it is the highest honor you will ever receive, and why the sorting that tests it leads not to your shame but to your surfacing.**

### Questions to carry

- The God who needs nothing asked you to help Him. When was the last time you *felt* the dignity of that — rather than the obligation?
- Where, in your life, has *saying* you believe something quietly replaced *being* it? What is the one declaration your deeds have not caught up to?
- The disciples climbed past Isa to "we are *Allah's* helpers." Is there an intermediary — a person, a group, a reputation — that your devotion secretly terminates on, instead of climbing through it to Him?
- If reality came tomorrow and sorted the *ṭāʾifahs* — the ones who only said from the ones who were — which group would your last week have placed you in?
- What would change if you truly believed that the truth you carry, though buried now, has *buoyancy* — that it is written to surface?

### One-sentence distillation

The God who needs nothing invites you to help Him — not because His cause depends on you, but because being included in it is the unearnable dignity He is offering, and history itself proves that those whose deeds answer the call do not stay buried, but rise.

### The closing invitation

You came to this verse perhaps reading it as a command — a demand laid on you, one more thing required. Let it shift. Read it now as what it is: an *invitation*, extended by the One who could have run all of creation without ever turning your way, kneeling to place a share of the eternal cause into your small, momentary hands. You are not being used. You are being *brought in.* The only question the verse leaves on the table is the one Isa left on the table two thousand years ago — *man anṣārī?* Who are My helpers? — and the disciples have already shown you how short, and how complete, the answer can be.

### A brief du'a

O Allah, You who are *al-Ghaniyy*, in need of nothing — we are stunned that You would ask for our help. Let us not merely *say* we are Yours; make us *be* it, until our deeds outrun our words. And when reality comes to sort the two groups, place us, by Your *taʾyīd*, among those You support and lift to the surface — the helpers of Allah. *Āmīn.*
