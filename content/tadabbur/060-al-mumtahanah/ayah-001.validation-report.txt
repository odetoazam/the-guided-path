ISSUES:
- [MODERATE] "It is roughly the year six after the Hijrah" — The Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿah incident, which the reflection immediately uses as the framing narrative, occurred in year **8 AH** during secret preparations for the conquest of Makkah — not year 6. Year 6 AH is when the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyyah was concluded. The reflection's own subsequent phrase ("the charged interval between the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyyah and the conquest of Makkah") correctly names the period, but then anchors it to the wrong year. A reader will come away with a false date for a key historical event.

- [MODERATE] "the doubled verb of love" — This phrase implies the same verb appears twice. What actually doubles is the **noun** مَوَدَّة, which appears identically in both تُلْقُونَ إِلَيْهِم بِٱلْمَوَدَّةِ and تُسِرُّونَ إِلَيْهِم بِٱلْمَوَدَّةِ. The verbs are distinct (تُلْقُونَ / تُسِرُّونَ). The rhetorical observation the reflection is reaching for — public love vs. secret love, same noun, different verb — is real and valuable, but naming it "doubled verb" is morphologically imprecise and could mislead students of the language.
