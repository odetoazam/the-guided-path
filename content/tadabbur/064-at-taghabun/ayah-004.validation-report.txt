ISSUES:
- [MODERATE] "Allah does not say He knows *what is in* the breasts. He uses a word — *dhāt* — that means something closer to *the companion of the breasts*, *the very intimate of the chest*." — The reflection presents "companion" as the primary, definitional meaning of ذات in this phrase and builds the entire title around it. The four classical tafsir sources provided uniformly render بِذَاتِ الصُّدُورِ as "what is within/contained in the chests": Jalalayn glosses it بما فيها من الأسرار والمعتقدات ("what is in them of secrets and beliefs"); Muyassar بما تضمره الصدور وما تخفيه النفوس ("what the chests contain and what the souls hide"); Qurtubi لا يخفى عليه شيء مما في صدوركم ("nothing in your chests is hidden from Him"). None of these render ذات as "companion." The "companion" reading is a legitimate lexical extension — ذو/ذات does carry the semantic range of "possessor/associate of" — but it is an interpretive choice, not the documented primary gloss. Presenting it as what the word "means" (rather than "can mean" or "scholars have noted") overstates the warrant. It should be framed explicitly as one reading rather than a lexical definition.
