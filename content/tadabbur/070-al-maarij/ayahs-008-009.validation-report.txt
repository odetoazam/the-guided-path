- [MODERATE] "The Day the sky will be like molten copper" (used as definitive translation in the title, header quote, and throughout the body) — *al-muhl* carries significant classical ikhtilāf that the reflection never surfaces. Jalālayn (one of the four cited sources) explicitly glosses it as **ذائب الفضة** (melted *silver*), not copper. Ibn Abbas, cited first by Qurṭubī and reflected in Muyassar and Baghawī, defines it as **دردي الزيت / عكر الزيت** (dregs/sediment of oil) — the dominant classical reading. Ibn Masʿūd offers a third view: **ما أذيب من الرصاص والنحاس والفضة** (what is melted from lead, *copper*, and silver), which is where "copper" comes from, but it is one metal in a trio, not the primary gloss. The reflection presents a single secondary reading as settled fact in the title and translation line, with no acknowledgment that the majority classical position points to oil dregs and that even among the "molten metal" camp, silver is the more common specification.

These alternatives don't undermine the reflection's reading, but presenting the elision analysis as having one clean resolution slightly overstates the grammatical consensus.
