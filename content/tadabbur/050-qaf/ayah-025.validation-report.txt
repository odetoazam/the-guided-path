- [MODERATE] "the doubled nūn makes you hold the sound — the air stops at the roof of the mouth and is blocked there, sustained, before it can release into the long āʿ. The very pronunciation enacts a withholding." — Phonetically inaccurate. Nūn (ن) is an alveolar **nasal** consonant, not a stop. When geminated, the nasal sound is lengthened but airflow continues unobstructed through the nasal passage; there is no complete blockage. The description ("air stops… blocked") correctly characterizes a stop consonant (like tāʾ or dāl) and cannot be applied to nūn. The poetic conclusion — "the word sounds like a closed gate" — cannot be grounded in this phonetic mechanism. The point would need to be reframed as aesthetic/impressionistic rather than articulatory fact.
