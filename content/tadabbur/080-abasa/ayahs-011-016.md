# No — She Is a Reminder

### Surah Abasa, Ayahs 11–16

---

## INTRODUCTION

Let me give you the words first, in Arabic, then in sound, then in plain English.

> **كَلَّآ إِنَّهَا تَذْكِرَةٌ ۝ فَمَن شَآءَ ذَكَرَهُۥ ۝ فِى صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ ۝ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ ۝ بِأَيْدِى سَفَرَةٍ ۝ كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ**

*Kallā, innahā tadhkirah. Fa-man shāʾa dhakarah. Fī ṣuḥufin mukarramah. Marfūʿatin muṭahharah. Bi-aydī safarah. Kirāmin bararah.*

*No! Indeed, it is a reminder — so whoever wills may remember it — in honored pages, exalted and purified, by the hands of envoys, noble and dutiful.*

Before we touch a single word, we have to walk back into the room where this was first heard.

### Rebuild the world

Mecca, in the early years. A man of enormous moral weight — the most truthful, the most generous, the most patient soul ever to walk — is sitting with the leadership of his city. The chiefs of Quraysh. The men whose single conversion could, humanly speaking, crack open the whole tribe and let the message flood through. He is leaning toward them. He is *yearning* for them. Not for himself — for them. He wants them saved.

And into that high-stakes diplomatic moment walks a poor, blind man. ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm, the commentaries tell us. He cannot see whom he is interrupting. He just knows the Messenger ﷺ is near, and he wants more of what only the Messenger can give. *"Teach me. Teach me from what Allah has taught you."* He asks again. And again.

And in that moment — pulled between the man whose heart was already softening toward the truth and the powerful men whose hearts he was straining to reach — the Messenger ﷺ frowned. He turned, slightly, away. Not in cruelty. In the most understandable human calculation in the world: *this one already believes; let me finish with the ones who might.*

Then the heavens opened. Not with reassurance. With **عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ** — *"He frowned and turned away."* Allah Himself narrates the scene. And He narrates it, at first, in the third person. Not *"you frowned."* He frowned. As if even the correction is wrapped in a kind of tenderness, a turning of the face, sparing the direct sting.

That is the world you must stand inside. A scene of the gentlest man in history making the smallest, most human turn of the face — and the Lord of the worlds stopping the heavens to address it.

### The hook

And here is what should keep you awake.

When Allah corrects this scene, He does not say, *"Be kinder to the poor."* He does not say, *"Manners matter."* He says one word — **كَلَّا** — *No.* And then He does something that, when you finally see it, will reorganize how you weigh every human being you will ever meet.

He lifts your eyes off the frown on the ground and points them upward — all the way up — to who is *really* holding the thing the blind man was begging for. [PAUSE]

Stay with that question as we begin: *whose hands is this revelation actually in?* Because the answer is going to invert the entire room.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: كَلَّا — the word that stops the world

Let me teach you the concept before we open the word.

In every language there are two kinds of "no." There is the *no* of information — *"Is it raining? No."* Flat. Factual. And there is the *no* of **arrest** — the *no* a parent says when a child's hand moves toward a flame. That second *no* is not answering a question. It is stopping an action mid-motion. It is grabbing the wrist.

Arabic has a dedicated word for that second kind. **كَلَّا** (*kallā*). The grammarians and the mufassirūn have a precise term for what it does. Al-Qurṭubī says it plainly: *كلا كلمة ردع وزجر* — **"*kallā* is a word of *radʿ* and *zajr*"** — of *repelling* and *rebuke*. Al-Baghawī, even more compressed, says simply: *"كلا، زجر"* — ***"kallā* — a rebuke."**

This is not the *no* of conversation. This is the *no* that seizes the wrist before the hand reaches the fire.

Now — open the word.

The surah has shown us the scene. The frown. The turning. The leaning-toward and the turning-away. And the first word of response from the Throne is:

> **كَلَّا**

Let's sit with this. Notice what Allah did *not* say. He did not say, "That was a small thing." He did not say, "Your intention was good, so it's fine" — even though, as Al-Qurṭubī carefully records, the scholars understood this was *tark al-awlā*, the leaving of *what was more excellent*, and "if it were carried as a minor lapse, that would not be far-fetched," as al-Qushayrī put it. The act was not a sin in the way you and I sin. And *still* — **kallā**. The wrist is still seized. Because the One correcting him loves him too much to let even the smallest turning-away from a sincere seeker pass without notice.

Here is what is remarkable. Think about who is on the receiving end of this *kallā*. The most beloved human being to Allah. And the rebuke does not diminish him — it *honors* him. Because **you do not grab the wrist of someone you have given up on.** The *kallā* is not Allah's disappointment. It is Allah's investment. It is the proof that this Messenger is held to the highest standard precisely *because* he is the highest soul.

And notice the direction of the correction. The world says: *attend to the powerful; the poor man can wait.* The *kallā* says: *No. Not like that. Never like that again.* In one syllable, the social physics of Mecca — and of every room you have ever walked into — is overturned.

**One word — *kallā* — and the entire ranking of human worth is declared void.** [PAUSE]

### Element Two: تَذْكِرَة — and the difference between *being reminded* and *remembering*

Now teach the concept first.

In Arabic, verbs come in *forms* — patterns stamped onto a root that change what the action *does*. Take a root and leave it bare, in what scholars call Form I, and you get the simple action. Put it through Form II — typically by doubling the middle letter — and the action usually becomes *intensive* or *causative*: not just to do a thing, but to *cause* it, to do it thoroughly, to do it again and again *to someone*.

Hold that. It is about to do real work.

After the *kallā*, Allah says **إِنَّهَا تَذْكِرَةٌ** — *"Indeed, it is a tadhkirah."* The four commentaries are in quiet agreement on the meaning: al-Jalālayn calls it *ʿiẓatun lil-khalq* — **an admonition for all creation.** Al-Baghawī: *mawʿiẓatun wa tadhkīrun lil-khalq.* A reminder. A summons to remember.

The word **تَذْكِرَة** comes from the root *dh-k-r* — to remember, to mention, to bring to mind — and it sits on the **Form II** pattern. This is the one piece of morphology the corpus confirms for us with certainty, so let us lean our whole weight on it. *Tadhkirah* is not the bare act of remembering. It is the **intensive, causative** noun: *the thing that does the reminding to you.* The active agent of remembrance. Something is being done *to* the human heart here. Allah is the one *causing* the remembering.

Now look at the very next ayah:

> [ayah:80:12] فَمَن شَآءَ ذَكَرَهُۥ

*"So whoever wills — *dhakarah* — remembers it."*

And here the same root drops back to its bare, simple form. **ذَكَرَ** — Form I. Just... remembering. The plain, unadorned act.

Do you see what just happened across two ayahs? [PAUSE]

**Allah does the heavy lifting.** *Tadhkirah* — the intensive, causative reminding — is *His* work, His revelation, His act upon you. And what is asked of *you*? Only **ذَكَرَ** — the simple, bare turn of the heart toward what is already there. He carries the mountain to your door. You only have to open the door.

Stay with this, because it dismantles a lie we tell ourselves. We imagine that drawing near to Allah requires us to *generate* something enormous — vast effort, vast feeling, vast worthiness. But the grammar says otherwise. The enormous thing — the *tadhkirah* — has already been built, intensified, perfected, and *sent*. Your part is the small verb. *Fa-man shāʾa dhakarah.* Whoever wills, remembers.

And notice the absence — read it as carefully as the presence. There is no condition attached to *man shāʾa*. Not "whoever is rich." Not "whoever is powerful." Not "whoever the Prophet has time for." **Whoever wills.** The blind man who was waved aside is, by this single open clause, given the exact same access as the chiefs of Quraysh. The door has no doorman. [PAUSE]

### Element Three: the ascending chain — مُكَرَّمَةٍ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ ... سَفَرَةٍ كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ

One more concept before we open these.

When a writer wants you to *feel* something rising, he does not tell you "this is exalted." He builds a staircase of words and makes you climb it. Each step a little higher than the last. By the time you reach the top, you are not informed that you have ascended — you have *ascended*, in your own breath, in your own mouth.

Now listen to what Allah does with the *ṣuḥuf* — the pages, the sheets, on which this reminder is inscribed. Read it aloud and feel the staircase:

> **فِي صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ • مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ • بِأَيْدِي سَفَرَةٍ • كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ**

Six descriptions, and every single one ends in that soft, rolling *-rah* sound. *Mukarramah. Marfūʿah. Muṭahharah. Safarah. Kirām. Bararah.* The repeated **ر** rolls forward like a current, lifting and lifting. The sound *itself* rises — which is the very meaning sitting in the middle of it: **مَّرْفُوعَة**, *raised, exalted.* The form mirrors the meaning. You can hear the ascent before you understand it.

Now climb the steps one at a time.

**مُّكَرَّمَة** (*mukarramah*) — from *k-r-m*, honor, nobility. And note the form: it is the doubled, intensive pattern again. Not merely honored — *made deeply, thoroughly honored.* Ennobled.

**مَّرْفُوعَة** (*marfūʿah*) — from *r-f-ʿ*, to raise. Elevated. Lifted high above. Held aloft.

**مُّطَهَّرَة** (*muṭahharah*) — from *ṭ-h-r*, purity. And again the intensive form: *thoroughly purified.* Al-Muyassar unfolds it beautifully — *muṭahharatun min ad-danas wa az-ziyādati wa an-naqṣ* — **purified from filth, from any addition, from any subtraction.** Untouched. Untampered. Pristine.

Now we reach the hands that hold these pages — and watch how the staircase climbs from the *object* to the *bearers*:

[ayah:80:15] بِأَيْدِى سَفَرَةٍ

And then the final two steps, the summit:

**كِرَامٍ** (*kirām*) — noble. Back to *k-r-m* again — the honor that began with the pages now crowns their carriers.

**بَرَرَةٍ** (*bararah*) — from *b-r-r*, the root of *birr*, the deepest, most active goodness; the same root in *barr*, devoted, dutiful. Al-Muyassar: *akhlāquhum wa afʿāluhum bārratun ṭāhirah* — **their characters and their deeds are devoted and pure.** Not merely good — *dutiful*, righteous in action, faithful in office.

Now stand back and see the whole staircase at once. Honored — exalted — purified — in the hands of envoys — noble — dutiful. [PAUSE]

### The bridge

Look at what these three discoveries have built together.

A single word, *kallā*, stopped the scene and voided the world's ranking of worth.

A single root, doubled then bared, showed that *He* does the reminding and we need only the small turn of remembering — and that the door is open to *whoever wills*.

And a rising staircase of six words lifted our gaze off the frown and carried it up to the noblest hands in existence.

Now let us ask what all of this reveals — not just about words, but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: Who decides what a person is worth

Begin where you actually live.

You have walked into a room and, in less than a second, ranked everyone in it. You did not choose to. Your nervous system did it for you — sorting by clothing, by accent, by confidence, by usefulness, by whether this person can do anything *for* you. We all carry an invisible scale in our chests, and it is constantly weighing the people around us: *this one matters more, this one can wait.* It is the most human machinery there is. It runs in boardrooms and at family gatherings and in the masjid parking lot.

Now watch the Quran walk straight into that machinery — in the most exposed place imaginable.

The man whose scale tips, in this scene, is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. And his calculation is not vanity. It is *strategic compassion*. The chiefs of Quraysh are leverage; the believing blind man is, in worldly terms, already secured. Any one of us would have made the same turn. It is, by every earthly metric, the *correct* move.

And the heavens say **كَلَّا**. *No.*

Let that land. It is not the corrupt version of the worth-scale that gets corrected here. It is the *most defensible* version. Which means the correction goes all the way down. Allah is not saying "don't be a snob." He is saying something far more radical: **the scale itself is not yours to hold.** You do not have the instrument to measure a human being's worth. The blind man you wave aside may be, in the sight of the One who actually weighs, heavier than the entire assembly of chiefs.

And here is the movement that should break the scale in your chest. Recall where Allah points after the *kallā*. He does not say, *"that poor man is precious."* He goes higher. He shows you the *revelation itself* — the very thing the poor man was reaching for — and reveals whose hands it is in: *kirāmin bararah*, the noble, dutiful angels. The thing the chiefs were too important to receive is held aloft by the noblest beings in creation. [PAUSE]

So the inversion is total. The downward gaze of *ʿabasa wa tawallā* — the frown, the turning-*down*-and-*away* — is answered by *marfūʿah*, the lifting *up*. The man who was looked down upon was reaching for the thing the angels are honored to carry.

What does this change about tomorrow? Tomorrow you will walk into a room and the scale will start weighing. You cannot stop it from starting. But now you have a *kallā* of your own to deploy. When you feel yourself leaning toward the impressive one and away from the overlooked one — the *kallā* is yours to say, inwardly, to your own nafs. *No. Not like that. I do not hold the scale.* And the practical test is brutally simple: **the next time the "unimportant" person seeks your attention — the one with nothing to offer you — give them the face you were saving for someone more useful.** That is the whole correction, lived.

### Theme Two: The treasure you already own, and forget to open

Here is a strange, specific kind of human grief.

It is the grief of discovering, too late, that you *had* the thing all along. The letter that sat unopened in the drawer. The number you never called. The water you were carrying while dying of thirst. There is a particular ache reserved for the realization: *it was within reach the whole time, and I simply did not turn toward it.*

Now hold the second ayah of our passage against that ache:

> **فَمَن شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ** — *"So whoever wills, remembers it."*

We tend to read our distance from Allah as a problem of *supply.* As if the reminder is far, locked, rationed, hard to obtain. The grammar we uncovered says the opposite. The *tadhkirah* — the intensive, causative reminder — has already been built and sent. The supply is infinite and the door has no lock. The entire bottleneck is one small Form I verb on *our* side: *dhakara.* The turning.

And then — *fī ṣuḥufin mukarramah* — Allah tells you exactly *what* it is you are neglecting to open. Not a triviality. Not a disposable note. It is inscribed in pages *honored, exalted, purified*, carried in the hands of *noble, dutiful envoys.* This is the cosmic valuation of the Book you have, perhaps, sitting on a shelf above your head, beautiful and closed.

Sit with the asymmetry. In the highest realm, the angels — *kirāmin bararah* — hold this revelation with a reverence we can barely imagine; al-Muyassar says they are *the noblest of creation*, their every character and deed pure. They handle it as something *exalted.* And down here? We let it gather dust between recitations. The *same words.* Treated as a treasure by beings of light, and as background by the ones it was actually sent to save.

Let the paradox stand without rushing it: the thing most honored in the heavens is among the things most neglected on the earth. [PAUSE]

But notice — and this is mercy — the verse does not shame you for the neglect. It does not say "whoever fails to remember is doomed." It says **فَمَن شَاءَ** — *whoever wills.* The whole weight is placed gently on a single act of the will. Not capacity. Not worthiness. Not eloquence. Will. The blind man *willed* it — *"teach me from what Allah taught you"* — and that act of will placed him, in the sight of Allah, above the men who could see perfectly and turned away.

What does this change about tomorrow? It relocates the problem. You will be tempted, as you always are, to think your spiritual distance is about *not having enough* — not enough knowledge, not enough time, not enough feeling. *Kallā.* You have the *tadhkirah.* What you have not yet done is the small thing: *dhakara* — turn toward it, today, with intention. The most concrete application I can give you is this: **open it before you feel ready.** Do not wait to *become* worthy of the pages the angels carry. *Fa-man shāʾa* — the willing *is* the worthiness. Open the drawer. The letter has been waiting.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture revealed

Now that we have done the work, look back at the shape of these six ayahs, because the structure is itself the argument.

The surah began on the ground, with a downward, inward motion: *ʿabasa wa tawallā* — a frown, a turning away. The human face contracting and turning *down.*

Then: **كَلَّا.** The arrest. The pivot.

And from that pivot, watch the camera *rise*, word by word, never once descending again:

- **تَذْكِرَة** — a reminder (the thing itself)
- **فَمَن شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ** — open to every will (the door)
- **صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ** — honored pages (the first step up)
- **مَّرْفُوعَةٍ** — *raised* (the ascent named outright)
- **مُّطَهَّرَةٍ** — purified (higher still)
- **بِأَيْدِي سَفَرَةٍ** — in the hands of envoys (off the earth entirely)
[ayah:80:16] كِرَامٍۭ بَرَرَةٍ

The structure is a *rescue elevation.* A frown on the ground, and then a single word lifts the whole scene clear off the earth and carries it up to the angels. The passage *enacts* the very inversion it teaches: what was looked down upon is lifted highest. The sound rises (all those rolling *-rah* endings), the meaning rises (*marfūʿah*), the realm rises (earth to heaven), and the worth rises (the waved-away seeker to the honored Book). [PAUSE]

### Bringing it together

The linguistic discoveries and the two themes are one insight seen from two sides.

*Kallā* and the inverted scale are the same truth: **you do not hold the instrument that measures a soul.**

*Tadhkirah* (Form II, His act) and *dhakara* (Form I, your act) and the open clause *man shāʾa* are the same truth as the unopened treasure: **everything heavy has already been done for you; what remains is small, and it is yours to will.**

And the rising staircase ties both together: the One who voids your scale is the same One who has placed, within arm's reach, in the most honored hands in the cosmos, the very thing that will recalibrate you — *if you will only turn toward it.*

### Questions to carry

1. Whose face have I been saving — and whom have I been giving the leftover of my attention?
2. When I feel far from Allah, am I actually short of *supply* — or short of *willing*?
3. If the angels hold these words as *exalted and purified*, what does the way I handle them say about what I truly believe they are?
4. The Prophet ﷺ was corrected for the gentlest turning-away. What turnings-away in me have I never even noticed enough to repent for?
5. Who is the Ibn Umm Maktūm in my life right now — the one with nothing to offer me, reaching for what I could give?

### One-sentence distillation

**No — stop weighing souls by the world's scale: the reminder you already hold is honored in the highest hands of heaven, and all that is asked of you is the small, willing turn to open it.**

### The closing invitation

Let something shift, gently. Not a resolution to be a better person in the abstract. Something specific. Let the next overlooked person you meet receive your full face. And let the Book above your shelf — the one the *kirāmin bararah* carry with reverence — be opened tonight, before you feel worthy of it. *Fa-man shāʾa.* You are the *whoever.* The willing is the door.

### A brief du'a

*O Allah, You who said* **كَلَّا** *to the most beloved of creation out of love — say it to our hearts whenever they lean toward the powerful and away from the poor. You who placed Your reminder in honored, exalted, purified hands — make us among those who* **شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ***, who willed and remembered. Do not let us be the ones who carried the treasure and never opened it. Āmīn.*
