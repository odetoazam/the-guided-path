# Proclaim What You Are Commanded; We Are Sufficient for You Against the Mockers

## Surah Al-Hijr (15:94-96)

[ayah:15:94] فَٱصْدَعْ بِمَا تُؤْمَرُ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ

[ayah:15:95] إِنَّا كَفَيْنَـٰكَ ٱلْمُسْتَهْزِءِينَ

[ayah:15:96] ٱلَّذِينَ يَجْعَلُونَ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَـٰهًا ءَاخَرَ ۚ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

*Fa-sda' bima tu'maru wa a'rid 'ani al-mushrikin.*

*Inna kafaynaka al-mustahzi'in.*

*Alladhina yaj'aluna ma'a Allahi ilahan akhar fa-sawfa ya'lamun.*

**So proclaim what you are commanded and turn away from the polytheists. Indeed, We are sufficient for you against the mockers, those who set up another god with Allah. They will soon know.**

[PAUSE]

## Introduction

There is a point at which carrying the truth quietly is no longer what obedience requires.

The heart would prefer to remain careful, measured, strategic, less exposed. But revelation reaches a moment when caution itself can become disobedience, and the messenger must step into the public weight of saying what Allah commanded him to say.

[PAUSE]

That is the threshold of this passage. The surah has named the Quran, exposed those who fracture it, and sworn that they will be questioned. Now the Prophet is not left in suspense. He is told exactly what to do: proclaim openly what you have been commanded. Turn away from the polytheists. We are sufficient for you against the mockers.

The hook of the passage is this: **Al-Hijr teaches that open proclamation of truth becomes possible when the servant stops making the mockers the axis of his emotional world and learns to move under Allah's sufficiency instead.**

## Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

### 1. **Fa-Sda'**: Come Out Into the Open With What You Have Been Commanded

The command begins:

**fa-sda' bima tu'mar**

So proclaim what you are commanded.

[PAUSE]

The classical commentators are united on what this command actually asks of the Prophet: open, undiluted, public proclamation. Ibn 'Abbas glossed **fa-sda'** simply as *"go ahead with it"* — carry it out, deliver it to your people and to everyone you were sent to. Another report renders it plainly as *"proclaim what you have been commanded,"* and Mujahid understood it as reciting the Qur'an aloud in prayer. Al-Muyassar captures the sense directly: *proclaim the call to truth aloud.* The heart of the word is that the message is to be brought fully into the open, not kept sealed inside private caution.

The root **s-d-'** itself carries the physical sense of splitting or cleaving apart — al-Tabari preserves this in an old line of poetry where *yasda'* means to divide and distribute. His own conclusion joins the two: *"proceed, and make the distinction"* (*imdi wa-fruq*). That literal resonance sharpens the force of the command. The truth is not to be murmured; it is to be delivered with a clarity that separates it out and lets it stand. But the load-bearing meaning — what the ayah is asking — is bold public proclamation. The splitting imagery only deepens why that proclamation must be so unmistakable.

[PAUSE]

And the phrase **bima tu'mar** matters just as much. He is not told to proclaim himself, his preferences, or a self-authored message. He is told to proclaim *what he has been commanded.* The courage of da'wah is grounded in the fact that the truth is not ours to invent or soften beyond recognition.

### 2. **Wa A'rid 'Ani al-Mushrikin**: Do Not Let Opposition Set Your Rhythm

Then:

**wa a'rid 'ani al-mushrikin**

And turn away from the polytheists.

[PAUSE]

This does not cancel proclamation. It protects proclamation from being swallowed by reactive entanglement.

The root of **a'rid** signals turning aside, not giving oneself over to fruitless orbit. Al-Muyassar draws out the emotional freedom in it: *do not concern yourself with the idolaters — Allah has cleared you of what they say.* The absolving is already done. The Messenger is not to become the manager of their hostility; his standing is settled by Allah, not by their reception. He is to deliver what he must deliver, but not let their resistance dictate the internal posture of his mission.

Many sincere people lose strength not because they lack truth, but because they become over-occupied with the people resisting truth. The ayah breaks that orbit.

### 3. **Inna Kafaynaka al-Mustahzi'in**: Allah Does Not Always Remove Mockery, but He Does Take Responsibility for It

Then comes one of the surah's most intimate assurances:

**inna kafaynaka al-mustahzi'in**

Indeed, We are sufficient for you against the mockers.

[PAUSE]

The verb **kafayna** from **k-f-y** is enoughness, sufficiency, adequacy. Allah does not first say, "They are harmless," or "You will not feel this." He says: We are enough for you regarding them. Al-Tabari draws the practical consequence directly out of this word: *so proclaim the command of Allah, and fear nothing but Allah — for the One who sufficed you against the mockers will suffice you against anyone who opposes or harms you.* The promise of sufficiency is meant to become fearlessness.

This was not an abstract reassurance. Al-Tabari, on the authority of Ibn Ishaq from 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, records that the ringleaders of the mockers were a known handful of Quraysh's most senior and honored men — figures like al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib Abu Zam'a among them. These were not anonymous hecklers; they were named men of standing whose ridicule carried social weight. And the classical narrative is that Allah Himself dealt with each of them, so that the Prophet was spared having to contend with them at all. That is the texture of *kafaynaka*: a concrete, historical sufficiency, not merely a feeling of comfort.

And who are these mockers, at the root? The next ayah names them as people of shirk:

**alladhina yaj'aluna ma'a Allahi ilahan akhar**

those who set up another god with Allah.

This is crucial. Their mockery is not just social rudeness. It emerges from a theological corruption. Once the heart is comfortable assigning rivals beside Allah, it becomes unsurprising that it also treats Allah's messenger with ridicule. And the passage closes on their reckoning — **fa-sawfa ya'lamun**, *they will soon know.* Al-Muyassar fills in the object of that knowing: they will come to know the outcome of what they did, in this world and in the next. The threat is quiet but total.

## Part 2: The Thematic Depths

### Theme 1: Mockery Gains Power When We Start Living Before It

One reason ridicule wounds so deeply is that people begin imagining their whole obedience in front of hostile eyes.

How will this sound?

How will this be received?

What will people say?

How much reputation will this cost?

[PAUSE]

Once that gaze settles over the heart, proclamation shrinks. A person starts editing truth, delaying truth, softening truth into something that no longer breaks open anything.

But this passage relocates the believer's field of vision. Proclaim what you are commanded. Turn away from the polytheists. We are sufficient for you against the mockers. In other words: do not stand before them while obeying Me. Stand before Me while they speak.

That is a whole liberation. The mockers are real — real enough that the tradition remembers their names — but they are demoted. They are no longer the court before which faithfulness is performed.

### Theme 2: Tawhid Produces Courage Because It Ends the Illusion of Rival Powers

The mockers are described as those who make with Allah another god.

That is not incidental description. It reveals the deep structure of fear. People lose courage when the world fills up with rival powers whose reactions feel decisive: audience, boss, crowd, elite, state, family, platform, market, reputation.

[PAUSE]

But tawhid keeps collapsing these false ultimates. If Allah is enough, then no created reaction can finally decide the value of obedience. This is exactly why al-Tabari reads *kafaynaka* as grounds to *fear nothing but Allah:* the One who is sufficient against the mockers is sufficient against every other power that presents itself as decisive. This does not remove pain, exposure, or slander. It removes their god-status.

That is why the ayah can combine public proclamation with interior steadiness. The servant does not become brave because mockery disappears. He becomes brave because shirk has been unmasked and sufficiency has been reassigned to Allah.

[PAUSE]

One way to sit with this passage is to ask whose reaction has become large enough in your life to make you hesitant about an obedience that is already clear. What truth are you still carrying privately because you are living too much before the mockers and too little before the One who said, "We are sufficient for you"?

## Closing Synthesis

Allah commands His Messenger to proclaim openly what he has been commanded, to turn aside from the polytheists, assures him that He is sufficient against the mockers — a sufficiency the tradition remembers as a concrete deliverance from named men of Quraysh — then identifies those mockers by the deeper crime from which their ridicule grows: setting up another god with Allah. And He closes with their unhurried reckoning: they will soon know.

[PAUSE]

The sequence is exact and healing: proclaim, do not orbit, trust sufficiency, remember tawhid. The heart becomes able to speak openly when it stops treating human reactions as rival lords.

### Questions to Carry

- What obedience have you delayed because you are imagining it too intensely before other people's reactions?
- Where has mockery, dismissal, or anticipated embarrassment made you carry truth too privately?
- Do you really believe Allah can be sufficient for you against the people whose opinions most unsettle you?
- Which created powers have quietly taken on god-size weight in your emotional world?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**This passage teaches that truth is proclaimed with courage when the servant stops revolving around the mockers and starts moving under Allah's enoughness.**

### Du'a

*Allahumma, make us people who proclaim what You command without turning Your truth into something timid and hidden. Free our hearts from captivity to mockery, make tawhid larger than every audience, and let Your sufficiency steady us whenever obedience becomes exposed.*
