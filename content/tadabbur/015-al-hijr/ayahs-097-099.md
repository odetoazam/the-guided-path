# We Know Your Chest Tightens, So Glorify, Prostrate, and Worship Until the Certainty Comes

## Surah Al-Hijr (15:97-99)

[ayah:15:97] وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ يَضِيقُ صَدْرُكَ بِمَا يَقُولُونَ

[ayah:15:98] فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّـٰجِدِينَ

[ayah:15:99] وَٱعْبُدْ رَبَّكَ حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَكَ ٱلْيَقِينُ

*Wa la-qad na'lamu annaka yadiqu sadruka bima yaqulun.*

*Fa-sabbih bi-hamdi rabbika wa kun mina al-sajidin.*

*Wa'bud rabbaka hatta ya'tiyaka al-yaqin.*

**And We certainly know that your chest tightens because of what they say. So glorify with praise of your Lord and be among those who prostrate. And worship your Lord until the certainty comes to you.**

[PAUSE]

## Introduction

The surah does not end by telling the Prophet to become less human.

It does not say: their words should not touch you.

It does not say: if your tawakkul were stronger, you would feel nothing.

[PAUSE]

Instead Allah names the pain with exquisite mercy. We know your chest tightens because of what they say.

That recognition alone is tenderness. The hurt is neither denied nor mocked. But the passage does not leave the Messenger inside constriction. It gives him a way through it: tasbih, praise, sujud, and continuous worship until certainty arrives.

And notice what is not offered. No promise that the mockery will stop. No timetable. What Allah gives instead is something to *do* with the pressure, and a horizon long enough to hold a whole life.

The hook of the passage is this: **Al-Hijr teaches that constriction of the chest is not healed by arguing endlessly with the voices that caused it, but by turning the burden into worship until the heart is reopened before Allah.**

## Part 1: The Linguistic Journey

### 1. **Yadiqu Sadruka**: The Quran Names Spiritual Pain as Constriction

Allah says:

**wa la-qad na'lamu annaka yadiqu sadruka bima yaqulun**

We certainly know that your chest tightens because of what they say.

[PAUSE]

The expression is embodied. **Yadiqu** from the root of narrowness and constriction. **Sadr**: the chest, the inner front of the self, the place where breath, courage, patience, and emotional bearing are felt.

This is not abstract sadness. It is compression. Reduced space. Difficulty carrying what one is carrying.

The commentators reach for the same physical picture when they explain it. Al-Tabari says that what they say *hemmed him in* — he uses a second word of narrowness, from the root that gives us *haraj*: the state of being boxed in with no room to move and no way out. Al-Muyassar describes it as *inqibad al-sadr*, the chest drawing inward, folding in on itself. Three different words, one experience: less space inside than the situation requires.

And the commentators are also specific about what "what they say" means. Al-Tabari names two things: *takdhib*, calling him a liar, and *istihza'*, making fun of him. And he notes the two targets — they aimed at the Messenger himself, and at what he had brought them. That double aim is part of why the words landed so heavily. A man can absorb an insult to his own name. It is harder when the thing being laughed at is the trust he was given to carry.

And notice the tenderness before the diagnosis: **na'lamu**. We know. Allah begins by informing the Messenger that his interior state is fully seen. The one carrying revelation is not unseen by the Lord who sent it.

Notice too that the verb is in the present tense, not the past. Not *we knew*, once. **Na'lamu** — we know, continuously, as it is happening. The knowledge is not a report filed after the fact. It is company kept during it.

### 2. **Fa-Sabbih bi-Hamdi Rabbika**: Praise Is Given as an Exit From Pressure

Then the ayah turns immediately:

**fa-sabbih bi-hamdi rabbika**

So glorify with praise of your Lord.

[PAUSE]

The command is not random consolation. **Tasbih** clears Allah of every deficiency. **Hamd** fills the tongue and heart with grateful praise. Together they reposition the servant inside a truer reality.

The constricted self becomes trapped inside what people said. Tasbih breaks that enclosure. It returns the heart to the Lord who is untouched by their distortions, who remains perfect, praiseworthy, and sovereign even while the servant feels pressed by human speech.

The **fa-** at the front of the command is worth pausing over. It is the particle of immediate consequence: *and so*, *therefore*. The tightening is stated, and the response follows straight out of it with nothing in between. No interval is left for the wound to settle in.

Al-Tabari's paraphrase of the command is striking. He does not say "then praise Him." He uses a verb of alarm: *faz'* — when something you hate comes at you from them, **bolt to** gratitude, to praise of Him, to the prayer. It is the word you would use for someone running from danger toward safety. And he anchors it in the Prophet's own habit: when a matter weighed heavily on him, he took refuge in prayer. So the command is not asking the Messenger to feel something new. It is telling him where to run.

Al-Tabari also attaches a promise to it that the ayah leaves implicit: do this, and *Allah will suffice you against the thing that is worrying you*. Al-Muyassar says the same — take refuge in your Lord when your chest tightens, and that will be enough for you against what weighs on you. The relief is not promised as a change in the mockers. It is promised as sufficiency for the one being mocked.

### 3. **Kun mina al-Sajidin ... Hatta Ya'tiyaka al-Yaqin**: Bowing Low Is the Long Road to Stability

Then:

**wa kun mina al-sajidin**

Be among those who prostrate.

And finally:

**wa'bud rabbaka hatta ya'tiyaka al-yaqin**

Worship your Lord until the certainty comes to you.

[PAUSE]

Sujud is the body's deepest enacted humility. The face goes down, the ego lowers, the servant comes nearest in helplessness and devotion.

And the wording is not "prostrate." It is **be among those who prostrate** — join the company, take your place inside a group that is already doing this. The command is about belonging, not about one act. The classical commentators read *al-sajidin* here as those who pray: al-Muyassar glosses it as "be among those who pray to Allah and worship Him," letting the prostration stand for the whole prayer it belongs to. Which fits al-Tabari's picture exactly. The place you bolt to when the chest tightens is the prayer.

Then the horizon widens all the way to **al-yaqin**.

[PAUSE]

This is the word that has been misused, so it is worth being exact. Some have suggested that *al-yaqin* names a spiritual rank — that once a worshipper reaches certainty, the obligation of worship falls away. The classical commentary leaves no opening for that reading.

Al-Tabari states it plainly: worship your Lord **until death comes to you**. He then cites the chain back to Salim ibn Abdullah, who said of this very phrase, in one word: *death*. And he adds that the people of interpretation said the same. Al-Muyassar gives the identical reading: continue in the worship of your Lord for the span of your life, until the certainty comes to you — and it is death.

Al-Tabari even explains *why* death gets called "the certainty." The word *yaqin* has an active shape but is doing passive work here — it means "the thing held certain," the one event nobody manages to doubt. He offers a small grammatical parallel: we say *aged wine* using an active-sounding word, when what we mean is wine that *has been* aged. So death is named for the effect it has on everyone's mind. Of all the futures a person can argue about, this is the one that is simply known.

The grammar of the phrase closes the door from the other side too. **Hatta ya'tiyaka al-yaqin** — until the certainty **comes to you**. The certainty is the one doing the arriving. The servant is the one it arrives at. It is not something you climb up to and then rest from; it is something that comes for you while you are still working. Read as a station to be attained, the sentence would have to run the other way around.

And al-Muyassar closes it with the strongest evidence available: the Messenger of Allah obeyed this command, and never stopped — he remained unceasing in the worship of Allah until the certainty came to him from his Lord. If any human being had certainty enough to be excused, it was him. He did not stop. Nobody after him has a better claim.

The surah closes not with a promised date of relief, but with a mode of fidelity. Keep worshipping until certainty arrives.

## Part 2: The Thematic Depths

### Theme 1: The Heart Often Tries to Heal Constriction by Staying Inside the Wound

When people say painful things, the soul often circles the injury.

Replay the sentence.

Rehearse the insult.

Imagine the rebuttal.

Return to the scene.

Search for some wording that would finally make the pain close.

[PAUSE]

But that keeps the chest inside the very speech that constricted it. The ayah offers another movement. Not suppression, but redirection. Allah does not say the pain is fake. He says: I know. Then He commands worship.

This means one of the deepest treatments for inner tightness is not endless inward rumination, but a return to the Lord through praise, prayer, and lowered nearness. The wound is carried upward rather than merely turned over inwardly.

And notice what the commentators say the outcome is. Not that the words stop. Not that you win the argument. Al-Tabari and al-Muyassar both end at the same place: *Allah will be enough for you against what is weighing on you*. The relief comes from the side of sufficiency, not from the side of resolution. Which is a mercy, because resolution is often not available. The people who said the thing may never take it back. Sufficiency does not need their cooperation.

### Theme 2: The Quran Does Not Promise the Worker a Finish Line in Dunya, Only Faithfulness Until the End

The final command of the surah is astonishingly spare:

**wa'bud rabbaka hatta ya'tiyaka al-yaqin**

[PAUSE]

Not: worship until they stop mocking.

Not: worship until the mission becomes easy.

Not: worship until you see full outward victory.

Worship until certainty comes.

This is one of the most stabilizing commands in the Quran. It teaches the servant that success is not surviving every emotional difficulty without feeling it. Success is remaining in worship until life ends.

That rescues us from a thousand false expectations. We stop bargaining with obedience. We stop telling ourselves, "I will stay faithful until this person changes, until this environment softens, until this burden passes." The surah closes that door. Worship is not tied to improved conditions. It is tied to the Lord.

Al-Muyassar puts the measurement in the plainest possible terms: keep worshipping **for the span of your life**. That is the unit. Not the span of the difficulty, not the span of your patience, not the span of your good season. The whole length of you.

And both commands name the same anchor. Glorify with praise of **your Lord**. Worship **your Lord**. The word *Rabb* carries ownership and upbringing together — the One who made you and is still raising you. The relationship named twice is the one that does not depend on anything the mockers do. They can withhold their approval indefinitely. They cannot touch who your Lord is.

[PAUSE]

One way to sit with this passage is to ask what words have been tightening your chest lately, and whether you have been trying to heal that pressure by thinking harder inside it rather than by returning more fully to tasbih, sujud, and steady worship.

## Closing Synthesis

Allah tells His Messenger that He fully knows the constriction caused by what the people say. Then He gives no argument back to the mockers, but a path for the heart: glorify with praise, be among those who prostrate, and worship your Lord until certainty comes.

[PAUSE]

The surah ends where the whole life must end: not with the disappearance of opposition, but with uninterrupted servanthood. Constriction is acknowledged, worship is prescribed, and the road stretches all the way to death.

### Questions to Carry

- What words or environments have been narrowing your chest lately?
- When you feel inwardly compressed, do you mostly replay the wound or return to worship?
- Have you been waiting for easier conditions before giving Allah a steadier form of devotion?
- What would change if you accepted that the assignment is not temporary emotional relief, but worship until certainty comes?
- When something weighs on you, where do you actually run first — and what would it take to make the prayer the first place instead of the last?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**This closing passage teaches that when the chest tightens under the speech of people, the believer is invited not into retreat from worship but deeper into praise, prostration, and lifelong servanthood until death.**

### Du'a

*Allahumma, You know every constriction inside our chests before we can explain it. Open us through Your remembrance, anchor us in sujud, and do not let the speech of people pull us away from steady worship. Let us meet the certainty while still bowed to You.*
