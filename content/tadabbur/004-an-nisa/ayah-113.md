# The Grace You Never Felt

### Surah An-Nisa, Ayah 113

---

## INTRODUCTION

Let me read it to you first. Sit with the sound before the meaning.

> **وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ لَهَمَّت طَّآئِفَةٌ مِّنْهُمْ أَن يُضِلُّوكَ وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَمَا يَضُرُّونَكَ مِن شَىْءٍ وَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَكَ مَا لَمْ تَكُن تَعْلَمُ وَكَانَ فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ عَظِيمًا**

*Walaw lā faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka wa raḥmatuhu lahammat ṭāʾifatun minhum an yuḍillūka, wa mā yuḍillūna illā anfusahum, wa mā yaḍurrūnaka min shayʾ. Wa anzala-llāhu ʿalayka l-kitāba wal-ḥikmata wa ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam. Wa kāna faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka ʿaẓīmā.*

*"And were it not for the grace of Allah upon you and His mercy, a group of them would have resolved to mislead you. But they mislead none except themselves, and they will not harm you in anything. And Allah has sent down upon you the Book and the Wisdom and taught you what you did not know. And the grace of Allah upon you has ever been immense."*

### Let's rebuild the world this came into

There was a man in Madinah named Tuʿma ibn Ubayriq. He stole — armor, by most of the narrations. And when the theft began to surface, he did what frightened people have always done. He didn't confess. He arranged the evidence so that it would point somewhere else — toward an innocent man, an outsider, someone whose word would carry less weight in the city.

And then — this is the part that should make us lean in — **his own people came to the Prophet ﷺ to lobby for him.** They came as a delegation. Respectable. Plausible. They came not with swords but with words, with framing, with the soft pressure of *"surely you don't think one of us would do such a thing — look at the evidence, look at this outsider."* They came to do something far more dangerous than steal armor.

They came to bend the judgment of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

Think about what that moment actually was. The man whose entire mission is to deliver the truth undistorted — and a coordinated group is working, quietly, skillfully, to get a single wrong verdict out of his mouth. Not to attack Islam from outside. To reach inside the one channel through which Allah's guidance was flowing to humanity, and introduce one drop of poison.

This whole stretch of Surah An-Nisa — verses 105 through 113 — is Allah responding to that moment. And verse 113 is where He pulls the camera all the way back and shows us what was *really* happening in that room.

### The hook

Here is what I want you to notice before we even begin, and then I want you to wait with the question.

This entire ayah — the threat, the scheming, the danger to the Prophet ﷺ — describes something **that never actually happened.**

The whole verse is built on a door that did not open.

And the question I want burning in you is this: *Why would Allah spend an entire verse describing a disaster that He prevented? Why narrate the catastrophe at all, if He already turned it away?*

[PAUSE]

Stay with that. Because the answer is one of the most tender things in the Qur'an.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Word That Opens a Door That Never Opened — *لَوْلَا*

Let me teach you a small piece of Arabic first, because the entire verse hangs on it.

In English we have a way of talking about things that *almost* happened but didn't. We say: *"If it weren't for my seatbelt, I'd be dead."* Notice what that sentence does. It describes a corpse — your corpse — in vivid certainty. And then, in the same breath, it tells you that corpse does not exist. The sentence exists only to make you feel the weight of a disaster you never experienced. It conjures the catastrophe precisely so it can show you the thing that stopped it.

Arabic has a dedicated word for exactly this move. It is **لَوْلَا — *lawlā*.** It means *"were it not for."* And grammatically it does something specific: it names a thing that *exists and is present*, and then says — because that thing is present, a certain consequence was prevented.

Now look at how the ayah opens:

> **وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ**
> *"And were it not for the grace of Allah upon you and His mercy..."*

The classical grammarians, and al-Qurtubī records this carefully, point out something about the structure here. What comes right after *lawlā* — *faḍlu-llāh*, the grace of Allah — sits in the nominative case as a subject, and its predicate is **deliberately left unspoken.** The sentence doesn't even finish saying "the grace of Allah upon you *exists*" — it doesn't need to. The existence of that grace is so assumed, so foundational, that the grammar just... rests on it. It's the ground you're already standing on.

Let's sit with what this means.

Allah does not begin by saying, *"I gave you a gift."* He begins by saying, *"Were it not for My grace—"* and then He shows you the cliff edge you were walking along the whole time without knowing it. He proves the blessing **by drawing the picture of the fall it prevented.**

Notice something. This is a completely different way of revealing love than we are used to. We usually feel grateful for what we *received* — the thing in our hands, the gift we can see. But here Allah is teaching the Prophet ﷺ — and through him, us — to feel the weight of a gift we **never even saw arrive,** because its entire work was to make sure something *else* never arrived.

Here's what's remarkable. *lawlā* presupposes its subject is real and present. So the grammar itself is a quiet act of intimacy. Before the verse describes a single danger, it has already established — as bedrock, as the unspoken predicate — that *the grace of Allah upon you is simply there. Always there. The given.*

The threat is hypothetical. **The grace is grammatically certain.**

And the classical commentators are strikingly precise about what this "mercy" concretely *was.* al-Tabari, al-Jalalayn, and al-Muyassar all read *raḥmatuhu* here as a particular kind of mercy: *ʿiṣma* — the divine safeguarding of the Prophet ﷺ from error, given to him as part of the very blessing of prophethood. And not error in general — al-Jalalayn pins down the exact danger: the faction tried to mislead him *ʿan al-qaḍāʾ bi-l-ḥaqq*, *away from judging by the truth,* by means of *talbīs* — the deliberate clouding of the evidence so that the false would wear the face of the true. This is the texture of the threat: not a sword raised at the Prophet ﷺ, but a fog rolled toward his judgment. And the "mercy" that *lawlā* rests on, so quietly that the sentence doesn't even finish naming it, is precisely the steadiness that kept his verdict clear when the fog came.

But notice *how* al-Tabari says that safeguarding actually worked, because it keeps the mercy from floating off into something abstract. He glosses it with two concrete verbs: Allah *protected you by His tawfīq and His tibyān of this affair to you* — *ʿaṣamaka bi-tawfīqihi wa-tibyānihi laka amra hādhā.* Two channels. *Tawfīq* — Allah's granting him the right direction, the inner alignment that bends a heart toward the true. And *tibyān* — Allah's *making the matter plain to him,* actively disclosing the reality of these people and their scheme. al-Muyassar echoes the same: the protection came *bi-tawfīqihi bimā awḥā ilayk* — through the right-guidance carried in *what He revealed to you.* Sit with what that means for the whole passage. The "mercy" was not a vague hand steadying the Prophet ﷺ from a distance; it was disclosure. The very verses just before this one — the ones that exposed the theft, named the framing, and forbade arguing on behalf of the deceivers (4:105–112) — *were* the ʿiṣma doing its work. The fog was rolled toward his judgment, and the answer Allah gave was not merely to harden the Prophet's resolve but to *turn on the light* — to send down the truth of the matter so the false could no longer wear the face of the true.

[PAUSE]

What this single word reveals: the deepest protections in your life are the ones you will never feel, because their entire success is measured by your never having to.

### Element Two: The Verb That Was Caught at the Threshold — *هَمَّت*

Now watch what *almost* happened. The verse says:

> **لَهَمَّت طَّآئِفَةٌ مِّنْهُمْ أَن يُضِلُّوكَ**
> *"...a group of them would have resolved to mislead you."*

I want to teach you the verb **هَمَّت — *hammat*** — from the root *hamm*, because translation flattens it and the flattening costs you everything.

*Hamm* is not the doing of a thing. It is not even the planning of a thing. *Hamm* is the **inner resolve** — the moment the intention forms in the chest and tilts toward action. It is the instant *just before* the hand moves. You know this state. It's the moment you've decided to send the angry message — your thumb is over the screen — but you haven't pressed yet. That tilt, that inner leaning toward the act: that is *hamm*.

You may recognize the word from the story of Yusuf, where the same root appears at the most charged moment of temptation. *Hamm* is the language of the threshold. The verge. The almost.

So read the ayah again with that precision restored:

The group did not *succeed* in misleading the Prophet ﷺ. According to the verse, **they did not even fully begin.** Allah's grace intercepted them at the level of *hamm* — at the inner resolve, before it could even become a plan, let alone an act.

Let's go deeper, because this is extraordinary.

Where does Allah's protection meet the threat in this verse? Not at the act. Not at the plan. **At the intention itself.** The grace of Allah reached so far upstream that it stopped the danger while it was still just a leaning in someone's chest. The shield was placed not in front of the Prophet ﷺ, but inside the very moment the plot was forming on the other side of the room.

Stay with me, because there's a tenderness here that's easy to miss. The verse uses *lahammat* — *would have resolved.* It honestly tells the Prophet ﷺ that there were people in his own community whose hearts genuinely tilted toward deceiving him. Allah does not hide that from him. He does not pretend the danger wasn't real. **He shows His Messenger the exact reach of the threat — and in the same sentence, the exact reach of the protection.** You were that close. And I was that much closer.

Notice one more thing — the precision of *ṭāʾifatun minhum*, "a *group* of them." Not all of them. A faction. al-Jalalayn identifies them concretely: *min qawm Ṭuʿma* — the clan and partisans of the thief himself. Allah is exact even in describing His Prophet's adversaries. He does not let the moment slide into a blanket condemnation of a whole people. The accuracy of the protection extends even to the accuracy of the accusation.

[PAUSE]

What this verb reveals: the grace of Allah doesn't wait for the blow to land so it can heal you. It reaches back into the moment the blow was *conceived* — and disarms it there, where you'll never even see it.

### Element Three: The Arrow That Turned Around in the Air — *وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ*

Now we come to the heart of the verse's reversal, and I need to show you something about *who the target was.*

Look at the verb they intended: **يُضِلُّوكَ — *yuḍillūka*.** From the root *ḍ-l-l*, in the causative Form IV — *aḍalla* means *to cause someone to go astray, to actively lead them off the path.* And the ending — that *-ka*, "**you**" — is the target. The object. *To cause **you** to stray.* The Prophet ﷺ was the intended destination of the arrow.

Now read the very next clause:

> **وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ**
> *"But they mislead none except themselves."*

Watch the object change.

The verb is the *same* — *yuḍillūn*, "they cause to stray." But the target — the *-ka*, the "you" — **has vanished.** And in its place: *illā anfusahum* — "none except *themselves.*"

Let this land slowly. They aimed the arrow at *you*, O Messenger. And the verse tells us that in the entire universe, the only thing that arrow could ever actually strike — was the archer.

This is what I'd call a load-bearing absence. The "you" they were aiming at is **grammatically absent** from where the harm lands. The target was swapped mid-flight. They drew back the bow toward the Prophet ﷺ, and Allah informs us with quiet finality: the only person you can lead astray by trying to corrupt the truth — is you.

And then Allah seals it, with a small word that does enormous work:

> **وَمَا يَضُرُّونَكَ مِن شَىْءٍ**
> *"And they will not harm you in anything."*

That little **مِن — *min*** before *shayʾ* — in classical grammar this is called *min* used for emphasis, and its function here is to make the negation **total.** Without it, the sentence would say "they won't harm you with a thing." With it, the sentence says: *not with so much as a particle, not with the faintest trace, not with anything that could even be called a thing.* It is the grammar of an *absolute zero*.

So we have the full reversal now, in three precise moves:

1. They *resolved* — but were caught at the resolve (*hammat*).
2. They *aimed at you* — but the only one they could strike was themselves (*illā anfusahum*).
3. And of harm to you — there was **not an atom** (*min shayʾ*).

[PAUSE]

What this construction reveals: deception is a boomerang with no return-to-sender option except the sender. When you bend toward corrupting the truth, the truth does not bend. *You* do. You become the only casualty of your own aim.

### Bridge

So the linguistic construction has shown us three things. A grace so certain it's the unspoken ground of the sentence (*lawlā*). A protection that reaches upstream into the very *intention* of the threat (*hammat*). And a deception that can strike no one but the deceiver (*illā anfusahum*, *min shayʾ*).

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself. About the shape of your life, and the shape of every plot you've ever feared, and every plot you've ever been tempted to set in motion yourself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Protections You Will Never Feel

Let me start where you live.

There is a strange feature of being human: **we can only count the things that hit us.** The illness that came. The accident that happened. The betrayal we felt. These leave marks, and we tally them, and on hard days we look at the tally and wonder why our life has so much pain in it.

But there is an entire other category of events in your life that you have **no access to at all.** The car you didn't get into. The person who quietly decided not to wound you. The illness that was forming and dissolved. The opportunity that would have destroyed you, that simply never came. The plot, somewhere, in some room you'll never know about, that tilted toward harming you — and was stopped at the level of *hamm*, before it ever became anything you'd feel.

You have no memorial for these. There is no scar to count. Statisticians have a name for the bias that makes us blind here — they call it *survivorship bias* — we study only the planes that came back full of bullet holes, and forget entirely the ones that were turned away before they ever took off. **We build our whole sense of our life out of the damage we can see, and we are structurally blind to the damage that was deflected.**

Now hear the ayah again, and feel what it's doing.

> *"And were it not for the grace of Allah upon you..."*

Allah is reaching into that invisible category — the deflected, the prevented, the catastrophe that never arrived — and He is **naming one of them out loud.** He is taking one of the planes that never had to fly, and showing it to His Messenger: *Look. Here. This is what I turned away from you. You never felt it, because My turning it away was complete.*

Let's go deeper. Why does this matter for your heart?

Because most of us, when we think about Allah's grace, think about what we *received*. The job. The health. The child. The provision. And those are real, and we should be grateful. But this ayah is teaching a second, deeper literacy of gratitude — gratitude for **the unfelt shield.** For the entire silent dimension of your life in which Allah has been turning things away from you, continuously, at the level of their very intention, and you have *never once noticed,* because the whole signature of His success is that you felt nothing.

Here's the paradox, and I want you to let it sit without resolving it too fast: **the greatest mercies in your life are precisely the ones you cannot remember,** because to remember them you would have had to live through them — and His mercy was that you didn't.

Stay with that for a moment. The mercy was so complete it erased its own evidence.

So what does this change about tomorrow?

It means there is a form of *dhikr* — remembrance — available to you that you may have never practiced. Not gratitude for the gift in your hand. But gratitude for **the empty space where a catastrophe should be.** Tonight, you can look at the ordinary, unremarkable safety of your day — the fact that nothing terrible happened — and instead of taking it as the boring default, you can recognize it as *faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka* — a grace upon you, immense and active and entirely invisible. The quiet is not absence. **The quiet is the shield, working.**

### Theme Two: The Only Person You Can Ever Truly Deceive Is Yourself

Now let me take you to the other side of the room — to Tuʿma, and the group who came to bend the truth. Because there is a mirror here, and most of us have stood where they stood more often than we'd like to admit.

Start with the human experience. You have, at some point, *arranged* something. Not necessarily armor and a frame-up. But you have shaded a story so it landed in your favor. You've managed an impression. You've let someone believe a version of events that protected you. You've curated, omitted, angled. We all have. It is one of the most universal human reflexes — when exposure threatens, we *manage the narrative*.

And here is the lie at the center of that reflex, the lie the whole maneuver depends on: *that you are acting on the other person.* That deception is a thing you do *to someone else.* That if you're skillful enough, you reshape *their* reality, and you walk away untouched.

The ayah detonates that lie in five words.

> **وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ**
> *"They mislead none except themselves."*

Remember what we found in the grammar: the arrow they aimed at the Prophet ﷺ could only ever strike the archer. Now feel what that means as a law of the moral universe, not just a fact of one story.

When you set out to lead someone away from the truth, **the truth is not actually in your power.** Reality doesn't move because you spun it. The Prophet's judgment didn't move, because Allah was holding it. What *moves* — what actually deforms, bends, gets led astray — is the only thing that was ever in your reach: **your own self.** *Anfusahum.* Your *nafs*. You wanted to relocate someone else's relationship to reality, and the only relationship to reality you can ever actually relocate is your own. And you relocated it *away from the truth.*

Let's build this further, because it gets more serious.

The manipulator believes he is the subject of the sentence — the one acting. The ayah reveals he is secretly the **object.** He thinks he is shaping; he is being shaped. Every act of deception is, underneath, a sculptor's chisel — and the deceiver has it pointed the wrong way. He thinks he's carving the world. He's carving *himself,* and into a worse shape each time.

al-Jalalayn names the mechanism in a single phrase: the harm lands on them *li-anna wabāl iḍlālihim ʿalayhim* — *because the ruinous consequence of their misleading falls back upon them.* *Wabāl* is the word Arabic reserves for the heavy aftermath of a wrong, the toxic residue an act leaves in the one who committed it. So the verse isn't only saying the Prophet ﷺ was untouched; it is naming where the touch actually went. The poison they mixed had only one possible drinker.

And al-Muyassar catches a thread that runs back through the whole passage. He describes this faction not merely as schemers but as *alladhīna yakhūnūna anfusahum* — *those who betray themselves* — echoing the language Allah used a few verses earlier (4:107) for the very same people. The betrayal they thought they were committing against the Prophet ﷺ and against an innocent man was, at its root, a betrayal of *themselves.* The grammar of *illā anfusahum* and the diagnosis of *khiyāna* converge on one verdict: the only self any of this could ever wound was their own.

This is the same law Allah states elsewhere when He says the evil plot *encircles only its own people* (35:43) — *wa lā yaḥīqu l-makru s-sayyiʾu illā bi-ahlih.* The scheme is a noose, and it has exactly one neck it can close around. And it is the same exact phrase — *wa mā yuḍillūna illā anfusahum* — that Allah uses in Surah Aal ʿImran (3:69) about another group who wished to lead the believers astray. It is stated as a *constant.* A standing law of how the moral world is built. **Deception does not transfer. It accrues — to the deceiver, alone.**

Now hold the tenderness of this together with its severity. To the Prophet ﷺ, this is pure comfort: *they cannot touch you, not by an atom.* But to Tuʿma's faction — and to us, when we're standing in their shoes — it is the most sobering possible warning: *the person you are working so hard to fool is the one person you cannot protect from the consequences. Yourself.*

What does this change about tomorrow?

The next time you feel the pull to manage a narrative — to shade, to spin, to arrange the evidence of your own life so it points away from your fault — pause and ask the ayah's question: *Who am I actually leading astray right now?* Because the verse has already answered. Not your audience. Not the person you're managing. **You.** The only *nafs* in reach of your deception is the one you carry. To lie to others is, first and always, to take your own self by the hand and walk it one more step away from the truth.

And the mercy hidden inside that severity is this: if the only person you can deceive is yourself, then **the only person you ever have to come clean to is also yourself — before Allah.** The door of honesty is always one step away, because you never actually left the room. You only fogged your own window.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture: A Verse Enclosed in Grace

Now that we've done the work, let me show you something about how this ayah is *built* — because I held it back until now on purpose.

Look at how the verse opens:

> **فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ** — *the grace of Allah upon you.*

And now look at how it closes:

> **وَكَانَ فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ عَظِيمًا** — *and the grace of Allah upon you has ever been immense.*

The **exact same phrase** — *faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka* — stands at the doorway and at the final seal. The verse is a ring. It is *enclosed* in grace.

And now notice what lives *inside* that enclosure. The threat. The scheming faction. The arrow that turned around. The boomerang of self-deception. The Book sent down. The Wisdom. The teaching of *what you did not know* — *ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam,* the verb in the form that means *to cause to know,* because even the Prophet's discernment to judge rightly was not his own possession but a gift downloaded into him.

And the commentators fill in what each of these gifts was. *al-Kitāb* — the Qur'an. *al-Ḥikma* — here the classical readings divide, gently: al-Jalalayn reads it as *the rulings (al-aḥkām)* contained within the revelation itself, while al-Muyassar reads it as *the Sunna that clarifies the Book* — the prophetic practice that turns a verse into a verdict. Both, notice, point the same direction: toward exactly the faculty the faction tried to corrupt — the capacity to judge rightly. And *mā lam takun taʿlam,* what you did not know? al-Jalalayn specifies its two halves: *al-aḥkām wa-l-ghayb* — the rulings, and the unseen. The very man they hoped to fog over with false evidence had been *taught*, directly from above, both how to judge and what lay hidden beneath appearances. They brought *talbīs* to the one mind on earth equipped, by gift, to see through it.

Every single thing in this verse — the danger and the deliverance, the plot and the protection, the ignorance and the teaching — **all of it sits inside the two walls of *faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka.*** Grace is not one item in the verse. Grace is the *room the whole verse happens in.*

[PAUSE]

That is the proof. The architecture is the meaning. You were never, for one instant of this story, outside the grace of Allah — not when the plot was forming, not when it was caught at the threshold, not when it boomeranged, not when the truth held. The opening *faḍl* and the closing *faḍl* are the two arms of an embrace, and everything that frightened you happened *within* them.

### Bringing It Together

Our linguistic discoveries and our two themes turn out to be a single insight, seen from two directions.

From the side of *protection*: the grace you never felt is real, and it reaches all the way upstream into the *intention* of every harm — *lawlā* and *hammat* together tell you that you have been shielded at a depth you cannot perceive, inside an enclosure you never left.

From the side of *deception*: the only thing your scheming can ever reach is your own self — *illā anfusahum* tells you that the truth is held by Allah, beyond the reach of every arrow, and the archer is the only target.

And they meet in the middle: **What is yours to keep safe is only your own *nafs*. What is Allah's to keep safe is everything else — including you.**

### Questions to Carry

1. What ordinary, uneventful safety in my day have I been treating as boring default — when it might be the silent signature of a shield I will never get to see?
2. Is there a narrative in my life I am currently *managing* — and if the only person I can truly mislead is myself, what is it costing the one *nafs* I have to answer with?
3. When I feel afraid of what others are plotting around me, do I actually believe — by an *atom* — that anything reaches me outside the grace of Allah?
4. Can I learn to thank Allah not only for what He gave me, but for the empty space where a catastrophe should have been?
5. What would change in how I judge, decide, and discern, if I held — as the Prophet ﷺ was taught to hold — that even my own knowing is *ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam,* a gift, not a possession?

### One-Sentence Distillation

The grace of Allah is most fully revealed not in what it places in your hands but in the catastrophe it silently turns away before you ever feel it — and the one who schemes against the truth bends no one but himself.

### The Closing Invitation

So here is what might shift after this.

Tonight, before sleep, do one thing. Don't list what you received today. List, instead, the *quiet* — the absence of disaster, the day that simply held. And recognize that absence not as nothing, but as *faḍlu-llāhi ʿalayka* — a grace upon you, active, immense, and so complete that it left no scar for you to notice.

And if there is somewhere you've been managing the truth — gently turn the chisel around. You were only ever carving yourself.

You were never outside the embrace. Not for one verse of your life.

### A Du'a from These Words

*O Allah, the grace upon us has ever been immense — and most of it we have never even seen. Let us recognize the shields we cannot feel, and thank You for the catastrophes You turned away before they reached us. And where we have tried to deceive, save us first from deceiving our own selves; turn our chisel toward truth, and hold our hearts steady as You held the heart of Your Messenger ﷺ. Āmīn.*
