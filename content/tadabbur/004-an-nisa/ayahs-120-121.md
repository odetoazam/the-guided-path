# The Promise That Lives Only in Tomorrow

## Introduction

Let me give you the two verses first, slowly.

> [ayah:4:120] يَعِدُهُمْ وَيُمَنِّيهِمْ ۖ وَمَا يَعِدُهُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ إِلَّا غُرُورًا
>
> *Yaʿiduhum wa yumannīhim — wa mā yaʿiduhumu-sh-shayṭānu illā ghurūrā.*
>
> "He promises them and stirs desires in them — and Satan promises them nothing but delusion." (4:120)
>
> [ayah:4:121] أُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ مَأْوَىٰهُمْ جَهَنَّمُ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ عَنْهَا مَحِيصًا
>
> *Ulāʾika maʾwāhum jahannamu wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā.*
>
> "Those — their refuge is Hell, and they will find no escape from it." (4:121)

Now I want to take you back a few lines, because these verses are not the beginning of a thought. They are the end of one.

A few ayahs earlier, in 4:119, something almost unheard-of happens in the Qur'an. Allah lets Satan speak — in the first person. Satan stands and swears an oath about what he will do to the children of Adam: *"I will surely take an appointed portion of Your servants. I will lead them astray. I will fill them with false desires (wa la-umanniyannahum). I will command them and they will slit the ears of cattle. I will command them and they will alter the creation of Allah."*

Listen to the grammar of that boast. It is loud. It is emphatic. Every verb carries the heavy *lām* of swearing and the *nūn* of emphasis — *la-uḍillannahum*, *la-umanniyannahum* — the Arabic equivalent of pounding the table: *I will absolutely, definitely, without fail do this.* It is a speech designed to sound enormous. A campaign. A strategy. A power.

And then comes our verse. And notice what Allah does. He does not answer the boast with a louder boast. He takes Satan's own swollen, table-pounding verb — *la-umanniyannahum* — and He sets it back down, quiet and flat, in the plain third person: *yumannīhim.* "He stirs desires in them." Yes. He does. That part is true.

And then, in five words, the whole campaign collapses.

Here is the hook I want you to carry into the next hour. Satan, in all of his ancient cunning, has exactly **one** verb tense available to him. He cannot touch your present. He cannot reach into the past. He owns only one piece of real estate in the human soul — and once you see which piece it is, you will never look at your own procrastination, your own "tomorrow," your own glittering "someday," the same way again.

Let's go and find it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: Two verbs, and the gap between them

Before we open the words, let me teach you something about Arabic verbs — a small thing that will unlock the whole verse.

In Arabic, a verb has a basic, bare form — Form I — and then it can be *built up*. One of the most common ways to build it up is to double the middle letter. Grammarians call this Form II. When you double that middle letter, you usually intensify the action, or you make it *happen to someone else* — you cause it, you cultivate it, you do it again and again. Think of the difference in English between "to break" and "to shatter," or between "to learn" and "to drill into someone." The second one in each pair is heavier. It's sustained. It's done *to* somebody.

Hold onto that. Now look at our verse, because Allah places two verbs side by side, and they are deliberately not the same weight.

The first is **يَعِدُهُمْ** — *yaʿiduhum*, from the root *w-ʿ-d*, "to promise." It is Form I. The bare, simple form. *He promises them.* One clean act. A pledge held out.

The second is **وَيُمَنِّيهِمْ** — *wa yumannīhim*, from the root *m-n-y*. And here — the corpus confirms it — we have **Form II**. The middle letter is doubled. The verb has been built up, intensified.

What is the root *m-n-y*? It is the root of *umniya* and *amānī* — wishes, fancies, the things you daydream about. So Form II of this root, *tamniya*, does not just mean "to wish." It means **to fill another person with wishes. To cultivate fantasies in someone. To keep their imagination busy with what isn't there.**

Now feel the gap between the two verbs.

*Yaʿiduhum* — he makes them a promise. That's the hook.

*Yumannīhim* — and then he sits with them, and he embroiders. He decorates the promise. He keeps the daydream alive, refreshes it, feeds it, intensifies it. The doubled letter is doing real work here: this is not a one-time offer. It is the patient, ongoing manufacture of fantasy.

[PAUSE]

This is exactly what al-Baghawi reaches for when he explains the pairing. He says Satan's promising and his *tamniya* are "what he casts into the heart of a person — the long life, the attainment of the world." And al-Qurtubi fills in the catalogue: "his falsehoods and his nonsense — wealth, status, leadership; and that there is no resurrection and no punishment." Notice that every single item on that list lives in **the future** or in **the imagination**. Long life — future. Attainment of wealth — future. "There's no resurrection coming" — a claim about a future that he wants you to stop fearing.

The terse old glosses only sharpen the picture. Al-Jalalayn splits the two verbs cleanly: *yaʿiduhum* — he promises them length of life; *yumannīhim* — he keeps them daydreaming of the hopes they'll seize in this world, and whispers *that there is no resurrection and no recompense*. Sit with that last item, because it is the master-fantasy. The enemy works hardest of all to keep warm the one wish that there is no future where the bill comes due. Strip away the fear of the reckoning, and every other "later" he sells becomes consequence-free.

And al-Tabari, reading the boast back in 119, catches something easy to miss. The wishes are not idle entertainment. Satan places the *amānī* in the soul *in order to steer it* — *li-uzīghahum*, "to bend them aside" — off the road of obedience and tawḥīd and onto the road of his own obedience, his own shirk. The daydream is a rudder. Every fantasy he embroiders turns the heart a few more degrees from where it was pointed. And al-Tabari is precise about where that steering is meant to end. In the very same passage he unfolds the partner-boast *wa la-uḍillannahum* — "I will surely lead them astray" — as Satan's vow to block the "appointed portion" he has seized of Allah's servants *off the clear highway of guidance* (*maḥajjat al-hudā*) and into misguidance, *out of Islam and into disbelief.* So the rudder is not nudging the heart toward some vague worldliness; it is aimed, deliberately, from one named road onto its exact opposite. That is why *yumannī* is intensive and ongoing: a rudder only works if a hand stays on it.

Stay with this, because here is the thing the two verbs reveal together:

**Satan never sells you the present moment. He cannot. The present is where Allah is. The present is where the deed actually gets done or doesn't.** What Satan does is take your eyes off the present and pin them on a fabricated tomorrow — *I'll repent later, there's time. I'll pray when life calms down. I'll give in charity when I'm wealthier. Enjoy now, the bill comes never.* He promises (*yaʿid*), and then he keeps the fantasy warm so you never quite arrive (*yumannī*).

That is the whole engine. *Yaʿiduhum wa yumannīhim.* The hook, and the slow embroidery of the hook.

### Element Two: غُرُورًا — and the word "nothing but"

Now watch how Allah demolishes the engine. He does it with a grammatical structure and a single chosen word.

First, the structure. Teach yourself this shape, because it runs all through the Qur'an: **مَا ... إِلَّا** — *mā ... illā* — "nothing... except." In Arabic this is called restriction, *qaṣr* or *ḥaṣr*. You take everything in the world, you negate all of it with *mā*, and then through the tiny door of *illā* you let exactly one thing back in. It is the most exclusive sentence-shape the language has. It says: of all the possibilities, *only this one, and no other.*

In English we do it casually — "I drink nothing but water." It means water, full stop, the door is closed on everything else.

Now hear the verse: **وَمَا يَعِدُهُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ إِلَّا غُرُورًا** — "and Satan promises them **nothing except** ghurūr."

Sit with how total that is. Allah does not say "Satan *sometimes* deceives." He does not say "much of what Satan promises is false." He negates the entire field — *mā yaʿiduhum* — and then opens the one narrow door and lets through a single word: *ghurūr.* That is the **whole** content of every promise the enemy has ever made, from the first whisper in the Garden until the last one whispered to the last human. One hundred percent. No exceptions. The door of *illā* lets in nothing else.

And notice the dignity of the naming here. In the surrounding verses he's been "the enemy," he's been a swearing, boasting voice. Here Allah names him plainly — **ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ**, *the* Satan, with the definite article — almost like reading a verdict aloud with the defendant's full name. *And the Satan promises them nothing but...*

[PAUSE]

Now the word itself. **غُرُورًا** — *ghurūr*, from the root *gh-r-r*.

Let me sound it for you before I define it: *ghu-rooo-ra.* Three letters, and the *rā* rolls and slips. The word almost slides off the tongue and is gone — which is exactly what the thing does. It is slippery. It does not stay in your hand.

What does *gh-r-r* mean? Here is where I want to slow all the way down, because al-Qurtubi preserves a definition so precise it could be carved in stone. He quotes Ibn ʿArafa:

> *al-ghurūr* is "**that which you see an outside to that you love — and within it is something hated, or unknown.**"

Read that again. *Ghurūr* is not a simple lie. A simple lie has no attractive surface; you can often smell it. *Ghurūr* is more dangerous than a lie. It is the thing with a **beautiful exterior wrapped around a rotten or hidden interior.** The polished surface you genuinely love. And behind it, either something you would hate if you could see it — or something you simply cannot see at all.

And then al-Qurtubi names the application directly: "And Satan is *gharūr* — because he carries you toward the loves of the lower self (*maḥābb al-nafs*), and behind that lies what will harm you."

[PAUSE]

Do you feel what just happened? The verse has handed us a complete anatomy of how we are fooled. Satan does not approach you with something ugly. He approaches you with **something you love** — the surface is genuinely, sincerely attractive. The wealth really does glitter. The forbidden glance really is sweet. The "I'll deal with it tomorrow" really does feel like relief. The surface is not the lie. The surface is real. The deception is entirely in what's *behind* it — *makrūh aw majhūl*, something hateful or something you simply have not been shown yet.

That is *ghurūr.* And Allah says that is *all* — *illā ghurūrā* — that the enemy has ever had in his hands to give you.

The earliest, most compressed readings hold exactly this line. Al-Jalalayn reduces the whole word to one syllable of meaning: *ghurūr* is *bāṭil* — the void, that which has nothing real beneath it. And al-Muyassar presses further still, glossing the promise as *a deception with no soundness to it and no proof behind it* (*khadīʿa lā ṣiḥḥata lahā wa lā dalīla ʿalayhi*). Sit with that second half, because it adds a blade to Ibn ʿArafa's picture. The promise is not only hollow when you finally turn it over — it never had a single shred of *evidence* to stand on in the first place. You were never shown a proof. You were shown a *surface*, and you supplied the belief yourself. That is the genius and the poverty of the entire operation: it runs wholly on a shine, and not once on a reason.

### Element Three: مَحِيصًا — the word with no door

We've been living, this whole time, in the world of verse 120 — a world of expansion. Promises stretching out. Desires being inflated. Long life, wide horizons, *someday, someday, someday.* The grammar itself has been open: imperfect verbs (*yaʿidu*, *yumannī*) that, in Arabic, denote ongoing, unfinished, still-rolling action. Everything is still in motion. Nothing has landed.

And then verse 121 lands it.

> **أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ مَأْوَىٰهُمْ جَهَنَّمُ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ عَنْهَا مَحِيصًا**

Hear how the sound changes. Verse 120 ended on that slippery, rolling *ghurūrā.* Verse 121 opens with **أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ** — *ulāʾika*, "those ones" — a demonstrative that points across a distance, the way a judge says "the accused." It holds them at arm's length. And al-Tabari fixes exactly who is being pointed at: *ulāʾika* are "those who took Satan as a *walī* in place of Allah." Let that irony land before we move on. A *walī* is the one you turn *to* — your protector, your refuge, the one whose door you run to when the road runs out. These are the people who made the deceiver himself their refuge. And the verse's very last word is about to tell them there is no refuge left to run to at all.

And then comes a word that is the precise opposite of everything that came before: **مَأْوَىٰهُمْ** — *maʾwāhum*, "their dwelling, their lodging, their final resting-place." From the root *ʾ-w-y* — to take shelter, to come home to. And here al-Tabari does something quietly devastating: he refuses to let the warmth of the word stand. He glosses *maʾwā* flatly as *maṣīr* — "the end they come to be at," the place they are *driven* to and end up in. The very word for a shelter you seek out, read as the destination you never chose.

Think about the cruelty of the irony the verse is building. The whole sales pitch was *motion* — keep moving, keep wishing, the next thing, the next thing. And it delivers you to a *maʾwā.* A fixed address. The place you finally stop. The promise economy that never let you arrive — arrives. *Jahannam.*

And now the last word. Feel it in your mouth before you know what it means: **مَحِيصًا** — *maḥīṣā.* The *ḥā* and the *ṣād* are both tight, constricted, back-of-the-throat sounds. There is no soft rolling here. The word closes like a door.

The root is *ḥ-y-ṣ*, and it means **to swerve aside, to turn off the road, to dodge, to find a way out to the side.** al-Tabari traces the verb for us directly — *ḥāṣa fulānun ʿan hādhā al-amr, yaḥīṣu*, "so-and-so turned aside, swerved away, from this matter" — and then he and al-Jalalayn both settle on a single gloss for what is being denied: *maʿdil*, "a place to turn aside to." A *maḥīṣ* is the escape-route, the swerve, the off-ramp. It is the move you make when you're trapped on a road and you look frantically left and right for any gap.

And Allah negates it — completely, with the indefinite: **وَلَا يَجِدُونَ عَنْهَا مَحِيصًا** — "and they will find, away from it, no swerve at all." Not "the escape is hard." Not "few escape." The indefinite *maḥīṣā* under the negation means: there is no off-ramp of any kind, named or unnamed, large or small. The road that began with infinite open horizons ends on a road with no shoulder, no exit, no swerve.

And al-Muyassar widens the closure with a second word to seal it shut: they find *lā maʿdilan wa lā maljaʾan* — no place to swerve to **and** no shelter to flee into. Two different escapes, both negated. The *maʿdil* is the swerve out — the lateral dodge off the road. The *maljaʾ* is the place you'd flee *toward* — the refuge, the safe house. The verse closes both at once: there is no turning aside, and there is nowhere to turn aside *to*. And remember who these are — the people who took Satan as their *walī*, their refuge of choice. The refuge they chose has become the one place that offers no refuge.

[PAUSE]

Now bring the three words together and feel the architecture, because it is exact:

- The enemy's tools were the **future** (*yaʿid*) and the **imagination** (*yumannī*).
- Their entire content was **a lovely surface over a hidden ruin** (*ghurūr*).
- And the destination — the one thing that was never imaginary, never future, never a surface — is a fixed home with **no swerve out of it** (*maḥīṣ*).

Everything that was open closes. Everything that moved, stops. The only thing in the whole passage that turns out to be *solid and real* is the very thing Satan spent all his energy convincing them was not coming.

### Bridge

So look at what the language alone has shown us. Two verbs that reveal the enemy owns only the future tense. A restriction-structure and a single rolling word that reduce his entire arsenal to *one* thing — a beautiful surface over a hidden harm. And a final, throat-tight word that slams every exit.

But these are not merely facts about words. They are a map of something happening *inside you, right now.* So let's ask what this reveals not just about Satan's speech — but about the architecture of the human heart, and the way every one of us is being worked on, today.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The economy of the unredeemed promise

Let me start where you actually live, not where the verse is.

Think about the last advertisement that genuinely moved you. Not annoyed you — *moved* you. I'd wager it didn't sell you a product. It sold you a **version of your future self.** The watch wasn't a watch; it was the man you'd become wearing it. The course wasn't a course; it was the life on the other side of it. The whole modern economy of attention runs on a single, ancient mechanic: *don't sell the thing, sell the tomorrow the thing unlocks.*

And here's what every marketer knows and rarely says out loud: **the anticipation is more reliable than the arrival.** Researchers who study the brain's reward chemistry found something almost unsettling — the spike of pleasure comes not at the moment you *get* the reward, but at the moment you *expect* it. The wanting is sharper than the having. Which is why the new phone thrills you for a week and then becomes furniture. Why the achievement you chased for years feels strangely flat the morning after. The future you bought never actually arrives, because the moment it becomes the present, it stops being the thing that was sold.

Now read your verse again. *Yaʿiduhum wa yumannīhim.* He promises them, and he keeps the wishing warm.

Subhan'Allah. The Qur'an named the mechanic of the entire attention economy fourteen hundred years before there was a word for it. The enemy is not, first and foremost, the voice telling you to do something evil right now. He is the voice telling you that the *real* living, the *real* repenting, the *real* turning-back to Allah, is something that happens **later** — in a tomorrow that is always being sold and never delivered.

Let me make it uncomfortably specific, because this is where it bites.

You know the prayer you've been meaning to pray with real presence — *after* this busy season. You know the parent you've been meaning to call and truly sit with — *when* things settle. You know the sin you've made peace with leaving — *eventually*, once you've gotten it out of your system. You know the relationship with the Qur'an you intend to build — *when* the kids are older, *when* work eases, *when, when, when.*

Every one of those is *yumannīhim.* Every one is a beautifully embroidered tomorrow. And the cruelty of it — the *ghurūr* of it — is that it doesn't even feel like a sin. It feels like *responsibility.* It feels like *being realistic.* "I'm not refusing to change; I'm just not changing *yet.*" That sentence is the most effective thing the enemy has ever built, because it lets you feel righteous about your own delay.

[PAUSE]

Here is the turn. Here is what the verse offers as the way out.

Look at where Allah locates the demolition: *wa mā yaʿiduhumu-sh-shayṭānu illā ghurūrā.* He doesn't tell you to win an argument with the enemy about the future. You will lose that argument every time, because the future is his home field — it's all imagination, and his imagination is older and more practiced than yours. **He tells you, instead, what the entire transaction is worth: nothing but ghurūr.** A loved surface over a hidden ruin.

And the antidote to a fabricated future is not a better fabricated future. It is the **present tense.** It is the one piece of ground he cannot stand on. The sajda you make *now.* The "astaghfirullah" you say *now*, before finishing the thought that says "later." The call you make *tonight.* The enemy has no purchase on the present moment, because the present is the only place an actual deed can be born — and he deals exclusively in deeds not yet born.

So tomorrow, when you catch the voice that says *later* — and you will, many times — I want you to do one small thing. Don't argue with it. Just name it: *that is yaʿiduhum wa yumannīhim. That is a promise with nothing behind it.* And then do the smallest real version of the thing, **now.** Two rakʿahs. One verse. One sentence of istighfar. You will have stepped, for one moment, onto the only ground the enemy cannot follow you onto.

[PAUSE]

### Theme Two: The surface you love, and the thing behind it

Now let's go to the heart of *ghurūr*, because Ibn ʿArafa's definition deserves a whole life of reflection, not a paragraph.

*Al-ghurūr*: a surface you love, wrapped around something hated or unseen.

Start, again, in your own world. Think of how a financial scam actually works — not the clumsy ones, the ones that catch *smart* people. They never start with a threat. They start with something that looks *exactly* like an opportunity you'd be foolish to refuse. The returns are real-looking. The testimonials are warm. The website is clean. Everything you can *see* is designed to be loved. And the entire harm is in the one place you are structurally unable to look: behind the surface, in the part marked *majhūl* — unknown. By the time the unknown becomes known, the *maḥīṣ* — the exit — is already gone.

That is not a metaphor for how the enemy works. That **is** how the enemy works. The Qur'an just named it first.

Here is what I want you to notice, and it's the thing that changes how you carry this verse. **The enemy does not, cannot, give you something with an ugly surface.** He has no access to a forbidden thing that looks repulsive — because a repulsive thing requires no enemy to make you avoid it. His entire craft is in the wrapping. The glance that genuinely is sweet. The wealth that genuinely does glitter. The revenge that genuinely would feel satisfying for an hour. The comfort of skipping the prayer that genuinely is comfortable. **The pleasure on the surface is not the lie. The surface is real.** That's what makes it *ghurūr* and not just falsehood.

So the spiritual question Ibn ʿArafa's definition forces on you is devastating in its simplicity. It is not: *does this feel good?* The enemy has already conceded that it feels good — that was the whole point of the wrapping. The question is: **what is behind it that I am being kept from seeing?**

[PAUSE]

Let's go one layer deeper, because the verse does.

Why does Allah immediately follow "nothing but ghurūr" with "those — their dwelling is Hell, no escape"? Because verse 121 is the **unwrapping.** It is the camera turning around to show you the *back* of the package — the *majhūl* made *maʿlūm*, the unknown made known at last. *Maʾwāhum jahannam.* This is what was behind the loved surface the whole time. This is the "something hated" that Ibn ʿArafa said was always there, hidden in the lining.

And here is the mercy buried inside this terrifying verse — and it *is* mercy, make no mistake. **Allah unwraps the package for you now, while you can still do something about it.** The defining horror of *ghurūr* is that you discover the hidden ruin *after the exit closes* — *wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā,* no swerve, the off-ramp is gone. That's the *maḥīṣ* of the Hereafter: discovery too late. But the discovery you are having *right now*, sitting with this verse — this discovery comes with every exit still open. The road still has a shoulder. You can still swerve. The whole reason Allah shows you the inside of the box in *this* life is so that you are never, ever surprised by it in the next.

[PAUSE]

So what does this change tomorrow? One practice. When something glitters at you — an opportunity, a temptation, a shortcut, a "harmless" indulgence, a way to get what you want a little faster — and you feel that pull of a *loved surface* — pause for the length of one breath and ask Ibn ʿArafa's question, made personal:

*This surface I love — what is in its lining that I am not being shown? And would I still want it if the lining were turned out for me to see?*

That single question is a *maḥīṣ.* It is an exit the enemy did not account for. He built the whole structure on the assumption that you would judge by the surface — *ẓāhiran tuḥibbuhu.* The moment you train yourself to ask about the lining, you have stepped outside the only game he knows how to play.

And then, knowing your own weakness, do what the believer does: you ask the One who *sees* every lining to protect you from the linings you cannot see. *Rabbi aʿūdhu bika min hamazāti-sh-shayāṭīn.* Because the final defense against a deceiver whose whole power is hidden interiors — is to lean on the One for whom there are no hidden interiors at all.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The architecture, revealed

Now stand back, because the two verses are built as a single descending staircase, and you can only see it now that we've walked it.

Verse 120 is **all motion and all surface.** Two verbs of open, ongoing action — *promising, wishing.* A horizon with no edge. Long life, wide wealth, endless someday. And the verse names what all of that motion is made of: *ghurūr* — surface over a void.

Verse 121 is **all stillness and all interior.** The motion stops at a fixed dwelling — *maʾwā.* The surface is turned inside-out to reveal the lining — *jahannam.* And the open horizon contracts to a road with no swerve — *lā maḥīṣ.*

Watch the precise inversions. *He keeps them moving* (120) → *they arrive and stop* (121). *He sells a future* (120) → *the future has come* (121). *A loved surface* (120) → *the hated interior shown* (121). *Endless open horizon* (120) → *no exit at all* (121). The second verse is not a new subject. It is the first verse **with the wrapping removed.**

### Bringing it together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes were the same insight approached from two directions.

The grammar told us the enemy owns only the future tense and the imagination — and the theme of false hope showed us that lived out as every "later" we have ever told ourselves.

The word *ghurūr*, with its loved surface and hidden ruin, told us the deception is never in what we can see — and the theme of the unwrapped package showed us that verse 121 is Allah mercifully turning the box inside-out *while we still hold the exit in our hands.*

It is all one message: **The enemy can only ever sell you the outside of a tomorrow. Allah is showing you the inside of it, today, while you can still turn.**

### Questions to carry

- What is the specific "later" you have been telling yourself for years — the repentance, the prayer, the relationship, the change — that is, when you name it honestly, *yumannīhim*: a beautifully embroidered tomorrow that never arrives?
- When something glitters at you, do you judge it by its surface — or have you trained yourself to ask about its lining?
- Of everything you are currently chasing, how much of it would survive the question: *would I still want this if its hidden interior were turned out in front of me?*
- The off-ramp (*maḥīṣ*) is still open to you today. What is one swerve — one small, real, present-tense deed — you could take onto the ground the enemy cannot follow you onto?
- Allah unwrapped the package in this verse out of mercy, so you'd never be surprised. Are you living as someone who has *already seen* what's in the box?

### One-sentence distillation

Satan can only ever promise you the lovely outside of a tomorrow that never comes — so Allah turns the box inside-out for you today, showing you the ruin in its lining while every exit is still open.

### The closing invitation

You don't have to win an argument with the enemy about the future — you'll lose, because the future is the only field he plays on. You only have to do one thing he genuinely cannot survive: act in the present. The smallest real deed, done *now*, is a country he has no passport to. So this is the invitation — not a burden, a liberation: stop negotiating with *later.* Step, even once today, onto the one ground that is wholly, gloriously outside his reach.

### Du'a

*O Allah, You see every lining we cannot see and every interior the enemy hides from us. Strip the false shine from what would harm us, and let us never be surprised in the Hereafter by what You have already shown us here. Keep our feet on the ground of the present moment, where Your servants act and the enemy cannot follow, and let the only promise we lean on be Yours — for Your promise alone has something real behind it.* Āmīn.
