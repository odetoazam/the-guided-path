# Everything Comes in Twos: The Pair That Points to the One

> وَمِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ
>
> *Wa min kulli shay'in khalaqnā zawjayn, laʿallakum tadhakkarūn.*
>
> **"And of all things We created pairs, that perhaps you may remember."**

---

## INTRODUCTION: The World Before the Words

Let me put you somewhere first. Before we touch a single word of this ayah, I want you to feel the ground it grows out of.

Surah Adh-Dhariyat does not open gently. It opens like a storm. *Wadh-dhāriyāti dharwā* — by the winds that scatter, scattering. The first verses are an oath sworn by moving, churning, driving forces — winds that scatter dust, clouds heavy with rain, ships running easy on the sea, angels distributing command. The surah begins in motion, in things being carried and dispersed and apportioned. And the oath lands on a promise: what you have been warned of is true, and the reckoning is real.

Then the surah does something. It takes you on a tour. It walks you through the cosmos and through your own life as if to say: *the evidence is everywhere, you are standing inside it.* It points to the sky — **"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed We are its expander"** (47). It points to the earth — **"And the earth We spread out — how excellently We smoothed it"** (48). And just before all of this, it had turned the lens inward: **"And in your own selves — will you not then see?"** (21).

So picture the original listener. This is Makkah. This is a community being told, through these scattering winds and this expanding sky, that nothing around them is idle. Everything is sworn by. Everything is constructed. Everything is *placed.* And the listener is being walked, step by step, up a staircase of signs.

And then comes our ayah — step forty-nine on that staircase:

**"And of all things We created pairs, that perhaps you may remember."**

[PAUSE]

Now here is the hook. Here is the thing I need you to be hungry for before we go in.

This ayah is not really teaching you biology. It is not just telling you that animals come in male and female. Watch what happens *immediately after* it. The very next verse says: **"So flee to Allah"** (50). And the verse after that: **"And do not set up another god beside Him"** (51).

So Allah tells you everything comes in pairs — and then, in the same breath, tells you to run to Him and to take *no* pair beside Him.

Do you feel the tension? Everything is two. **Except One.**

What does the duality of the entire universe have to do with the singularity of its Maker? Why would Allah point at a world full of pairs and use it as the launchpad for *"flee to the One"*? That connection is the secret of this ayah. And by the time we are done, I want you to see that this short verse is not a footnote about creation. It is one of the most quietly devastating arguments for *tawḥīd* in the entire Qur'an.

Let's earn it.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: *Zawjayn* — The Word That Cannot Stand Alone

Let me teach you something about Arabic before we open the word.

In English, when we say "a pair," we are usually naming the set — two shoes, two gloves, the couple. The word "pair" points at the *togetherness.* But Arabic has a word that works from the opposite direction, and the difference is everything.

The word is **زَوْج** — *zawj.* And here is the strange, beautiful thing about it: *zawj* does not mean "the pair." *Zawj* means **one member of a pair.** A single husband is a *zawj.* A single wife is a *zawj.* One shoe is a *zawj* of the other. The word names an individual — but an individual *defined entirely by its relationship to another.*

Sit with that. *Zawj* is a word that, by its very meaning, cannot stand alone. You cannot be a *zawj* by yourself. The moment you say "*zawj*," you have implied a second thing that this first thing belongs to. It is a word with a built-in incompleteness. A word that always points beyond itself.

Now look at what Allah actually says in our ayah. He does not say *khalaqnā zawjan* — "We created a *zawj*," one of a pair. He says:

> **خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ**
>
> *khalaqnā zawjayn*

And *zawjayn* is the **dual** — the *-ayn* ending in Arabic means *exactly two.* So He is saying: of every single thing, **We created two-of-a-pair.** Two members, each of which is itself a *zawj* — each one already leaning toward, already defined by, the other.

[PAUSE]

Notice something. Allah could have built this picture with the language of *opposites* — black and white, hot and cold, enemy and enemy. He didn't. He built it with the language of *mates.* *Zawj* is the word for spouse. It is the word of marriage, of fitting-together, of complement. The Qur'an is not describing a universe at war with itself. It is describing a universe **married to itself** — paired, matched, every piece made to lock into its counterpart.

Let me give you the analogy that I think this word is doing real work with — and watch, because this analogy is going to carry the entire ayah.

You know the word "up" only because there is "down." Erase "down" from existence — not just the word, the *reality* — and "up" collapses into meaninglessness. There is no up in a universe with no down. The same is true of left and right, of inside and outside, of before and after, of light — which you only perceive because there is darkness to perceive it against. Every one of these is a *zawj.* Each one is real, but each one is real *only in reference to its mate.* Pull the mate away and the thing dissolves.

This is the deep structure of created reality. **To be created is to be a *zawj.*** To be created is to be one half of something — to be defined by contrast, to be knowable only against a backdrop of what you are not. The male is known against the female. The day is known against the night. The sky, raised up in verse 47, is known against the earth, spread out in verse 48. Mercy is known against justice. Even your own existence — you know you are *something* because there was a time you were *nothing,* and there will be a death against which this life is measured.

Everything that has been made is one half of a pair.

[PAUSE — let it land]

And now — *now* — feel the trapdoor open beneath this.

If to be **created** is to be a *zawj,* one of a pair, defined by your opposite… then what about the One who created all of it? Can He be a *zawj?* Can He be one half of something? Can He be defined against an opposite, known by contrast, completed by a mate?

The classical mufassirūn caught this immediately. Al-Baghawī and al-Jalālayn both close their comment on this very ayah with the same line: *fa-taʿlamūn anna khāliq al-azwāj **fard*** — *so that you may know that the Creator of all these pairs is Himself **single** — unpaired.*

**Fard.** Without a second. Without a mate. Without an opposite to define Him. The entire universe is built out of pairs precisely so that the one thing in all of existence with *no* pair would stand out against it like a single light in a hall of mirrors.

That is what the word *zawjayn* is doing. It is not telling you about reproduction. It is drawing the one boundary line that cannot be crossed: **everything made is two; only the Maker is One.**

[PAUSE]

### Element Two: *Min Kulli Shayʾin* — The "Of" That Refuses to Let Anything Escape

Now I want to slow down on three little words at the front of the ayah, because the mufassirūn argued over them in a way that opens something up.

> **وَمِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ**
>
> *wa min kulli shayʾin* — "and of every thing"

The phrase is *kull* — "every," "all" — attached to *shayʾ,* which is the most universal noun in the Arabic language. *Shayʾ* means simply "a thing" — anything that can be pointed to, anything that *is.* You cannot name a category broader than *shayʾ.* It is the widest net the language can cast.

So Allah is not saying "some things come in pairs." He is saying **of every thing — of all that exists — We made pairs.** Al-Muyassar renders it: *of every kind of existing thing, We created two distinct types.* Al-Qurṭubī lists them until you lose count: male and female, sky and earth, sun and moon, night and day, light and darkness, plain and mountain, jinn and human, sweetness and sourness, even, he says, the different colors and the different tastes and the different sounds — *this one set against that one,* he writes, *as a proof of Our power.*

But here is the small word I do not want you to skip: **مِن** — *min.* "Of." Not "We made every thing a pair," but "**of** every thing We made pairs."

In Arabic this *min* is partitive — it carries the sense of "from among," "drawn out of." And that tiny preposition does something theologically precise. It quietly says: *the pairing is a feature of the created order, drawn through it from end to end — but the One doing the drawing stands outside it.* The *min* keeps the Creator on the far side of the net. He casts the net of pairs over "every thing," over all of *shayʾ* — and He is not a *shayʾ* caught in His own net.

[PAUSE]

Let me make this concrete, because it is easy to let it stay abstract.

Imagine someone who has decided that God must be *like* the things they know. They picture a being who is the "opposite" of evil, the "counterpart" to the world, the other end of some cosmic seesaw. They are, without realizing it, trying to make God a *zawj* — one half of a pair, defined against something else.

This ayah dismantles that with a single preposition. *Min kulli shayʾin* — pairing belongs to *every thing.* And God is *not a thing among things.* He is the One *from whom* things receive their pairing. To imagine Him as one pole of a duality is to drag Him down into the created order, to make the Maker into one more piece of the machine. The *min* will not allow it. The net covers everything it can reach — and by covering everything, it shows you, by its very edge, the One it cannot reach.

This is what I mean when I say read the *absence* as carefully as the presence. The ayah names pairs everywhere. The thundering silence is the one place pairs are *not* — and that silence has a name. *Al-Fard. Al-Aḥad. Al-Wāḥid.* The Single.

### Element Three: *Tadhakkarūn* — Remembering What You Already Knew

Now the last word. And this one changes what the whole ayah is *for.*

> **لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ**
>
> *laʿallakum tadhakkarūn* — "that perhaps you may remember."

Let me teach you the grammar first, because it is doing quiet work. The verb is from the root *dhāl-kāf-rāʾ,* the root of *dhikr* — remembrance, recollection, bringing-to-mind. But notice the *shape* of the word. The Leeds morphology marks it as **Form V** — in Arabic, *tafaʿʿal,* the pattern *تَذَكَّرَ.*

What is Form V? It is the reflexive, effortful form. When Arabic wants to say that an action is done *to oneself, by oneself, with effort and process,* it reaches for Form V. *ʿallama* (Form II) means "he taught"; *taʿallama* (Form V) means "he taught *himself,* he *learned* — bit by bit, with labor." So *tadhakkara* is not the passive flicker of a memory drifting up. It is the **active, deliberate, self-directed work of calling something back to mind.** It is you, sitting with something, turning it over, until it surfaces.

And here is a detail the grammarians of this very ayah point out — al-Jalālayn names it directly. The word *tadhakkarūn* is **lighter than its own origin.** The full form would be *tatadhakkarūn* — with two *tā's* back to back. But one of them has been dropped, elided, to make the word flow: *tadhakkarūn.* Allah eased the very word for "remember" on your tongue.

I offer this gently, as something the form *invites* us to consider rather than something the grammar forces: the act of remembering Him was made *lighter* than its full weight. The word for recollection is itself shortened, smoothed, made easy to say. As if to whisper that this remembering is not meant to be a crushing labor — it is meant to be *near.* It is meant to come easily, the way your own name comes easily.

[PAUSE]

But here is the heart of *tadhakkur,* and it is the most important thing in this whole section. To *remember* something is to bring back something **you already knew.**

You do not "remember" a fact you are learning for the first time. You *learn* that. You "remember" only what was, at some point, already inside you. And that is the precise word Allah chose. He did not say *laʿallakum taʿlamūn* — "that you may *know*," as if this were new information. He said *tadhakkarūn* — "that you may **remember.**"

Which means the conclusion this ayah is driving toward — *the Maker is One, flee to Him, take no pair beside Him* — is being treated by Allah not as breaking news, but as **something you already know and have forgotten.**

This connects to the deepest teaching about the human being in the Qur'an: the *fiṭrah.* The covenant of *Alastu bi-rabbikum* — "Am I not your Lord?" — when every soul, before this life, answered *Balā,* "Yes." The knowledge of the One is not a foreign import into the human heart. It was the first thing written there. The signs in creation — the pairs, the sky, the earth — are not there to *install* the knowledge of *tawḥīd.* They are there to **jog your memory.** To shake the dust off something that was buried, not absent.

That is why the pairs work the way they do. You walk through a world of twos — and the structure of every two whispers the same thing to a part of you that already knew it: *there is One who is not a two.* The signs are the nudge. The remembering is yours.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### Bridge

So look at what these three words have built, standing together.

*Zawjayn* told us: everything created is one half of a pair, defined by its opposite, leaning on its mate.

*Min kulli shayʾin* told us: this is true of **every** thing — and so, by its very edge, it shows us the One who is *not* a thing, the *Fard* who has no pair.

And *tadhakkarūn* told us: you are not being informed of this. You are being **reminded** of it. The knowledge is already in you. The universe of pairs is just turning the key.

Now let's ask what this reveals — not only about words, but about how to *live* inside a world like this.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### Theme One: The Loneliness of Being Half, and the One Who Is Whole

Let me start where you actually live.

There is an ache that nearly every human being carries, and almost no one names out loud. It is the sense of being *incomplete.* Of being one half of something, reaching for the other half. We feel it in romance, of course — the whole language of "soulmates" and "my other half" and "you complete me." But it runs far deeper and wider than romance. It is the reason we cannot bear to be fully alone. It is the reason a person can be in a crowd and still feel a hollow space inside them, a space shaped exactly like *something missing.*

Modern psychology circles this constantly. Attachment theory says we are wired from infancy to bond, to need a counterpart, to regulate ourselves through another. We are not built to be self-contained. We lean. We seek the mate, the friend, the belonging, the *other* against whom we feel real.

Now hear our ayah again, but hear it as a description of *you,* not of the cosmos:

**"And of all things We created pairs."**

You are a *zawj.* That ache of incompleteness is not a malfunction. It is the **truth of your nature** speaking. You were *made* as one half of pairs — made dependent, made leaning, made to need. You feel incomplete because, as a created thing, **you are.** Completeness is not a property creation was given. To be created is to be a *zawj,* and to be a *zawj* is to be, in yourself, not enough.

[PAUSE]

Most people respond to that ache in one of two ways, and both are quiet disasters.

The first: they try to *become whole on their own.* The cult of self-sufficiency. "I don't need anyone." They build a fortress of independence and call the loneliness inside it "freedom." But you cannot self-complete a thing that was made to be half. You can only build a more elaborate prison around the gap.

The second, and more common: they try to fill the gap with *another half.* They take a created thing — a spouse, a career, a child, an image, a following — and they make it the missing piece. They say to it, silently, *complete me.* And here is the heartbreak built into that strategy: **you are asking one *zawj* to complete another *zawj.*** You are asking one incomplete, leaning, defined-by-its-opposite thing to be the ground beneath another. Two halves cannot make a whole when *both* are half. They can only lean on each other and call it love until one of them moves and they both fall.

This is why the ayah does not stop at "you are a pair." Read what comes next, two verses later: **"So flee to Allah… and do not set up another god beside Him."**

Do you see it now? The disease and the cure, side by side. The disease is taking a *zawj* and treating it as your completion — that is *exactly* what "setting up another god" means at the level of the heart. And the cure is to *flee* — *firrū* — from every false completion **to the only One who is not a half.** The only One who is *Fard,* whole in Himself, needing no mate, leaning on nothing.

The reason He alone can hold the weight you keep putting on people is that He alone is not *also* falling. Everything you have ever asked to complete you was, itself, reaching for completion. He is the only One in all of existence who is not reaching.

[PAUSE]

So what changes tomorrow?

Here is the practical turn. Notice, this week, the moment you hand your completion to a *zawj.* The moment you think, *if I just get this, I'll be whole. If this person loves me, I'll be enough. If I achieve this, the hollow will close.* Catch it. And in that exact moment, do the small internal act this ayah is calling for — *firrū,* flee. Not flee *from* the spouse or the work or the people. Flee *with* them, and *through* them, to the One they all point to. Love your *zawj.* But stop asking your *zawj* to be your God. Let the people in your life be the gift, and let Allah be the Giver you were actually aching for the whole time.

The ache was never a sign that the right *person* was missing. It was a sign that you were built to lean — and there is only One who was built to be leaned on.

### Theme Two: A Universe Designed to Jog Your Memory

Let me bring you to the second theme through a very ordinary human experience.

You know the feeling of *almost* remembering something. A name on the tip of your tongue. A melody you can't quite place. A face you know you know. And you know the strange certainty that comes with it — you are *sure* you know this, you just can't reach it. And then someone says one word, gives you one nudge — and it all comes flooding back, and you think: *of course. I knew that the whole time.*

That experience — the *click* of recognition, the thing that was always there snapping back into place — is, I want to suggest, the closest thing in your daily life to what this ayah is actually asking of you.

Because the ayah does not say *laʿallakum taʿlamūn* — "that you may *know.*" It says *laʿallakum **tadhakkarūn*** — "that you may **remember.**" And we said: you only remember what you already knew.

So picture what Allah has actually done here. He has built an entire universe — every grain of it, *min kulli shayʾin* — and threaded a single pattern through the whole of it: **everything is paired.** Sky and earth. Day and night. Male and female. Light and dark. Land and sea. And He has placed *you,* a being who already knew Him before you were born, who answered *Balā* at the covenant, inside this universe of pairs. And the entire structure is engineered to be the *nudge* — the one word someone says that makes the whole forgotten thing come flooding back.

[PAUSE]

This reframes what a "sign" even *is.*

We tend to think of the signs of Allah as *arguments* — data points that *prove* God to a mind that didn't have Him. But *tadhakkarūn* tells you something gentler and more profound. The signs are not arguments to a stranger. They are **reminders to someone who forgot.** The pairs in creation are not lecturing your intellect into a new belief. They are tapping the shoulder of a soul that already met its Lord, saying: *remember? Remember the One who has no opposite? You knew Him before any of this. Look — everything here leans, everything here is half, everything here needs its mate. Now remember the One who needs nothing. You knew Him first.*

This is why heedlessness — *ghaflah* — is the great enemy in the Qur'an, and not, primarily, ignorance. The problem the Qur'an diagnoses in the human being is almost never "you never knew." It is **"you knew, and you forgot."** *Ghaflah* is not an empty mind. It is a buried memory. And the cure for a buried memory is not a lecture. It is a *reminder.* It is *dhikr.* It is precisely the act this ayah ends on.

And remember the gift hidden in the word's own shape: *tadhakkarūn,* lightened from *tatadhakkarūn,* one letter dropped to ease it on the tongue. The remembering He asks of you was made *light.* It is not a mountain to climb. It is a name to recall — and the whole sky is helping you recall it.

[PAUSE]

So what changes tomorrow?

Here is the practice, and it is so simple it is almost embarrassing — which is exactly the point, because *tadhakkur* was made light.

Pick one pair. Just one. The sunrise and the sunset. The breath in and the breath out. Waking and sleeping. The man and the woman in your own home. And the next time you meet that pair — really meet it, not as scenery but as a *sign* — let it do the one job Allah built it for. Let it jog your memory. Let the two-ness of it remind you of the One who is not two. *He made the night and the day a pair so that the One who is no one's pair would stand out against them.*

You don't have to manufacture the knowledge. You already have it. You just have to stop walking past the reminders. The whole universe is on the tip of your tongue, trying to help you say His name.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture: Three Steps Up a Staircase

Now let me show you the structure, because it has been standing over us this whole time and it is breathtaking once you see it.

Go back to the sequence. Three verses, climbing:

- **47** — *And the heaven We built with power, and We are its expander.* (The sky.)
- **48** — *And the earth We spread out — how excellently We smoothed it.* (The earth.)
- **49** — *And of all things We created pairs, that perhaps you may remember.* (Everything.)

Watch the camera pull back. First one pair is named — sky. Then its mate — earth. And then, in verse 49, Allah *names the pattern itself:* it's not just sky-and-earth, it's **everything, all of it, paired.** Verse 49 is the moment the listener is handed the key to the whole sequence: *oh — it's all like this. The whole thing is twos.*

And the instant you are holding that key — the instant you understand that all of creation is pairs — the surah springs its conclusion:

- **50** — *So **flee to Allah.*** (*Fa-firrū ila Allāh.*)
- **51** — *And do not set up with Allah another god. Indeed, I am to you from Him a clear warner.*

[PAUSE]

Feel the engineering. Verses 47–49 spend all their force establishing one fact: **everything that exists comes in twos.** And then verse 51 delivers the blow it was loaded for: **so do not make a two out of God.** *Lā tajʿalū maʿa Allāhi ilāhan ākhar* — do not place another god *with* Him, beside Him, as His *zawj.*

The pairs were never the point. The pairs were the *setup.* Allah spends three verses showing you that pairing is the signature of the **created** — and then forbids you, in the strongest terms, from stamping that signature onto the **Creator.** To take a second god is to treat the *Fard* as a *zawj.* It is the one category error the entire universe was built to warn you against. Every pair you have ever seen is creation's way of saying: *He is not one of us.*

That is the proof. The architecture *is* the argument.

### Bringing It Together

And now see that the linguistic journey and the two themes were one insight, approached from different doors.

The **word** *zawjayn* said: to be made is to be half. The **first theme** said: so stop asking other halves to complete you, and flee to the only Whole. They are the same truth — once from grammar, once from the ache in your chest.

The **word** *tadhakkarūn* said: you already know this; the signs only remind you. The **second theme** said: so stop walking past the reminders and let the world jog your memory. The same truth — once from a verb form, once from the tip of your tongue.

And the **architecture** said: everything is two, so never make God a two. Which is just *zawjayn* and *Fard* standing in the open at last.

One ayah. Seven words. And every road through it arrives at the same place: **everything has a pair except the One you were made to run to.**

### Questions to Carry

1. What is the *zawj* in my life that I have quietly turned into my completion — the person, the achievement, the image I am asking to fill a space only the *Fard* can fill?
2. When I feel that hollow ache of incompleteness, do I read it as a flaw to fix, or as my created nature honestly telling me what it is — and Whom it needs?
3. The ayah says "remember," not "learn." What do I *already know* about my Lord that I have let myself forget?
4. How many signs do I walk past every day — sunrise, sleep, the faces in my home — without letting a single one jog my memory?
5. If everything created leans, and only Allah does not — what would my week look like if I leaned a little less on the halves, and a little more on the Whole?

### One-Sentence Distillation

> Allah threaded pairs through every single thing He made so that the one mind contemplating them would trace the twos back to the One — and remember the God it already knew, the only One in all of existence with no pair, no opposite, and no second.

### The Closing Invitation

You do not need to go and *acquire* this. That is the mercy hidden in *tadhakkarūn.* You are not being asked to climb to a knowledge you lack. You are being asked to stop and *remember* a knowledge you were born with and buried under the noise. The universe is not testing you. It is *reminding* you — gently, constantly, in every pair you pass.

So let the next pair you notice do its quiet work. And when it nudges you, don't argue with it, and don't walk past it. Just turn — *firrū* — and run, lightly, the way the lightened word *tadhakkarūn* runs off the tongue, toward the only One who was never half.

### A Brief Duʿā'

*O Allah, You are Al-Fard, the Single — You have no pair, no opposite, and no second, and everything You made bears the mark of two so that we would recognize the One.* We confess that we are halves, leaning, incomplete — so do not let us ask any half to be what only You can be. *And You commanded us to remember, and made the remembering light upon our tongues — so unbury what we already knew of You, and let every pair we pass for the rest of our lives flee us home to You.* Āmīn.
