## INTRODUCTION

**Arabic:** قُتِلَ ٱلْخَرَّٰصُونَ

**Transliteration:** *Qutila al-kharrāṣūn*

**Translation:** *Cursed be the conjecturers.*

---

Two words.

A verb. A noun. No object, no explanation, no softening. No subordinate clause, no justification offered, no appeal to evidence. Just a verdict, cast into the air like a stone thrown from a great height.

The Quran contains the longest ayah in Arabic literature — Ayat al-Dayn, the debt verse in Al-Baqarah, filling nearly half a page with legal precision. It contains ayahs of extraordinary theological elaboration, narrative sweep, and linguistic complexity. And then here, in the middle of Surah Adh-Dhariyat, we have two words.

*Qutila al-kharrāṣūn.*

**Cursed be the conjecturers.**

Most of us pass over this ayah quickly. Two words — maybe it's a transition, maybe it's a minor rebuke. But I want to suggest to you that these two words contain one of the most severe theological statements in the entire surah. And that the *brevity* is part of the severity.

To understand what's happening here, we need to understand where we are.

---

### Rebuilding the World

Surah Adh-Dhariyat opens with a sequence of four oaths. Allah swears by *adh-dhāriyāt dhurwā* — the winds that scatter with scattering. By *al-ḥāmilāt wiqrā* — the clouds bearing a heavy load. By *al-jāriyāt yusrā* — the ships gliding with ease. By *al-muqassimāt amrā* — the angels distributing commands.

Four images. Four categories of created reality. And what do they share?

Each one is an entity moving with **complete correspondence between what it is and what it does**. The wind that scatters doesn't try to carry. The cloud that carries doesn't also try to glide. The ship that glides doesn't presume to distribute commands. Each thing operates within its proper domain, with integrity, without overreaching.

And after swearing by all of these — four witnesses to the principle of purposeful order — Allah declares: **"What you are promised is true. And the Day of Judgment will certainly come."** (51:5-6) The argument is not stated in the form of a syllogism. It is *felt*. The cosmos itself is marshaled as a witness: everything that moves moves with integrity. There is no entity in creation that *fabricates* what it does.

Then ayahs 7-9 push further: Allah swears by the heavens with their woven tracks — *ḥubuk*, the layered, interwoven structure of celestial paths — and states that speech about this truth differs among people. Whoever is *turned away* from it has been turned from something real. The sky is woven. The message is structured. The truth has been delivered with the same precision as every other ordered thing in the cosmos.

And then: *Qutila al-kharrāṣūn.*

**The verdict after the evidence. The curse after the cosmic testimony.**

Here is the question I want to leave with you before we go into the text — the question that will orient everything we discover:

**Why didn't Allah argue with them? The winds are evidence. The clouds are evidence. The heavens' woven tracks are evidence. Why, after all this evidence, does the response to the people who deny it take the form of a curse rather than a counter-argument?**

Hold that question. The answer will come. But it has to be earned.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### The Word That Hits Like a Hammer — *Qutila*

قُتِلَ

Before we look at this word in the ayah, I need to teach you something about Arabic rhetoric that will completely change how you hear it.

In English, when we want to curse something, we use a word whose *job* is to curse — "damn you," "curse you," "woe to." The cursing is explicit in the verb. Arabic works differently. Classical Arabic has a well-attested rhetorical device in which a verb built on the root **q-t-l** — to *kill* — when placed in the **passive perfect form**, functions as what grammarians call an imprecatory formula: a pronouncement of damnation.

The classical Arabic grammarian al-Farrāʾ documented this explicitly — al-Qurtubi cites him in his tafsir of this very ayah: *"ma'nā qutila la'ana"* — "the meaning of *qutila* here is *la'ana*, cursed." It is not describing a murder. It is not reporting a historical death. **It is pronouncing cosmic condemnation.**

You see this rhetorical pattern in the Quran in other places. And you see it in classical Arabic poetry. The passive perfect of q-t-l, applied to a person or group, means: *may they perish — may they be destroyed — may they be condemned.* It is the verbal equivalent of a divine malediction.

Now. Stay with the *form* for a moment.

قُتِلَ is a **passive perfect**. Not present tense. Not future tense. **Perfect.** In Arabic grammar, the perfect tense carries a sense of settled completion. The action is done. The state is established. When a judge delivers a verdict in a court, the verdict is in the perfect: *it has been decided.* When a sentence has been carried out, the report is in the perfect: *it was done.*

Allah does not say: "May the kharrāṣūn be cursed at some future point." He does not say: "The kharrāṣūn are currently being cursed." He uses the **passive perfect**. The verdict is **settled**. It is established. It is already the case.

[PAUSE]

And the root. Remember: the root here is **q-t-l** — killing. Not diminishment. Not embarrassment. Not a light rebuke. The imprecatory formula chosen here is built on the most irreversible thing in human experience: **death.** The permanence of ending. The closure that cannot be undone.

**This is not: "may you be slightly inconvenienced."** This is the language of total, irreversible ending. And it arrives in the perfect tense, as a completed matter.

Why would Allah reach for this severity? Why not a gentler formula? Why not a corrective? Why not a refutation?

That answer is entirely contained in the second word.

### The Word That Grinds — *Al-Kharrāṣūn*

ٱلْخَرَّٰصُونَ

Say this word slowly: *al-kharr-āṣ-ūn.*

Feel what happens. The guttural *khā'* — produced at the very back of the throat, almost a scraping. The *rā'* doubled by the shadda, rolled and intensified. The *ṣād* — the emphatic consonant that closes with pressure against the roof of the mouth. And then the masculine plural ending: *ūn.*

This word does not flow. It does not glide the way the ships glide in ayah 3. It **grinds**. It is phonetically abrasive. And I want you to hold that texture, because it is doing theological work — it is the sound of something that moves against the grain of reality.

The root is **خ-ر-ص (kh-r-ṣ)**. Its core meaning, documented across the classical Arabic lexicons, is: to conjecture, to estimate, to speak about something one does not know as though one knows it. Al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī in his *Mufradāt* — the great lexicon of Quranic vocabulary — captures the specific nuance: *kharaṣ* means speaking about what one does not know with the posture of one who knows.

Not ignorance. Not honest uncertainty. Not "I think, I'm not sure, let me check." **Fabricated certainty.** The claim of knowledge in the absence of knowledge.

There is even a mundane, legitimate usage of this root: classical Arabic used *kharaṣa* for estimating the weight of fruit still hanging on a tree — the approximate, imprecise, professional guess of the estate assessor who hasn't weighed the fruit but has to put a number on it. Even in that *legitimate* usage — an expert making an informed estimate — the word carries the connotation of something that falls short of verified knowledge. It is the epistemological category of the educated guess, the working hypothesis, the approximation.

The Quranic usage — as al-Jalalayn, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi, and al-Baghawi all confirm — takes this root and uses it for something darker: **the fabricators.** The ones who invent about the unseen. The ones who claim certainty about what they have not witnessed.

But the key — **the thing I need you to see** — is the *form* of this word. Not just what the root means. The *morphological form*.

This is not *khāriṣ* — the one who guesses. The form here is **خَرَّاص (kharrāṣ)** — with a doubled middle consonant, the shadda on the *rā'*. This is the Arabic morphological pattern called **faʿʿāl** (فَعَّال). In classical Arabic morphology, this form is the **intensive habitual agent noun**. It does not mean someone who does something occasionally. It means someone for whom that action is their defining, constitutive, compulsive characteristic.

Think of the difference between someone who sometimes writes poetry and a *sha'ir* — a poet, someone for whom poetry is not something they do but something they *are*. Think of *ḥallāl* — not "one who makes permissible occasionally" but one for whom permitting is the very mode of their being. Think of *ṭawwāf* — not a person who has circled the Kaaba once, but someone whose circling is their identity.

**The *faʿʿāl* form in Arabic encodes ontological commitment.** This is not behavior. This is *substance*.

[PAUSE]

So ٱلْخَرَّٰصُونَ — the plural of *kharrāṣ* in the *faʿʿāl* form — are not people who made a careless guess once. They are not people who, under pressure, produced an imprecise answer. They are not people who slipped into conjecture in a moment of weakness.

They are people for whom **fabricated certainty is now their identity**. Conjecture-without-knowledge has become their constitutive mode of engaging reality. It is not what they do on bad days. It is **what they are.**

This is why the curse arrives in the passive perfect. The *kharrāṣ* — as a *faʿʿāl* — has completed the process of becoming. The sentence is in the perfect tense because the verdict on someone whose identity is fabrication is itself settled. You cannot argue someone out of a *faʿʿāl* identity with a better point. The problem is not the point they hold. The problem is *how they hold everything.*

[PAUSE]

Now let's bring in the classical scholars' identification of who these people historically were — because their composite picture illuminates something extraordinary.

Al-Qurtubi records three separate traditions. Ibn Abbas — may Allah be pleased with him — said they are *"al-murtābūn"* — the doubters, specifically meaning the *kuhhan*, the fortune-tellers. Al-Hasan al-Basri said they are *"those who say: we will not be resurrected."* Al-Farrāʾ said they are those who called the Prophet ﷺ a madman, a liar, a sorcerer, a poet — "conjecturing what they do not know." Al-Baghawi adds that they include those who divided up the roads entering Mecca to position themselves at entry points and tell arriving pilgrims contradictory things about the Prophet ﷺ to turn them away — one saying "poet," another "sorcerer," another "madman."

What do these descriptions share?

Each group is defined not by *what they don't know* — but by *how they speak about what they don't know*. The fortune-tellers claimed access to the unseen without having it. The deniers of resurrection asserted certainty about what happens after death without having been there. Those who called the Prophet ﷺ a poet or sorcerer spoke about his nature without genuine investigation. The road-blockers in Mecca weren't even coordinated in their fabrications — al-Jalalayn calls them *ashāb al-qawl al-mukhtalif*, "people of contradictory speech." They contradicted *each other*.

Here is the stunning detail al-Baghawi gives us: these people could not even get their fabrications to agree. One said: poet. Another said: sorcerer. Another said: madman. They had no common anchor because they had no common foundation in truth.

**This is the epistemological signature of fabricated certainty: it fractures internally.** Truth coheres. People who engage reality carefully, investigating the same evidence, tend to arrive at converging conclusions. Fabrication multiplies contradictions because every fabricator is anchored in themselves, not in the subject of their speech.

The winds all know what to do. The clouds all know what to carry. The ships all know how to glide. The *kharrāṣūn* — who were supposedly experts in this man, this message, this phenomenon — couldn't agree on what to invent about him.

[PAUSE — major pause]

### The Bridge

Before we move into the thematic depths — let's name what the linguistics has shown us.

قُتِلَ: A divine verdict delivered in the language of total, irreversible ending, in the passive perfect of settled finality.

ٱلْخَرَّٰصُونَ: An intensive *faʿʿāl* agent noun for people whose habitual, constitutive practice is speaking with false certainty about what they do not know.

Notice what is absent: no object for the curse. No reason listed. No exception carved out. The verse doesn't say "cursed be the kharrāṣūn who do *this specific thing*." **The curse is total, unconditional, and without qualification.** In classical Arabic rhetoric, leaving the scope unspecified expands it — there is no subcategory of *kharrāṣ* that escapes this verdict.

And notice: Allah did not debate them. Did not issue a counter-argument. Did not correct their specific claims about whether Muhammad ﷺ was a poet or a sorcerer or a madman. He issued a verdict and moved on.

That absence — the missing argument, the missing refutation — is itself a message. We will come to it.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about words — but about reality itself.

## PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

### The Epidemic We Cannot See in Ourselves

I want to start somewhere you have already been this week.

You opened a platform — a phone in your hand, a feed of people speaking. Someone whose voice carries weight in your community posted something about a Quranic verse. With complete confidence. No citation, no qualification, no "I think" or "as far as I understand." The tone of someone who has personally verified this over years of study. And it was forwarded. And forwarded again. And became, by the afternoon, a settled piece of information in the minds of people who trust that voice.

Or: you were in a conversation about a contested historical question, and someone said — with the casual certainty of someone stating the weather — a claim about what "Islamic scholars believe" or "the Quran actually says" about something. And rather than challenge it, you nodded, because the certainty itself was persuasive.

Or — and this one is harder — you were the person doing the speaking.

This is the world we live in now. **The infrastructure for broadcasting fabricated certainty has never been cheaper or faster.** The social reward for confident speech has never been higher. The friction of saying "I don't know — let me find out" has never felt steeper.

We have built our information environment largely by *kharrāṣūn* dynamics.

[PAUSE]

Here's what I need you to understand about the psychology of this. **Fabricated certainty does not usually feel dishonest from the inside.** This is what makes it so spiritually dangerous — it often feels like sincerity. The person who says "the Prophet ﷺ did this because of political necessity" has usually convinced themselves they've engaged with the evidence. The person who misattributes a hadith to the Prophet ﷺ when it is actually a saying of a companion — they spread it because they believe it and want others to have what they have.

Al-Muyassar describes the *kharrāṣūn* as *ghāfilūn* — heedless, not calculating. Many of them are not lying in the sense of knowingly stating falsehoods. They are **heedless of what knowing would require**. They have not paused to ask: what would it actually take to know this? What is the epistemic cost of this claim? What kind of investigation would verify it?

The Dunning-Kruger research in modern psychology identified a specific and disturbing pattern: **the less someone actually knows about a domain, the less they are aware of what knowing that domain would require.** The person with the most confident hot take about the Arabic morphology of an ayah is often the person who hasn't studied Arabic grammar. The person most certain about "what all scholars agreed on" is often the person who hasn't opened a commentary.

And here is where the *faʿʿāl* form becomes personally confrontational.

The *faʿʿāl* is not describing a lapse. It's describing a **settled habit**. A person can make a mistake — anyone can be wrong. But the *kharrāṣ* — with the doubled *rā'*, with the intensified form — is someone who has **practiced** this until it is their mode. They have gotten so good at performing certainty that the performance no longer feels like a performance. The habit has become identity.

[PAUSE]

Ask yourself honestly: in what domains have you done this? Not lying — not deliberate deception — but speaking from the posture of knowing, about something you have not genuinely investigated?

Maybe about a verse you think you understand because you've heard it quoted many times. Maybe about a historical event in early Islamic history that you have a strong feeling about but have never read primary sources for. Maybe about what "Islam says" about a contemporary question you've never traced to scholarship.

**The *kharrāṣ* is not always the villain. Sometimes it is simply the unreflective believer who has never been asked to slow down and account for the basis of their certainty.**

[PAUSE — major pause]

### The One Who Sets Themselves Against the Cosmos

Now I want to take you back to the beginning of the surah — because there is something architectural happening here that will explain why the severity is what it is.

The surah opened with four oaths: the scattering winds, the cloud-bearing winds, the gliding ships, the distributing angels. Four entities moving with **complete integrity toward their purpose**. Each thing doing precisely what it knows, within precisely the domain it was given.

The wind does not claim to distribute commands. The cloud does not claim to glide like a ship. The angel does not decide to scatter randomly. Everything in the cosmos moves within the limits of its nature, without overreaching, without fabricating a role it was not assigned.

And then: *qutila al-kharrāṣūn.*

The surah has set up a contrast. On one side: the winds, the clouds, the ships, the angels — operating with integrity, within their domains, within their knowledge. On the other side: human beings who take their one faculty — reason — and **apply it without limit**, without acknowledgment of its edges, to domains that require revelation to access.

The resurrection. The unseen. The nature of prophethood. The reality of divine judgment. These are not domains where human reason, operating alone, produces reliable output. They require the divine input — the *waḥy*, the revelation — to be navigated truthfully.

The *kharrāṣūn* bypassed that. They did not await the message. They conjectured the answer. **They acted as though their estimate — produced in the absence of the evidence — was sufficient.** Where the clouds waited for the right conditions to carry rain, the *kharrāṣūn* announced rain without looking at the sky.

[PAUSE]

Here is the deepest thing I want to say, and I want you to sit with it.

In the context of this surah — which has sworn by four witnesses to the principle of ordered integrity, which has declared the promise of Allah to be true with the same certainty as the winds that scatter — **the *kharrāṣūn* are not merely wrong about specific facts. They are out of alignment with the entire structure of the cosmos.**

Every ordered thing in creation is an act of epistemic humility: it does what it was made to do and nothing beyond. The cloud doesn't decide it knows better than the atmospheric pressure that determines where it should be. The ship doesn't decide it can fly because it moves so smoothly on the water. **Created things operate within the knowledge given to them, and that integrity is what makes the cosmos run.**

The *kharrāṣūn* are the breaking of that principle. They are human beings — the only creatures given the freedom to align with reality or fabricate against it — who chose fabrication. And in a surah that is specifically about the ordered integrity of the cosmos as evidence for divine truth, **this is the deepest possible violation**.

[PAUSE]

And here, at last, is the answer to the question I asked at the beginning.

**Why didn't Allah argue with them?**

Because a counter-argument assumes the person you're addressing is operating in a domain where argument and evidence are relevant. It assumes they arrived at their conclusion through some form of investigation, however flawed, and therefore a better investigation could reach them. It assumes *method* is shared.

But the *kharrāṣūn* — the *faʿʿāl*, the habitual conjecturers — **bypassed method entirely.** They did not arrive at their conclusions through investigation. They performed conclusions. Refuting their specific claims — "you said he was a sorcerer, but here is why he wasn't" — doesn't address the posture. You cannot debate your way into the heart of someone who decided the outcome before examining the evidence.

**When the problem is the method, a counter-argument is the wrong response.** The only response to a method problem is a verdict on the method itself.

And so: *qutila.* Cursed. In the passive perfect. Settled. Final.

[PAUSE — major pause]

There is something almost clarifying in the directness of this. The curse does not dignify the specific fabrications by treating them as positions worthy of extended theological rebuttal. **The act of issuing a detailed counter-argument would, itself, imply that the *kharrāṣ* posture is a respectable epistemological mode.** It would say: you are playing the evidence game, just badly — let me show you how to play it better.

But the surah refuses that. Instead: *qutila.* **This posture — speaking without knowing, with the identity of one who always speaks without knowing — is not a faulty argument. It is a faulty orientation toward reality itself.** And the cosmos, which has borne witness to its own integrity in ayahs 1-6, has no patience for the one who sets themselves against that integrity.

## CLOSING SYNTHESIS

### The Architecture Revealed

Now — after the linguistic work and the thematic journey — I want to show you the structure that was there all along.

The first movement of this surah has three stages:

**Stage 1 (51:1-6): The Evidence.** Four cosmic witnesses to ordered integrity. The verdict: what you are promised is true.

**Stage 2 (51:7-9): The Testimony.** The heavens with their woven tracks bear witness. The truth is structured and delivered. Some are turned away from it.

**Stage 3 (51:10-11): The Verdict.** *Qutila al-kharrāṣūn* — cursed be the habitual fabricators. *Al-ladhīna hum fī ghamratin sāhūn* — those who are drowned in heedlessness, oblivious.

This is a judicial movement: evidence → testimony → verdict. The curse is not emotionally scattered throughout the surah. It arrives at precisely the right moment, as the conclusion of a case that has been built carefully. The brevity of the verdict — two words — is itself judicial. A good judge does not repeat the entire case before pronouncing sentence. The case has been made. The verdict is final. **Two words.**

And notice: the verse that follows (51:11) tells us what the *kharrāṣūn* are doing — *fī ghamratin*, drowned in a depth, *sāhūn*, oblivious. They are not anxious. They are not conflicted. They are floating in the fog of their own fabrication, heedless of the cosmos bearing witness around them.

**The kharrāṣ is not the person wracked with doubt. It is the person so accustomed to performing certainty that they have lost the capacity to feel the weight of their own ignorance.**

---

### Bringing It Together

The linguistic discovery and the thematic insight are the same truth from two directions.

Morphologically: the *faʿʿāl* form of *kharrāṣ* means this is not a behavioral lapse but an identity. Thematically: the danger of fabricated certainty is precisely that it becomes constitutive — it replaces the *habit of seeking* with the *habit of performing certainty*, until the person no longer experiences the discomfort of not-knowing that would otherwise drive them toward truth.

Linguistically: the passive perfect *qutila* signals settled judgment, irreversibility, finality. Thematically: there is a category of person who has moved beyond the reach of counter-argument — not because Allah cannot guide them, but because the *method* of the *kharrāṣ* is systematically resistant to the kind of honest encounter with evidence that guidance requires. **You cannot be guided by a revelation you have already decided to perform certainty about.**

And the sound of the ayah itself — *qutila al-kharrāṣūn* — the blunt hammer blow of *qutila*, the grinding grain of *kharrāṣūn* — is the sound of cosmic judgment delivered with cosmic economy. The cosmos speaks briefly. Only fabrication requires elaborate speech.

### Questions to Carry

1. **In what domains am I most likely to speak with confidence I have not earned?** Where does my certainty outpace the depth of my actual investigation?

2. **When I encounter a question about faith, about God, about what the Quran or the Prophet ﷺ says — is my first instinct to *seek*, or to *speak*?** Am I more comfortable performing an answer than sitting with a question?

3. **What would it cost me — socially, in terms of how others perceive me — to say "I genuinely don't know" in the conversations where I usually project confidence?** Am I willing to pay that cost?

4. **The voices I amplify — the accounts I share, the people I forward, the claims I repeat in conversation — are they speaking from knowledge, or from the well-performed posture of knowing?** Have I checked?

5. **Have I ever had a moment where I realized, after the fact, that I had been a *kharrāṣ* about something I had been confidently wrong about?** What made me realize it? What changed in how I hold my certainty after that?

### One-Sentence Distillation

**Allah does not curse the one who doubts — He curses the one who has made fabricated certainty their constitutive identity, speaking about what they cannot know as though they know it, and thereby breaking ranks with the entire cosmos, which holds together precisely because every created thing does what it knows and nothing beyond.**

### The Closing Invitation

What changes after today?

Not everything. Just one thing.

Find a place in your life — one specific place — where you have been performing certainty you have not earned. A question about this religion you have been answering with confidence you never traced to a source. A position you hold on something sacred that you have never actually studied. A claim you regularly share because it *sounds* right, because it comes from a voice you trust, because you have never asked: *what would it actually take to know this?*

And in that place — **choose the cosmos over the conjecture.** Not with shame. Not with self-flagellation. Simply with the quiet dignity of a created thing returning to its proper function.

The winds scatter with integrity. The clouds carry with integrity. The ships glide with integrity. The angels distribute with integrity. The entire visible cosmos bears witness to what it means to operate within the knowledge you have been given, within the domain you were assigned, without fabricating what you were not equipped to know.

You were given reason. You were given the capacity to investigate, to ask, to trace claims to their sources, to say "I don't know, let me find out." You were given — in this very revelation — direct access to knowledge of the unseen that no amount of conjecture could ever produce.

**Use what you were given. And let what you were not given be a question, not a performance.**

That is the invitation. That is alignment with the cosmos. That is, perhaps, the beginning of the path away from the identity the *faʿʿāl* form describes — back toward the person who can receive truth rather than manufacture it.
