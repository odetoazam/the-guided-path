# The Three Retractions: How Denial Contradicts Itself

## The Ayah

**Arabic:**

> بَلْ قَالُوٓاْ أَضْغَـٰثُ أَحْلَـٰمٍۭ بَلِ ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ بَلْ هُوَ شَاعِرٌۭ فَلْيَأْتِنَا بِـَٔايَةٍۢ كَمَآ أُرْسِلَ ٱلْأَوَّلُونَ

**Translation:**

> "Rather, they say, 'A jumble of confused dreams; rather, he has invented it; rather, he is a poet. So let him bring us a sign just as the earlier messengers were sent.'"

---

## Introduction: The World This Ayah Walks Into

Picture a courtroom. The accused stands before the bench. The prosecutor rises, faces the jury, and says:

*"Your honour, this man stole the watch. Actually — no — he didn't steal it, he found it on the street. Actually — no — there is no watch. There never was a watch."*

What just happened?

The prosecutor didn't make one accusation. He made three. And each one **cancels the previous one**. If the man stole it, the watch existed. If he found it, he didn't steal it. If there's no watch, neither of the first two stories can be true.

A jury would dismiss the case in thirty seconds. Not because they decided the accused was innocent. Because the accusation **collapsed under itself**.

[PAUSE]

This is the world we are walking into. The Quran is being recited in Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ is standing in front of his own tribe, the people who knew him as *al-Amīn* — the Trustworthy — for forty years. They cannot say he is a liar by character, because the entire city has spent four decades knowing he is not. So what do they say?

They say everything. All at once. Each accusation eating the last.

And Allah — in the ayah immediately before this one — has just said something that should make every word in this verse tremble. He said: **"My Lord knows the speech in the heaven and the earth, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing."** *(21:4)*

The next ayah — this ayah — opens with the word **"qālū"** — they said.

He hears speech.
They speak.
And what comes out of their mouths is preserved here forever, locked into the Quran, recited by hundreds of millions of tongues every day for fourteen centuries — as evidence of a single thing.

When you reject truth, you cannot stop talking. And nothing you say will hold together.

This is not a verse about *them*. This is a verse about a **mechanism** — the architecture of denial itself. And once you see it, you will see it everywhere. Including, sometimes, inside yourself.

# PART 1 — THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element One: The Three بَلْ — A Word That Cancels What Came Before

Let me teach you a small piece of Arabic before we open this ayah.

In Arabic, there is a particle called **bal** (بَلْ). It is a single syllable. Two letters. And it does something very specific. It is called a particle of *idrāb* — which comes from the root meaning "to turn aside, to retract, to abandon."

When an Arab speaker says **bal**, they are doing one of two things:

1. **Correcting themselves** — "I went to the market, *bal* — actually, the bakery." (I'm taking back the first thing and replacing it with something more accurate.)
2. **Transitioning to a stronger or different point** — "He is rich, *bal* he is the richest man in the city." (I'm not contradicting; I'm escalating.)

In English, the closest equivalents are *"rather,"* *"nay,"* or *"actually."* The word doesn't translate cleanly because English doesn't have a single particle that does this work.

Classical commentary (al-Jalalayn) actually classifies all three of these particular *bal*s the same way: as *intiqāl* — transition from one aim to the next — rather than the sharper *ibṭāl*, outright correction of an error. That distinction matters less than it looks. Even a string of pure "transitions" has to land somewhere, and here it lands three times in a row on theories that cannot coexist. Call it transition, call it retraction — the destination is the same collapse.

Now hold that in your mind, and look at this ayah:

> **بَلْ** قَالُوٓاْ أَضْغَـٰثُ أَحْلَـٰمٍۭ **بَلِ** ٱفْتَرَىٰهُ **بَلْ** هُوَ شَاعِرٌۭ
>
> ***Bal*** they say: a jumble of dreams. ***Bal*** he invented it. ***Bal*** he is a poet.

Three **bal**s. Three retractions. In a single sentence.

[PAUSE]

Notice what this means. Each **bal** is taking back the claim that came before it.

- The **first bal** retracts whatever Allah just described them saying in the previous ayahs. *No, no — actually they say it's confused dreams.*
- The **second bal** retracts the dream theory. *No — actually, he made it up.*
- The **third bal** retracts the fabrication theory. *No — actually, he's a poet.*

Stay with me. This is not three separate accusations made by three different people. The grammar makes it one collective voice — **qālū**, *they said*, plural — moving through three positions in a single breath.

(Worth a brief honest pause here: not every classical commentator reads it exactly that way. Al-Muyassar frames it instead as three factions — *"some said... others said... others said..."* — each holding one theory rather than one voice cycling through all three. The Quran's own plural **qālū** allows both readings. But notice it doesn't matter which one is correct. Whether it is one mind restlessly discarding its own excuses or three minds each settling on a different excuse, the outcome is identical: mutually exclusive theories attached to the same text, and none of them able to survive contact with it.)

And here is what is devastating about that. The three accusations **cannot all be true at the same time.**

If the Quran is "**aḍghāth aḥlām**" — confused dreams the Prophet is reporting — then he isn't *fabricating*; he's recounting what he believes is a genuine vision. He is sincere but mistaken.

If he has "**iftarāhu**" — *deliberately fabricated it* — then it isn't dreams; it is calculated invention. He is dishonest, not deluded.

If he is "**shāʿir**" — a *poet* — then it is neither dreams nor fabrication; it is artistic composition flowing from natural eloquence. He is neither sincere-but-wrong nor calculated-and-lying; he is just gifted.

Three theories. **Sincere delusion. Calculated lying. Natural artistry.** Each one excludes the other two.

And they say all three. In one sentence.

Here is what's remarkable. Allah did not need to refute them. He just had to **record them**. Their words refute each other.

[PAUSE]

This is the linguistic signature of **a position with no foundation.** When someone has truth, they say one thing and stand on it. When someone has only the desire to reject, they cycle through positions because no single position survives examination. The first **bal** appeared because "dreams" couldn't account for the Quran's coherence. The second **bal** appeared because "fabrication" couldn't account for its beauty. The third **bal** appeared because "poetry" couldn't account for its content. And the cycle could keep going. Allah just shows us three iterations because three is enough.

Notice something else. The Quran is the very text being denied. And inside the text being denied, Allah preserves their denial *verbatim*. Forever. Their three contradicting sentences are now the most-recited record of their own incoherence in human history.

This is what it looks like when a word — a single two-letter particle — does theological work. **Bal**, repeated, is a confession of instability. Allah did not have to call them confused. He let their grammar do it.

## Element Two: أَضْغَاثُ أَحْلَامٍ — The Texture of a Word

Now the first accusation: **aḍghāth aḥlām** — translated as "confused dreams" or "a jumble of dreams."

But that translation is doing the word a disservice. Let me show you what it actually means.

**Aḍghāth** is the plural of **ḍighth**. And a **ḍighth**, in classical Arabic, is a very specific physical thing.

It is what you get when a person walks through a meadow and grabs a fistful of whatever's growing there — grass, weeds, twigs, dry stalks, fresh shoots, herbs, thistles. A handful of mixed plants. Some useful, some useless. Some sweet-smelling, some thorny. All grabbed together with no sorting, no system, no purpose. A **ḍighth** is *a tangled handful*.

Feel the word in your mouth: **aḍ-ghāth**. The *ḍ* is a heavy, full-mouthed letter. The *gh* is a deep, throat-clearing sound. The word itself sounds tangled. It sounds like something dense and matted. The sonic texture of the word is doing the work of describing the thing.

[PAUSE]

So when they say the Quran is **aḍghāth aḥlām**, they are not saying "a confused dream." They are saying:

*"It's like he reached into the long grass of his sleeping mind and pulled out a fistful — some recognisable shapes, some random debris, some thorns, some flowers — all tangled together. There's no design. It's just whatever happened to be there."*

This is a sophisticated accusation. It's not crude. They are not calling him a liar in this first claim. They are saying: *the man is sincere, but what he's bringing us is the random output of a sleeping mind.* It's the seventh-century equivalent of "his subconscious is generating these words." They are explaining away the Quran by reducing it to neurological noise.

Now sit with what's happening. The accusation **aḍghāth aḥlām** is itself coherent and structured. It uses a precise Arabic metaphor. It deploys a sophisticated psychological theory. It is the kind of statement that requires a working, ordered mind to produce.

And it is being levelled at the most coherent, most ordered, most internally consistent text the Arabs had ever heard. A text whose every chapter is structured. Whose every metaphor connects to every other metaphor. Whose grammar is precise and whose meanings are layered.

You cannot call a text **a tangled fistful of weeds** when it is, demonstrably, the most carefully ordered speech in your language.

So they say it. And then they immediately retract it. *Bal — actually, no, he invented it.* Because the moment they test "tangled fistful of weeds" against the actual text, it fails. So they reach for the next theory.

Notice what Allah is doing by recording the *exact* word they used. He could have written "*they said it was nonsense.*" He preserved **aḍghāth**. Why? Because the word's specificity becomes evidence against itself. To accuse the Quran of being *aḍghāth* — a chaotic handful — you must use a word that demonstrates your own capacity for orderly speech. You disprove your accusation by being able to make it.

[PAUSE]

This is what it means that Allah's Book is His own defence. He doesn't refute their first claim. He records it in the very text it is denying. The text's coherence — the sheer fact that *this* sentence we are reading right now is a piece of it — annihilates the accusation.

Here is what stays with me about this word. **Aḍghāth** is what your hand looks like after grasping in the dark. You don't know what's there; you just close your fist on whatever's in reach. It is the physical posture of someone reaching without seeing.

And that is precisely the posture of every person who rejects truth without examining it. Their hand closes on whatever theory is nearest. *Dreams. Fabrication. Poetry.* A fistful of weeds. **Aḍghāth.**

The word they used to accuse the Quran was the word that diagnosed themselves.

## Element Three: The Passive That Slips Out — كَمَآ أُرْسِلَ ٱلْأَوَّلُونَ

Now the demand at the end: *"Let him bring us a sign **just as the earlier ones were sent**."*

In Arabic: **kamā ursila al-awwalūn**.

There is something strange in the grammar here. Stay with it for a moment.

Notice that the verb **ursila** — *was sent* — is in the **passive voice**. Arabic has both an active form (*arsala*, "He sent") and a passive form (*ursila*, "was sent / he/it was sent"). The grammatical choice matters.

If they wanted to say *"as the first ones brought signs,"* the verb would be active. *Atā al-awwalūn bi-āyatin* — "the earlier ones brought a sign."

But that is not what they said. They said: **as the earlier ones *were sent*.** Passive. Past tense. With the subject *al-awwalūn* — *the earlier ones*.

Here is the question. **Sent by whom?**

The grammar of the passive doesn't name the agent. But every Arabic speaker hearing this sentence knows: a messenger doesn't send himself. A messenger is sent. By Allah.

[PAUSE]

So look at what just happened. They accused him of three things — confused dreams, fabrication, poetry — all three of which deny that the Quran is from Allah. And then in the *same breath*, they slip into a grammatical construction that **presupposes** Allah sends messengers. *"Like the first ones were sent."* By whom? By the One they just spent three sentences denying is speaking through this man.

Let me sit with this. The three **bal**s denied that the Quran is divine speech. The closing demand uses a passive verb that confesses divine sending. They cannot keep their grammar consistent with their theology for the length of one sentence.

The word **al-awwalūn** — *the first ones, the earlier ones* — is also worth pausing at. Who are the *awwalūn*? The earlier prophets. Mūsā. ʿĪsā. Ṣāliḥ. Hūd. The very prophets whose stories the Quran is telling. The prophets they grew up hearing about.

So what they are actually saying is: *"We accept that earlier prophets were genuinely sent by Allah and given signs. We accept the framework. We just reject this one."*

This is the move. They have not rejected prophethood. They have rejected *this* prophet. They believe in the institution; they refuse the latest installment.

And here is what is devastating about that. The previous prophets came with signs that their peoples *also rejected*. Classical commentary (al-Jalalayn) glosses "the earlier ones" here concretely — *ka-l-nāqati wa-l-ʿaṣā wa-l-yad*, "like the she-camel, and the staff, and the hand" — naming exactly which signs are being invoked: Ṣāliḥ's she-camel, Mūsā's staff, and Mūsā's own hand drawn glowing from his cloak. The Pharaoh saw the staff become a snake and called it magic. He saw the glowing hand and called that sorcery too. Thamūd saw the camel emerge from the rock and slaughtered it. The signs did not produce belief. The signs were given, and rejection followed anyway.

So when they say *"Let him bring us a sign as the earlier ones were sent,"* they are asking for something that — by their own retrospective acknowledgment — did not work even when it was given. They are demanding the very thing that, in every prior case, was rejected by the people it was sent to.

What they actually want is **not a sign**. What they want is the reassurance that they would have believed the earlier prophets — without having to face the fact that they are doing exactly what those rejecters did.

The passive voice gave them away. *Ursila* — *was sent*. The grammar confessed what the soul refused.

## Bridge

We have looked at three pieces of this ayah. The triple **bal** — three retractions cancelling each other. The word **aḍghāth** — a tangled fistful, which described their own grasping rather than the Quran. And the passive **ursila** — a verb that confessed divine sending while the speaker was busy denying it.

What do these three things have in common?

In every case, the rejection **undermines itself with its own grammar.** The form of the denial is evidence against the denial. Allah didn't have to argue. He just recorded the speech and let it speak.

Now let's ask what this reveals — not just about words, but about the architecture of the human heart when it refuses to see.

# PART 2 — THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme One: The Architecture of Denial

You have seen it in your own life. Maybe in someone you love. Maybe — if you are honest — in yourself.

Someone is faced with a truth they don't want to accept. Maybe it's a doctor's diagnosis. Maybe it's evidence about a relationship. Maybe it's a financial reality. Maybe it's the truth about their own behaviour.

Watch what their mind does. Not in one moment, but across hours, days, weeks.

First they say: *"This isn't real. The test is wrong. The evidence is fabricated."*

When that doesn't hold up, they shift: *"Okay, it's real, but it's not what it seems. It's been blown out of proportion. Context is missing."*

When that doesn't hold up, they shift again: *"Okay, even if it is what it seems, it's not my fault. I had reasons. They made me. The system failed."*

Each new position **retracts the previous one**. Each one is a **bal**. The mind is not converging on truth. The mind is *generating successive theories whose only common feature is that they all conclude with: "therefore I do not have to change."*

[PAUSE]

This is what the Quran is showing us in 21:5. It is not just describing the Makkans. It is describing **the universal mechanism of motivated rejection**. The triple **bal** is the X-ray of a heart that has decided in advance.

Stay with this. The Makkans were not stupid. They were among the most linguistically sophisticated people on earth. They could hear that the Quran was extraordinary. They could feel its weight. They were not failing to perceive it. They were perceiving it perfectly — and refusing to act on what they perceived.

So their minds did what minds do. Generated theories. *Dreams. Fabrication. Poetry.* Each theory had to fail before the next one was reached for. And each theory failed because **the truth was actually true** — there was no theory that could account for the Quran except the one they refused: that it was from Allah.

Here is the diagnostic. **When you hear yourself moving through a sequence of explanations for the same fact, each one cancelling the last — pause.** That is the sound of *bal, bal, bal.* That is not the sound of seeking truth. That is the sound of fleeing it.

Truth-seeking sounds different. Truth-seeking sounds like: *"I thought it was X. Then I tested X and it failed. So I now believe Y, and here is why Y survives where X did not."* That is not retraction. That is **revision under evidence**.

The triple **bal** is not revision. It is *evasion in the costume of revision*. Each new theory is grabbed not because the previous one was tested and failed honestly, but because the previous one **threatened to require something** of the speaker.

And the practical question this raises: in what part of your life are you currently saying *bal* to yourself?

Where are you cycling through explanations rather than facing the one that would actually account for the evidence — because that one would require you to change? Maybe in a relationship. Maybe in your worship. Maybe in a habit. Maybe in how you treat someone. The Quran is not asking you to interrogate the Makkans. The Makkans are settled; their case is closed. The Quran is showing you a mirror.

The next time you hear yourself reaching for a third theory in five minutes — stop. Ask: *what would I have to do if the simplest explanation were true?* That answer is usually the explanation you are running from.

## Theme Two: The Cosmic Listener

Return now to where we started. The ayah immediately before this one — 21:4 — said:

> "My Lord knows the speech in the heaven and the earth, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing."

And the very next breath — 21:5 — opens with **bal qālū** — *"rather, they said..."*

[PAUSE]

Feel the placement. Allah declares that He hears every word in the heavens and the earth. Then immediately, He records what was said. The structural pairing is not accidental. He is *demonstrating* the claim of 21:4 by *executing* it in 21:5.

You said it. He heard it. He wrote it down.

Now widen the camera. Every word in this ayah — every syllable of the triple **bal**, every consonant of **aḍghāth**, every grammatical choice they made — was spoken in seventh-century Makkan air, in some street, perhaps in someone's home, or in a gathering by the Kaʿba. The speakers thought they were having a conversation. They thought their words would dissolve into the air.

Their words are now the most-recited speech of the rejecters in human history. Hundreds of millions of tongues have repeated, are repeating right now, will repeat tomorrow: **bal qālū aḍghāthu aḥlām, bal iftarāhu, bal huwa shāʿir.**

They are speaking, fourteen centuries after they died, every time the surah is read.

[PAUSE]

This is what it means that *He is As-Samīʿ, the All-Hearing.*

It is not just that He hears in the moment. It is that **what He hears, He preserves**. The words you speak in private are not private to Him. The conversations you have in your car. The things you say about people when they are not there. The complaints you whisper to yourself. The denials you mutter when Allah's signs come close to your heart.

He hears.

And what He chooses to preserve, He preserves. And what He chooses to dignify with His Book, He dignifies with His Book. And what He chooses to leave to your record alone, He leaves to your record alone — to be returned to you on the Day when *every leaf is opened*.

Connect this to a moment of your own life. Think of a specific thing you have said — about someone, about Allah, about your own situation — that you would not want recorded. You said it. *Did you think it dissolved?*

The Makkans thought their conversations dissolved. They thought their three retracting theories were the chatter of a moment. They were wrong. The All-Hearing was listening. He preserved the chatter as evidence of itself.

This is not a verse about *fear* — though fear is a legitimate response. This is a verse about **the seriousness of speech**. Speech is not a free resource. Speech leaves a record. Some of the record is on your own scroll. Some of it, occasionally, the All-Hearing chooses to preserve in His Book — as warning, as mercy, as instruction for those who come after.

What is the practical movement here? It is this. **Watch your speech the way you would watch your speech if you knew you were on a permanent record.** Because you are. The Quran does not say *"some* speech is heard." It says **al-qawl** — *the speech*, with the definite article — generic, comprehensive. All of it.

And the gentlest form of this lesson: not all preservation is a record of error. The All-Hearing also preserves the whisper of the one who calls Him at three in the morning. He preserves the *du'a* of the parent over the child. He preserves the **subḥān Allāh** murmured under the breath. The same Hearing that captured **bal qālū** captures **alḥamdulillāh**.

The question is not: *will my speech be heard?* It is: **what kind of speech am I giving Him to hear?**

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## Bringing the Threads Together

Look at what we have seen.

The triple **bal** showed us *the architecture of denial*. The word **aḍghāth** showed us how an accusation can describe its own speaker. The passive **ursila** showed us how the rejecter's grammar betrays the theology he claims to hold. And the placement of the ayah — right after Allah's declaration that He hears all speech — showed us that **none of this dissolves**. The speech that was meant to escape is now the speech that cannot escape.

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions. Linguistically, the rejecter cannot keep his words consistent with themselves for a single sentence. Thematically, this is because the rejecter is not seeking coherence; he is seeking exit. And the All-Hearing — who is Himself the standard of coherence — is recording every step of the exit attempt.

The whole ayah, in one architectural movement, says this: **You speak. He hears. And what you say will be tested against what you say.**

## One-Sentence Distillation

When the heart refuses what the mind has perceived, the mouth produces a chain of theories that cancel each other — and the All-Hearing preserves every link of the chain as evidence of a refusal that needed no other proof.

## The Closing Invitation

Listen to yourself this week.

Not to others — to yourself. To the way you explain things to yourself when something uncomfortable is being asked of you. Listen for the *bal*. Listen for the moment when one explanation fails and you reach for another, not because you have new evidence but because the first one was about to require something of you.

When you hear it — pause. Don't argue with yourself. Just notice it. Ask the simplest question: *what would I have to accept if I stopped reaching for the next theory?*

That answer is where the ayah is pointing.

And alongside that — let the second movement land. **Let your speech this week be speech you would be content to find preserved.** Not perfect speech. Not unnaturally pious speech. Just speech that remembers it is being heard.

The All-Hearing does not need our speech to be impressive. He asks only that it be *true* — true to the situation, true to the person, true to the reality of His knowing.
