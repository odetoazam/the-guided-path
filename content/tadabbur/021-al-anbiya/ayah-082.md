# The Divers Who Worked While He Slept — And the One Who Watched the Workers

## Surah Al-Anbiya, Ayah 82

**وَمِنَ ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينِ مَن يَغُوصُونَ لَهُۥ وَيَعْمَلُونَ عَمَلًۭا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ ۖ وَكُنَّا لَهُمْ حَـٰفِظِينَ**

*Wa mina-sh-shayāṭīni man yaghūṣūna lahu wa yaʿmalūna ʿamalan dūna dhālika — wa kunnā lahum ḥāfiẓīn.*

*"And of the devils were those who dived for him and did other work besides that. And We were of them Guardians."*

---

## Building the World

I want you to picture a kingdom.

Not a kingdom of marble courtyards and silk banners — though it had those. I want you to picture the **invisible infrastructure** of a kingdom. The pipes under the streets. The accountants in back rooms. The night-shift workers who keep the city running while everyone else sleeps.

Now imagine that workforce is not human.

Imagine the laborers cannot be seen. Imagine they descend into the deepest parts of the sea and bring up what no human hand could reach. Imagine they build what no human craft could build. Imagine they work through the night and you wake to find the work done.

This is Sulayman's kingdom.

The surah has been climbing. In 21:79, Allah subjected the mountains and birds to praise alongside Dawud. In 21:80, He taught Dawud the craft of armor — *to protect you from your own violence.* In 21:81, the wind itself was placed under Sulayman's command — moving by his order to wherever he willed. And now, in 21:82, the climb reaches its summit.

Not the wind anymore. **The unseen.**

The very beings whose original mission was to whisper rebellion into the human heart — those same beings, in this kingdom, are diving for pearls.

[PAUSE]

But the ayah doesn't end with the diving. It ends with a sentence that should stop us cold:

***"And We were of them Guardians."***

Who is being guarded? And from what?

That is the question this ayah will not let go of. Stay with me.

# PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

## Element One: The Word "Min" That Quietly Refuses to Say "All"

Before we touch the ayah, I want to teach you a small Arabic feature that English speakers consistently miss. It looks like nothing. It is doing enormous work.

In Arabic, the preposition **مِنْ** (min) has many uses. One of its most important is what grammarians call **مِنْ التَّبْعِيضِيَّة** — *partitive min*. It means *some of, a portion of, a subset of.*

Compare two sentences:

> "I called the team."
> "I called **some of** the team."

The first sounds total. The second is precise. It tells you something happened to a part — and by saying so, it tells you something *did not* happen to the whole.

Now read the opening of our ayah:

**وَمِنَ ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينِ مَن يَغُوصُونَ لَهُۥ**

*"And **of** the devils were those who dived for him..."*

Not *the devils dived for him.* Not *all the devils were his workers.*

**Of** the devils. **Some** of them. A portion.

Let's sit with this.

The shayāṭīn — the rebellious unseen beings whose job description across the Quran is opposition — were not collectively conscripted. Sulayman did not command the entire host of rebellion. He commanded a **subset** that Allah specifically subjugated. Al-Muyassar's reading captures this precisely: Allah employed a portion of the shayāṭīn for him in tasks other creatures were incapable of performing — divers cannot be human labor, this is a workforce assembled *from* the unseen for what the seen world cannot do.

Notice the absence here: where is the rest? The rest is doing what shayāṭīn do. The rest is whispering. The rest is conspiring. The rest is testing every human heart on earth.

Sulayman's kingdom — even at its peak — did not abolish the shayāṭīn. It pulled a portion into service. The opposition continued. The world was not cleansed. **The most powerful prophet-king in human history did not eliminate the unseen enemy. He simply put a portion of it to work.**

[PAUSE]

Here is what this invites us to consider: When we are given any portion of authority — over a household, over a project, over our own desires — we are not given total control. We are given **a partition.** A "min." Some of the resistance is conscripted; the rest remains.

The mistake of the powerful is to confuse the partition for the whole. Sulayman never did. The very grammar of his gift refuses the illusion.

[PAUSE — major]

## Element Two: The Verb That Goes Underwater — and the Work Allah Refuses to Name

Now move to the next two verbs:

**يَغُوصُونَ ... وَيَعْمَلُونَ عَمَلًۭا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ**

*"...those who dived for him and did other work besides that."*

The first verb is غاص (ghāṣa). In Arabic, this is not generic submersion. It means **to dive — specifically into water, specifically downward, specifically to retrieve.** It is the word used for pearl divers. The classical lexicons are united on this: غَوْص is what a person does when they leave the world of breath and descend into a world they cannot survive in, to bring back something the surface world cannot reach.

So the first thing the shayāṭīn do is **descend into a realm humans cannot enter, to retrieve what human hands cannot grasp.**

That itself is the stepping stone. Hold the physical image — divers going into the deep — and ask what unseen reality it is pointing toward. Allah is showing us that **a portion of the unseen world was bringing things from a place the seen world could not reach.**

The wealth of Sulayman's kingdom did not come only from the surface. It came from **depths the human eye cannot penetrate.**

But notice what Allah does next.

He says **وَيَعْمَلُونَ عَمَلًۭا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ** — *"and they did **a work** besides that."*

Stop on those words.

عَمَلًۭا is **indefinite.** Not *the work.* Not *the building of the temple* or *the carrying of the throne* or *the construction of the high arches* — though other ayahs (Saba 34:13) tell us they built such things, and al-Tabari, glossing this very ayah, names the category further still: construction, statuary, and prayer-niches. Yet even having named the category, the classical exegete still leaves the ayah's own wording untouched — the verse itself, in this surah, refuses the inventory. Here, in this ayah, in this surah, Allah simply says: **"a work" — other than that.**

دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ — *besides that, beyond that, other than that.*

Read the absence. Allah names one task — diving — and then leaves the second task **deliberately unspecified.**

Why?

Because the point of the ayah is not the inventory. The point is **the principle.** If Allah listed every task, our minds would lock onto the catalogue — *ah, so the shayāṭīn built X, Y, and Z.* But by leaving the second task open, Allah keeps our attention on what actually matters: **that an entire category of unseen labor was operating in his kingdom, doing things we are not even being told about.**

[PAUSE]

Here is what this invites us to consider, and stay with me because it is uncomfortable: **You do not know everything that is working in your life.**

You can name some of it. You can name the diving — the visible retrievals, the obvious answered prayers. But there is a **عَمَلًۭا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ** in every life — a work besides that, going on beneath the surface of awareness, that Allah does not always name to us. Sometimes He shows us only the diving. The other work continues, unnamed.

The grammar is teaching humility about what we know is being done on our behalf — and what we don't.

[PAUSE — major]

## Element Three: The Final Sentence That Breaks Open the Whole Ayah

Now we arrive at the sentence that, if you sit with it long enough, dismantles every assumption you had about the previous lines.

**وَكُنَّا لَهُمْ حَـٰفِظِينَ**

*"And We were over them Guardians."*

Four things to notice — slowly.

**First: كُنَّا. We were.**

Past tense. Continuous. This is not "We placed a guard once at the beginning and let it run." This is **ongoing, sustained, never-paused divine oversight** for the entire duration of Sulayman's reign. Every day. Every dive. Every act.

**Second: لَهُمْ — "over them" or "for them."**

And here we hit the question the classical commentators have wrestled with for centuries. **Who is "them"?**

Two readings exist in the tradition, and the grammar permits both. They are not contradictions — they are two camera angles on the same reality. Let me build the composite.

**Reading One:** "Them" refers to the shayāṭīn themselves. Allah was guarding *them* — meaning, holding them in their assigned task, preventing them from rebelling, escaping, or sabotaging the work. Sulayman commanded; Allah enforced. The chain that held them was not Sulayman's — it was Allah's. Al-Muyassar makes the enforcement explicit: the shayāṭīn "were not able to refuse" what was demanded of them, held there by Allah's power and might. That detail matters — these are the same beings whose nature, everywhere else in the Quran, is to resist and rebel. Here, refusal itself was taken off the table.

**Reading Two:** "Them" refers to the work, or to Sulayman's kingdom, or implicitly to Sulayman himself. Allah was guarding *what they did* — preventing the shayāṭīn from corrupting the output, from inserting harm into the diving and the work, from turning their service into sabotage. Al-Jalalayn gives this reading a strikingly concrete reason: left to themselves, if they finished a task before nightfall, they would go back and *undo* it — unless kept occupied with something else. The guarding, on this reading, was not abstract oversight. It was Allah preventing a workforce that would otherwise sabotage its own labor the moment it was left idle.

Now hold both at once.

In one reading, Allah guards the workers. In the other, Allah guards the work. **Both are true.** Sulayman's kingdom required *both* — the workers held in place AND the output protected from contamination.

Notice what this does to the previous lines. We were impressed that Sulayman commanded shayāṭīn. The closing sentence quietly tells us: **the command was not actually Sulayman's strength. The command was held in place by Allah's continuous guarding.**

If Allah's *kunnā ḥāfiẓīn* lifted for one moment — the kingdom would dissolve.

[PAUSE]

**Third: حَـٰفِظِينَ — Guardians.**

The root is ح-ف-ظ. To preserve. To watch over. To protect from loss, from harm, from corruption. This is the same root as **al-Hāfiẓ** (the Preserver) and **hifẓ al-Qurʾān** (the preservation of the Quran in the heart).

Allah uses the active participle — حَـٰفِظِينَ — *ones-who-guard,* in the plural of majesty. The form denotes ongoing identity, not occasional action. He doesn't say "We guarded them" once. He says **We were, of them, guardians** — guarding was Our standing relationship to this arrangement.

**Fourth: notice the asymmetric absence between this ayah and the one before it.**

In 21:81, Allah said the wind was *subjugated* (سَخَّرْنَا) for Sulayman. He did not add "and We were over the wind guardians."

The wind doesn't need guarding. The wind has no will. The wind cannot rebel.

The shayāṭīn can. So Allah adds the guardian-clause for them and **omits it for the wind.**

This is the asymmetric absence the methodology trains us to read. The presence of *ḥāfiẓīn* in one ayah and its absence in the previous ayah is not random. It is precisely calibrated to the nature of what is being commanded. **Allah only mentions the guarding where guarding is required.** And by doing so, He silently teaches us that *the more rebellious the workforce, the more continuous the divine oversight required to keep it in service.*

[PAUSE — major]

## Bridging from Words to Reality

Look at what these three elements have shown:

The word *min* refused to give Sulayman the whole opposition — only a portion. The verb *yaghūṣūn* opened a depth no human can reach, and Allah named it. The phrase *ʿamalan dūna dhālika* opened a depth Allah refused to name. And the closing *kunnā lahum ḥāfiẓīn* revealed that the entire arrangement was suspended on Allah's continuous, unceasing guard.

Now let's ask what this reveals not just about Sulayman's kingdom — but about every kingdom, including the one you call your life.

# PART 2: THE THEMATIC DEPTHS

## Theme One: The Hidden Infrastructure of Your Life

Think about the last meal you ate.

The wheat in the bread was planted by someone you will never meet, in a country you may never visit, watered by a rain you did not pray for, harvested by hands whose names you will never learn. It was milled, transported, sold, baked, sliced, and placed in front of you by a hidden infrastructure of human labor and natural systems and economic arrangements that you contributed to none of.

You ate. The work was done.

Now extend the picture. The water in your glass came through pipes you cannot see, treated by people you do not thank, drawn from a source you cannot name. Your house is held up by foundations laid before you arrived. The internet you use was built by engineers whose contributions are invisible to you. Your immune system, *right now*, is fighting battles you are not even aware are being fought.

**This is your kingdom.** And like Sulayman, you did not build most of it. You walked into it. You inherited it. You command it without comprehending it.

[PAUSE]

Now layer the unseen on top of the seen.

The Quran tells us — and this ayah is one of its most explicit teachings on it — that **there are unseen agencies operating in the world.** Some of them whisper toward harm. Some of them, by Allah's command, are pulled into service for things we don't even know are happening. The angel of provision arranges a meeting that changes your life. The shaytan whispers a thought you reject and never speak of. The community of jinn lives parallel to ours, mostly invisible.

You think you are running your day. You are walking through an enormous unseen workforce, most of which Allah has not named to you.

The ayah is teaching us: *Sulayman knew this about his kingdom. Do you know this about yours?*

Most of us live as though our visible work explains our visible results. Sulayman's kingdom shows us the lie in that. The visible kingdom — the marble, the throne, the wisdom — was upheld by an invisible workforce. **And the invisible workforce was upheld by an even more invisible Guardian.**

[PAUSE]

Here is the practical turn. When you next sit down to eat, when you next wake up healthy, when you next have a thought that pulls you toward Allah instead of away — pause and ask:

*Who dived for this? What ʿamalan dūna dhālika is going on right now that I am not being told? And Who is guarding the workers?*

The answer to the third question is always the same. **He is. كُنَّا لَهُمْ حَـٰفِظِينَ.**

Tomorrow, before you give thanks for what you can see, give thanks for the diving you cannot.

[PAUSE — major]

## Theme Two: The Authority That Is Actually a Loan

Sulayman is the most powerful king in human history. The Quran does not deflate him. The wind moves at his command. The shayāṭīn dive at his command. The mountains echo praise around his father's recitation. He understands the language of birds and ants. He builds an army no enemy can withstand. By any earthly measure, he has won.

And then — at the end of the ayah, almost as a footnote — Allah says: **and We were over them Guardians.**

Read that as the king reading it, sitting on his throne. Imagine him hearing this revelation and stopping.

*Wait. The shayāṭīn were not actually obeying me. They were being held in place for me. By You.*

*If You stopped guarding for one breath, my entire kingdom would turn on me.*

[PAUSE]

This is what the ayah is doing. It is taking the most impressive resume in prophetic history and quietly revealing that **the resume was a sustained gift, not an earned possession.**

Now bring this home, because it is not about Sulayman.

Think about every form of authority you have ever held. Authority over a child. Authority over an employee. Authority over your own anger. Authority over your own appetite. Authority over a body you live in.

Each one feels like *yours.* You speak; the child responds. You decide; the body moves. You resolve; the anger subsides.

But every one of those authorities is suspended on a continuous *kunnā ḥāfiẓīn.* The body that obeys you today can stop obeying you tomorrow with no warning. The child that responds to you this year can become unrecognizable next year. The desire you have controlled for ten years can rise tomorrow with a force you cannot name. The mind that has worked your whole life can dim in a single afternoon.

**You do not actually command any of it. You are a king whose subjects are being held in place by Someone Else.**

The ayah is not trying to humiliate Sulayman, and it is not trying to humiliate you. It is trying to **rescue you from a delusion that will eventually break you** — the delusion that your authority is yours.

[PAUSE]

Here is why this matters now.

When the ḥifẓ lifts — and it will lift, in some area of every life — the person who thought their authority was theirs experiences it as catastrophe. *How could this happen? I had control of this.* The grief is unbearable because the assumption was unexamined.

The person who already knew their authority was a loan experiences the same loss differently. They grieve, deeply. But they do not feel betrayed. Because they always knew — the way Sulayman knew, the way this ayah teaches us to know — that **their command had always been a permission, and the permission had always been the gift.**

[PAUSE]

The practical turn is small but seismic. Tonight, before you sleep, pick one thing in your life that you usually feel in command of. A skill. A relationship. A discipline. A faculty.

Then say to Allah, slowly: *I know that this is not actually mine. I know that I command it because You guard it for me. I know that if You lift Your guarding, it ends. Thank You for guarding it today. Please guard it tomorrow.*

That sentence is the disposition Sulayman lived in. The sentence after our ayah, in 21:83, introduces a man who had to learn it the hard way — Ayyub. The surah places these two side by side for a reason. **Better to learn it Sulayman's way, while the kingdom is still standing.**

[PAUSE — major]

# CLOSING SYNTHESIS

## The Architecture We Can Now See

Stand back from the ayah. Look at the shape.

The verse opens with **مِنَ** — a partition. Some of the shayāṭīn, not all. It moves to **يَغُوصُونَ** — a descent into a realm we cannot enter. It widens into **عَمَلًۭا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ** — work Allah refuses to inventory. And it closes with **وَكُنَّا لَهُمْ حَـٰفِظِينَ** — Allah's continuous guarding of the entire arrangement.

The structure mirrors Sulayman's kingdom itself: a *partition* of authority, a *descent* into the unseen, *unnamed work* happening in the depths, all suspended on the continuous *guarding* of Allah.

It also mirrors **your life**. A partition of authority. A descent of unseen labor. Unnamed work in the depths. Suspended on the continuous guarding of Allah.

The ayah is not biography. It is mirror.

## Bringing It Together

The linguistic discoveries and the themes are the same insight from two directions.

The linguistic side told us: *Allah refuses to give Sulayman the whole opposition, refuses to name the second task, and inserts a guardian-clause exactly where rebellion was possible.*

The thematic side told us: *Your kingdom is held up by hidden labor you did not arrange, and your authority is a loan that requires continuous divine guarding to remain in your hands.*

These are one teaching. The grammar is the theology. **What Allah withheld in the language is exactly what He withheld in the kingdom — total ownership.** What He inserted in the language — *kunnā ḥāfiẓīn* — is exactly what He inserts in every life — continuous guarding without which everything would dissolve.

## Questions to Carry

1. Where in your life do you currently feel a sense of command — and how would you live differently today if you remembered it was being *guarded* into existence rather than *generated* by you?

2. What is the *ʿamalan dūna dhālika* — the work besides that — that Allah might be doing in your life right now that He has not yet named to you?

3. If Allah lifted His ḥifẓ from one specific faculty you take for granted — your sight, your memory, your patience, your faith — for one hour, what would that hour reveal about how much you had been crediting yourself?

4. The shayāṭīn whose service Sulayman commanded were not destroyed; they were held in place. What in your inner life is not yet eliminated but is currently being held in place by Allah's mercy — and how should that change your gratitude?

5. Sulayman's ayah is followed immediately by Ayyub's. Why do you think Allah placed the king of everything next to the king of nothing — and which of the two is closer to your current season?

## One-Sentence Distillation

**Even the most powerful kingdom in human history was not commanded by its king — it was guarded into obedience by its King's King, and the same is true of yours.**

## The Closing Invitation

Something should shift after sitting with this ayah. Not a resolution to do more. A subtle change in how you walk through tomorrow.

When you wake, before you check your phone, take ten seconds to notice that your eyes opened. They did not have to. Someone guarded them through the night.

When you stand and your legs hold you, take five seconds to notice. They did not have to. Someone guarded them.

When you swallow your morning water and it goes down without choking, when your heart beats without arrhythmia, when your mind boots up without failing — pause. **Someone is diving for you. Someone is doing work besides that. And Someone is guarding the workers.**

You did not arrange any of it. You inherited a Sulayman-scale kingdom and called it your ordinary morning.

Live tomorrow as a guest in your own kingdom. Sulayman did. The closing four words of this ayah were the sentence he never let himself forget.

## Brief Du'a

*Yā Ḥāfiẓ — You who guarded the divers in Sulayman's seas, guard the unseen labor of my life today. Show me only as much of the work as I can bear to see without forgetting that You are the One doing it. And when my own kingdom feels like mine, return to my heart these four words: **wa kunnā lahum ḥāfiẓīn** — and let me rest in the truth that You have always been the One holding everything in its place.*

*Āmīn.*
