ISSUES:
- [MODERATE] "The mu'min — the believer — is, at the deepest level of the word, *the one who has been made safe*" — grammatically imprecise. **مُؤْمِن** is the *active* participle of Form IV **آمَنَ**, meaning "one who believes" or "one who gives security." The *passive* participle — "one who has been made safe" — would be **مُؤْمَن** (muʾman). The root connection between أ-م-ن (security) and ʾīmān (faith) is real and well-attested in Mufradāt al-Rāghib, but the framing "has been made safe" assigns passive morphology to an active-participle word. A classical grammar reader would catch this immediately.

CONFIRMED ACCURATE (against reference data):
- Both verbs أَطْعَمَ and ءَامَنَ are correctly identified as Form IV transitive perfects.

- Al-Baghawi's note that food came "by bringing the grain-supply to Mecca" (بحمل الميرة إلى مكة) is confirmed.
- Al-Baghawi's note that security came "through the sanctuary" (بالحرم) is confirmed.
- Root derivations listed for أ-م-ن (*amn*, *amān*, *amāna*, *ʾīmān*) are all lexically attested.
- Ibrāhīm's reference "a valley without cultivation" (14:37) is correctly grounded.

SUMMARY: One moderate grammatical imprecision — the reflection maps **مُؤْمِن** (active participle) to passive meaning ("the one who has been made safe"); the spiritual root-bridge is valid but the morphological framing needs a one-word correction ("the one who *gives* security" or "the one who *finds* security") to avoid implying the passive form.
