**PART 1 — Fixes applied**

**[MODERATE #1] Translation corrected.** Both the frontmatter `translation:` field and the inline transliteration block rendered *kharāju Rabbika* as "sustained provision of your Lord." Al-Tabari's gloss is unambiguously *ajr* — recompense, reward — and al-Muyassar confirms *thawāb wa ʿaṭāʾ*. All three instances changed to **"recompense of your Lord."** The *rizq*/provision language belongs to the closing clause (*wa Huwa khayru r-rāziqīn*), which remains untouched.

**[MODERATE #2] False certainty on the kharj/kharaj contrast removed.** The original presented the one-time/sustained distinction as a classically established literary device at this ayah and attributed it to "classical scholars." Al-Tabari treats both forms as synonyms for *ajr* at this passage without activating any semantic differential. A new paragraph in Element One now states this plainly: the lexical distinction is attested in the dictionaries; whether the Quran consciously deploys it as a device here is *our inference from the word shapes, not a transmitted teaching.* The observation is preserved as a legitimate reader-level linguistic note, not a classical authority claim.

**PART 2 — Enrichments woven in**

Four insights from the tafsir sources, integrated into the flow:

1. **Al-Tabari's *mustakbirīna bil-ḥaram*** (Rebuilding the World): The Quraysh's arrogance was specifically territorial — inflated by their custodianship of the sanctuary. Added to the introduction to give the historical rejection scene its classical texture.

2. **Al-Muyassar's double-edged rhetorical question** (Rebuilding the World): The question does not only ask "are you after their money?" — it also exposes the alternative: perhaps *they* were too stingy to pay even if there were a fair price. Both horns of the dilemma eliminate the excuse.

3. **Al-Tabari's precision on *kharāju Rabbika*: reward for following His command and seeking His pleasure** (Element One): This is the richest addition. The recompense is not just "better" in quantity — it is differently sourced, calibrated to *ibtighāʾ al-mardāt*. Also woven in: al-Tabari's connection to 42:23 (love of kinship as the Messenger's only named "request," itself framed as their benefit).

4. **Al-Tabari's *al-ṭarīq al-qāṣid*** (Element Two): His description of the ṣirāṭ mustaqīm as the "aimed road, with no crookedness in it" deepens the definite-article analysis — the path is *the* path in part because it has nothing bent in it that could be confused for something else.
