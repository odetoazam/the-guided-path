# The One Who Knows Both Halves
## Why Shirk Is a Failure of Knowledge

> [ayah:23:92] عَـٰلِمِ ٱلْغَيْبِ وَٱلشَّهَـٰدَةِ فَتَعَـٰلَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ
>
> *ʿĀlimi-l-ghaybi wa-sh-shahādati fa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn*
>
> **"Knower of the unseen and the witnessed — so exalted is He above whatever they associate."**

---

## INTRODUCTION

### Where We Are Standing

We are near the end of Surah Al-Mu'minun. The surah opened with a promise — *qad aflaha al-mu'minūn* — the believers have succeeded. It then walked us through creation: a drop of fluid becoming a clinging clot becoming bones clothed in flesh. It walked us through prophets: Nūḥ in his ark, Mūsā with his staff, ʿĪsā and his mother given refuge on a high ground with springs. It showed us the Day of Resurrection, when ties of lineage will dissolve and each soul will be measured by the weight of what it brought.

And now, in this final movement of the surah, the camera turns to the deniers. To those who said: *"Allah has taken a son."* To those who said: *"There is another god alongside Him."* To those who pointed at idols, at intercessors, at planets, at prophets — and said *this too is divine.*

Ayah 91 stripped those claims away. *No son. No other god. If there were, each god would carry off what he created and they would dominate one another.* The argument from order — from the simple fact that the universe holds together — was completed.

And then comes our ayah. Six words. Allah does not give us a new argument. He gives us a **diagnosis.** He tells us *why* their shirk happened in the first place. He tells us what kind of failure it actually is.

### The Hook

We tend to think of shirk as a moral failure. A heart that loves the wrong thing. A will that bows to the wrong master.

This ayah is going to suggest something stranger.

That shirk is also — perhaps first — a **failure of vision.** A failure of knowing. That the mushrik is doing something that only makes sense if you have only seen half of reality. And that the One being denied is precisely the One who sees the half they cannot.

Stay with that. Six words are about to redraw the map of why human beings end up worshipping things that are not God.

## PART 1: THE LINGUISTIC JOURNEY

### Element One: The Pairing — *Al-Ghayb wa-sh-Shahādah*

#### The concept before the word

Imagine someone trying to describe a house, but they have only ever stood outside it. They can describe the colour of the bricks, the angle of the roof, the placement of the windows. Their description will not be wrong. It will simply be **half.** They have never seen the inside. They do not know which rooms are large, which corridors are narrow, where the light falls in the afternoon, what is stored in the cellar.

Now imagine a second person who has been inside the house but has never seen it from the outside. They know the staircase, the smell of the kitchen, the creak of the floorboards. Their description, too, is half.

Only a third person — one who has stood both outside and inside — knows the **whole** house.

In Arabic, there is a pair of nouns that captures exactly this duality of all reality: **al-ghayb** and **al-shahādah**.

#### Opening the words

Allah says: **عَـٰلِمِ ٱلْغَيْبِ وَٱلشَّهَـٰدَةِ** — *Knower of the unseen and the witnessed.*

Notice He does not say "Knower of the unseen" alone. He does not say "Knower of the witnessed" alone. He pairs them. **Both halves.**

Let's sit with each half.

**Al-Ghayb** comes from the root *gh-y-b*, meaning that which is absent, hidden, beyond perception. Lexicographers in *Lisān al-ʿArab* describe it as everything veiled from the senses — what is behind a wall, what is inside a heart, what has not yet happened, what happened before any witness existed. It is the entire realm of the not-yet-seen.

**Al-Shahādah** comes from the root *sh-h-d*, meaning to witness, to be present at. The same root that gives us *shahīd* (witness, martyr) and *shahādah* (testimony of faith). It is the realm of the **present, the visible, the testifiable** — what stands before the eye, what fingers can touch, what ears can hear.

Notice something. Together, these two words exhaust the categories of being. There is nothing in existence that is not either *ghayb* or *shahādah.* Either you can perceive it or you cannot. Either it is currently witnessed or it is currently veiled. The pair is a **complete partition** of all that is.

Here's what's remarkable. To know all of *al-ghayb* alone would already place a being beyond every creature. To know all of *al-shahādah* — every grain of sand, every word spoken, every neuron firing — would already exceed any creature's capacity. To know **both, completely, simultaneously** is to know **everything that is.** This is not "He knows a lot." This is the closing of every category.

#### Deeper

Stay with the order. *Al-ghayb* comes **first.** The unseen is mentioned before the seen.

Why? Among classical commentators there is a beautiful observation: the *ghayb* is harder to know than the *shahādah*. Anyone with eyes can speak about the visible. The unseen requires a different kind of perception entirely. So Allah lists first what is most impressive — *I am the One who knows what no one can see* — and then, almost as a casual addition, *and also the witnessed.*

But there is another layer. From a creature's perspective, the *ghayb* is what defines our limitation. We do not know what tomorrow holds. We do not know what is in another's heart. We do not know the hour of our own death. *Al-ghayb* is the wall we keep walking into. By naming it first, Allah is naming **exactly where our blindness is.** *I know what you cannot.*

#### Deeper still

Now look at the structure. **عَـٰلِمِ** — *ʿĀlim,* the active participle from the root *ʿ-l-m* (to know). Not the verb *yaʿlamu* (He knows). The participle.

In Arabic grammar, the active participle (*ism al-fāʿil*) describes someone in a permanent state of doing something. *Yaʿlamu* could mean *He knows right now.* But *ʿĀlim* means **the Knower** — this is what He **is.** It is not an action He performs; it is His description.

And it is **annexed** (*muḍāf*) directly to *al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah.* No preposition between them. In Arabic, this construction *(iḍāfah)* binds two nouns together so tightly that the second becomes part of the very identity of the first. It is not "the One who knows things including the unseen." It is **"Knower-of-the-unseen-and-the-witnessed"** as a single, indivisible name.

The unseen is not something added to His knowledge. **Knowing the unseen is part of what makes Him Him.**

Al-Muyassar's commentary concentrates all of this into a single word: *huwa waḥdahu yaʿlam* — it is **He alone** who knows. Not "He knows more than others," not "He knows what others cannot" — *waḥdahu.* Alone. The completeness of this dual knowledge is not a capability distributed in lesser degrees across the creation. It belongs to no one else. There is no partial membership in this category. Either you know both halves entirely, or you are a creature. Allah is the only exception.

[PAUSE]

#### What this single feature reveals

When you say *ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah,* you are not describing one of Allah's many capabilities. You are describing **the only Being who occupies a position no creature can occupy.** Every creature lives in *shahādah* and is blind to *ghayb.* The angels know more *ghayb* than humans, but they too do not know all of it. *Wa lillāhi ghaybu-s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* — to Allah belongs the unseen of the heavens and the earth (16:77).

To know both halves of reality, completely, is the very signature of divinity itself.

And from that vantage point, al-Tabari draws a conclusion that reaches beyond this single ayah. Since only Allah knows both the unseen and the witnessed — since only He occupies that complete position — *His account of reality (khabaruhu) is the Truth, and their account is not.* The mushrikūn are not merely associating the wrong things with the wrong Being. They are testifying about a house they have never fully entered, from a position that grants them only half the evidence. When Allah speaks — through revelation, through the Quran — He speaks as the only witness present for all of it: before creation and within it, in the visible and in the hidden. *Fa-taʿālā* therefore does not only describe His rank. It describes His epistemic standing. The ground of His transcendence is also the ground of His word's authority.

### Element Two: The Verb With No Object — *Yushrikūn*

#### The concept before the word

In Arabic, when a verb takes an object, that object is usually stated. *He associated X with Y.* The grammar wants to know: with whom did he associate, and what did he associate? When the object is dropped, that silence is doing work. The mind has to ask: *what is missing here, and why?*

Pay attention to what Allah does **not** say.

#### Opening the silence

The ayah ends: **فَتَعَـٰلَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ** — *so exalted is He above whatever they associate.*

The verb is **يُشْرِكُونَ** — *yushrikūn.* They associate. From the root *sh-r-k,* meaning to share, to make a partner of, to associate one thing with another.

Now ask: associate **what** with **whom**?

Notice that Allah does not specify either. He does not say "above what they associate **with Him.**" He does not say "above the **partners** they associate." He simply leaves the verb with a relative pronoun (*ʿammā* — "from whatever") and stops.

In Arabic grammatical analysis, when a transitive verb is left without its expected object, several things can be happening. Sometimes the object is so obvious it doesn't need to be stated. Sometimes the omission is meant to **generalise** — *whatever it might be that they associate, in any form, of any kind, with anyone.* Sometimes the silence is meant to **shame** the act itself by refusing to dignify it with specifics.

Here, all three are at work.

#### Deeper

What are the things human beings associate with Allah? An idol of stone. A celestial body. A prophet. A saint. A government. A bank account. An ego. A craving. Each generation invents new partners for its own god.

Had the ayah said *"above the idols they worship,"* it would have been easy for a modern reader to think: *Well, I don't bow to idols, so this isn't about me.* Had it said *"above the angels they associate,"* it would have addressed only the Arab pagans of the seventh century.

By leaving the verb open, **the ayah catches every form of shirk that has ever been invented and every form that ever will be.** The wealth that becomes a god. The career that becomes a god. The reputation that becomes a god. The state that becomes a god. The lover that becomes a god. *Whatever they associate.*

#### Deeper still

Now ask the most important question. Where do all of these associates **live?**

Every single one of them lives in **al-shahādah** — the witnessed realm. Stone idols are visible. Celestial bodies are visible. A bank account, a career, a reputation — these are all things in the world the eye can see and the hand can touch.

**No one in human history has ever made an idol out of *al-ghayb.*** No one has ever bowed down to "the unseen knowledge of what is in the cellars of the deepest sea." No one has ever built a temple to "tomorrow's weather." No one has ever wept before "the secret thoughts of strangers in another land." Why? Because you cannot worship what you cannot see.

Now feel the architecture. Allah has just said: *I am the Knower of both the unseen and the witnessed.* And then immediately He says: *exalted above whatever they associate.* The **whatever they associate** is, by definition, drawn entirely from the **half of reality they can see.** Their gods come from *al-shahādah* alone. They have shrunk the divine down to the visible.

**Their shirk is not just sinful. It is structurally too small.** They are taking something from one half of reality and treating it as ultimate, while the One who knows **both halves** stands behind everything they cannot see. The mushrik is, quite literally, missing half the picture — and worshipping a piece of the half he can see.

Al-Tabari names this with precision in his commentary. He says the mushrikūn say what they say *ʿan ghayri ʿilm* — not from knowledge — *bal ʿan jahl minHum biH* — but rather from their ignorance of Him. He is not reaching for a rhetorical insult. He is making a structural observation: the mushrik speaks about the One who knows everything from a position of knowing almost nothing. He claims authority over the divine from a position that grants him access only to *al-shahādah* — and nothing of *al-ghayb.* He is, in al-Tabari's own framing, a witness testifying about a house he has only ever seen from the outside. And a witness who cannot see inside is not a sufficient witness for what the inside contains.

#### What this silence reveals

The unspoken object in *yushrikūn* is not laziness in the language. It is precision. By refusing to name the partners, Allah accomplishes two things at once. He generalises the indictment to every form of shirk in every era. And He withholds dignity from objects that do not deserve to be named alongside His name.

The mushrik wants their idol included in the conversation. Allah will not even mention it.

### Element Three: *Fa-Taʿālā* — The Self-Elevating Transcendence

#### The concept before the word

In Arabic morphology, verbs follow patterns. The same root can be put through different patterns to produce different shades of meaning. The simple Form I (*ʿalā*) means "to be high." Form II (*ʿallā*) means "to make something high, to elevate it." Form VI (*taʿālā*) — the form Allah uses here — has a special character.

Form VI typically describes a **mutual** action (two parties doing something to each other) or a **self-contained, sustained** action (the subject existing in a continuous state of being something). It is the pattern that gives us *takātub* (mutual correspondence), *tabādul* (mutual exchange), *taḥāwur* (mutual dialogue) — and *taʿālā* (the state of being elevated).

This is not "He raised Himself once." This is **the continuous condition of being utterly above.**

#### Opening the word

The ayah pivots on a single particle. **فَ** — *fa.* "So." "Therefore." "And as a consequence."

This *fā'* is what grammarians call *fā'-al-sababiyyah* — the *fā'* of consequence. It says: *because of what just preceded, this is what follows.*

Because Allah is *Knower of the unseen and the witnessed,* **therefore** He is *taʿālā* — elevated, transcendent, far above — *whatever they associate.*

Stay with that logical link. His transcendence is not an arbitrary claim. It is the **conclusion** of the previous statement. *Knowing both halves of reality* leads directly to *being above all that they associate.*

Why? Because the partners they invent come from the small half. And He encompasses the whole.

#### Deeper

**تَعَـٰلَىٰ.** Form VI of *ʿ-l-w* (to be high). The root that gives us *al-ʿAlī* (the Most High), *al-Aʿlā* (the Highest), *taʿālā* (He is exalted).

In Form VI, the verb takes on a peculiar quality. It is not transitive in the normal sense. *Taʿālā* does not mean "He elevated something." It means **He is in a continuous, self-contained state of elevation.** It is, in a way, untranslatable into a single English verb. We reach for *exalted* or *transcendent* or *far above*, but each translation captures only a facet. What the Arabic encodes is closer to: **a being that permanently occupies an ontological altitude that no act of comparison can measure and no act of reaching can close.** The elevation is not relative — not high compared to X or elevated beyond Y. It is absolute. It is constitutive.

Classical grammarians were careful here. While Form VI in Arabic typically implies a mutual or reciprocal dimension between parties, they noted that *taʿālā* in the divine context stands outside that pattern. There is no second party. There is no dialectic of elevation. There is only Allah — and a state of transcendence that is simply what He is. The mutuality that Form VI conventionally implies has no application when the subject is the sole ground of being. *Taʿālā* is Form VI pressed into service for a reality that exceeds Form VI's ordinary range, which is itself a kind of testimony: even the most sophisticated grammatical pattern strains at the edges when it approaches the divine.

#### Deeper still

Now bring together everything this word carries.

*Fa-taʿālā.* **Therefore He is transcendent.** The *fa* of consequence makes His elevation the **logical conclusion** of His being the Knower of both halves.

Why does knowing both the unseen and the witnessed make one *taʿālā* — elevated beyond what is being associated?

Because everything the mushrik associates with Allah comes from the witnessed realm. Every idol, every star, every person who has been deified — every candidate for association was first *seen.* Every candidate lived in *al-shahādah.* And the One being denied encompasses not only *al-shahādah* but *al-ghayb* — the entirety of what is hidden from all creatures simultaneously.

The logical gap is therefore not merely moral. It is ontological. To place a creature of *al-shahādah* alongside the Knower of *al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah* is not simply wrong — it is structurally impossible. It is like trying to measure the ocean with a teaspoon and calling them equivalent. The gap is not one of degree but of category.

*Taʿālā* is the word for that categorical distance.

#### What this single feature reveals

The ayah's architecture is now visible in full. Three movements. The Name — *ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah*: the complete Knower of both realms. The silence — *yushrikūn*: whatever it is they are associating, from whichever half they can see. And the conclusion — *fa-taʿālā*: and therefore He is above it. Ontologically, not comparatively. Categorically, not by degree.

The *fā'* does not say "He is better." It says: **the act of comparing does not apply.** The partners are drawn from one half. He encompasses both. There is no scale that holds them together.

And so the surah's indictment of shirk is complete — not through condemnation, but through diagnosis. The mushrik has not merely chosen the wrong object of worship. He has testified about the whole from a position of knowing only half. His gods are products of *al-shahādah* alone. His theology is, in al-Tabari's own framing, testimony offered from *jahl* — speaking about the unseen from a position of having never reached it. The One he is denying is the One who holds the key to both halves, and whose account, therefore, is the only account that counts.

## PART 2: THE CLAIM THIS AYAH IS MAKING

Bring together now what six words have built.

The claim is not subtle. It is not decorative. Placed at the end of a long surah, after prophets and creation and resurrection and the dismantling of every counter-claim, this ayah says something precise:

**The reason shirk is wrong is not only ethical. It is epistemological.**

To associate a partner with Allah is to make a claim about ultimate reality. It is to say: *this thing I can see, touch, name, and control — this thing deserves the rank of the divine.* And that claim is made, as al-Tabari observes, from *jahl* — from a position of ignorance about the very reality being described. The mushrik does not know what is in the unseen. He does not know the origins of what he sees. He does not know what holds the visible world in place, what preceded it, what the grain of sand was before it was sand, what the idol was before it was clay. He testifies about the divine from the position of a creature who has only ever stood outside the house.

*ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah.* The One being denied is the One who has been everywhere. Outside the house and inside it. Before the house was built and after every wall has returned to dust. He alone knows the whole map, because He alone encompasses every part of it — the visible and the hidden, the witnessed and the absent — simultaneously, permanently, as what He simply *is.*

This is why the surah ends here, with this particular name. Not *al-Qādir* (the Powerful). Not *al-Malik* (the King). Not *al-Jabbār* (the Compeller). The name chosen to seal this argument is *ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah* — because the fundamental problem with shirk is not that it defies a powerful King. It is that it contradicts the testimony of the only reliable Knower of what is real.

And then *fa-taʿālā.* **Therefore.** The transcendence follows from the knowledge, not the other way around. He is not transcendent and therefore unknowable. He is the Knower — and therefore unreachable by the categories of those who know only half.

## PART 3: SITTING WITH THIS IN YOUR OWN LIFE

### The modern mushrik does not bow to stone

Think about what this means for a generation that does not bow to idols of clay.

The idols of the twenty-first century are also made of *al-shahādah.* They are visible. They trend. They have follower counts and share prices and quarterly earnings. They are productivity metrics and social hierarchies and scientific consensus — which is itself a form of witnessed knowledge, and not wrong in its own domain, but wrong when it seals the *ghayb* off rather than remaining humble before it. They are the approval of the people, which the eye can see, and the fear of death, which the eye can almost see.

The pattern is the same across every era. *Something from al-shahādah, elevated to the rank of ultimate.* Something from the half we can see, treated as though it explains the whole.

The ayah does not condemn the visible. It condemns the elevation of the visible to the rank of the total.

### The question this ayah leaves in the chest

Sit with this. Not as theology to memorise. As a question to carry.

*What are the things in my life that I am treating as ultimate — things that come from the witnessed half of reality?*

The goal is not to distrust everything visible. The eyes were given as a gift. The *shahādah* is real. But the *ghayb* is also real — and larger. And the One who knows both has spoken. Through revelation. Through the Quran. Through this very ayah.

The mushrik's tragedy is not that he loved visible things. It is that he stopped there — as though the visible were the whole map. As though the house could be fully described from the garden.

There is a Knower who has been inside.

*Trust the One who has seen the whole house.*

[PAUSE]

### What changes in prayer

If this ayah lands — if *ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah* lands — something shifts in *duʿāʾ.*

When you raise your hands and speak to Allah, you are speaking to the One who already knows what you are about to say (*al-ghayb of your own future words*) and what you have already done (*al-shahādah of your history*). He knows the outcome of what you are asking for, which you cannot see. He knows the consequences of your request that you cannot anticipate. He knows what is in your chest, which you are struggling to name even to yourself.

You are not informing Him. You are orienting yourself toward the One who already holds both halves.

This is why *duʿāʾ* is not negotiation. You are not bringing new information. You are bringing yourself — the whole weight of your *shahādah* and your blindness to your own *ghayb* — and placing it before the Knower of both. That is a completely different posture than supplication to a stone. And it is a completely different posture than the quiet assumption — so common, so modern — that what you can see and calculate and control is the full extent of what is real.

## CLOSING

The surah will end in a few more ayahs with the Prophet ﷺ being instructed to say: *Lord, forgive and have mercy — You are the best of those who show mercy* (23:118). It ends in mercy. But mercy is only the right name when it comes from One who has seen everything — who knows the *ghayb* of what we carry and the *shahādah* of what we have done — and chooses mercy anyway.

*ʿĀlim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahādah* is not just a theological title. It is the ground beneath forgiveness. It means: He knows everything about you — the half you show and the half you hide — and He is still the One calling you back.

*Fa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn.*

And therefore He is above whatever they associate.

Above the idols of clay and the idols of trending. Above the partners made of stone and the partners made of data. Above every substitute that human hands have lifted to the rank of ultimate. He encompasses both halves of reality — the seen and the unseen — and stands, in *taʿālā*, above the entire project of inventing a god from the visible fragment.

Six words. A complete diagnosis of the human condition. And an invitation:

*To turn toward the One who knows both halves.*

**يَا عَالِمَ الْغَيْبِ وَالشَّهَادَةِ**

*Yā ʿĀlima-l-ghaybi wa-sh-shahādah*

O Knower of the unseen and the witnessed —

You know what I have shown and what I have hidden. You know what I have not yet done and what I carry in my chest that I cannot name. You know the outcomes I am afraid of and the futures I cannot see.

I bring myself before You — the whole weight of my *shahādah* and all of my blindness to my own *ghayb* — and I ask You to be, for me, what You already are: the One who holds both halves.

Make me of those who turn toward You alone. Who do not shrink the divine down to the visible. Who trust that the map is larger than what my eyes can reach.

*Fa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn.*

And You are far above what they associate.

*Āmīn.*
