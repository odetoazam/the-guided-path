Start Here — The Story

This is the whole life of the prophet Sulaymān, from his youth beside his father Dāwūd to the day he died. It comes from the Quran and from the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ, with a few reports from his Companions. Here it is told in order.

A son given to Dāwūd

Sulaymān was the son of the prophet Dāwūd, a king of the Children of Israel. God says He gave Sulaymān to Dāwūd as a gift. Then He praises him as an excellent servant, one who was truly awwāb. God also sent revelation to Sulaymān, as He did to Nūḥ and the prophets after him.

The word awwāb comes from a root that means to come back. It describes a person who keeps turning back to God and obeying Him.

He grew up in a house full of knowledge. God gave knowledge to both Dāwūd and Sulaymān, and the two of them thanked God together. They said that all praise belongs to God, who had favoured them above many of His believing servants.

The field and the sheep

While Dāwūd was still king, people came to him with a quarrel. One man's sheep had wandered into the other man's field at night and eaten it. The Quran uses the word nafashat here, which means the sheep grazed loose at night with no shepherd to stop them.

Ibn Masʿūd said the field was a vineyard whose grapes had already formed. Dāwūd ruled that the sheep should be given to the owner of the vineyard. Then Sulaymān said there was a better way. The owner of the sheep should take the ruined vineyard and tend it until it grew back as it was. Meanwhile the owner of the vineyard should keep the sheep and live from their milk and young. When the vineyard was whole again, each man would take back his own. Ibn Kathīr reports from early scholars that Dāwūd then ruled that way.

The Quran says God gave Sulaymān the understanding of that case. Then it adds at once that God gave both of them judgment and knowledge. So the son was honoured, and the father was not blamed.

Two mothers and one child

The Prophet ﷺ told of another case from those years. Two women were out with their sons when a wolf came and carried off one of the boys. Each woman said the wolf had taken the other's child. So they brought the living boy to Dāwūd, and he gave him to the older woman.

On their way out they passed Sulaymān and told him what had happened. He said, "Bring me a knife, and I will cut the boy in two between them." The younger woman cried out, "Do not do it, may God have mercy on you. He is her son." So Sulaymān gave the boy to the younger woman (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). Only the real mother would give up her child to save his life.

He inherits from his father

When Dāwūd died, Sulaymān took his place. The Quran says that Sulaymān inherited from Dāwūd. Ibn Kathīr explains that he inherited prophethood and kingship, not money. Dāwūd had other sons, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "No one inherits from us. Whatever we leave behind is charity" (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

Sulaymān stood before the people and told them what God had given him.

وَوَرِثَ سُلَيْمَـٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ ۖ وَقَالَ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ عُلِّمْنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيْرِ وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ ۖ إِنَّ هَـٰذَا لَهُوَ ٱلْفَضْلُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

"And Sulaymān inherited Dāwūd. He said: O people, we have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given of all things. Indeed, this is the clear favour."

An-Naml (27:16)

He understood the speech of birds and what they meant. God had also given him everything a king could need, from armies to knowledge.

The army and the ant

God gathered for Sulaymān an army of jinn, people and birds. They marched in ranks, and officers held each part in its place so that no one ran ahead or fell behind. One day they came to a valley full of ants, and an ant called out to the rest.

حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَتَوْا۟ عَلَىٰ وَادِ ٱلنَّمْلِ قَالَتْ نَمْلَةٌ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّمْلُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ مَسَـٰكِنَكُمْ لَا يَحْطِمَنَّكُمْ سُلَيْمَـٰنُ وَجُنُودُهُۥ وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

"Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said: O ants, enter your dwellings so that Sulaymān and his soldiers do not crush you while they do not perceive it."

An-Naml (27:18)

The ant warned her people and gave them a clear order. She even made an excuse for Sulaymān, saying his army would crush them only because they did not see them. Sulaymān understood every word. He smiled, laughing at what she said. Then he turned to God in prayer.

فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا مِّن قَوْلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِىٓ أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَىَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَىَّ وَأَنْ أَعْمَلَ صَـٰلِحًا تَرْضَىٰهُ وَأَدْخِلْنِى بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِى عِبَادِكَ ٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَ

"So he smiled, amused at her speech, and said: My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favour which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents, and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants."

An-Naml (27:19)

By his parents he meant Dāwūd and his mother. A king at the head of a great army stopped to thank God because of one small ant.

The missing hoopoe

Another day Sulaymān looked over the birds and could not find the hoopoe. Sulaymān said, "Why do I not see the hoopoe? Or is it absent?" He said he would punish it severely or even slaughter it, unless it brought him a clear reason.

The hoopoe was not gone long. When it came back it spoke to the king. "I have learned something you do not know," it said. "I have come to you from Sabaʾ with news that is certain." It had found a woman ruling over the people there. She had been given plenty of everything, and she had a great throne. But she and her people bowed down to the sun instead of God, because Satan had made their deeds look good to them.

Sulaymān answered, "We will see whether you have told the truth or lied." He wrote a letter and told the hoopoe to drop it down to them. Then it was to step back and watch how they answered.

The queen's letter

The queen gathered her chiefs. "A noble letter has been dropped down to me," she said. "It is from Sulaymān. It begins in the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful." The letter told them not to rise up against him, and to come to him in submission.

She asked her chiefs for advice, because she never decided anything important without them. They told her they had great strength and were fierce in war. But they left the choice to her. She answered them:

قَالَتْ إِنَّ ٱلْمُلُوكَ إِذَا دَخَلُوا۟ قَرْيَةً أَفْسَدُوهَا وَجَعَلُوٓا۟ أَعِزَّةَ أَهْلِهَآ أَذِلَّةً ۖ وَكَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ

"She said: indeed kings, when they enter a city, ruin it and render the honoured of its people humbled — and that is what they do."

An-Naml (27:34)

Then she said she would send Sulaymān a gift and wait to see what her messengers brought back.

Sulaymān would not take the gift. "Are you offering me wealth?" he said. "What God has given me is better than what He has given you. It is you who delight in your gifts." He sent the messenger home with a warning. He would come to them with armies they could not stand against, and drive them out humbled.

Her throne is brought

The queen and her people set out to come to Sulaymān. Before they arrived, he asked his court which of them could bring him her throne. A powerful jinn said he could bring it before Sulaymān rose from his seat, and that he was strong and trustworthy enough to carry it. Then a man who had knowledge of the Book said he could bring it before Sulaymān's glance came back to him. The Quran does not name this man, and the scholars gave different answers about who he was.

In the blink of an eye the throne stood before Sulaymān. It had come all the way from Yemen. His first words were about the favour of his Lord.

قَالَ ٱلَّذِى عِندَهُۥ عِلْمٌ مِّنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبْلَ أَن يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ ۚ فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسْتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَـٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّى لِيَبْلُوَنِىٓ ءَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ ۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِۦ ۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّى غَنِىٌّ كَرِيمٌ

"Said one who had knowledge of the Book: I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you. So when he saw it settled before him, he said: this is from the favour of my Lord, to test me — whether I am grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful is grateful only for himself; and whoever is ungrateful — then indeed my Lord is Free of need, Generous."

An-Naml (27:40)

The queen comes

Sulaymān told his people to disguise her throne. "We will see whether she is guided," he said. When she arrived, someone asked her, "Is your throne like this?" She answered, "It is as though it were the same." Ibn Kathīr says this answer showed her sharp mind. She had left her throne far behind in Yemen, and she could not imagine anyone moving it. So she neither said yes nor no.

The Quran explains what had held her back from faith until then. It was the thing she used to worship instead of God, because she came from a people who did not believe. Then she was asked to enter the palace.

قِيلَ لَهَا ٱدْخُلِى ٱلصَّرْحَ ۖ فَلَمَّا رَأَتْهُ حَسِبَتْهُ لُجَّةً وَكَشَفَتْ عَن سَاقَيْهَا ۚ قَالَ إِنَّهُۥ صَرْحٌ مُّمَرَّدٌ مِّن قَوَارِيرَ ۗ قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّى ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِى وَأَسْلَمْتُ مَعَ سُلَيْمَـٰنَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"It was said to her: enter the palace. Then when she saw it she took it for a body of water and uncovered her shins. He said: it is a palace paved smooth with glass. She said: my Lord, I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulaymān to God, Lord of the worlds."

An-Naml (27:44)

So the queen who once bowed to the sun now submitted to God alongside Sulaymān. Later reports call her Bilqīs, but the Quran never gives her name. Many popular retellings add that Sulaymān married her, but no reliable source says so, and the Quran says nothing about what happened to her afterward.

The horses

One late afternoon, fine horses were paraded before Sulaymān. They stood poised and ready, and they were fast. He spoke of his love for good things, and then something passed out of sight. He asked for the horses to be brought back, and he began to pass his hands or his sword over their legs and necks.

Scholars read these verses in two ways. Most early scholars said the horses kept him busy until the sun set and the time of the afternoon prayer had passed. So he slaughtered them for the sake of God, and this view is reported from ʿAlī. Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī preferred another view reported from Ibn ʿAbbās. In that reading the horses ran out of sight, and Sulaymān stroked their legs and necks to wipe them down. The Quran does not settle it, so both views are given here.

A test and a prayer

God then tested Sulaymān by placing a body on his throne. After that he turned back to God. The Quran does not say what the body was. Many popular retellings tell a long story about a ring and a devil who took his throne. Ibn Kathīr says those stories come from the People of the Book, and they have no reliable source.

After the test Sulaymān asked God for two things.

قَالَ رَبِّ ٱغْفِرْ لِى وَهَبْ لِى مُلْكًا لَّا يَنۢبَغِى لِأَحَدٍ مِّنۢ بَعْدِىٓ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْوَهَّابُ

"He said: My Lord, forgive me, and grant me a kingdom that will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You are the Bestower."

Ṣād (38:35)

He asked for forgiveness first, and only then for a kingdom. God answered him.

The wind and the jinn

God placed the wind under Sulaymān's command. Its morning journey covered a month's travel, and its evening journey covered another month. The Quran describes this wind in two ways. In one place it is ʿāṣifa, a strong storm wind that carried him to the blessed land. In another it is rukhāʾ, a gentle wind that went softly wherever he wished.

God also put the devils among the jinn to work for him. Some were builders and some were divers who brought up treasures from the sea. Others who rebelled were bound together in chains. The jinn worked for him by God's permission, and any of them who turned from God's command would taste the punishment of the blazing fire.

God made a spring of molten qiṭr flow for him, which Ibn ʿAbbās and others said was copper. The jinn built him whatever he wished. They made fine buildings and statues, and bowls as large as pools, and huge cooking pots fixed firmly in the ground. Then God said to the family of Dāwūd, "Work in gratitude." And He added that few of His servants are truly grateful.

God told Sulaymān that all this was His gift, and that he could give it away or keep it without being called to account. It was a kingdom like no other. Many centuries later, the Prophet ﷺ caught a jinn who attacked him while he prayed. He wanted to tie it to a pillar of the mosque so the people of Madinah could see it in the morning. Then he remembered his brother Sulaymān's prayer for a kingdom that no one after him would have, and he sent it away humbled (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

The house of prayer in Jerusalem

Sulaymān built the mosque in Jerusalem, but he was not its first builder. The Prophet ﷺ said the mosque in Jerusalem was set up forty years after the Sacred Mosque in Makkah (al-Bukhārī). Ibn Kathīr notes that Sulaymān lived more than a thousand years after Ibrāhīm, so he rebuilt a house of prayer that was already very old.

The Prophet ﷺ said that Sulaymān asked God for three things. He asked for judgment that would agree with God's own judgment, and God gave it to him. He asked for a kingdom that no one after him would have, and God gave it to him. Then he asked that anyone who left home only to pray in this mosque would come out free of his sins, like the day his mother gave birth to him. The Prophet ﷺ said he hoped God had granted that too (Aḥmad, graded sound by al-Arnaʾūṭ; also in al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Mājah).

An oath without "if God wills"

The Prophet ﷺ told of one more moment from Sulaymān's life. "Tonight I will go round to my wives," Sulaymān once said. "Each one will give birth to a horseman who will fight in God's path." His companion told him to say "if God wills," but Sulaymān forgot and did not say it. He went to all of them, yet only one became pregnant, and she gave birth to half a child.

The Prophet ﷺ swore by God that if Sulaymān had said "if God wills," his sons would all have been born and fought in God's path. He said the words would also have kept Sulaymān from breaking his oath, and would have helped him reach what he wanted (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). The versions give the number of wives differently, and al-Bukhārī judged ninety the most correct.

His death

Sulaymān died while the jinn were still at work for him. They did not know he had died. They went on working, because they thought the king was still alive.

فَلَمَّا قَضَيْنَا عَلَيْهِ ٱلْمَوْتَ مَا دَلَّهُمْ عَلَىٰ مَوْتِهِۦٓ إِلَّا دَآبَّةُ ٱلْأَرْضِ تَأْكُلُ مِنسَأَتَهُۥ ۖ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ ٱلْجِنُّ أَن لَّوْ كَانُوا۟ يَعْلَمُونَ ٱلْغَيْبَ مَا لَبِثُوا۟ فِى ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْمُهِينِ

"And when We decreed death for him, nothing showed them his death except a creature of the earth eating his staff. So when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that if they had known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating punishment."

Sabaʾ (34:14)

A small creature of the earth ate away at his staff until the staff gave way and his body fell. Only then did the jinn understand. If they had known the unseen, they would not have kept labouring for a king who was no longer alive.

The Quran does not say how long he stayed like that, or whether he was standing or sitting. Some early reports say he died leaning on his staff in prayer and stayed that way for a whole year. Ibn Kathīr found weakness in some of these reports and said others came from the People of the Book. The reports about his age and the length of his reign also disagree, and none of them is firm.

What God says of him

The Quran also speaks for Sulaymān against a false charge. Some people followed what the devils recited about his kingdom. Sulaymān did not disbelieve. It was the devils who disbelieved and taught people magic.

God says that Sulaymān has a place near Him and a beautiful place of return. He was given more than any king before or after him, and he kept turning back to God through all of it.
