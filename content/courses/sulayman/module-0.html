Start Here — The Story

This is the story of Sulaymān, told simply from beginning to end. Most of it comes in one long passage of the Quran, and a few other chapters add pieces of his life. Here they are put together in order.

A son for Dāwūd

Sulaymān was a prophet and a king, and the son of the prophet Dāwūd, whose story has its own course. God calls Sulaymān an excellent servant who was always turning back to Him.

While Dāwūd was still alive, father and son once judged a case about some sheep that had got into a field at night and eaten the crop. God gave Sulaymān the right understanding of it, and gave both of them judgment and knowledge. The Quran does not tell us what each of them ruled.

He inherits from his father

God gave both Dāwūd and Sulaymān knowledge, and both of them praised Him for it. Then Sulaymān inherited from his father, and he told the people what God had given him.

وَوَرِثَ سُلَيْمَـٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ ۖ وَقَالَ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ عُلِّمْنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيْرِ وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ ۖ إِنَّ هَـٰذَا لَهُوَ ٱلْفَضْلُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

"And Sulaymān inherited Dāwūd. He said: O people, we have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given of all things. Indeed, this is the clear favour."

An-Naml (27:16)

The army and the ant

One day Sulaymān's forces of jinn, people and birds were gathered, and they marched in ordered ranks. When they came to a valley of ants, an ant called out to the others.

حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَتَوْا۟ عَلَىٰ وَادِ ٱلنَّمْلِ قَالَتْ نَمْلَةٌ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّمْلُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ مَسَـٰكِنَكُمْ لَا يَحْطِمَنَّكُمْ سُلَيْمَـٰنُ وَجُنُودُهُۥ وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

"Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said: O ants, enter your dwellings so that Sulaymān and his soldiers do not crush you while they do not perceive it."

An-Naml (27:18)

Sulaymān smiled at what the ant said, and then he prayed.

فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا مِّن قَوْلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِىٓ أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَىَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَىَّ وَأَنْ أَعْمَلَ صَـٰلِحًا تَرْضَىٰهُ وَأَدْخِلْنِى بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِى عِبَادِكَ ٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَ

"So he smiled, amused at her speech, and said: My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favour which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents, and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants."

An-Naml (27:19)

The missing hoopoe

On another day, Sulaymān looked over the birds and could not see the hoopoe. He said he would punish it severely, or even slaughter it, unless it brought him a clear excuse. The hoopoe soon came back with certain news from a land called Sabaʾ. A woman ruled there, it said, who had been given plenty of everything and had a great throne, and she and her people bowed down to the sun instead of God.

Sulaymān said he would see whether the bird had told the truth or lied. He sent it with a letter to drop down to them, and told it to watch how they answered.

The queen's letter

The queen told her chiefs that a noble letter had been delivered to her from Sulaymān. It began in the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful, and it told them not to rise up against him, and to come to him in submission. When she asked her chiefs for advice, they said they were strong and ready to fight, but that the decision was hers. She answered them:

قَالَتْ إِنَّ ٱلْمُلُوكَ إِذَا دَخَلُوا۟ قَرْيَةً أَفْسَدُوهَا وَجَعَلُوٓا۟ أَعِزَّةَ أَهْلِهَآ أَذِلَّةً ۖ وَكَذَٰلِكَ يَفْعَلُونَ

"She said: indeed kings, when they enter a city, ruin it and render the honoured of its people humbled — and that is what they do."

An-Naml (27:34)

So she sent Sulaymān a gift, to see what her messengers would come back with. Sulaymān would not take it. He said that what God had given him was better than what God had given them, and he sent the messenger back with a warning that he would come to them with forces they could not stand against.

Her throne is brought

Sulaymān asked his chiefs which of them would bring him the queen's throne before she and her people came to him in submission. A powerful one of the jinn said he could bring it before Sulaymān rose from his place. Then someone who had knowledge of the Book said he could bring it before Sulaymān's glance came back to him. The Quran does not tell us who he was. When Sulaymān saw the throne standing before him, he said:

قَالَ ٱلَّذِى عِندَهُۥ عِلْمٌ مِّنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبْلَ أَن يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ ۚ فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسْتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَـٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّى لِيَبْلُوَنِىٓ ءَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ ۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِۦ ۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّى غَنِىٌّ كَرِيمٌ

"Said one who had knowledge of the Book: I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you. So when he saw it settled before him, he said: this is from the favour of my Lord, to test me — whether I am grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful is grateful only for himself; and whoever is ungrateful — then indeed my Lord is Free of need, Generous."

An-Naml (27:40)

The queen comes

Sulaymān had her throne changed to test her. When she arrived, she was asked whether her throne was like this one, and she said, "It is as though it were it." Then she was invited into the palace. She thought its floor was a pool of water, so she lifted her dress off her shins, and Sulaymān told her it was a palace paved smooth with glass. Then she spoke:

قِيلَ لَهَا ٱدْخُلِى ٱلصَّرْحَ ۖ فَلَمَّا رَأَتْهُ حَسِبَتْهُ لُجَّةً وَكَشَفَتْ عَن سَاقَيْهَا ۚ قَالَ إِنَّهُۥ صَرْحٌ مُّمَرَّدٌ مِّن قَوَارِيرَ ۗ قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّى ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِى وَأَسْلَمْتُ مَعَ سُلَيْمَـٰنَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"It was said to her: enter the palace. Then when she saw it she took it for a body of water and uncovered her shins. He said: it is a palace paved smooth with glass. She said: my Lord, I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulaymān to God, Lord of the worlds."

An-Naml (27:44)

The Quran never tells us her name, and it says nothing about what happened to her afterward.

A test and a prayer

Other chapters add more of his life, without saying when these things happened. Once, fine and swift horses were paraded before him in the late afternoon. He spoke of his love for good things, and he asked for the horses to be brought back to him. Scholars have understood what happened next in more than one way.

God also tested Sulaymān by placing a body on his throne, and after that he returned. The Quran does not say what the test was or whose body it was. Then Sulaymān asked God to forgive him, and to give him a kingdom that would not belong to anyone after him.

What God gave him

God answered him, and put the wind and the devils under his command. The wind travelled a month's journey in a morning and another month's journey in an evening. God made a spring of molten copper flow for him, and jinn made for him whatever he wished, by God's permission. God told the family of Dāwūd to work in gratitude, and said that few of His servants are grateful.

The Quran also says plainly that Sulaymān did not disbelieve, and that it was the devils who disbelieved and taught people magic.

His death

Sulaymān died while the jinn were still working for him. Nothing showed them that he had died except a small creature of the earth that was eating his staff. When he fell, the jinn saw that if they had known the unseen, they would not have stayed on in their humiliating labour.

God says that Sulaymān has nearness to Him, and a beautiful place of return.
