Module 1 — The Vow and the Girl

This course begins a generation before Maryam, because that is where the Quran begins her story. It begins with a pregnant woman making a promise. That promise teaches the first lesson of her whole life. God accepts what people did not think to offer Him.

A vow shaped for a boy

The wife of ʿImrān promises the child in her womb to God. The word she uses is muḥarraran, which means consecrated. The child would be freed from every claim except serving Him. That is a big thing for a parent to say, and she took one thing for granted without saying it. Serving in the temple was the work of sons, so in every practical way she made the vow with a boy in mind.

Then the baby is born.

فَلَمَّا وَضَعَتْهَا قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّى وَضَعْتُهَآ أُنثَىٰ وَٱللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا وَضَعَتْ وَلَيْسَ ٱلذَّكَرُ كَٱلْأُنثَىٰ ۖ وَإِنِّى سَمَّيْتُهَا مَرْيَمَ وَإِنِّىٓ أُعِيذُهَا بِكَ وَذُرِّيَّتَهَا مِنَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنِ ٱلرَّجِيمِ

"Then when she delivered her, she said: My Lord, I have delivered a female. And God was most knowing of what she delivered. And the male is not like the female. And I have named her Maryam, and I seek refuge for her in You, and for her descendants, from the accursed Satan."

Āl ʿImrān (3:36)

Two different voices speak in the middle of this verse, so it helps to go slowly. The mother speaks first and says I have delivered a female. The early scholars hear an apology in her words, because she is afraid her vow has gone wrong. Then comes the clause and God was most knowing of what she delivered. Al-Qurṭubī argues from the grammar that this clause is God speaking. It is a comment of His own placed inside her sentence. If she were still the one talking to God, it would have said "and You know best" instead. A reading passed on from Ibn ʿAbbās points the same way.

The scholars also discussed how far God's comment goes. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī count the next clause as part of what God says. That clause is the male is not like the female. Al-Qurṭubī reads it as the words of the righteous mother herself. One of the accepted ways of reciting the Quran treats the whole section as her speech. Abū Bakr's reading from ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir's reading both have the word waḍaʿtu, which means "what I delivered."

Scholars also took the meaning of this clause in two directions. Both begin from the same idea: the male [asked for] is not like the female [given]. Al-Jalālayn reads it as the mother being realistic about her vow, since temple service needed a boy. Al-Zamakhsharī reads the same words the other way, as praise for the baby who was born. In his reading the boy she asked for could not compare with the girl she was given. The course does not need to choose between them, because none of these readings changes what happens next.

Accepted, beautifully

God takes the girl. The verse says fa-taqabbalahā rabbuhā bi-qabūlin ḥasan. He accepted her with a beautiful acceptance and made her grow up beautifully. The vow was made with a boy in mind. Yet the girl is received into the temple as though the vow had always been about her. God's comment in the verse has just told us that from His side it always was. The mother thought she was explaining a disappointment. In fact she had given birth to the most chosen woman in the whole book.

Perhaps you have judged how useful you could be to God by a pattern you do not fit, or perhaps someone else judged you that way. If so, these two verses speak to that judgment. The pattern came from people. The acceptance comes from God, and it is ḥasan, beautiful.

The room where provision appears

She grows up in the miḥrāb in the care of the prophet Zakariyyā, given over to God. The Quran shows us one scene from those years that happens again and again. Her guardian keeps finding food in her room that nobody brought, so he asks her about it. Her answer is the first thing the Quran records Maryam saying.

فَتَقَبَّلَهَا رَبُّهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا وَكَفَّلَهَا زَكَرِيَّا ۖ كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا ٱلْمِحْرَابَ وَجَدَ عِندَهَا رِزْقًا ۖ قَالَ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَـٰذَا ۖ قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

"So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner, and put her in the care of Zakariyyā. Every time Zakariyyā entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said: O Maryam, from where is this for you? She said: It is from God. Indeed, God provides for whom He wills without account."

Āl ʿImrān (3:37)

She says huwa min ʿindi llāh: it is from God. Then she adds God provides for whom He wills without account. Here a young girl calmly tells a prophet the very thing prophets teach. She grew up in a room that ran on a truth most of us only half believe all our lives. Yet she says it as simply as you might mention the weather. Bi-ghayri ḥisāb means without account. Nobody is counting it out, and nobody can trace where it comes from.

Right there, a prophet's prayer changes

Everything in this passage turns on one word.

هُنَالِكَ دَعَا زَكَرِيَّا رَبَّهُۥ ۖ قَالَ رَبِّ هَبْ لِى مِن لَّدُنكَ ذُرِّيَّةً طَيِّبَةً ۖ إِنَّكَ سَمِيعُ ٱلدُّعَآءِ

"Right there did Zakariyyā call upon his Lord. He said: My Lord, grant me from Yourself a good offspring. Indeed, You are the Hearer of supplication."

Āl ʿImrān (3:38)

Hunālika means right there, in that place and at that moment. Zakariyyā is an old man whose wife cannot have children. His scenes in later sūrahs show how completely he had given up hope of a child. Then he stands in a room where food arrives that no one can account for. He hears a girl say that God provides without counting, and hope comes back to him. Right there he prays for the child that seemed impossible. He does not wait until later or go somewhere else, and God gives him Yaḥyā.

That is the main point of this module. The girl whose birth her mother almost apologised for becomes the reason a prophet dares to ask God again. This happens before Maryam carries the Word and before any angel speaks to her. Already the way she trusts God teaches trust back to her own teacher. That trust was growing in the miḥrāb. It is the nabātan ḥasanan of the verse, the beautiful growth it describes. In the next module we follow her to the day when everything is asked of her at once.
