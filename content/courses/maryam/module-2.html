Module 2 — The Stranger and the Palm Tree

This module takes two scenes up close. In the first, Maryam meets the moment her whole consecrated life had been preparing her for, and it shows what a trained soul does in the first three seconds of fear. In the second, the Quran records the hardest day of her life and keeps its hardest sentence in. Together they are the course's answer to a question most of us have asked: what does trusting God look like when it is happening to a body?

Scene one: the stranger behind the screen

She has withdrawn from her family to an eastern place and screened herself off. She is a young woman, deliberately alone for worship. God sends His Spirit, who takes the form of basharan sawiyyā, a man, well-formed and complete. The test could hardly be built more precisely. She is unseen and unaccompanied, and as far as she knows the presence in front of her is exactly what her purity has most to fear.

Her first recorded move is this.

قَالَتْ إِنِّىٓ أَعُوذُ بِٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ مِنكَ إِن كُنتَ تَقِيًّا

"She said: Indeed, I seek refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you should be fearing of God."

Maryam (19:18)

There are two moves in that verse, and both are instruction. The first is refuge, before anything else. She flees to God in her first breath, and she flees to Him by the name ar-Raḥmān, the Most Merciful. Module 3 shows that this name is the signature of her whole sūrah. The second move is in kunta taqiyyā, "if you fear God." She appeals to whatever conscience the stranger has, assuming that the best lever in him is his piety, if he has any. Refuge in God first, then an address to the God-fearing part of the other person. A lifetime of the miḥrāb had compressed into a reflex.

The stranger answers that he is only her Lord's messenger, come to give her a pure boy. Her question carries no doubt in it, only the honest arithmetic of her situation: how can I have a boy when no man has touched me, and I have not been unchaste? The reply is one the courses on this shelf keep meeting at every miracle: thus said your Lord, it is easy for Me. Two purposes come with it. The boy will be a sign for the people, and he will be a mercy from Us (19:21), which is worth keeping in mind in a sūrah built around ar-Raḥmān. Then the scene closes with a stamp of settledness: it was a matter decreed.

Scene two: the palm tree

فَأَجَآءَهَا ٱلْمَخَاضُ إِلَىٰ جِذْعِ ٱلنَّخْلَةِ قَالَتْ يَـٰلَيْتَنِى مِتُّ قَبْلَ هَـٰذَا وَكُنتُ نَسْيًا مَّنسِيًّا

"And the pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree. She said: Oh, I wish I had died before this and was in oblivion, forgotten."

Maryam (19:23)

The most chosen woman in the book, told by angels that she was elected twice over, carrying a child given by God's own Spirit, is alone in labour against a tree trunk. What comes out of her is this: I wish I had died before this and been something forgotten.

The Quran kept that sentence. It could have given us a Maryam who laboured serenely, and it recorded the one who wished she had never existed. Al-Saʿdī handles her cry gently, which is how the old books handle it at their best. He says the wish rose from the crushing weight of the moment and from what she feared people would say, and he adds that there was no good for her in the wish, since the good was entirely in what God had in fact decreed. There is no rebuke anywhere. The answer that comes to her is do not grieve.

Who calls out to her from below her is a point on which the masters rule against each other. Al-Qurṭubī rules that it was the angel, and Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlqama, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatāda are on that side. Al-Ṭabarī rules that it was her newborn son himself, speaking his first words before the cradle-speech, and Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb read it that way. On either ruling, the comfort reaches her from exactly where her shame was pointed. So chosenness does not anesthetize. The elect of God can hit a moment that squeezes a death-wish out of them, and the response of Heaven is a stream, and food, and do not grieve.

Shake the trunk

وَهُزِّىٓ إِلَيْكِ بِجِذْعِ ٱلنَّخْلَةِ تُسَـٰقِطْ عَلَيْكِ رُطَبًا جَنِيًّا

"And shake toward you the trunk of the palm tree; it will drop upon you ripe, fresh dates."

Maryam (19:25)

Put this beside the room she grew up in, because the Quran has built a deliberate pair. In the miḥrāb, provision simply appeared, with no request and no effort, bi-ghayri ḥisāb. Here at the palm, the stream is already given, but the dates require her to shake the trunk. A woman exhausted by labour is told to pull a palm tree's trunk toward her. Measured against what a palm actually requires, her contribution to that harvest is almost nothing. The dates were coming from God either way, and the shaking was still asked of her.

The two provision scenes bracket her life, and between them they teach the whole fiqh of receiving. Sometimes the gift arrives without your hand. Sometimes the same Giver asks your hand to move first, not because the gift depends on it, but because you do. Do the small assigned thing. The dropping of the dates was never your department.

The vow of silence

فَكُلِى وَٱشْرَبِى وَقَرِّى عَيْنًا ۖ فَإِمَّا تَرَيِنَّ مِنَ ٱلْبَشَرِ أَحَدًا فَقُولِىٓ إِنِّى نَذَرْتُ لِلرَّحْمَـٰنِ صَوْمًا فَلَنْ أُكَلِّمَ ٱلْيَوْمَ إِنسِيًّا

"So eat and drink and cool your eye. And if you see from among humanity anyone, say: Indeed, I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak today to any person."

Maryam (19:26)

Eat, drink, cool your eye. The tenderness comes first. Then comes the strangest instruction in her story. She is walking back toward the worst accusation her society can level, and she is told to give up the one tool everyone reaches for first, which is her own voice. The vow of silence is made to ar-Raḥmān, the second time she herself anchors a beat of this scene on that name. The root has sounded once more between the two, in the boy given as raḥma (19:21).

Why silence? The story answers that in front of her people, and Module 5 turns the answer toward you. For now, see what the command implies about who will be doing the defending. She is not told to prepare a case. She is told to prepare a fast, because the case has been assigned to someone else, and he is in her arms.
