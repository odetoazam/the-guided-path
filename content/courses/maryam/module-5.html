Module 5 — Carrying What No One Believes

Every course here ends by turning to you, the learner. This one is about a situation you have been in, or one day will be: you carry something true that you cannot prove, in front of people who have already decided to believe the worst. It might be a change of faith no one takes seriously, an innocence no one accepts, or a calling that sounds mad when you say it out loud. Maryam's last scene is the Quran's guidance for exactly that situation.

The defense she did not run

Let us walk slowly through the scene once more. She comes back carrying the child. The crowd has already made up its mind, saying you have done a monstrous thing, and they are all the harsher because of how respected her family was. Anyone wrongly accused wants desperately to speak up. But she has been told to keep a vow of silence, which she made to ar-Raḥmān before anyone had accused her of anything.

فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ ۖ قَالُوا۟ كَيْفَ نُكَلِّمُ مَن كَانَ فِى ٱلْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا

"So she pointed to him. They said: How can we speak to one who is in the cradle a child?"

Maryam (19:29)

She points to the baby. In the whole Quran, that one gesture is all she does to defend herself, and it points to where God has placed the answer. Then the baby speaks: I am the servant of God; He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet. Nothing she could have said would have matched it, and it came from a witness no one could accuse of being told what to say. Her innocence is proven by a miracle, in public and for good, and she never said a word.

Think about what the order of events tells us. She was told to keep silent before she faced the crowd, at the palm tree, by the same voice that gave her the stream and the dates. So her silence was a task she had been given. In effect, she was told that defending herself was not her job that day. The whole course has shown how she was trained so that she could obey such a command. First she had a childhood in a room where food came without her lifting a hand. Then came a hard day when all that was asked of her hand was to shake a tree trunk a little. Now came a day when nothing at all was asked of her voice, because the One she believed had taken on her case.

What it cost, and what it did not

The Quran does not pretend her silence was easy, and neither should we. Only a few verses earlier, this same woman had said would that I had died. Keeping quiet in front of your accusers is one of the heaviest things a person can bear, and she bore it with a heart that, as the text shows us, could shake. Notice also what the vow did not forbid. She was fasting from defending herself to people, but she was never fasting from crying out to God. The Yūsuf course ends on the same difference, in the words of Yaʿqūb: I only complain of my anguish and grief to God. She stayed silent toward people, but she was never silent toward God.

God's protection of mother and son did not end when the baby spoke from the cradle, either. Later the Quran comes back to them in a verse full of shelter: and We made the son of Maryam and his mother a sign, and We sheltered them to high ground, of rest and running water (23:50). The rest and running water are the gifts of the palm tree, now made lasting. The two people the crowd surrounded are the two people God kept giving a home.

Three questions

1. What are you carrying that no one believes? Name it exactly, the true thing you cannot prove. Maryam's story does not promise that a baby will speak up for you. What it promises is quieter and stronger than that. God knows the gap between what is true about you and what people can check about you, and He works inside that gap. In the end, what God said about her outlasted and replaced every voice in that crowd.

2. Which defenses are really yours to make? Some of them are. In Yūsuf's story, the shirt torn from behind was examined as evidence, so the Quran is not against proof. But somewhere in your life you may be fighting a case that was never given to you to fight, and it may be costing you dearly. At the palm tree, Maryam's test was to do the small thing she was asked to do and let the dates fall. In front of the crowd, her test was to do nothing she had not been asked to do and let the Word speak. So ask yourself which of these two tests is the one that is wearing you down.

3. What is your first name for God? When fear hit her suddenly, the name she called on was ar-Raḥmān, straight away, before she had time to think. The name you reach for when there is no time to collect yourself shows what you truly believe about God. If you want her instinct, you gain it the way she did: through years of worship in a room where no one was watching, until the name of mercy is the first language your fear speaks.

The close

Look back over the whole course. A mother made a vow with a boy in mind, and God accepted a girl with a beautiful acceptance. She grew up in a room that taught her God's provision is not counted out. A stranger came to her, and she first ran for protection to the Most Merciful. At a palm tree she wished she had died, and the Quran chose not to leave those words out. Then she was given a stream and asked to shake the trunk. She was told to be silent before the accusation came, and God defended her through the mouth of a baby. Finally, in her last verse in the book, comes the judgment on her life: she believed the words of her Lord... and was of the devoutly obedient.

The command was uqnutī, be devoted, and the final judgment is kānat mina l-qānitīn, she was one of the devoted. Between a command and its fulfilment lies a whole life. Hers is the life the Quran chose to name, and the title it gave her in the end was ṣiddīqa, the woman whose whole being was believing God.

Her chapter uses the name ar-Raḥmān sixteen times, more than any other chapter in the book, and because of her it never feels distant there. The Most Merciful is the One who accepted the child the vow had not been made for. He sent water flowing beneath her when she was breaking, gave dates to her when she was exhausted, and took over the defense of a woman who was slandered. And at the end of His book He records that she did what she was asked. That name has not changed. Whatever you are carrying that no one believes, you already know from her which name to call on first.
