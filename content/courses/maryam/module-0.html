Start Here — The Story

This is the whole life of Maryam, the mother of ʿĪsā. It is told from the Quran and the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ, in the order it happened. She is the only woman the Quran calls by name, and a whole chapter of the Quran carries her name.

A family God chose

Maryam's story begins before she was born, with her family. Her father was a man named ʿImrān, and the Quran calls her "the daughter of ʿImrān." It says that God chose Ādam and Nūḥ above all the worlds. He also chose the family of Ibrāhīm and the family of ʿImrān, one generation coming from another.

Ibn Kathīr writes that there is no disagreement that Maryam came from the line of the prophet Dāwūd. So she was born into a house of prophets and people of worship.

A promise before she was born

Maryam's mother was expecting a child. Before the baby came, she made a promise to God and offered Him the child she was carrying.

إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱمْرَأَتُ عِمْرَٰنَ رَبِّ إِنِّى نَذَرْتُ لَكَ مَا فِى بَطْنِى مُحَرَّرًا فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنِّىٓ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

"When the wife of ʿImrān said: My Lord, indeed I have vowed to You what is in my womb, consecrated — so accept it from me. Indeed, You are the Hearing, the Knowing."

Āl ʿImrān (3:35)

The word she used was muḥarrar, which means "set free." She wanted her child freed from every other work in the world so that it could serve God alone. Ibn Kathīr explains that in those days families would give a child to serve in the house of worship in Jerusalem. Her mother hoped her child would be one of them.

Many popular retellings add that her mother was named Ḥanna and that she longed for a child after watching a bird feed its chick. These details do not come from a reliable source.

A girl is born

When the baby came, it was a girl. Her mother turned to God and told Him what had happened.

فَلَمَّا وَضَعَتْهَا قَالَتْ رَبِّ إِنِّى وَضَعْتُهَآ أُنثَىٰ وَٱللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا وَضَعَتْ وَلَيْسَ ٱلذَّكَرُ كَٱلْأُنثَىٰ ۖ وَإِنِّى سَمَّيْتُهَا مَرْيَمَ وَإِنِّىٓ أُعِيذُهَا بِكَ وَذُرِّيَّتَهَا مِنَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنِ ٱلرَّجِيمِ

"Then when she delivered her, she said: My Lord, I have delivered a female. And God knew best what she had delivered, and the male is not like the female. And I have named her Maryam, and I seek refuge for her in You, and for her children, from Satan the accursed."

Āl ʿImrān (3:36)

Ibn Kathīr explains that a girl could not serve in the house of worship the way a boy could. Still her mother kept her promise. She named the baby Maryam, and she asked God to protect her and her children from Satan.

God answered that prayer. The Prophet ﷺ said that every child is touched by Satan at the moment it is born, and so it cries out. The only ones spared were Maryam and her son (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). Abū Hurayra told this hadith, and then he would recite her mother's prayer from the Quran.

Zakariyyā takes her in

The Quran says that God accepted Maryam with a beautiful acceptance and made her grow up in a good way. Then came the question of who would look after her. Some men argued over it, so they threw their pens to decide. The one chosen to care for her was Zakariyyā.

Zakariyyā was a prophet of God, and he also worked with his hands. The Prophet ﷺ said simply, "Zakariyyā was a carpenter" (Muslim).

He was family too. On the night of his ascension the Prophet ﷺ met Yaḥyā and ʿĪsā in the second heaven, and he said they were maternal cousins (al-Bukhārī). Yaḥyā was Zakariyyā's son and ʿĪsā was Maryam's son. So most scholars say Zakariyyā's wife was Maryam's sister, while some said she was Maryam's aunt.

Food in her prayer room

Maryam spent her days in a room set apart for prayer. The Quran calls it the miḥrāb. Each time Zakariyyā came to see her there, something surprised him.

فَتَقَبَّلَهَا رَبُّهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا وَكَفَّلَهَا زَكَرِيَّا ۖ كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا ٱلْمِحْرَابَ وَجَدَ عِندَهَا رِزْقًا ۖ قَالَ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَـٰذَا ۖ قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

"So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner, and put her in the care of Zakariyyā. Every time Zakariyyā entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said: O Maryam, from where is this for you? She said: It is from God. Indeed, God provides for whom He wills without account."

Āl ʿImrān (3:37)

The early scholars said this food came out of its season. Mujāhid said Zakariyyā found grapes with her when it was not the time for grapes. Ibn ʿAbbās said he found summer fruit with her in winter and winter fruit in summer.

Zakariyyā was an old man with no children, and his wife had never been able to have a child. Ibn ʿAbbās said that when Zakariyyā saw this fruit, he said that the One who brings it to Maryam out of its time is able to give me a child. So right there he turned to his Lord and prayed.

Zakariyyā's son

Zakariyyā called on his Lord in a quiet voice. He said that his bones had grown weak and his head was ablaze with white hair. He had never been disappointed when he called on God before. He asked for a good child who would inherit from him and from the family of Yaʿqūb.

The angels called to him while he stood praying in his prayer room. They gave him the news of a son named Yaḥyā, and no one had carried that name before him. Yaḥyā would confirm a word from God, and many early scholars said this word was ʿĪsā. He would be a leader who kept himself chaste, and a prophet among the righteous.

Zakariyyā was amazed. He asked how he could have a son when he was so old and his wife was barren. He was told that this was easy for God, who had created him before when he was nothing at all. He asked for a sign. The sign was that he could not speak to people for three days except by gestures, even though he was healthy. So he came out of the prayer room and signalled to his people to glorify God morning and evening.

God made his wife able to bear a child, and Yaḥyā was born. The Quran says Yaḥyā was given wisdom while he was still a boy. God gave him tenderness and purity. He was dutiful to his parents, and he was never proud or disobedient.

Chosen above the women of the worlds

As Maryam grew, the angels spoke to her.

وَإِذْ قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصْطَفَىٰكِ وَطَهَّرَكِ وَٱصْطَفَىٰكِ عَلَىٰ نِسَآءِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"And when the angels said: O Maryam, indeed God has chosen you and purified you, and chosen you above the women of the worlds."

Āl ʿImrān (3:42)

Then they told her to be devoutly obedient to her Lord, and to prostrate and bow with those who bow. The word for this devotion is qunūt. Mujāhid, a student of Ibn ʿAbbās, said it means standing long in prayer. He said that when Maryam heard this command, she stood in prayer until her feet swelled.

Centuries later the Prophet ﷺ spoke of her rank. He said, "The best of her women is Maryam daughter of ʿImrān, and the best of her women is Khadīja" (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). He also said that many men reached perfection. Among women only two did, and they were Āsiya the wife of Pharaoh and Maryam daughter of ʿImrān (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

The news of a son

Then the angels brought Maryam good news. God was giving her a word from Him, and his name would be the Messiah, ʿĪsā son of Maryam. He would be honoured in this world and the next, and he would be one of those brought near to God. He would speak to people in the cradle and as a grown man, and he would be one of the righteous.

Maryam asked her Lord how she could have a child when no man had touched her. She was told that this is how God creates what He wills. When He decides a matter, He only says to it "Be," and it is. The angels said God would teach her son the Scripture and wisdom, and the Torah and the Gospel. He would be sent as a messenger to the Children of Israel.

The messenger behind the screen

The chapter named after Maryam tells how this news came to her. She went away from her family to a place toward the east, and she put up a screen to be alone. Then God sent His Spirit to her, and the early scholars understood this Spirit to be the angel Jibrīl. He came to her in the form of a man, well formed in every way. Her first words were a prayer for protection.

قَالَتْ إِنِّىٓ أَعُوذُ بِٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ مِنكَ إِن كُنتَ تَقِيًّا

"She said: Indeed, I seek refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you should be fearing of God."

Maryam (19:18)

Abū al-ʿĀliya, one of the early scholars, said she knew that a man who feared God would hold himself back when he heard those words.

He told her that he was only a messenger from her Lord, sent to give her a pure boy. She asked how she could have a boy when no man had touched her and she had never been unchaste. He answered that her Lord said this was easy for Him. The boy would be a sign for people and a mercy from God, and it was a matter already decided.

God says in two places that He breathed into her from His Spirit (21:91 and 66:12). So Maryam carried a son who had no father. The Quran says that ʿĪsā is like Ādam in the sight of God. God made Ādam from dust and then said to him "Be," and he was (3:59).

Alone under the palm tree

So Maryam became pregnant, and she went away with the child to a far place. The Quran does not say how long she carried him, and the early scholars gave different answers. When the pains of birth came, they drove her to the trunk of a palm tree.

فَأَجَآءَهَا ٱلْمَخَاضُ إِلَىٰ جِذْعِ ٱلنَّخْلَةِ قَالَتْ يَـٰلَيْتَنِى مِتُّ قَبْلَ هَـٰذَا وَكُنتُ نَسْيًا مَّنسِيًّا

"And the pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of the palm tree. She said: I wish I had died before this and been a thing forgotten, utterly forgotten."

Maryam (19:23)

Her people knew her as a woman who gave her life to worship. Now she was alone with a newborn and no husband, and she knew what they would say. Ibn Kathīr held that she gave birth at Bethlehem near Jerusalem, relying on reports he accepted.

The voice from beneath her

Then a voice called to her from beneath her. The early scholars differed over whose voice it was. Some said it was the angel Jibrīl, and others said it was the newborn ʿĪsā. The voice told her not to grieve, because her Lord had placed a small stream beneath her.

It told her to shake the trunk of the palm tree toward herself, and fresh ripe dates would fall down to her. She was to eat and drink and let her eye be cool. The Arabic phrase is qurrī ʿaynan. The phrase comes from a word for coolness. The old Arabic scholars explained that tears of joy are cool and tears of grief are hot.

The voice also told her what to do if she met anyone. She was to say that she had vowed a fast to the Most Merciful, and so she would not speak to any person that day. Qatāda and other early scholars said that in their religious law a fast could include keeping silent.

Back to her people

Then she came back to her people, carrying the baby. They told her that she had done a monstrous thing. Then they called her "sister of Hārūn." They said her father was not a man of evil and her mother was not unchaste. They were saying that she had shamed a good family.

Long afterwards the Companion al-Mughīra ibn Shuʿba travelled to Najrān. The people there asked him about this verse, because Mūsā and his brother Hārūn had lived long before ʿĪsā. When al-Mughīra came back, he asked the Prophet ﷺ about it. The Prophet ﷺ said that people used to name their children after the prophets and righteous people who came before them (Muslim). So the Hārūn in their words was a man of her own people who carried the prophet's name.

Maryam did not answer them. She pointed to the child.

فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ ۖ قَالُوا۟ كَيْفَ نُكَلِّمُ مَن كَانَ فِى ٱلْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا قَالَ إِنِّى عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ ءَاتَىٰنِىَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَجَعَلَنِى نَبِيًّا

"So she pointed to him. They said: How can we speak to one who is in the cradle a child? He said: Indeed, I am the servant of God. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."

Maryam (19:29–30)

The baby went on speaking from the cradle. He said that God had made him blessed wherever he was. God had told him to pray and give zakāh as long as he lived. God had made him dutiful to his mother, and had not made him a proud and wretched tyrant. Ibn Kathīr notes that ʿĪsā named only his mother, because she was the only parent he had. He ended with these words: "Peace be on me the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I am raised alive."

The Prophet ﷺ said that only three children ever spoke in the cradle, and the first one he named was ʿĪsā son of Maryam (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). The Quran does not record a single word Maryam said to defend herself. Her son spoke for her.

Raising her son

After this the Quran gives only a few lines about Maryam's life. God says He made the son of Maryam and his mother a sign, and He gave them both shelter on high ground (23:50). It was a place of rest with a spring of flowing water. The Quran does not name the place, and the early scholars suggested several places without agreeing.

Many popular retellings add that Maryam fled with her son to Egypt and lived there for years. That story does not come from a reliable source.

ʿĪsā grew into a man, and God sent him as a messenger to the Children of Israel. When the Quran lists God's gifts to ʿĪsā, it names his mother too. God tells ʿĪsā to remember His favour on him and on his mother (5:110). One of those favours was that ʿĪsā spoke to people in the cradle and again as a grown man.

Not everyone accepted what had happened. The Quran says that some of the Children of Israel spoke a great slander against Maryam, and it lists this among the reasons they were punished (4:156). The Quran also answers those who later made her more than human. It says the Messiah was only a messenger, and his mother was a ṣiddīqa, a woman of truth. Both of them ate food like everyone else (5:75).

When her son was raised

In time ʿĪsā's enemies plotted to kill him. The Quran says they did not kill him and they did not crucify him, but it was made to look that way to them. God raised him up to Himself (4:157-158).

The Quran does not say where Maryam was on that day or what she saw. Some later retellings show her weeping near the cross or meeting her son afterwards. None of these stories comes from a reliable source.

The end of her life

No reliable source describes Maryam's death. We do not know when she died, how old she was, or where she was buried. One later report gives her age at death, but it has no chain back to the Prophet ﷺ or his Companions.

How the Quran remembers her

The last verse in the Quran to name Maryam comes near the end of the book. There God gives two women as an example for everyone who believes. The first is the wife of Pharaoh, and the second is Maryam.

وَمَرْيَمَ ٱبْنَتَ عِمْرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَـٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ ٱلْقَـٰنِتِينَ

"And Maryam, the daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity — so We breathed into [the opening of her garment] of Our Spirit; and she believed in the words of her Lord and His scriptures, and was of the devoutly obedient."

At-Taḥrīm (66:12)

The angels had once told her to be devoutly obedient to her Lord. In this verse God says that she was one of the devoutly obedient.

The Prophet ﷺ also placed her name inside a statement of faith. He said that whoever bears witness to certain truths will be admitted to Paradise. One of those truths is that ʿĪsā is God's servant and messenger. He is God's word which He gave to Maryam, and a spirit from Him (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

Once the Prophet ﷺ praised the women of Quraysh as the best women who ever rode camels. Abū Hurayra, who heard him, added that Maryam daughter of ʿImrān never rode a camel at all (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). The praise was for the women of the Arabs, and it did not place anyone above her.

On the Day of gathering

The Quran tells of a Day when God will gather the messengers. On that Day He will ask ʿĪsā whether he told people to take him and his mother as gods besides God. ʿĪsā will answer that he never said what he had no right to say. He only told them to worship God, his Lord and theirs (5:116-117).

In Paradise

The Prophet ﷺ also spoke of her place in the next life. Once he drew four lines in the ground and asked his Companions if they knew what they were. They said that God and His Messenger knew best. Then he named the four best women of Paradise. They were Khadīja and Fāṭima, and Āsiya the wife of Pharaoh and Maryam daughter of ʿImrān (Aḥmad, graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Arnaʾūṭ).

In the year Makkah was opened, the Prophet ﷺ whispered to his daughter Fāṭima and she wept. Then he spoke to her again and she laughed. After his death Umm Salama asked her why. Fāṭima said he had told her that he would die, so she wept. Then he told her that she would be the chief of the women of Paradise except for Maryam daughter of ʿImrān. So she laughed (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it ḥasan).
