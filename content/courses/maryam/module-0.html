Start Here — The Story

She is the only woman the Quran names. Not one woman among several — the only one, in the entire book, and it names her in thirty-one verses and gives her a chapter of her own. Her story is told in two main places — the family telling in Āl ʿImrān and the birth telling in the sūrah that bears her name — and this module assembles them into one arc, saying so honestly where the seams are.

A vow before she existed

The story starts before her birth, with her mother — the wife of ʿImrān — making a vow over her own pregnancy:

إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱمْرَأَتُ عِمْرَٰنَ رَبِّ إِنِّى نَذَرْتُ لَكَ مَا فِى بَطْنِى مُحَرَّرًا فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنِّىٓ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

"When the wife of ʿImrān said: My Lord, indeed I have vowed to You what is in my womb, consecrated — so accept it from me. Indeed, You are the Hearing, the Knowing."

Āl ʿImrān (3:35)

Muḥarraran — consecrated, set free for God's service alone. She is promising her unborn child to the temple, and the shape of that vow assumed a boy; temple service was a son's work. Then the child is born, and it is a girl — and what the mother says, and what God says over her words, is Module 1's opening scene. For now, the headline: the girl is accepted for the consecration anyway. The first female temple-dedicate, accepted with a beautiful acceptance, grown with a beautiful growth, and placed under the guardianship of the prophet Zakariyyā.

The room with provision in it

She grows up in the miḥrāb — a private chamber of worship. And something starts happening in that room:

فَتَقَبَّلَهَا رَبُّهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا وَكَفَّلَهَا زَكَرِيَّا ۖ كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا ٱلْمِحْرَابَ وَجَدَ عِندَهَا رِزْقًا ۖ قَالَ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَـٰذَا ۖ قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

"So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner, and put her in the care of Zakariyyā. Every time Zakariyyā entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said: O Maryam, from where is this for you? She said: It is from God. Indeed, God provides for whom He wills without account."

Āl ʿImrān (3:37)

Provision, appearing where no one brought it. Her guardian — a prophet — asks the obvious question, and her answer is the first sentence the Quran records from her: it is from God; God provides for whom He wills without account. Hold that sentence. A girl explains to a prophet how provision works — and the very next verse begins hunālika, "right there": right there Zakariyyā prayed for the impossible child of his own old age. Module 1 sits inside that chain.

Chosen, and told so

Angels speak to her — and the sentence they bring is unlike anything said to any other woman in the book: God has chosen you, and purified you, and chosen you above the women of the worlds (3:42). Chosen — twice in one verse. And with the choosing comes a command: be devoutly obedient to your Lord, and prostrate and bow with those who bow (3:43). Keep the word of that command — uqnutī, devote yourself — because the last verse in the Quran to name her answers it.

The annunciation

In the sūrah that bears her name, the scene comes close. She withdraws from her family to an eastern place, screens herself — and God sends His Spirit, who appears to her as a well-formed man. She is alone; a stranger has appeared behind her screen. Her first move is the whole portrait:

قَالَتْ إِنِّىٓ أَعُوذُ بِٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ مِنكَ إِن كُنتَ تَقِيًّا

"She said: Indeed, I seek refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you should be fearing of God."

Maryam (19:18)

Refuge first — in ar-Raḥmān, the Most Merciful. He answers that he is only a messenger of her Lord, come to give her a pure boy. Her question is the reasonable one — how can I have a boy when no man has touched me, and I have not been unchaste? — and the answer is the answer this whole shelf of courses keeps meeting: thus said your Lord: it is easy for Me — and the boy will be a sign for the people and a mercy from Us; it is a matter decreed (19:21).

The ordeal

She conceives and withdraws to a remote place. Labour drives her to the trunk of a palm tree, and the Quran records — without softening it — the sentence she says there: would that I had died before this and been a thing forgotten (19:23). The answer comes at once: a voice from below her — do not grieve; your Lord has placed beneath you a stream — and the instruction to shake the palm trunk, eat the fresh dates, drink, and cool her eye. And then a strange command: if you see any human being, say — I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak today to any person (19:26). She is about to face the worst accusation a woman can face, and she is told to meet it in silence.

The silence, and the defense she did not run

She carries the baby to her people. They say the worst — O Maryam, you have done a monstrous thing; O sister of Hārūn, your father was not an evil man, nor was your mother unchaste (19:27–28). And she says nothing. She points to the child:

فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ ۖ قَالُوا۟ كَيْفَ نُكَلِّمُ مَن كَانَ فِى ٱلْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا قَالَ إِنِّى عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ ءَاتَىٰنِىَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَجَعَلَنِى نَبِيًّا

"So she pointed to him. They said: How can we speak to one who is in the cradle a child? He said: Indeed, I am the servant of God. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."

Maryam (19:29–30)

The baby speaks. Her defense is run for her, by a miracle, in front of everyone — and the infant's first two sentences are about God and about his own servanthood, which answers the slander and pre-answers a later error at the same time. She never says a word in her own defense in the entire story. Module 5 is about what that costs, and what it teaches.

The verdict, at the far end of the book

And then — sixty-three sūrahs after the command, in Sūrah 66 — she appears one final time. It is the last verse in the entire Quran to name her, in a passage where God holds up two women as the example for all believers, men and women alike. The second is Maryam:

وَمَرْيَمَ ٱبْنَتَ عِمْرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَـٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ ٱلْقَـٰنِتِينَ

"And Maryam, the daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity — so We breathed into it of Our Spirit; and she believed in the words of her Lord and His scriptures, and was of the devoutly obedient."

At-Taḥrīm (66:12)

She believed the words of her Lord — ṣaddaqat — and was of the qānitīn, the devoutly obedient. The command given to a girl in the miḥrāb — uqnutī — comes back, at the far end of the Quran, as a verdict in the past tense: she was of those who did. The whole course lives between that command and that verdict.
