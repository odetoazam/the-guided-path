Start Here — The Story

Maryam is the only woman the Quran calls by name. It names her in thirty-one verses and gives her a chapter of her own. Her story is told in two main places: Āl ʿImrān tells it as a family story, and the sūrah that carries her name tells the story of the birth. This module joins the two tellings into one story, and it points out where they meet.

A vow before she existed

The story starts before Maryam is born, when her mother, the wife of ʿImrān, makes a vow over the child she is carrying.

إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱمْرَأَتُ عِمْرَٰنَ رَبِّ إِنِّى نَذَرْتُ لَكَ مَا فِى بَطْنِى مُحَرَّرًا فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنِّىٓ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

"When the wife of ʿImrān said: My Lord, indeed I have vowed to You what is in my womb, consecrated — so accept it from me. Indeed, You are the Hearing, the Knowing."

Āl ʿImrān (3:35)

The word muḥarraran means consecrated, set free to serve God alone, so she is promising her unborn child to the temple. She expected a boy when she made that vow, because serving in the temple was a son's work. Then the child is born and it is a girl. What the mother says next, and how God answers her words, is where Module 1 begins. For now, the main point is that God accepts the girl for the temple anyway. Ibn ʿAbbās said that only boys had ever been given to the temple in this way, and al-Qurṭubī passes on his report. Even so, God accepted Maryam with a beautiful acceptance, made her grow up beautifully, and placed her in the care of the prophet Zakariyyā.

The room with provision in it

She grows up in the miḥrāb, a private room for worship, and something unusual starts happening there.

فَتَقَبَّلَهَا رَبُّهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنۢبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا وَكَفَّلَهَا زَكَرِيَّا ۖ كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا ٱلْمِحْرَابَ وَجَدَ عِندَهَا رِزْقًا ۖ قَالَ يَـٰمَرْيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَـٰذَا ۖ قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ ۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرْزُقُ مَن يَشَآءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

"So her Lord accepted her with good acceptance and caused her to grow in a good manner, and put her in the care of Zakariyyā. Every time Zakariyyā entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said: O Maryam, from where is this for you? She said: It is from God. Indeed, God provides for whom He wills without account."

Āl ʿImrān (3:37)

Provision keeps appearing in her room, even though no one brought it. Zakariyyā, her guardian and a prophet, asks her where it comes from, and her reply is the first sentence the Quran records from her: it is from God; God provides for whom He wills without account. So a young girl is the one explaining to a prophet how God provides. The very next verse opens with hunālika, which means "right there", because it was right there that Zakariyyā prayed for a child of his own, even though he was old and it seemed impossible. Module 1 follows that chain of events.

Chosen, and told so

The angels speak to her, and they tell her something the Quran says to no other woman: God has chosen you, and purified you, and chosen you above the women of the worlds (3:42). She is told she is chosen twice in the same verse. Along with the choosing comes a command: be devoutly obedient to your Lord, and prostrate and bow with those who bow (3:43). The word used in that command is uqnutī, which means devote yourself, and the last verse in the Quran to name her answers it.

The annunciation

In the sūrah that carries her name, we see the scene up close. She goes away from her family to a place in the east and puts up a screen to be alone. God then sends His Spirit to her, who appears as a well-formed man. She is by herself, and a stranger is suddenly standing behind her screen. What she does first tells us who she is.

قَالَتْ إِنِّىٓ أَعُوذُ بِٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ مِنكَ إِن كُنتَ تَقِيًّا

"She said: Indeed, I seek refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you should be fearing of God."

Maryam (19:18)

Before anything else she asks God for protection, and she calls on Him as ar-Raḥmān, the Most Merciful. The man tells her he is only a messenger from her Lord, sent to give her a pure boy. She asks the sensible question: how can I have a boy when no man has touched me, and I have not been unchaste? The answer is the same one we meet again and again across these courses: thus said your Lord: it is easy for Me. He adds that the boy will be a sign for the people and a mercy from Us; it is a matter decreed (19:21).

The ordeal

She becomes pregnant and goes away to a remote place. When the pains of labour come, they drive her to the trunk of a palm tree, and the Quran keeps the words she says there: would that I had died before this and been a thing forgotten (19:23). An answer comes straight away. A voice from below her says do not grieve; your Lord has placed beneath you a stream, and she is told to shake the palm trunk, eat the fresh dates, drink, and let her eye be comforted. Then she is given a strange instruction. If she sees any person, she is to say I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak today to any person (19:26). She is about to face the worst accusation a woman can face, and she is told to meet it by staying silent.

The silence, and the defense she did not run

She carries the baby back to her people, and they accuse her of the worst: O Maryam, you have done a monstrous thing; O sister of Hārūn, your father was not an evil man, nor was your mother unchaste (19:27–28). She does not answer them. Instead, she points to the child.

فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ ۖ قَالُوا۟ كَيْفَ نُكَلِّمُ مَن كَانَ فِى ٱلْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا قَالَ إِنِّى عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ ءَاتَىٰنِىَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَجَعَلَنِى نَبِيًّا

"So she pointed to him. They said: How can we speak to one who is in the cradle a child? He said: Indeed, I am the servant of God. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."

Maryam (19:29–30)

Then the baby speaks, and so her defense is made for her by a miracle in front of everyone. His first two sentences are about God and about himself being God's servant, so they answer the slander against her and also answer, ahead of time, a mistake people would make about him later. In the whole story she never says one word to defend herself. Module 5 looks at what that cost her and what it teaches us.

The verdict, at the far end of the book

Sixty-three sūrahs after that command to devote herself, in Sūrah 66, she appears one last time. It is the last verse in the whole Quran to name her. In that passage God holds up two women as an example for all believers, men and women alike, and the second of them is Maryam.

وَمَرْيَمَ ٱبْنَتَ عِمْرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَـٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ ٱلْقَـٰنِتِينَ

"And Maryam, the daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity — so We breathed into [the opening of her garment] of Our Spirit; and she believed in the words of her Lord and His scriptures, and was of the devoutly obedient."

At-Taḥrīm (66:12)

The verse says she believed the words of her Lord, using the verb ṣaddaqat, and that she was one of the qānitīn, the devoutly obedient. So the command given to a girl in the miḥrāb, uqnutī, comes back at the far end of the Quran in the past tense, as a description of her life. She was one of those who did it. The whole course takes place between that command and that final description.

  • Module 5 — Carrying What No One Believes. Her silence, the defense she did not make, and the name to take home.

If you remember one thing as you begin, remember this. The only woman the Quran names is the one it shows believing a word from God that no one would have believed from her, and the book's final word about her is that this believing is who she was.
