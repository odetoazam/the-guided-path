Start Here — The Story

This is the whole life of Dāwūd, from the first time his name was spoken to the day his son took his place. It comes from the Quran and the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ, with a few words from the early scholars. The Quran tells his story in pieces across nine chapters, so here the pieces are put together in order.

Before he was born

Dāwūd's story begins long before he was born. The Prophet ﷺ said that when God created Ādam, He wiped Ādam's back. Out of it came every soul God would create from his children until the Day of Judgment. Between the eyes of each one was a shining light. God showed them all to Ādam, and Ādam asked who they were. God told him they were his descendants.

Among them Ādam saw one man and admired the light between his eyes. He asked who this was. God told him it was one of his children from the nations that would come later, and his name was Dāwūd. Ādam asked how long his life would be, and God said sixty years. So Ādam said, "My Lord, add forty years to his life from mine."

When Ādam's own life came to its end, the angel of death came to him. Ādam said, "Are there not forty years of my life still left?" The angel answered, "Did you not give them to your son Dāwūd?" The Prophet ﷺ said that Ādam denied it, so his children deny. Ādam forgot, so his children forget. Ādam made a mistake, so his children make mistakes (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it sound).

So before Dāwūd ever drew breath, the father of all people had admired him and given him part of his own life.

A people who wanted a king

Some time after Mūsā, the leaders of the Children of Israel came to a prophet of their time. They asked him to appoint a king over them so that they could fight in God's way. The Quran does not give this prophet's name, but most of the early commentators said he was Shamwīl (Ibn Kathīr).

The prophet asked whether they might hold back once fighting was made their duty. They said they would never hold back, because they had been driven out of their homes and away from their children. But when fighting was made their duty, all but a few of them turned away.

Their prophet told them that God had sent them Ṭālūt as their king. They objected at once. They said they had more right to rule than he did, and that he had not even been given much wealth. Their prophet answered that God had chosen Ṭālūt over them. God had given him more knowledge and more strength of body, and God gives His kingship to whomever He wills.

Then the prophet gave them a sign. The Ark would come back to them, carried by the angels. In it would be calm from their Lord and some of what the families of Mūsā and Hārūn had left behind. Ibn Kathīr says their enemies had taken the Ark from them in an earlier war. So its return was a blessing that came with this good man's rule.

The river

Ṭālūt set out with the army and told them that God would test them with a river. Whoever drank from it was not with him, and whoever did not taste it was with him. Only a single scoop taken in the hand was allowed. Ibn ʿAbbās said this river was the Jordan. Most of the soldiers drank from it anyway.

So only a small band crossed the river with Ṭālūt. The Companions of the Prophet ﷺ who fought at Badr used to say that they were the same number as the men who crossed with Ṭālūt. There were a little over three hundred and ten of them, and only believers crossed with him (al-Bukhārī).

When those few had crossed, some of them said they had no strength that day against Jālūt and his soldiers. But those who were sure they would meet God answered them. They said that many times a small group has beaten a large one by God's leave, and God is with those who are patient. So when they went out to face Jālūt and his army, they prayed. They asked God to pour patience over them and make their feet firm. They asked Him to help them against the people who disbelieved.

Dāwūd kills Jālūt

Then, in the middle of this battle, the Quran names Dāwūd for the first time.

فَهَزَمُوهُم بِإِذْنِ ٱللَّهِ وَقَتَلَ دَاوُۥدُ جَالُوتَ وَءَاتَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُلْكَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَهُۥ مِمَّا يَشَآءُ ۗ وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍ لَّفَسَدَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ وَلَـٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"So they defeated them by God's leave, and Dāwūd killed Jālūt — and God gave him the kingship and wisdom, and taught him from what He willed. And were it not for God pushing back some people by means of others, the earth would have fallen into ruin. But God is full of bounty to all the worlds."

Al-Baqarah (2:251)

That is all the Quran says about the fight. Many popular retellings add details about how Dāwūd killed Jālūt, but Ibn Kathīr says those reports contain things that are strange and doubtful. What the verse does show, he says, is Dāwūd's courage. He killed the enemy's leader, and the enemy's army was broken and humbled.

The reliable sources do not tell us how Ṭālūt's reign came to an end. Ibn Jarīr said that most scholars held Dāwūd became king after he killed Jālūt. God gave him the kingship and wisdom together. Ibn Kathīr points out that kingship and prophethood had been in two different tribes of the Children of Israel. In Dāwūd, God brought them together, the good of this world and the good of the next.

The Zabūr and his voice

God made Dāwūd a prophet and gave him a book of his own. The Quran says, "And We gave Dāwūd the Zabūr." Ibn Kathīr says the Zabūr is full of counsel and wisdom for anyone who reads it.

The Prophet ﷺ said that reciting was made easy for Dāwūd. He would order his riding animals to be saddled, and he would finish reciting his book before they were ready. And he ate only from the work of his own hands (al-Bukhārī).

His voice was famous. One night the Prophet ﷺ listened to his Companion Abū Mūsā reciting the Quran. Later he told him, "If only you had seen me listening to your recitation last night! You have been given a mizmār from the mizmārs of the family of Dāwūd" (Muslim). A mizmār is a reed pipe. The Prophet ﷺ used it to mean a sweet voice like the one Dāwūd was given.

The mountains and the birds

۞ وَلَقَدْ ءَاتَيْنَا دَاوُۥدَ مِنَّا فَضْلًا ۖ يَـٰجِبَالُ أَوِّبِى مَعَهُۥ وَٱلطَّيْرَ ۖ وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ ٱلْحَدِيدَ

"And We gave Dāwūd a favour from Us: 'O mountains, repeat God's praise with him, and you birds too.' And We made iron soft for him."

Sabaʾ (34:10)

When Dāwūd glorified God, the mountains glorified with him in the evening and after sunrise. The birds were gathered around him and joined in. Ibn Kathīr says that when Dāwūd recited his book, the birds would stop in the air and repeat after him.

The word God used for the mountains is awwibī. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid said it means "glorify with him," and it comes from a root that means to return. The same root gives awwāb, one who keeps turning back to God, and that is the word the Quran uses for Dāwūd himself.

Iron in his hands

God made iron soft for Dāwūd. He told him to make full coats of mail and to measure the links with care. Mujāhid said this means each pin should be neither too thin nor too thick, so that the rings neither come loose nor split. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Qatāda said the iron was so soft for him that he shaped it with his hands, with no need of fire or hammer. Qatāda also said he was the first to make armour from linked rings. Before him, armour was made of solid plates.

The Quran says God taught him to make this armour to protect people in their battles. Then it asks, "So will you be grateful?" God also told Dāwūd and his family to do good, because He sees everything they do.

Dāwūd was a king, yet he lived from his own work. The Prophet ﷺ said, "No one has ever eaten better food than what he earns with his own hands. And the prophet of God Dāwūd used to eat from the work of his own hands" (al-Bukhārī).

A man of prayer and fasting

God told the Prophet ﷺ to be patient with what people said and to remember "Our servant Dāwūd, the man of strength." God said he was always turning back to Him. Ibn ʿAbbās said this strength was strength in worship (al-Bukhārī).

The Prophet ﷺ told us how Dāwūd worshipped. He said the prayer most loved by God is the prayer of Dāwūd, and the fast most loved by God is the fast of Dāwūd. Dāwūd slept for the first half of the night. Then he stood in prayer for a third of it, and then he slept for the last sixth. He fasted one day and ate the next (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). In another telling the Prophet ﷺ added that Dāwūd did not run away when he met the enemy (al-Bukhārī).

Whenever the Prophet ﷺ spoke about Dāwūd, he would say that Dāwūd was the most devoted worshipper of all people. He also taught a prayer that Dāwūd used to say. "O God, I ask You for Your love and the love of those who love You and the deeds that bring me to Your love. O God, make Your love dearer to me than myself and my family and cold water" (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it good).

God told the family of Dāwūd to work in thankfulness to Him, and He said that few of His servants are truly thankful. Ibn Kathīr says that no hour of the night or day passed without someone in Dāwūd's house at worship.

A king who judged

God made his kingdom strong. He gave him wisdom and decisive speech. Mujāhid said this means speech that settles a matter clearly, both in talk and in judgment.

The Quran also says that the disbelievers among the Children of Israel were cursed by the tongue of Dāwūd and by the tongue of ʿĪsā son of Maryam. That was because they disobeyed and kept going too far.

The test

Then came a test in his prayer room, though the Quran does not say when it happened. Two visitors with a dispute climbed over the wall and came in on him, and Dāwūd was alarmed. They told him not to be afraid. They said that one of them had wronged the other, and they asked him to judge between them fairly and guide them to the right way. Then one of them said, "This is my brother. He has ninety-nine ewes and I have one ewe. He said, 'Hand her over to me,' and he got the better of me in the argument."

Dāwūd answered that the brother had wronged him by asking for his one ewe on top of his own. He said that many partners wrong one another, except those who believe and do good, and they are few. Then Dāwūd realized that God had been testing him. So he asked his Lord for forgiveness, fell down bowing, and turned back to God.

فَغَفَرْنَا لَهُۥ ذَٰلِكَ ۖ وَإِنَّ لَهُۥ عِندَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَـَٔابٍ

"So We forgave him that. And he has a place near Us and a good place of return."

Ṣād (38:25)

The Quran does not say who the two visitors were. It does not say what Dāwūd asked forgiveness for, and the scholars have differed about it. Ibn Kathīr wrote that many commentators told stories here, but most of them come from the People of the Book and some are plainly false. So he left them out and kept only what the Quran says. The tale of a woman and her husband that many people attach to this scene is one of those stories.

The Prophet ﷺ prostrated when he recited this passage. Ibn ʿAbbās explained why. God had told the Prophet ﷺ to follow the guidance of the prophets before him, and Dāwūd was one of them. Dāwūd prostrated here, so the Prophet ﷺ prostrated here too (al-Bukhārī). He once called this prostration "the repentance of a prophet" (Abū Dāwūd, with a chain Ibn Kathīr said meets the standard of the Ṣaḥīḥ). Some early commentators said he stayed in prostration for forty days. Ibn Kathīr says the only saying of the Prophet ﷺ about this comes through a weak narrator.

Forgiven and trusted

After forgiving him, God spoke to him directly. He said, "O Dāwūd, We have made you a khalīfa on the earth." A khalīfa is someone put in charge of a place and trusted to look after it. God had used the same word for Ādam before Ādam was even created. So God told Dāwūd to judge between people with truth and not to follow his own desires, because they would lead him away from God's path.

Father and son

God gave Dāwūd a son, Sulaymān.

وَوَهَبْنَا لِدَاوُۥدَ سُلَيْمَـٰنَ ۚ نِعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ ۖ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ

"And We gifted to Dāwūd — Sulaymān. What an excellent servant! Truly he was ever-returning."

Ṣād (38:30)

One night some people's sheep strayed into a field and grazed on it, and both Dāwūd and Sulaymān gave judgment in the case. Shurayḥ the judge and other early scholars said the field was a vineyard, and the sheep ate it bare. Dāwūd ruled that the owners of the vineyard should be paid its value. Sulaymān said he would have judged another way. He would give the sheep to the vineyard's owners to use their milk and young, while the sheep's owners restored the vineyard. Then each side would take back what was theirs. When Dāwūd heard this, he ruled by it.

فَفَهَّمْنَـٰهَا سُلَيْمَـٰنَ ۚ وَكُلًّا ءَاتَيْنَا حُكْمًا وَعِلْمًا ۚ وَسَخَّرْنَا مَعَ دَاوُۥدَ ٱلْجِبَالَ يُسَبِّحْنَ وَٱلطَّيْرَ ۚ وَكُنَّا فَـٰعِلِينَ

"So We gave Sulaymān understanding of the case, and to each of them We gave judgment and knowledge. And We made the mountains glorify Us with Dāwūd, and the birds too. It was We who did it."

Al-Anbiyāʾ (21:79)

The Prophet ﷺ told of another case. Two women had their sons with them when a wolf came and carried off one of the boys. Each woman said to the other, "It took your son." So they brought the case to Dāwūd, and he gave the child to the older woman. Then they went out to Sulaymān and told him. He said, "Bring me a knife, and I will cut him in two between you." The younger woman said, "Do not do it, may God have mercy on you! He is her son." So Sulaymān gave the boy to the younger woman (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

Ibn Kathīr says both rulings may have been allowed in their law, though Sulaymān's was the stronger one. God praised the son for his understanding, and then He praised the father too. The Quran says God gave knowledge to Dāwūd and Sulaymān. Together they said, "Praise be to God who has favoured us over many of His believing servants."

The end of his life

Dāwūd lived the life that was written for him. The Prophet ﷺ said it had been set at sixty years, and Ādam gave him forty more from his own. The authentic sources tell us nothing more about his age.

No authentic saying of the Prophet ﷺ describes how Dāwūd died. Imam Aḥmad records an account in which the angel of death came into his locked house and Sulaymān had birds shade his body. Ibn Kathīr called its chain strong, but al-Arnaʾūṭ showed a break in it, so it cannot be counted as authentic.

What the Quran does tell us is who came after him.

وَوَرِثَ سُلَيْمَـٰنُ دَاوُۥدَ ۖ وَقَالَ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ عُلِّمْنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيْرِ وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَىْءٍ ۖ إِنَّ هَـٰذَا لَهُوَ ٱلْفَضْلُ ٱلْمُبِينُ

"And Sulaymān inherited from Dāwūd. He said, 'O people, we have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given something of everything. This is truly a clear favour.'"

An-Naml (27:16)

Ibn Kathīr explains that Sulaymān inherited his father's prophethood and kingship. Dāwūd had other sons as well, and prophets do not pass on their wealth as other people do. The Prophet ﷺ said that no one inherits from the prophets, and what they leave behind is charity (al-Bukhārī).
