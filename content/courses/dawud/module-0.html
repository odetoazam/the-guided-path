Start Here — The Story

This is the story of Dāwūd, told simply from beginning to end. The Quran tells it in pieces across nine different chapters, and it rarely says when each part happened. Here the pieces are put together in the order they seem to fit, so you can know the whole story before we look at any part of it closely.

A people who wanted a king

Some time after Mūsā, the leaders of the Children of Israel came to one of their prophets and asked him to appoint a king over them, so that they could fight in God's way. The Quran does not tell us this prophet's name. He asked them whether they might hold back once fighting was made their duty. They said they would never hold back, since they had been driven out of their homes and away from their children. Then fighting was made their duty, and all but a few of them turned away.

God raised up a man named Ṭālūt as their king, and they objected at once. They said they had more right to rule than he did, and that he had not even been given much wealth. Their prophet told them that God had chosen Ṭālūt over them and had given him great knowledge and strength of body, and that God gives His kingship to whomever He wills.

The river and the battle

Ṭālūt led the army out and tested them at a river. Whoever drank from it, he said, was not with him, unless he took only one scoop in his hand. Most of them drank anyway. When he and the believers who were left had crossed the river, some of them said they had no strength that day against Jālūt and his soldiers. But those who were sure they would meet God answered that many times a small group has beaten a large one by God's leave, and that God is with those who are patient. So as they went out to face Jālūt and his army, they prayed to God to pour patience on them, to make their feet firm, and to help them against the people who disbelieved.

Then, in the middle of this battle, the Quran names Dāwūd for the first time.

فَهَزَمُوهُم بِإِذْنِ ٱللَّهِ وَقَتَلَ دَاوُۥدُ جَالُوتَ وَءَاتَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُلْكَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَهُۥ مِمَّا يَشَآءُ ۗ وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍ لَّفَسَدَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ وَلَـٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"So they defeated them by God's leave, and Dāwūd killed Jālūt — and God gave him the kingship and wisdom, and taught him from what He willed. And were it not for God pushing back some people by means of others, the earth would have fallen into ruin. But God is full of bounty to all the worlds."

Al-Baqarah (2:251)

That is all the Quran says about the fight. It never says Dāwūd was a shepherd or a boy, it never calls Jālūt a giant, and it never mentions a sling or a stone.

A king, a prophet and a craftsman

God made his kingdom strong and gave him wisdom and clear, decisive speech. God calls him "Our servant," a man of strength who was always turning back to Him. Dāwūd was a prophet, and God gave him a book of his own, the Zabūr.

God made the mountains glorify Him together with Dāwūd in the evening and after sunrise, and the birds were gathered to join them. God also made iron soft for him and taught him to make armour, to protect people in their battles. He told him to make full coats of mail and to measure the links carefully, and He told Dāwūd and his family to do good, because He sees everything they do.

The Quran also says that those of the Children of Israel who disbelieved were cursed by the tongue of Dāwūd, and by the tongue of ʿĪsā son of Maryam, because they disobeyed and kept going too far.

The test

Then came a test in his prayer room, though the Quran does not say when it happened. Two visitors with a dispute climbed over the wall and came in on him, and Dāwūd was alarmed. They told him not to be afraid. They said that one of them had wronged the other, and they asked him to judge between them fairly and to guide them to the right way. Then one of them said, "This is my brother. He has ninety-nine ewes and I have one ewe, and he said, 'Hand her over to me,' and he got the better of me in the argument."

Dāwūd answered that the brother had wronged him by asking for his one ewe on top of his own, and that many partners wrong one another, except those who believe and do good, and they are few. Then Dāwūd realized that God had been testing him. So he asked his Lord for forgiveness, fell down bowing, and turned back to God.

The Quran does not say who the two visitors were, and it does not say exactly where Dāwūd went wrong. Later storytellers attached a tale about a woman and her husband to this scene, but that tale is not in the Quran.

Forgiven, and trusted

God forgave him, and said that Dāwūd would have a place near Him and a good place to return to. Then God spoke to him directly and said, "O Dāwūd, We have made you a khalīfa on the earth." A khalīfa is someone put in charge of a place and trusted to look after it. So God told him to judge between people with truth and not to follow his own desires, because they would lead him away from God's path. His mistake ended with forgiveness, a place near God, and a trust.

Father and son

God gave Dāwūd a son, Sulaymān.

وَوَهَبْنَا لِدَاوُۥدَ سُلَيْمَـٰنَ ۚ نِعْمَ ٱلْعَبْدُ ۖ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابٌ

"And We gifted to Dāwūd — Sulaymān. What an excellent servant! Truly he was ever-returning."

Ṣād (38:30)

Once, some people's sheep got into a field at night and grazed on it, and both Dāwūd and Sulaymān gave judgment. God gave Sulaymān the right understanding of the case, and the Quran says that He had given each of them judgment and knowledge.

God gave knowledge to both of them, and together they thanked Him, saying, "Praise be to God, who has favoured us over many of His believing servants." After Dāwūd, Sulaymān inherited from him. The Quran does not tell us how Dāwūd died. It praises the son as it praised the father, as a servant who was always turning back to God.
