Module 1 — The Complete Man

You may think you already know this man, because the boy with a sling who brings down a giant with a stone is one of the most famous stories any child hears. Yet the Quran barely mentions the sling. It gives the giant half a verse and moves on, because for the Quran the stone was only the way into Dāwūd's life, and the rest of that life matters more. What follows is a portrait unlike any other in the book, of one human being who was given nearly everything a person can be given. In this module we walk through all of it once, so that you can see how much he had. The Quran then says one surprising thing about him, and it only makes sense once you have seen how much he was given.

It starts with a giant, and then keeps going

فَهَزَمُوهُم بِإِذْنِ ٱللَّهِ وَقَتَلَ دَاوُۥدُ جَالُوتَ وَءَاتَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُلْكَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَهُۥ مِمَّا يَشَآءُ ۗ وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍ لَّفَسَدَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ وَلَـٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"So they defeated them by God's leave, and Dāwūd killed Jālūt, and God gave him kingship and wisdom and taught him from what He willed. And were it not for God's repelling people by means of one another, the earth would have been corrupted — but God is full of bounty to the worlds."

Al-Baqarah (2:251)

Notice how quickly that verse moves. The battle takes one short phrase, and then the verse turns straight to what he was given: kingship, wisdom and teaching. The fight only opens the story, and the verse is really about the gifts. A shepherd who was nobody the night before ends up with a crown, and that crown is only the first of six things God places in his hands.

The six gifts

One: the crown, and the clear mind to carry it. He is made a king, and a real one. The Quran says God strengthened his kingship and gave him wisdom, along with something it calls faṣl al-khiṭāb (38:20). This means the decisive word, the ability to listen to a tangled argument and then say the one sentence that settles it. So he was given power, and also the clear thinking he needed to use it well.

Two: a book of his own. He is given the Zabūr, and he is the only person in the Quran whose scripture is named for him alone:

وَرَبُّكَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَن فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۗ وَلَقَدْ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ۖ وَءَاتَيْنَا دَاوُۥدَ زَبُورًا

"And your Lord knows best whoever is in the heavens and the earth. And We favored some of the prophets over others, and We gave Dāwūd a Zabūr."

Al-Isra (17:55)

Here we meet the first strange thing about his story, and Module 4 is built on it. The Quran names his book and honors it, yet it never quotes a single line from it, and that is no accident.

Three: a voice the world answered. This gift is the hardest to picture and the easiest to feel. When he praised God, the rest of creation joined in with him:

إِنَّا سَخَّرْنَا ٱلْجِبَالَ مَعَهُۥ يُسَبِّحْنَ بِٱلْعَشِىِّ وَٱلْإِشْرَاقِ

"Indeed, We subjected the mountains to glorify [God] with him, at evening and at sunrise."

Ṣād (38:18)

The mountains echoed his praise, and the birds gathered overhead to join it, so that one man's voice stood at the center of a whole landscape singing along with him.

Four: hands that shaped iron. He was a craftsman as well as a king. The Quran says iron went soft for him, and God taught him how to work it into armor:

وَعَلَّمْنَـٰهُ صَنْعَةَ لَبُوسٍ لَّكُمْ لِتُحْصِنَكُم مِّنۢ بَأْسِكُمْ ۖ فَهَلْ أَنتُمْ شَـٰكِرُونَ

"And We taught him the making of armor for you, to protect you from your [own] violence. So will you be grateful?"

Al-Anbiya (21:80)

So here is a prophet and a king working at a forge, and the Quran does not treat this work as beneath him. It counts what he made with his hands as one of God's gifts to him.

Five: the scales of justice. He is the judge people bring their disputes to. One case is about a crop that sheep trampled (21:78). The harder case, which we will spend real time on later, is about the two men who climb the wall into his private room, one with ninety-nine ewes and the other with only one (38:21–24).

Six: the title, above all of it. He is given a title the Quran gives to almost no one else, khalīfa fī l-arḍ, a steward placed over the earth (38:26). So the full portrait is of a warrior, a king, a prophet, a poet, a craftsman, a judge and a steward. If you sat down to design the complete human being, your list would probably look like this one.

The question to carry

This brings us to the question the whole course is built around.

Think of a man like that, with every strength a person could have gathered into one life. If God were going to repeat one word about him, as the thing most worth remembering, what would you expect that word to be? Would it be king, or warrior, or the just?

The Quran does choose one word, and it is none of those. It is a quieter and stranger word, and once you see it, you will look at everything else in his life differently.

Keep that question with you. In Module 2 we will slow down inside two of his scenes and look closely at what the Quran does with them, and then in Module 3 we will find the word.
