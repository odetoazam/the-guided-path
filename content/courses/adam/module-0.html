Start Here — The Story

Before we study any part of it, you should be able to tell this story out loud from start to finish. It helps to know that the Quran tells Ādam's story seven times, in seven different chapters, and each telling keeps different pieces. No single chapter has all of it, so what follows is put together from those seven. Module 4 looks at why the story is spread out this way.

An announcement, and an objection

The story begins before the man even exists, when God tells the angels what He is about to do.

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى جَاعِلٌ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوٓا۟ أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَن يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ ٱلدِّمَآءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّىٓ أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"And when your Lord said to the angels: Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority. They said: Will You place upon it one who causes corruption in it and sheds blood, while we declare Your praise and sanctify You? He said: Indeed, I know that which you do not know."

Al-Baqarah (2:30)

Two details in this verse matter for the rest of the course. The first is the word God uses for this creature, khalīfa, which means a steward or a deputy, someone put in charge of a place. The second is that the place is named, and it is the earth. Nothing has gone wrong yet and there is nothing to punish, but the earth is already where he is being sent to work. Keep that in mind when we reach the part people call "the Fall."

The angels then ask an honest question. This creature will spread corruption and kill, they say, while we already worship You perfectly. God does not argue with the facts. He simply answers, I know what you do not know. The angels are about to be shown some of what He knew, and the rest takes the whole story to come out.

Made, taught, honoured

The man is formed from clay, and the Quran uses several words for the stages of that clay, from dust to dark mud that has been shaped. Then God does something the Quran says about nothing else: He breathes into him of His spirit, and He commands the angels to fall down before him. In Sūrat Ṣād, God says He created him with My two hands. In the Baqarah telling, the new creature is taught something the angels were not taught, the names, all of them. When the angels cannot recite the names and he can, their question is answered right in front of them.

All the angels bow, except one being who is there and refuses. He argues that he is made of better material than the man, and he is thrown out. His refusal is a whole story of its own, with its own course, so from here on he appears in this story only where he touches Ādam. The part that matters for us is what he says as he leaves. He asks to be spared until the Day of Resurrection, and he is given a respite, though God sets when that respite ends, not him. Then he promises to come after this creature and its children.

The garden, the warning, the whisper

Ādam and his wife are settled in the garden and told they may eat freely from wherever they like, except from one tree. The warning they get is clear and personal. In Sūrat Ṭā Hā, God tells Ādam by name, this one is an enemy to you and to your wife, so do not let him drive you both out of the garden.

Then the enemy goes to work, and he does it with a whisper instead of force. In the Ṭā Hā telling he comes to Ādam with a question: shall I show you the tree of eternity, and a kingdom that never fades? In al-Aʿrāf he works on the two of them together. He gives them a new explanation for why God forbade the tree, and he swears to them both that he is only giving them sincere advice. Before any of this, Ṭā Hā prepares us with one very gentle sentence about Ādam.

وَلَقَدْ عَهِدْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ ءَادَمَ مِن قَبْلُ فَنَسِىَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًا

"And We had already taken a promise from Ādam before, but he forgot; and We found not in him determination."

Ṭā Hā (20:115)

He forgot, and his resolve was not firm. We will look at that sentence closely in Module 2.

The eating, and the two sentences

As they eat, none of what was promised arrives, neither the immortality nor the kingdom that never ends. Instead, they suddenly see their own bodies and feel ashamed, and they start covering themselves with leaves. Then the Quran says it plainly: and Ādam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray. Module 4 looks at that sentence exactly as it stands, because those are the words the Quran chose.

Next comes the moment that this course and its companion course both turn on. Ādam and his wife speak together, saying the same words in one breath.

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ

"They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:23)

Al-Baqarah tells us where that sentence came from: Ādam received words from his Lord, and then God turned to him. So Ādam did not make up his own repentance. It was handed to him by the very One he had disobeyed, and Module 5 is about that.

Down, with a promise

They leave the garden for the earth, which is where a khalīfa was always going to stand. It is worth listening to what God tells them as they are sent down. He warns them, some of you enemies to some, and then, above that warning, He gives them this promise.

قُلْنَا ٱهْبِطُوا۟ مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"We said: Go down from it, all of you. And when guidance comes to you from Me — whoever follows My guidance, there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve."

Al-Baqarah (2:38)

In the Ṭā Hā telling, one more verse ends the story on a hopeful note for Ādam: his Lord chose him, turned to him, and guided him. Notice that the choosing comes after the wrong, not before it. The order of those words is important enough to get a module of its own.

The course map

  • Module 0 — Start Here (you are here). The whole story, in order, assembled honestly.
  • Module 1 — Made, and Taught. Before the fall: what kind of creature this is, what the honour was actually for, and what the angels could and could not see.
  • Module 2 — The Whisper and the Forgetting. The deception step by step, the door it came through, and the door out.
  • Module 3 — Two Sentences. Two beings break one command. One says we wronged ourselves and one says You made me stray, and the Quran carries the whole difference in the grammar of a single root.
  • Module 4 — What the Quran Won't Tell You. The things left out: no name for the woman, no species for the tree, no serpent, no inherited guilt. Also the hard verse, with the scholars' views set out.
  • Module 5 — The Words You Were Given. The ending that faces you. The way back was never something you had to invent.

This is the only story in the Quran where the first wrong a human being did is followed straight away by the first mercy a human being was shown. That mercy came as words.
