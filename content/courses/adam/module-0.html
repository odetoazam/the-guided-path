Start Here — The Story

This is the whole life of Ādam, from the day God made him to the day he was buried. It comes from the Quran and from the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ, told in order. Where a detail rests on a weaker report, the page says so.

God tells the angels

Before Ādam was created, God told the angels that He was going to place a new creature on the earth.

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى جَاعِلٌ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوٓا۟ أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَن يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ ٱلدِّمَآءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّىٓ أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"And when your Lord said to the angels: Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority. They said: Will You place upon it one who causes corruption in it and sheds blood, while we declare Your praise and sanctify You? He said: Indeed, I know that which you do not know."

Al-Baqarah (2:30)

God called this creature a khalīfa. The word means someone who is put in charge of a place and trusted to look after it. So from the very beginning, the earth was meant to be his home and his work.

The angels were worried. They asked why God would make a being who would cause harm and shed blood, when they already praised Him all the time. God did not argue with them. He only said, "I know what you do not know."

Made from the earth

The Prophet ﷺ said that God made Ādam from a handful of soil that He took from every part of the earth. So Ādam's children came out like the earth itself. Some are red, some are white and some are black, and some are in between. Some are gentle and some are hard. Some are good and some are bad (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it sound).

The Quran says that Ādam was made from dark mud that dried into clay that rang like pottery. God shaped him Himself. In the Quran He speaks of Ādam as the one "I created with My two hands."

The Prophet ﷺ said that Ādam was created on a Friday, the best day the sun rises on (Muslim). He also said that when God had shaped Ādam in the garden, He left him there for as long as He wished. Iblīs began to walk around the still figure to see what it was. When he saw that it was hollow inside, he knew this was a creature that would not be able to hold itself back (Muslim).

Ādam was very tall. The Prophet ﷺ said that God created him sixty cubits in height. A cubit is the length of a forearm. Everyone who enters Paradise will have the form of Ādam, and people have kept growing smaller ever since (al-Bukhārī).

The first breath and the first greeting

Then God breathed into Ādam of His spirit, and Ādam came to life. The Quran says the angels had been told that when this moment came, they were to fall down in prostration before him.

The Prophet ﷺ said that when the spirit entered Ādam, he sneezed. He said, "All praise belongs to God." He praised God by God's own leave. His Lord answered him with the words "May God have mercy on you, Ādam" (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it fair).

Then God told Ādam to go and greet a group of angels and to listen to how they answered. Their answer would be his greeting and the greeting of his children after him. Ādam said, "Peace be upon you." The angels replied, "Peace be upon you and the mercy of God." So they added the mercy of God to his greeting (al-Bukhārī). Muslims still greet one another with these words today.

The names and the bow

God also taught Ādam the names, all of them. When He asked the angels to tell Him these names, they could not. They said they only knew what God had taught them. Then Ādam told them the names, and the angels' question was answered in front of them.

All the angels bowed down before Ādam. Iblīs was there with them, but he refused. He said he was better than Ādam, because God had made him from fire and made Ādam from clay. So God threw him out.

Before he left, Iblīs asked God to give him time until the Day of Resurrection. God gave him time, but only until a day that God Himself had set. Iblīs then swore that he would lead Ādam's children astray, all except God's sincere servants. His story has its own course, so from here on we meet him only where he touches Ādam.

Ādam sees his children

The Prophet ﷺ said that after God created Ādam, He wiped Ādam's back. Out of it came every soul that God would create from Ādam's line until the Day of Resurrection. Between the eyes of each one was a gleam of light. God showed them all to Ādam, and Ādam asked who they were. God told him that they were his children.

Among them Ādam saw a man whose light he admired. He asked who this was. God said, "This is a man from the last of the nations among your children. He is called Dāwūd." Ādam asked how long a life God had given him. God said sixty years. So Ādam said to God, "Give him forty more years from my own life" (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it sound). Dāwūd would be born many generations later, and he would become a prophet and a king.

The Quran also speaks of a covenant that God took from the children of Ādam, when He brought them out from their backs and asked them a question.

وَإِذْ أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ مِنۢ بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ ۖ قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ ۛ شَهِدْنَآ ۛ أَن تَقُولُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَـٰمَةِ إِنَّا كُنَّا عَنْ هَـٰذَا غَـٰفِلِينَ

"And when your Lord took from the children of Ādam, from their backs, their descendants and made them testify about themselves: Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes, we testify. Lest you should say on the Day of Resurrection: Indeed, we were unaware of this."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:172)

The Prophet ﷺ spoke of the man in the Fire whose punishment is lightest. God will ask him whether he would give everything on the earth to be free of it, and he will say yes. God will tell him that He once asked something far easier of him, while he was still in the loins of Ādam. He asked only that the man never set up partners with Him, but the man insisted on doing it (al-Bukhārī). Ibn Kathīr notes that scholars differ on exactly how this covenant took place.

Long after Ādam's death, the Prophet ﷺ saw him looking at his children once more. It happened on the night the Prophet ﷺ was taken up through the heavens. In the nearest heaven he found a man sitting with shadowy figures on his right and on his left. When the man looked to his right he laughed, and when he looked to his left he wept. The man welcomed him with the words, "Welcome to the righteous prophet and the righteous son." Jibrīl told him that this was Ādam. The figures were the souls of his children. Those on the right were the people of Paradise and those on the left were the people of the Fire (al-Bukhārī).

Life in the garden

God made a wife for Ādam from Ādam himself, so that he could find rest and comfort with her. The Quran does not give her name. The Prophet ﷺ calls her Ḥawwāʾ in a hadith recorded by al-Bukhārī.

God settled Ādam and his wife in the garden. The Prophet ﷺ said that this too happened on a Friday (Muslim). They could eat freely from anything they liked, except one tree, which they were told not to go near. God told Ādam that in the garden he would never go hungry or be without clothes. He would never be thirsty or suffer from the heat of the sun.

God also warned Ādam clearly that Iblīs was an enemy to him and to his wife. He told him not to let Iblīs drive them out of the garden, or he would end up in hardship.

The whisper

Iblīs did not use force. He whispered to them. He asked Ādam if he wanted to be shown the tree of eternity and a kingdom that never fades. He told them both that God had only forbidden the tree so they would not become angels or live forever. He even swore to them that he was giving them sincere advice.

The Quran describes what happened to Ādam in a very gentle way.

وَلَقَدْ عَهِدْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ ءَادَمَ مِن قَبْلُ فَنَسِىَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًا

"And We had already taken a promise from Ādam before, but he forgot; and We found not in him determination."

Ṭā Hā (20:115)

Ādam had been told, but he forgot. He was not firm enough to hold on to what he had been told.

They eat from the tree

Ādam and his wife ate from the tree, and none of what Iblīs had promised came true. Instead, they suddenly became aware of their nakedness. They felt ashamed, and they began covering themselves with leaves from the garden. The Quran says it plainly: "Ādam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray."

Then God called out to them. He reminded them that He had forbidden them that tree. He reminded them that He had told them Iblīs was their open enemy.

They turn back to God

Ādam and his wife admitted what they had done. Together they said:

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ

"They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:23)

Another chapter says that Ādam received words from his Lord, and that God then accepted his repentance. Many early scholars, among them Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, said those words were this very prayer (Ibn Kathīr). If so, Ādam did not have to invent his own way back. God gave him the words, and then accepted them from him.

Down to earth

God sent them down from the garden to the earth, the place where a khalīfa was always meant to be. The Prophet ﷺ said that this too happened on a Friday (Muslim). God told them that some of them would be enemies to others. He told them the earth would be their home and would give them what they needed for a time. But with that warning He also gave them a promise.

قُلْنَا ٱهْبِطُوا۟ مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"We said: Go down from it, all of you. And when guidance comes to you from Me — whoever follows My guidance, there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve."

Al-Baqarah (2:38)

The Quran says that after all of this, Ādam's Lord chose him. He accepted his repentance and guided him. That choosing came after his mistake.

The Prophet ﷺ told of a time when Mūsā met Ādam and argued with him. Mūsā said, "You are Ādam, whose mistake brought you out of the garden." Ādam answered, "You are Mūsā, whom God chose for His message and His speech. Do you blame me for something that was decreed for me before I was created?" The Prophet ﷺ said that Ādam won the argument, and he said it twice (al-Bukhārī). Some scholars explained that Mūsā was blaming him for a sin he had already repented of. As Ibn Kathīr puts it, the one who repents of a sin is like one who has no sin.

Ādam the prophet

Ādam was also a prophet. The Quran says that God chose Ādam and Nūḥ, and the family of Ibrāhīm and the family of ʿImrān, above all the worlds. A man once asked the Prophet ﷺ whether Ādam was a prophet. He answered, "Yes, one to whom God spoke." The man asked how much time lay between Ādam and Nūḥ. He said, "Ten qarns" (Ibn Ḥibbān, graded authentic by al-Arnaʾūṭ). A qarn can mean a generation or a century, so scholars have read this both ways.

The two sons

On the earth Ādam and his wife had children. The Quran tells the story of two of his sons, but it does not give their names. Each of them offered a sacrifice to God. The offering of one was accepted and the offering of the other was not.

The one whose offering was refused said to his brother, "I will surely kill you." His brother answered, "God only accepts from those who fear Him." Then he said:

لَئِنۢ بَسَطتَ إِلَىَّ يَدَكَ لِتَقْتُلَنِى مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍ يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ لِأَقْتُلَكَ ۖ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"If you stretch out your hand against me to kill me, I will not stretch out my hand against you to kill you. Indeed, I fear God, Lord of the worlds."

Al-Māʾidah (5:28)

He added that he would rather his brother carry the sin of killing him along with his own sins. That is how Mujāhid and al-Ṭabarī explained his words (Ibn Kathīr). But the other brother's soul made the killing seem easy to him. So he killed his brother, and he became one of the losers.

Then he did not know what to do with the body. So God sent a crow that scratched at the ground to show him how to hide his brother's body.

فَبَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ غُرَابًا يَبْحَثُ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ لِيُرِيَهُۥ كَيْفَ يُوَٰرِى سَوْءَةَ أَخِيهِ ۚ قَالَ يَـٰوَيْلَتَىٰٓ أَعَجَزْتُ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِثْلَ هَـٰذَا ٱلْغُرَابِ فَأُوَٰرِىَ سَوْءَةَ أَخِى ۖ فَأَصْبَحَ مِنَ ٱلنَّـٰدِمِينَ

"Then God sent a crow searching in the ground to show him how to hide the body of his brother. He said: Woe to me! Am I unable to be like this crow and hide the body of my brother? And he became one of the regretful."

Al-Māʾidah (5:31)

The Prophet ﷺ said that no soul is ever killed unjustly without the first son of Ādam carrying a share of its blood. That is because he was the first to make killing a way that others followed (al-Bukhārī). The Prophet ﷺ once warned of a time of civil strife to come. The Companion Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ asked him what to do if a man broke into his house to kill him then. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Be like the son of Ādam." He meant the brother who would not raise his hand (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it fair).

Many retellings call the brothers Qābīl and Hābīl and say they fought over a marriage. Neither the names nor that quarrel come from the Quran or from an authentic hadith.

The death of Ādam

Ādam lived a long life on the earth, and his children spread across it. The Quran says that God created all people from a single soul and made its mate from it. From the two of them He spread out many men and women.

Then the angel of death came to Ādam. The Prophet ﷺ said that Ādam told the angel that forty years of his life were still left. The angel asked whether he had not given them to his son Dāwūd. Ādam had forgotten the gift he made on the day he saw his children. He denied it, and so his children deny. He forgot, and so his children forget. He made a mistake, and so his children make mistakes (al-Tirmidhī, who graded it sound). The Quran had used the same word about the tree: Ādam forgot.

Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, described what happened next. When death came near, Ādam told his sons that he longed for some fruit of Paradise. They went out to look for it. On the way they met angels who were carrying his shroud and perfume, with axes and spades and baskets. The angels asked the sons what they were looking for. They said their father was ill and wanted fruit of Paradise. The angels told them, "Go back. What was decreed for your father has come."

So the angels came to Ādam and took his soul. They washed him and wrapped him in the shroud and perfumed him. They dug his grave and prayed over him. Then they laid him in it and set bricks over him and covered him with earth. Then they said, "Children of Ādam, this is your sunna." A sunna is a path that has been walked, so others can follow it after you. Muslims still bury their dead in these same steps. Ibn Kathīr judged the chain of this report to Ubayy sound, but al-Arnaʾūṭ judged it weak. So it is told here as Ubayy's report and not as certain.

Many retellings also name the place where Ādam was buried. The reports disagree with one another, and none of them comes from a reliable source. Wherever that grave is, Ādam was returned to the earth he was made from.
