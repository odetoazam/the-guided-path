Start Here — The Story

This is the story of Ādam, told simply from beginning to end. The Quran tells it in seven different chapters, and each one gives some of the details. Here they are put together in order, so you can know the whole story before we look at any part of it closely.

God tells the angels

Before Ādam was created, God told the angels that He was going to place a new creature on the earth.

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى جَاعِلٌ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوٓا۟ أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَن يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ ٱلدِّمَآءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّىٓ أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"And when your Lord said to the angels: Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority. They said: Will You place upon it one who causes corruption in it and sheds blood, while we declare Your praise and sanctify You? He said: Indeed, I know that which you do not know."

Al-Baqarah (2:30)

God called this creature a khalīfa, which means someone put in charge of a place and trusted to look after it. So from the very beginning, the earth was meant to be his home and his work.

The angels were worried. They asked why God would make a being who would cause harm and shed blood, when they already praised Him all the time. God did not argue with them. He only said, "I know what you do not know."

Ādam is created and honoured

God made Ādam from clay. He says He made him "with My two hands," and when He had shaped him, He breathed into him of His spirit. Then He commanded the angels to bow down before him.

God also taught Ādam the names, all of them. When He asked the angels to tell Him these names, they could not. They said they only knew what God had taught them. Then Ādam told them the names, and the angels' question was answered in front of them.

Iblīs refuses

All the angels bowed. Iblīs, who was there with them, refused. He said he was better than Ādam, because God had made him from fire and made Ādam from clay. So God threw him out.

Before he left, Iblīs asked God to give him time until the Day of Resurrection. God gave him time, but only until a day that God Himself had set. Iblīs then swore that he would lead Ādam's children astray, all except God's sincere servants. His story has its own course, so from here on we meet him only where he touches Ādam.

Life in the garden

God settled Ādam and his wife in the garden. The Quran never tells us her name. They could eat freely from anything they liked, except one tree, which they were told not to go near. God also warned Ādam clearly that Iblīs was an enemy to him and to his wife, and that he must not let Iblīs drive them out of the garden.

The whisper

Iblīs did not use force. He whispered to them. He asked Ādam, "Shall I show you the tree of eternity, and a kingdom that never fades?" He told them both that God had only forbidden the tree so they would not become angels or live forever. He even swore to them that he was giving them sincere advice.

The Quran describes what happened to Ādam in a very gentle way.

وَلَقَدْ عَهِدْنَآ إِلَىٰٓ ءَادَمَ مِن قَبْلُ فَنَسِىَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُۥ عَزْمًا

"And We had already taken a promise from Ādam before, but he forgot; and We found not in him determination."

Ṭā Hā (20:115)

Ādam had been told, but he forgot, and he was not firm enough to hold on to what he had been told.

They eat from the tree

Ādam and his wife ate from the tree, and none of what Iblīs had promised came true. Instead, they suddenly became aware of their nakedness. They felt ashamed, and they began covering themselves with leaves from the garden. The Quran says it plainly: "Ādam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray."

Then God called out to them. He reminded them that He had forbidden them that tree, and that He had told them Iblīs was their open enemy.

They turn back to God

Ādam and his wife admitted what they had done. Together they said:

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ

"They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:23)

Another chapter says that Ādam received words from his Lord, and that God then accepted his repentance. Al-Ṭabarī, who wrote one of the earliest great commentaries on the Quran, understood those words to be this very prayer. If so, Ādam did not have to invent his own way back. God gave him the words, and then accepted them from him.

Down to earth, with a promise

God sent them down from the garden to the earth, the place where a khalīfa was always meant to be. He told them that some of them would be enemies to others. But with that warning He also gave them a promise.

قُلْنَا ٱهْبِطُوا۟ مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"We said: Go down from it, all of you. And when guidance comes to you from Me — whoever follows My guidance, there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve."

Al-Baqarah (2:38)

The story ends with hope. The Quran says that after all of this, Ādam's Lord chose him, accepted his repentance, and guided him. That choosing came after his mistake.
