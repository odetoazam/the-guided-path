Module 5 — The Words You Were Given

Every course on this site ends by turning to face you. This one has been facing you the whole time — you may have noticed — because unlike a king with an army or a prophet with a kingdom, this is the story of the one figure in the Quran whose situation is exactly yours: warned, forgetful, deceived by something that sounded like advice, and standing somewhere after the wrong thing has already been done. So this last module has no application step — it is the story itself, finishing in you.

What were the words?

فَتَلَقَّىٰٓ ءَادَمُ مِن رَّبِّهِۦ كَلِمَـٰتٍ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلتَّوَّابُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

"Then Ādam received from his Lord words, and He turned to him in forgiveness. Indeed, it is He who is the Accepting of repentance, the Merciful."

Al-Baqarah (2:37)

The verse says kalimāt — words — and does not quote them. The early scholars asked the obvious question, and their answers are on record. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, and al-Ḥasan said: the words are the sentence of al-Aʿrāf 7:23 — Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves... Mujāhid (in another report) and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī preserved a different wording, a fuller confession ending in the very names this verse ends in. And al-Ṭabarī, after weighing the reports, ruled — on the ground that the Book itself points there: the words Ādam received are the words the Quran itself shows him saying, rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā. He adds, with characteristic fairness, that the other reports are not disproven — only unevidenced.

Feel what that ruling means for the two verses you have been carrying since Module 3. The sentence of ownership — the one that separated Ādam from Iblīs forever — was, on al-Ṭabarī's reading, itself the gift. Ādam did not reach the sentence by moral brilliance. The sentence was handed to him, and his part was to receive it and say it — about himself, to his Lord, in time.

The reading where the words come walking

One more layer, preserved in the transmitted readings of the Quran. In the reading of Ibn Kathīr of Makkah — one of the canonical reciters — the grammar of the verse turns around: fa-talaqqā Ādama min rabbihi kalimātun — "then words from his Lord came to Ādam." Al-Baghawī records it plainly: on this reading the words are the subject; they came to him, and were the cause of his repentance.

Both readings are transmitted, and they agree on the substance while lighting it from two sides: in one, a man reaches out and receives; in the other, the rescue is on its way to him before he has moved. Either way, the direction of the mercy is the same — downward, first.

The promise on the way down

قَالَ ٱهْبِطَا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًۢا ۖ بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ

"He said: Go down from it, both of you, together — some of you an enemy to some. But if there comes to you from Me guidance, then whoever follows My guidance will not go astray nor suffer."

Ṭā Hā (20:123)

Listen to the ending with Ṭā Hā's own ears. When the enemy was first pointed out, the warning ended on one word: fa-tashqā — "lest you suffer" (20:117). Now the descent ends on the same word, negated: whoever follows My guidance lā yaḍillu wa-lā yashqā — does not lose the way, and does not suffer. The sūrah closes the loop it opened: the very suffering the warning feared is the thing the guidance-clause cancels. And this is the first thing ever said about life on earth: not "survive," not "be punished," but guidance will come, and following it is the way home. The whole religion is that clause, unfolded.

Three questions

1. What is your sentence, and whose shape is it in? You have a story you tell about the wrong things you have done. Listen to it the way Module 3 taught you. Does it run like 7:23 — we wronged ourselves, the wrong owned, the speaker inside the sentence — or like 7:16 — because of what was done to me, some version of they made me, it made me, you made me? Be precise; the two shapes lead out of the same garden through different gates, and 28:63 proves the owning sentence can be said too late to matter.

2. What have you forgotten, rather than defied? The first sin came through a warning gone quiet, not through rebellion. Somewhere in your life is a thing you know perfectly well and have stopped remembering at the moment it matters. Name it. That is where your version of this story is currently being written.

3. Are you waiting to deserve the return? Ādam was chosen after. The words were supplied, not composed. The Turner turns first, again and again — that is what the intensive form means. If you are postponing your turning until you are worthy of the reception, you have the direction of this entire story backwards.

The close

Stand back and look at what this course actually taught. A creature was made for the earth, honoured with teaching, and beaten — once — by an enemy using a forged oath on a forgotten warning. Everything after that hinged on one sentence, and the sentence was a gift: we wronged ourselves. Owned wrong, spoken upward, in time. Then: chosen. Then: sent down not with a curse but with a promise, to do the job announced before the fall was ever part of the story.

And behind the whole arc, one name, in the intensive form: al-Tawwāb. The One who turns, and turns, and turns again — toward. The first human being learned that name from inside the first forgiveness. It has not changed since.

The other being in this pair of courses — the one who stood in the same garden, broke his own command, and studied the fork from its far side — refused the sentence and kept the reason. That anatomy is its own course, and if you have not walked it, walk it next: the two stories are one lesson, told from its two possible endings. On the last day, the Quran records him telling his followers: do not blame me — blame yourselves (14:22). Which is, in substance, Ādam's sentence — the fault lies with yourselves — pointed at everyone but the speaker. He hands out at the end of the world the very sentence Ādam said in the garden, about himself, in time. The entire difference between the two — the Quran has been telling you for six modules — is who the sentence is about, and when it gets said.

Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā. The words are still given. They were given to you six modules ago.
