Module 5 — The Words You Were Given

Every course on this site ends by turning to face you. This one has been facing you the whole time. Other figures in the Quran come with armies or kingdoms, and this one comes with your own situation: warned, forgetful, deceived by something that sounded like advice, standing somewhere after the wrong thing has already been done. So this last module has no application step. It is the story itself, finishing in you.

What were the words?

فَتَلَقَّىٰٓ ءَادَمُ مِن رَّبِّهِۦ كَلِمَـٰتٍ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلتَّوَّابُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

"Then Ādam received from his Lord words, and He turned to him in forgiveness. Indeed, it is He who is the Accepting of repentance, the Merciful."

Al-Baqarah (2:37)

The verse says kalimāt, words, and does not quote them. The early scholars asked the obvious question, and their answers are on record. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan said the words are the sentence of al-Aʿrāf 7:23, Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves... Mujāhid, in another report, and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī kept a different wording, a fuller confession that ends in the very names this verse ends in. Al-Ṭabarī weighed the reports and then ruled, on the ground that the Book itself points there. The words Ādam received are the words the Quran shows him saying, rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā. He adds, fairly, that the other reports are not disproven. They are only unevidenced.

Think about what that ruling means for the two verses you have been carrying since Module 3. The sentence of ownership, the one that separated Ādam from Iblīs forever, was itself the gift on al-Ṭabarī's reading. Ādam did not arrive at the sentence by moral brilliance. It was handed to him, and his part was to receive it and say it, about himself, to his Lord, in time.

The reading where the words come walking

There is one more layer, kept in the transmitted readings of the Quran. In the reading of Ibn Kathīr of Makkah, one of the canonical reciters, the grammar of the verse turns around. It becomes fa-talaqqā Ādama min rabbihi kalimātun, "then words from his Lord came to Ādam." Al-Baghawī records it plainly. On this reading the words are the subject. They came to him, and they were the cause of his repentance.

Both readings are transmitted, and they agree on the substance while lighting it from two sides. In one, a man reaches out and receives. In the other, the rescue is already on its way to him before he has moved. Either way the mercy moves in the same direction, which is downward and first.

The promise on the way down

قَالَ ٱهْبِطَا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًۢا ۖ بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّى هُدًى فَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ هُدَاىَ فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ

"He said: Go down from it, both of you, together — some of you an enemy to some. But if there comes to you from Me guidance, then whoever follows My guidance will not go astray nor suffer."

Ṭā Hā (20:123)

Read the ending with Ṭā Hā's own ears. When the enemy was first pointed out, the warning ended on one word, fa-tashqā, "lest you suffer" (20:117). Now the descent ends on the same word, negated. Whoever follows My guidance lā yaḍillu wa-lā yashqā, does not lose the way and does not suffer. The sūrah closes the loop it opened, since the guidance clause cancels the very suffering the warning feared. And this is the first thing ever said about life on earth. It does not say survive, and it does not say be punished. It says that guidance will come, and that following it is the way home. The whole religion is that clause, unfolded.

Three questions

1. What is your sentence, and whose shape is it in? You have a story you tell about the wrong things you have done. Listen to it the way Module 3 taught you. Does it run like 7:23, we wronged ourselves, with the wrong owned and the speaker inside the sentence? Or does it run like 7:16, because of what was done to me, in some version of they made me, it made me, You made me? Be precise. The two shapes lead out of the same garden through different gates, and 28:63 shows that the owning sentence can be said too late to matter.

2. What have you forgotten, rather than defied? The first sin came through a warning that had gone quiet, not through rebellion. Somewhere in your life is a thing you know perfectly well and have stopped remembering at the moment it matters. Name it. That is where your version of this story is being written right now.

3. Are you waiting to deserve the return? Ādam was chosen afterwards. The words were supplied to him, not composed by him. The Turner turns first, again and again, which is what the intensive form means. If you are putting off your turning until you are worthy of the welcome, you have the direction of this whole story backwards.

The close

Stand back and look at what this course taught. A creature was made for the earth and honoured with teaching. He was beaten once, by an enemy who used a forged oath on a forgotten warning. Everything after that hung on one sentence, and the sentence was a gift: we wronged ourselves. The wrong was owned, spoken upward, in time. Then he was chosen. Then he was sent down with a promise rather than a curse, to do the job that had been announced before the fall was ever part of the story.

Behind the whole arc is one name in the intensive form, al-Tawwāb. The One who turns, and turns, and turns again, toward us. The first human being learned that name from inside the first forgiveness, and it has not changed since.

The other being in this pair of courses stood in the same garden, broke his own command, and saw the fork from its far side. He refused the sentence and kept the reason. That anatomy is its own course, and if you have not walked it, walk it next, because the two stories are one lesson told from its two possible endings. On the last day, the Quran records him telling his followers: do not blame me, blame yourselves (14:22). In substance that is Ādam's sentence, the fault lies with yourselves, pointed at everyone except the speaker. He hands out at the end of the world the sentence Ādam said in the garden, about himself, in time. For six modules the Quran has been showing you where the difference lies. It lies in who the sentence is about, and when it gets said.

Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā. The words are still given. They were given to you six modules ago.
