Module 1 — Made, and Taught

Before you can feel the weight of the fall, you have to feel the height of the honour. This module walks through the portrait of the creature before anything goes wrong. What was given to him explains two things: what the test was, and why the way back took the shape it did.

A steward, announced in advance

Go back to the announcement from Module 0: I will make upon the earth a khalīfa. The word means someone who stands in and takes charge, a deputy or a steward. Two things about it repay slow reading.

The first is that the earth is in the sentence. The garden is where the story starts, but the earth is what the job description says. So whatever else the leaving of the garden is, it is also a khalīfa arriving at his posting.

The second is that the announcement invites a question, and the angels ask it, respectfully but pointedly. This creature will corrupt and shed blood. They themselves already praise without pause. What is it for? God's reply is I know what you do not know, and He gives no other answer here. The rest of this module is the Quran showing, in scenes, part of what He knew.

What the angels could see, and what they couldn't

Al-Rāzī, in his commentary on this passage, sets out the structure of the moment. The angels had their eyes on the corruption and the killing, he says, and so they thought little of this creature. They also had their eyes on the obedience of Iblīs, who at that point was known for his devotion, and so they thought much of him. But the Knower of the unseen knew what each of them would say later. The children of this blood-shedding creature would come to say "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves." The devoted one would come to say "I am better than him."

That quietly reorganises the whole question of worth. On the visible record, the angels were right. The hidden thing was a sentence, which is what each being would say at the moment of being wrong. That is the thing this whole course turns on, and it was already in play before the man was made.

Taught the names

وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى بِأَسْمَآءِ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ

"And He taught Ādam the names — all of them. Then He showed them to the angels and said: Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful."

Al-Baqarah (2:31)

The angels cannot do it. They answer with a sentence worth memorising for its manners, we have no knowledge except what You have taught us, and then Ādam recites what they could not. God's closing line over the scene repeats the phrase from the announcement: did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth?

It is worth being clear about what the honour actually was. The angels have strength, and they have devotion beyond measure. The gift shown off in front of the objectors is a different one, the ability to learn and to carry meanings. This creature can be taught. That is exactly the ability the mercy at the end of the story will use.

The two hands, and the breath

Two more details of the making come from two different tellings. In Ṣād, when the refuser is questioned, God's question carries the honour inside it: what prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Al-Jalālayn says plainly what the phrase is doing there. It honours Ādam and singles him out. Everything is God's making, but this making is claimed. In al-Ḥijr, the command to prostrate hangs on the breath.

فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَـٰجِدِينَ

"So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down to him in prostration."

Al-Ḥijr (15:29)

Read the order of the command. The bowing was never to clay. It was commanded for after the proportioning and the breath, so it was for what God had done and placed in the material everyone could see. Anyone who ranks the creature by the clay has measured the wrong thing. One being present did exactly that, out loud.

Where this leaves the portrait

Put the pieces in one frame. A creature announced as a steward of the earth, over an honest objection. Taught what the objectors could not recite. Made with the two hands, carrying the breath. Bowed to by command.

Then comes the last brushstroke, which is also the door into the next module. The first thing that happens to this honoured creature, in the story's own order, is that somebody refuses to bow to him. He argues his way out of the command, is expelled for it, and leaves swearing to prove the angels' low estimate right. The honour and the enmity are born in the same scene.

So the test that follows is the first engagement of a war declared at the creature's unveiling, rather than an accident that happens to a favourite. And the creature walks into it carrying exactly one advantage. He can be taught.
