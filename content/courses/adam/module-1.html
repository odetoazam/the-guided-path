Module 1 — Made, and Taught

You cannot really feel how far Ādam fell until you have seen how highly he was honoured. So this module looks at him as the Quran describes him before anything goes wrong. The gifts he was given explain two things: what his test was, and why the way back took the shape it did.

A steward, announced in advance

Go back to the announcement from Module 0: I will make upon the earth a khalīfa. The word means a deputy or a steward, someone who stands in for another and takes charge. Two things about it are worth reading slowly.

The first is that the earth is in the sentence. The story starts in the garden, but the earth is where the job is. So whatever else leaving the garden means, it is also a khalīfa arriving at the place he was appointed to.

The second is that the announcement raises a question. The angels ask it politely but directly. This creature will spread corruption and shed blood. They already praise God without pause, so what is he for? God answers only, I know what you do not know. He gives no other answer here. The rest of this module follows the Quran scene by scene as it shows part of what He knew.

What the angels could see, and what they couldn't

Al-Rāzī explains what was happening in that moment in his commentary on this passage. He says the angels were looking at the corruption and the killing, so they thought little of this creature. They were also looking at the obedience of Iblīs. At that time Iblīs was known for his devotion, so they thought highly of him. But God knows the unseen, and He knew what each of them would say later on. The children of this blood-shedding creature would one day say: "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves." The devoted one would one day say, "I am better than him."

That quietly changes the whole question of who is worth more. Going by what anyone could see, the angels were right. But they could not see a sentence. They could not see the words each being would say at the moment it was caught in the wrong. This whole course turns on those words, and they were already part of the picture before the man was even made.

Taught the names

وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى بِأَسْمَآءِ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ

"And He taught Ādam the names — all of them. Then He showed them to the angels and said: Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful."

Al-Baqarah (2:31)

The angels cannot do it. Their answer is a sentence worth memorising for its good manners: we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Then Ādam recites the names they could not. God closes the scene by repeating what He said in the announcement: did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth?

It helps to be clear about what the honour actually was. The angels have strength, and their devotion is beyond measure. But the gift God showed off in front of them was a different one. It was the ability to learn and to hold meanings. This creature can be taught, and that is exactly the ability God's mercy will use at the end of the story.

The two hands, and the breath

Two more details about how he was made come from two other tellings. In Ṣād, the one who refused is questioned. God's question itself carries the honour: what prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Al-Jalālayn explains simply what the phrase is doing there. It honours Ādam and sets him apart. God made everything, but He claims this making as His own in a special way. In al-Ḥijr, the command to bow down is tied to the breath.

فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَـٰجِدِينَ

"So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down to him in prostration."

Al-Ḥijr (15:29)

Look at the order of the command. The angels were never told to bow to clay. They were told to bow after God had shaped the man and breathed into him. So they were bowing to what God had done and had placed inside the clay that everyone could see. Anyone who judges this creature by his clay is measuring the wrong thing. One being who was there did exactly that, and he said it out loud.

Where this leaves the portrait

Now put the pieces together. God announces this creature as a steward of the earth, even though the angels honestly object. He teaches him what the angels could not recite. He makes him with His two hands and breathes into him, and He commands the angels to bow to him.

Then comes the last detail, which also leads into the next module. In the story's own order, the first thing that happens to this honoured creature is that someone refuses to bow to him. That someone argues his way out of the command and is thrown out for it. He leaves swearing that he will prove the angels' low opinion of the man right. So the honour and the hostility begin in the same scene.

So the test that follows is more than an accident that happens to a favourite. It should be read as the first battle in a war declared on the day the creature was first shown to the angels. And he walks into it with exactly one advantage, which is that he can be taught.
