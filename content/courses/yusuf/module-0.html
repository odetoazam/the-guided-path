Start Here — The Story

Every course on this site begins by telling the story straight through, and this time the Quran has already done that work. Yūsuf's story is the longest single narrative in the book, and it is told once, in order, in one chapter, from the dream he has as a boy to the day that dream comes true. Before the story even begins, the Quran gives it a title:

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ لَمِنَ ٱلْغَـٰفِلِينَ

"We relate to you the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Quran, although you were, before it, among the unaware."

Yūsuf (12:3)

Aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ means "the best of stories." That is the Quran's own claim about this story, and by the end of the course you can judge it for yourself.

A dream, and a warning

The story opens with a boy telling his father about a dream: he saw eleven stars, and the sun and the moon, and I saw them prostrating to me. His father Yaʿqūb is a prophet, and the son and grandson of prophets, so he understands the dream straight away. The first thing he does is warn his son: do not tell this to your brothers. He even says where the danger lies, because they would plot against him, for Satan is to man a clear enemy (12:5). So the best of stories begins with a father teaching his son that some gifts have to be carried quietly.

The well

The brothers do not need to hear the dream to envy him, because their father's love for him is enough. Yūsuf and his brother are dearer to our father than we are, they say. They talk themselves from resentment all the way to murder, and then settle on a compromise: they will throw him into the bottom of a well and let some passing caravan pick him up. First they win their father's trust, saying send him with us tomorrow, we will play and look after him. Then they come home at nightfall in tears, carrying his shirt dipped in false blood. Their father does not believe them, and he answers with a sentence he does not yet know he will need again:

وَجَآءُو عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ بِدَمٍ كَذِبٍ ۚ قَالَ بَلْ سَوَّلَتْ لَكُمْ أَنفُسُكُمْ أَمْرًا ۖ فَصَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ

"And they brought upon his shirt false blood. He said: Rather, your souls have enticed you to something. So patience is most fitting. And God is the one sought for help against that which you describe."

Yūsuf (12:18)

Ṣabrun jamīl means "a beautiful patience." Decades later in the story, he will say these same words again, word for word.

Sold, and bought

A passing caravan lowers its bucket into the well and pulls up a boy. He is sold for a reduced price, a few coins, and the verse adds that they valued him lightly (12:20). Al-Ṭabarī ruled, following the majority reading, that the people who sold him so cheaply were the brothers themselves, selling their own flesh and blood. The caravan takes him to Egypt, and the man who buys him there tells his wife to treat him with honour, because the boy may turn out to be useful to them. At this low point, with a trafficked child in a stranger's house, the Quran states its main point for the first time: thus We established Yūsuf in the land... and God is predominant over His affair, but most people do not know (12:21).

The house, the prison

Yūsuf grows into a strikingly beautiful young man, and the wife of his master sets her mind on having him. She locks the doors, but he refuses her, saying I seek refuge in God; he is my master, who has made good my residence, and he runs for the door. His shirt is torn from behind as he runs, and that tear later becomes the evidence that proves he is innocent. When the women of the city gossip about her, she shows him to them, and they are so astonished that they cut their own hands. Even though the evidence has cleared him, the powerful people around him still want him, and in the end he is sent to prison anyway, because locking him up was easier than admitting the truth. He had asked for this himself in his prayer: my Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they invite me to (12:33).

The dungeon years

While he is in prison, two young men come to him with their dreams. First they tell him what they think of him: we see you as one of the muḥsinīn, the people of excellence (12:36). Later in the story, very different people will say the same thing about him. He does interpret their dreams, telling them that one will pour wine for his king and the other will be crucified, but first he teaches them who he is and which religion he follows. He asks the man who will be freed to mention him to the king, but the man forgets. So he remained in prison several years (12:42), and the Quran never tells us exactly how many.

The king's dream

Then the king of Egypt has a dream in which seven fat cows are eaten by seven thin ones, and he sees seven green ears of grain and seven dry ones. His advisers call it a jumble of dreams, and at this point the freed prisoner finally remembers Yūsuf, who is still in the dungeon. Yūsuf interprets the dream without asking for anything in return: there will be seven years of plenty and then seven years of famine, and he gives them a plan to survive. When the king sends for him, Yūsuf refuses to come out until his name is cleared, and it is. The women of the city say they know nothing bad about him, and the wife of al-ʿAzīz confesses, now the truth has become evident (12:51). The king puts him in charge of the storehouses of Egypt, so the boy who was thrown into a well ends up feeding the whole region through a famine.

The brothers return

The famine drives his brothers to Egypt to buy grain. He recognized them, and they did not recognize him (12:58). Over two journeys he quietly guides what happens to them: he has their money put back in their saddlebags, he demands that they bring their youngest brother, he has his cup found in the youngest brother's bag, and in the end the eldest brother refuses to go home without him. Back home, their father loses his sight from grief, and he says his old sentence again, word for word: your souls have enticed you to something; so patience is most fitting (12:83). When his sons scold him for still grieving, he tells them where his grief really goes: I only complain of my suffering and my sorrow to God (12:86).

The unveiling

On their third visit, the brothers are desperate and they beg. Yūsuf asks them one question, do you know what you did to Yūsuf and his brother, when you were ignorant?, and at that moment they realise who he is. All he says to the men who threw him in a well is one sentence: no blame upon you today; God will forgive you (12:92). He sends his shirt home to heal his father's eyes, and the whole family moves to Egypt. When his parents and his eleven brothers bow before him, the dream he had as a boy finally comes true, and the story comes full circle. Module 3 spends most of its time on the speech he gives at that moment, which includes the words my Lord is subtle in what He wills (12:100).

The course map

  • Module 0 — Start Here (you are here). The story, in the Quran's own order.
  • Module 1 — Loved, and Thrown Away. The fall, step by step: the dream kept in trust, the envy, the well, and the sale. How much is taken from him before anything is given back.
  • Module 2 — The Locked Room and the Dungeon. Two scenes up close: the torn shirt, and what Yūsuf did with his time in prison.
  • Module 3 — Nothing Was Wasted. One sentence follows Yūsuf through every stage of his life, and one verse late in the story changes how you read everything that happened before it.
  • Module 4 — What the Quran Won't Tell You. The names it keeps back, the years it does not count, and the hardest verse in the story, read alongside the scholars who disagree about it.
  • Module 5 — Your Own Wasted Years. The course turns to your own life, and the question in its title gets its answer.

Keep in mind as you go that in this story every disaster also carries Yūsuf forward. The Quran tells you this in the middle of the story, and not only at the end, because over the worst scenes it keeps saying: and thus We established him.
