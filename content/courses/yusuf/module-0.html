Start Here — The Story

This is the whole life of Yūsuf, from his childhood dream to his death. It comes from the Quran and from the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ, and it is told in order. Most stories of the prophets are spread across many chapters of the Quran. Yūsuf's story is told once and in one chapter, and God calls it the best of stories.

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ لَمِنَ ٱلْغَـٰفِلِينَ

"We relate to you the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Quran, although you were, before it, among the unaware."

Yūsuf (12:3)

A family of prophets

Yūsuf was the son of Yaʿqūb, the son of Isḥāq, the son of Ibrāhīm. Once some people asked the Prophet ﷺ who was the most honourable of all people. He said it was the one who feared God the most. They said they meant something else. So he said it was Yūsuf. Yūsuf was a prophet of God and so were his father and his grandfather. His great-grandfather was Ibrāhīm, the close friend of God (al-Bukhārī). In another report the Prophet ﷺ called Yūsuf the noble son of the noble son of the noble son of the noble one (al-Bukhārī).

The dream

When Yūsuf was still a boy, he told his father about a dream he had seen.

إِذْ قَالَ يُوسُفُ لِأَبِيهِ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ إِنِّى رَأَيْتُ أَحَدَ عَشَرَ كَوْكَبًا وَٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ رَأَيْتُهُمْ لِى سَـٰجِدِينَ

"When Yūsuf said to his father: O my father, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon. I saw them prostrating to me."

Yūsuf (12:4)

Yaʿqūb knew the dream meant something great. He told his son not to tell it to his brothers, because they would plot against him. He said that Satan is a clear enemy to people. Then he told Yūsuf that God would choose him and teach him the meaning of dreams and events. God would complete His favour on him and on the family of Yaʿqūb, just as He had completed it on his forefathers Ibrāhīm and Isḥāq.

The brothers' plan

The Quran says there are signs in the story of Yūsuf and his brothers for those who ask. His brothers were jealous of him. They said that Yūsuf and his brother were dearer to their father than they were, even though they were a strong band of men. They thought their father was plainly in the wrong. The Quran never tells us this brother's name.

So they talked of killing Yūsuf or throwing him out into some far land. Then their father's attention would be theirs alone, and after that they could become good people. But one of them told them not to kill him. He said they should drop him into the bottom of a well, where some travellers would pick him up.

They went to Yaʿqūb and asked why he did not trust them with Yūsuf. They asked him to send the boy with them the next day to enjoy himself and play, and they promised to look after him. Yaʿqūb said it saddened him to let him go. He was afraid a wolf would eat him while they were not paying attention. They said that if a wolf ate him while they were such a strong group, they would truly be losers.

Into the well

So they took him, and they all agreed to put him down into the depths of the well. At that moment God revealed to Yūsuf that one day he would tell his brothers about this very deed.

At nightfall they came home to their father weeping. They said they had gone off racing one another and left Yūsuf with their things, and a wolf had eaten him. They added that he would never believe them, even if they were telling the truth. And they brought his shirt with false blood on it.

وَجَآءُو عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ بِدَمٍ كَذِبٍ ۚ قَالَ بَلْ سَوَّلَتْ لَكُمْ أَنفُسُكُمْ أَمْرًا ۖ فَصَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ

"And they brought upon his shirt false blood. He said: Rather, your souls have enticed you to something. So patience is most fitting. And God is the one sought for help against that which you describe."

Yūsuf (12:18)

Ibn ʿAbbās said that Yaʿqūb looked at the shirt and saw that it was not torn. So he told them they were lying, because a wild animal that ate the boy would have torn his shirt. He chose ṣabrun jamīl, which means beautiful patience. Mujāhid said it is patience with no panic in it. Then Yaʿqūb turned to God for help against what they described.

Found and sold

Then a caravan of travellers came by. They sent their water-drawer to the well, and he let down his bucket. When he pulled it up he cried out, "Good news! Here is a boy!" They hid him among their goods to sell, and God knew what they were doing. He was sold for a low price of a few coins, and they did not value him at all.

The man in Egypt who bought him told his wife to treat the boy with honour. He said the boy might be useful to them, or they might adopt him as a son. The Quran says this is how God gave Yūsuf a place in the land, so that He could teach him the meaning of events. When Yūsuf reached his full strength, God gave him judgement and knowledge. The Quran says this is how God rewards those who do good.

The locked doors

The Quran never gives this man's name or his wife's, and refers to him by the title al-ʿAzīz. When Yūsuf was a grown young man, the woman of the house tried to seduce him. She shut the doors and said, "Come to me." He said he sought refuge in God, and that wrongdoers never succeed. The Quran says that God turned evil and indecency away from him, because he was one of His chosen servants.

They both ran for the door, and she tore his shirt from behind. At the door they met her husband, and she at once accused Yūsuf. She told her husband that a man who wanted evil for his wife should be put in prison or painfully punished. Yūsuf said, "It was she who tried to seduce me."

Then a witness from her own family spoke. He said that if the shirt was torn at the front, she was telling the truth. If it was torn at the back, she was lying. The shirt was torn at the back, so Yūsuf was cleared. When her husband saw it, he said this was the scheming of women, and their scheming was great. He told Yūsuf to put the matter aside, and he told his wife to ask forgiveness for her sin.

The women of the city

Soon the women of the city were gossiping. They said the wife of al-ʿAzīz was trying to seduce her young servant, and that love for him had struck deep into her heart. The Quran's word here is shaghafahā. It comes from shaghāf, the thin covering around the heart, and Mujāhid said his love had entered that covering.

When she heard their talk, she invited them to a meal and gave each of them a knife. Then she told Yūsuf to come out before them. When they saw him they were amazed, and they cut their own hands. They said, "God forbid! This is no human being. This is nothing but a noble angel."

She told them this was the one they had blamed her for. She admitted she had tried to seduce him, and that he had held himself back. Then she said that if he did not do what she ordered, he would be put in prison and humbled. So Yūsuf prayed to his Lord.

قَالَ رَبِّ ٱلسِّجْنُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىَّ مِمَّا يَدْعُونَنِىٓ إِلَيْهِ ۖ وَإِلَّا تَصْرِفْ عَنِّى كَيْدَهُنَّ أَصْبُ إِلَيْهِنَّ وَأَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْجَـٰهِلِينَ

"He said: My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they invite me to. And unless You turn their scheming away from me, I will incline towards them and be among the ignorant."

Yūsuf (12:33)

God answered his prayer and turned their scheming away from him. Even so, after they had seen the signs of his innocence, they decided to put him in prison for a while.

Two dreams in prison

Two young men went into prison with him. One said he had dreamed he was pressing grapes for wine. The other said he had dreamed he was carrying bread on his head, and birds were eating from it. They asked Yūsuf what their dreams meant, because they could see he was a good man.

Yūsuf said no food would be brought to them before he had told them its meaning. He said this knowledge was something his Lord had taught him. He had left the way of a people who did not believe in God or the Hereafter. He followed the way of his fathers Ibrāhīm, Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb. Then he asked them whether many different lords were better than God, the One who overcomes all. He told them that the gods they worshipped were only names that they and their fathers had made up. Judgement belongs to God alone, and He has commanded that none be worshipped but Him.

Then he gave them the meanings. One of them would pour wine for his master. The other would be crucified, and birds would eat from his head. He said the matter they had asked about was now decided. He asked the one he thought would be freed to mention him to his master. But Yūsuf stayed in prison for biḍʿ sinīn. The word biḍʿ means a few, somewhere between three and nine, and the Quran does not say exactly how many.

The king's dream

Then the king of Egypt had a dream. He saw seven fat cows being eaten by seven thin ones. He saw seven green ears of grain and others that were dry. He asked his chiefs what it meant. They said it was a jumble of dreams, and they did not know how to explain dreams.

At this the man who had been freed at last remembered Yūsuf. He asked to be sent to him. He came to Yūsuf and called him al-ṣiddīq, the man of truth, and asked him about the dream. Yūsuf did not ask to be freed first. He simply gave the answer.

He said they should farm for seven years as usual. Whatever they harvested they should leave in its ears, except the little they needed to eat. After that would come seven hard years that would eat up what they had stored, except a little they kept back. Then would come a year when people would be given rain, and they would press grapes and olives again.

Cleared before he came out

The king said, "Bring him to me." But when the messenger came, Yūsuf would not leave the prison. He told him to go back to the king and ask about the women who had cut their hands. So the king called the women and asked them what had happened when they tried to seduce Yūsuf. They said, "God forbid! We know nothing bad about him."

Then the wife of al-ʿAzīz spoke. "Now the truth has come out," she said. "I tried to seduce him, and he is truthful."

Long afterwards the Prophet ﷺ spoke of Yūsuf in prison. He said that if he had stayed in prison as long as Yūsuf did, he would have answered the one who came to call him out (al-Bukhārī). He was praising Yūsuf's patience.

Over the storehouses

Now the king said, "Bring him to me. I will take him for myself." When he spoke with Yūsuf, he told him that from that day he would hold a high and trusted place with him. Yūsuf asked to be put in charge of the storehouses of the land. He said he would guard them well and he knew how to manage them. So God gave Yūsuf power in the land, to settle wherever he wished. The Quran says God never lets the reward of those who do good go to waste, and the reward of the Hereafter is even better.

Many popular retellings say that Yūsuf later married the wife of al-ʿAzīz, and they call her Zulaykhā. This does not come from a reliable source.

The brothers come for grain

Then Yūsuf's brothers came to Egypt for food and stood before him. He knew them, but they did not know him. When he had given them their supplies, he told them to bring their brother from their father next time. He reminded them that he gave full measure and was the best of hosts. If they did not bring that brother, they would get no more grain and should not come near him. They promised to try to persuade their father. Then Yūsuf told his servants to put the goods they had paid with back into their saddlebags, so they would find them at home and come back.

Yaʿqūb lets the brother go

At home they told their father that they would be refused grain unless the brother came with them. They promised to guard him. Yaʿqūb asked if he should trust them with this son, as he had trusted them with his brother before. He said that God is the best guardian and the most merciful of those who show mercy. Then they opened their bags and found their goods returned to them. "What more could we want?" they said. Now they could bring food for their family, guard their brother and get an extra camel-load.

Yaʿqūb would only let the boy go if they swore a promise before God to bring him back, unless they were all overwhelmed. When they gave their promise, he said that God was witness over what they said. Then he told them not to enter the city by one gate. They should go in by separate gates. He said he could not protect them from anything God had decreed, and that he put his trust in God alone. Qatāda and other early scholars said he feared the evil eye for them, because they were handsome men. The Quran says this did not help them against God at all, and it was only a wish in Yaʿqūb's heart that he carried out.

The king's cup

When they came before Yūsuf, he drew his brother close to him. He told him quietly, "I am your brother. So do not grieve over what they used to do."

When he had given them their supplies, he placed the drinking cup in his brother's bag. Then a caller cried out, "O caravan, you are thieves!" The brothers turned and asked what was missing. They were told it was the king's measuring cup. Whoever brought it back would get a camel-load of grain, and the caller promised it himself. The brothers swore by God that they had not come to cause trouble in the land, and they were no thieves.

Yūsuf's men asked what the punishment should be if they were lying. The brothers answered that whoever had the cup in his bag would himself be the payment. That was how they punished wrongdoers. Yūsuf searched their bags first, before his brother's bag, and then he took the cup out of his brother's bag. The Quran says God planned this for Yūsuf. Under the king's law he could not have kept his brother, unless God willed it. God raises in rank whomever He wills, and above everyone who has knowledge is someone who knows more.

The brothers said, "If he has stolen, a brother of his stole before." They meant Yūsuf. He kept this to himself and did not show it to them. He said to himself that they were in a worse place, and God knew best the truth of what they described. Then they pleaded with him. They said the boy's father was a very old man, and they begged him to take one of them instead. Yūsuf said, "God forbid that we take anyone except the one with whom we found our property. Then we would be wrongdoers."

A father's grief

When they gave up hope, the brothers went aside to talk in private. The eldest reminded them of the promise they had made before God, and of how they had failed Yūsuf before. He said he would not leave that land until his father gave him permission or God judged for him. He told the others to go home and tell their father that his son had stolen. They could only speak of what they had seen. Their father could ask the town and the caravan they had travelled with.

Yaʿqūb answered with the words he had used on the night of the shirt. Their souls had tempted them into something, and so he would show beautiful patience. He hoped God would bring them all back to him. Then he turned away and said, "Oh, my sorrow for Yūsuf!" His eyes turned white from grief, and he held his sorrow inside. They told him he would keep remembering Yūsuf until he wasted away or died.

قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشْكُوا۟ بَثِّى وَحُزْنِىٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعْلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"He said: I only complain of my grief and my sorrow to God, and I know from God what you do not know."

Yūsuf (12:86)

Then he told his sons to go and search for Yūsuf and his brother. He told them never to lose hope in God's mercy, because only people who reject God lose hope in His mercy.

"I am Yūsuf"

So the brothers went back to Egypt and came before Yūsuf. They said hardship had struck them and their family, and they had brought only goods of little worth. They asked him to give them full measure and to be charitable to them, because God rewards the charitable.

Then Yūsuf asked them if they knew what they had done to Yūsuf and his brother when they were ignorant. They said, "Are you really Yūsuf?" He said, "I am Yūsuf, and this is my brother." He said God had been gracious to them. God never lets the reward of those who fear Him and stay patient go to waste. They swore by God that God had preferred him over them, and they admitted they had been in the wrong.

قَالَ لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ ۖ يَغْفِرُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۖ وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ

"He said: No blame upon you today. God will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful."

Yūsuf (12:92)

The shirt

Yūsuf gave them his shirt. He told them to lay it over his father's face so that his sight would come back, and to bring the whole family to him.

As the caravan left Egypt, Yaʿqūb said he could smell Yūsuf, if only they would not call him a senile old man. Ibn ʿAbbās said a wind rose and carried the smell of Yūsuf's shirt to him from eight nights' journey away. The people around him said he was still lost in his old mistake. Then the bearer of good news arrived and laid the shirt on his face, and his sight came back. Al-Shaʿbī said there were three signs in Yūsuf's shirt. It came home with false blood, it was torn from behind, and it gave his father back his sight.

Yaʿqūb reminded them that he had told them he knew from God what they did not know. His sons asked him to pray for God to forgive their sins, and he promised that he would. Ibn Masʿūd said he waited to pray for them until saḥar, the last part of the night before dawn.

The dream comes true

Then the family came to Egypt. Yūsuf drew his parents close to him and said, "Enter Egypt in safety, if God wills." He raised his parents up onto the throne, and they fell down before him in prostration.

He said, "My father, this is the meaning of my dream from long ago. My Lord has made it come true." He thanked God for being good to him when He took him out of prison. He thanked Him for bringing the family from the desert after Satan had stirred up trouble between him and his brothers. He did not mention the well. Then he said that his Lord is gentle in bringing about whatever He wills, and He is the All-Knowing and the All-Wise.

His prayer

Then Yūsuf turned to God with a prayer.

۞ رَبِّ قَدْ ءَاتَيْتَنِى مِنَ ٱلْمُلْكِ وَعَلَّمْتَنِى مِن تَأْوِيلِ ٱلْأَحَادِيثِ ۚ فَاطِرَ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ أَنتَ وَلِىِّۦ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ ۖ تَوَفَّنِى مُسْلِمًا وَأَلْحِقْنِى بِٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَ

"My Lord, You have given me some of the kingdom and taught me some of the meaning of events. Creator of the heavens and the earth, You are my protector in this world and the Hereafter. Let me die as one who submits to You, and join me with the righteous."

Yūsuf (12:101)

His father's last words

The Quran tells of the day death came to Yaʿqūb. He asked his sons what they would worship after him. They said they would worship his God and the God of his fathers Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq. He was one God, and they submitted to Him.

The end of Yūsuf's life

The Quran does not describe how Yūsuf died. It gives no ages and no years for his life, and the reports that give numbers disagree with one another. It does tell us what the Egyptians said after he died. In the time of Mūsā, a believing man from Pharaoh's own family spoke up to defend Mūsā. He reminded his people of Yūsuf.

وَلَقَدْ جَآءَكُمْ يُوسُفُ مِن قَبْلُ بِٱلْبَيِّنَـٰتِ فَمَا زِلْتُمْ فِى شَكٍّ مِّمَّا جَآءَكُم بِهِۦ ۖ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا هَلَكَ قُلْتُمْ لَن يَبْعَثَ ٱللَّهُ مِنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ رَسُولًا ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُضِلُّ ٱللَّهُ مَنْ هُوَ مُسْرِفٌ مُّرْتَابٌ

"And Yūsuf came to you before with clear signs, but you never stopped doubting what he brought you. Then when he died, you said: God will never send a messenger after him. This is how God leaves astray whoever goes beyond all limits and doubts."

Ghāfir (40:34)

So Yūsuf had been a messenger to the people of Egypt, and they doubted him until the day he died.

There is also a report about his grave. Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī said the Prophet ﷺ told the story of an old woman of the Children of Israel. When Mūsā led his people out of Egypt, they lost their way. Their scholars told him that when death came to Yūsuf, he made them promise before God not to leave Egypt without taking his bones. Only one old woman knew where his grave was. She would show Mūsā the place if he promised she would be with him in Paradise. Mūsā did not like to promise this, but God told him to grant her wish. She led them to a pool of standing water and told them to drain it and dig. When they lifted out Yūsuf's remains, the road became as clear as daylight. Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Ḥākim graded this report sound. Ibn Kathīr thought it was more likely Abū Mūsā's own words. Mujāhid also said that Mūsā carried Yūsuf's bones out of Egypt with him.

In the third heaven

On the night journey the Prophet ﷺ rose through the heavens with Jibrīl. In the third heaven he met Yūsuf. The Prophet ﷺ said that Yūsuf had been given half of beauty, and that Yūsuf welcomed him and prayed for good for him (Muslim). In another report Jibrīl said, "This is Yūsuf, so greet him." The Prophet ﷺ greeted him, and Yūsuf returned the greeting. He said, "Welcome to the righteous brother and the righteous prophet" (al-Bukhārī).
