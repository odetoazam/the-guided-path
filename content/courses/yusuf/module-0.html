Start Here — The Story

This is the story of Yūsuf, told simply from beginning to end. Unlike most stories in the Quran, it is told once, in order, in one chapter, and God calls it the best of stories.

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ لَمِنَ ٱلْغَـٰفِلِينَ

"We relate to you the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Quran, although you were, before it, among the unaware."

Yūsuf (12:3)

A dream, and a warning

When Yūsuf was a boy, he told his father Yaʿqūb that he had seen eleven stars, and the sun and the moon, bowing down to him. His father told him that God would choose him, but warned him not to tell his brothers, because they would plot against him.

Into the well

Yūsuf's brothers were jealous, because their father loved Yūsuf and his brother more than them. The Quran never tells us this brother's name. They talked of killing him, but one of them said they should drop him into a well for travellers to find.

They asked their father to send Yūsuf out with them, though Yaʿqūb was afraid a wolf would eat him, and they put him down into the well. God told Yūsuf then that one day he would tell his brothers about what they had done. That night they came home weeping, and told their father a wolf had eaten him.

وَجَآءُو عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ بِدَمٍ كَذِبٍ ۚ قَالَ بَلْ سَوَّلَتْ لَكُمْ أَنفُسُكُمْ أَمْرًا ۖ فَصَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ ۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ

"And they brought upon his shirt false blood. He said: Rather, your souls have enticed you to something. So patience is most fitting. And God is the one sought for help against that which you describe."

Yūsuf (12:18)

Yaʿqūb did not believe them, and he chose to be patient and to turn to God for help.

Sold in Egypt

Some travellers let down a bucket into the well and found the boy, and he was sold for a few coins. The man in Egypt who bought him told his wife to treat him with honour, because he might be useful to them, or they might take him as a son. The Quran says this is how God settled Yūsuf in the land.

The woman of the house

The Quran never gives this man's name or his wife's, and refers to him by the title al-ʿAzīz. When Yūsuf grew up, the woman of the house shut the doors and tried to seduce him, but he said he sought refuge in God, and refused. The Quran says that God turned evil and indecency away from him, because he was one of His chosen servants.

They both ran for the door, and she tore his shirt from behind. At the door they met her husband, and she at once accused Yūsuf. A witness from her own family said that if the shirt was torn at the back, she was lying, and it was torn at the back, so Yūsuf was cleared.

When the women of the city gossiped, she invited them to a meal and gave each a knife. When Yūsuf came out, they were so amazed that they cut their own hands. She told them that if he did not obey her, he would go to prison. Yūsuf prayed that prison was dearer to him than what they wanted, and God turned their scheming away from him. Even so, after they had seen the signs of his innocence, they put him in prison.

Two dreams in prison

Two young men in prison asked him about their dreams. First he told them about God, the One, and then he told them that one would pour wine for his master, and the other would be crucified. He asked the one who would be freed to mention him to his master, but Yūsuf stayed in prison for several years. The Quran does not say how many.

The king's dream

Then the king dreamed of seven fat cows being eaten by seven thin ones, and of seven green ears of grain and others that were dry. When his advisers could not explain it, the freed man at last remembered Yūsuf and went to him. Yūsuf told him that seven good years would come, when they should store grain, and then seven hard years that would use it up, and then a year of relief.

The king sent for Yūsuf, but Yūsuf would not come out until the king had asked about the women who cut their hands. The women said they knew nothing bad about him, and the wife of al-ʿAzīz confessed that she had tried to seduce him. So the king took Yūsuf into his service, and Yūsuf asked to be put in charge of the storehouses of the land.

The brothers come for grain

Yūsuf's brothers came to Egypt for food. He knew them, but they did not know him. He gave them their grain and told them to bring their brother next time, or they would get no more. After what had happened to Yūsuf, Yaʿqūb let this son go only when they swore before God to bring him back.

In Egypt, Yūsuf took his brother aside and told him quietly who he was. Then he had the king's cup put in his brother's bag, and a caller cried out that they were thieves. When the cup was found in that bag, Yūsuf kept his brother. The others begged him to take one of them instead, but he refused. The eldest stayed behind because of their promise, and the rest went home.

A father's grief

Yaʿqūb answered with the words he had used on the night of the shirt, that their souls had enticed them to something and patience was most fitting. He hoped God would bring them all back to him. He grieved for Yūsuf until his eyes turned white, but he told his sons that he complained of his grief only to God. Then he sent them to search for Yūsuf and his brother, and never to lose hope in God's mercy.

"I am Yūsuf"

The brothers went back to Egypt poor, and asked for charity. Yūsuf asked them whether they knew what they had done to Yūsuf and his brother. When they asked if he was really Yūsuf, he said that he was. They admitted that they had done wrong. He told them there was no blame on them that day, and that God would forgive them.

The dream comes true

Yūsuf sent his shirt home to be laid on his father's face. As the caravan left Egypt, Yaʿqūb said he could smell Yūsuf, and when the shirt was laid on his face, his sight came back. His sons asked him to pray for their forgiveness, and he promised he would.

When the family came to Egypt, Yūsuf raised his parents up onto the throne, and they fell down before him in prostration. He told his father that this was the meaning of his old dream, and that his Lord had made it come true. He thanked God for taking him out of prison and bringing his family to him, after Satan had come between him and his brothers. Then he prayed that God would let him die in submission to Him and join him with the righteous.
