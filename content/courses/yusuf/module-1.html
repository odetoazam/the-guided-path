Module 1 — Loved, and Thrown Away

Before this course can mean anything, you have to stay with the first third of the story without skipping ahead. This is the part where everything is taken from Yūsuf, slowly, one layer at a time. He is loved, then envied, then betrayed, then dropped into a hole, and then sold for pocket change. If you have ever watched your own life fall apart and wondered where God was in it, this module is where the Quran answers, and it answers while things are still falling apart.

A gift you have to hide

The story opens with the boy's dream of eleven stars, the sun and the moon, all bowing to him. When Yaʿqūb hears it, he does two things at once. He tells his son that the dream is a great one, thus your Lord will choose you and teach you the interpretation of events (12:6), and he also tells him to keep it hidden:

قَالَ يَـٰبُنَىَّ لَا تَقْصُصْ رُءْيَاكَ عَلَىٰٓ إِخْوَتِكَ فَيَكِيدُوا۟ لَكَ كَيْدًا ۖ إِنَّ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنَ لِلْإِنسَـٰنِ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

"He said: O my son, do not relate your vision to your brothers, lest they contrive against you a plan. Indeed Satan, to man, is a manifest enemy."

Yūsuf (12:5)

Yaʿqūb is being realistic here. He does not call his other sons evil. Instead he says that Satan works between people, and that envy is the door he comes in through. So the first lesson of the best of stories is a practical one: sometimes a real calling has to be carried in silence, because announcing it only feeds the envy that will try to destroy it.

The arithmetic of envy

إِذْ قَالُوا۟ لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىٰٓ أَبِينَا مِنَّا وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ إِنَّ أَبَانَا لَفِى ضَلَـٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ

"When they said: Yūsuf and his brother are more beloved to our father than we, while we are a clan. Indeed, our father is in clear error."

Yūsuf (12:8)

The brothers reason their way to the crime in three steps, and the same three steps show up in almost every kind of envy. First they compare: he is loved more. Then they point to what they think should count, we are many and strong, as if love were owed to whoever is most capable. Then they reach the conclusion that makes the crime possible: our father is in clear error. Once they have decided that the person in the way is wrong, anything they do to "correct" him starts to feel like justice. Within two verses they go from comparing their father's love to planning a killing, and then one of the brothers talks them down to the well instead. Envy rarely calls itself envy. It usually feels like putting something right.

The bottom of the well

So they win their father's trust, take Yūsuf out to play, and drop him into the dark. At the lowest point of the story, when a child betrayed by his own family is alone in a well in the wilderness, the Quran does something it will keep doing for the rest of the sūrah. It speaks directly into the scene:

فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا۟ بِهِۦ وَأَجْمَعُوٓا۟ أَن يَجْعَلُوهُ فِى غَيَـٰبَتِ ٱلْجُبِّ ۚ وَأَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْهِ لَتُنَبِّئَنَّهُم بِأَمْرِهِمْ هَـٰذَا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

"So when they took him and agreed to put him into the bottom of the well — We revealed to him: You will surely inform them of this affair of theirs while they do not perceive."

Yūsuf (12:15)

God does not rescue him from the well. Instead, He sends him a revelation about the future: one day Yūsuf will stand in front of his brothers and tell them what they did, and they will not recognise him. The promise is exact, and it comes decades before it happens, but it does not make the hole any less dark. This is how the whole sūrah works. God tells Yūsuf about the plan while he is still inside the disaster, and the plan does not take the disaster away.

Sold cheap

وَشَرَوْهُ بِثَمَنٍۭ بَخْسٍ دَرَٰهِمَ مَعْدُودَةٍ وَكَانُوا۟ فِيهِ مِنَ ٱلزَّٰهِدِينَ

"And they sold him for a reduced price — a few dirhams — and they were, concerning him, of those content with little."

Yūsuf (12:20)

He is sold for a few counted coins, and the scholars have long asked who set that price. Qatāda thought the sellers were the people of the caravan. Al-Ṭabarī rules, together with most of the commentators, that the sellers were the brothers, and al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī and others read it the same way. On that reading, the brothers sold their own flesh and blood for pocket change, being of those content with little where he was concerned. So the man who will one day run Egypt's storehouses is sold for the smallest sum in the story, and the price is set by the people who had known him since he was born. The Quran records how small the price was on purpose, and there is quiet comfort in that for anyone who has been valued at far less than they are worth. Ever since this scene, what people are willing to pay for someone and what that person is really worth have been two different things, and the story shows the gap plainly.

The thesis, planted at the bottom

Then, as the boy who was sold enters a stranger's house in a foreign country, owned by someone else and cut off from everything he was, the Quran says the sentence that this whole course is built on:

وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِى ٱشْتَرَىٰهُ مِن مِّصْرَ لِٱمْرَأَتِهِۦٓ أَكْرِمِى مَثْوَىٰهُ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَنفَعَنَآ أَوْ نَتَّخِذَهُۥ وَلَدًا ۚ وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَكَّنَّا لِيُوسُفَ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلِنُعَلِّمَهُۥ مِن تَأْوِيلِ ٱلْأَحَادِيثِ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَالِبٌ عَلَىٰٓ أَمْرِهِۦ وَلَـٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ ٱلنَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"And the one from Egypt who bought him said to his wife: Make his residence honourable; perhaps he will benefit us, or we will adopt him as a son. And thus We established Yūsuf in the land, that We might teach him the interpretation of events. And God is predominant over His affair, but most of the people do not know."

Yūsuf (12:21)

Wa-kadhālika makkannā li-Yūsuf means "and thus We established Yūsuf." The important word is thus, which here means "by means of all this": the envy, the well, the caravan and the sale. The verse does not say that God established him in spite of what happened to him. That one small word says God established him through it. The well was his road to Egypt, and the sale put him in the one house that would lead him to the king's court. Nobody in the story can see any of this yet, and the verse says so in its last words: God is predominant over His affair, but most people do not know.

Everything Yūsuf lost in this module was a real loss, and the Quran never pretends otherwise, so this course will not make it smaller either. But twice now, in the middle of the worst scenes, the Quran has told you that God is also at work. The next two modules follow that work through a locked room, a false accusation, and a dungeon that nobody came back to rescue him from.
