Module 2 — The Machine

He asked for time and was given it. This module is about what he does with it, and how little of it reaches past your own choice.

We will be precise here, because precision is what actually helps. Vague warnings about an unseen enemy only make people afraid, but the Quran is not vague. It uses specific verbs for what he does, and once you notice what they all have in common, the whole picture changes.

The one place the whole thing runs start to finish

The Quran shows the complete process only once, in three verses in a row, with the first two human beings. Everything else it says about his work is a variation on this.

فَوَسْوَسَ لَهُمَا ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ لِيُبْدِىَ لَهُمَا مَا وُۥرِىَ عَنْهُمَا مِن سَوْءَٰتِهِمَا وَقَالَ مَا نَهَىٰكُمَا رَبُّكُمَا عَنْ هَـٰذِهِ ٱلشَّجَرَةِ إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَا مَلَكَيْنِ أَوْ تَكُونَا مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰلِدِينَ

"Then Satan whispered to them both, to expose to them what had been hidden from them of their private parts, and he said: Your Lord did not forbid you this tree except that you two become angels, or become of those who last forever."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:20)

Notice that there is no command anywhere in the whisper. God's instruction about the tree was real, they knew it, and he does not deny a word of it. He only offers a different explanation of why it was given: He did not forbid it to protect you. He forbade it to keep something from you. The rule stays the same, and all he adds is a second, false reason behind it, which they are still free to reject.

The bait is about status rather than pleasure. He tells them they could become angels or live forever, and in the telling in Sūrat Ṭā Hā it becomes a tree of immortality and a kingdom that never decays. So he is not really offering a fruit but the chance to become something greater, which he has no power to give.

وَقَاسَمَهُمَآ إِنِّى لَكُمَا لَمِنَ ٱلنَّـٰصِحِينَ

"And he swore to them both: Indeed, I am to you among the sincere advisers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:21)

Then he swears an oath. He needs one because he has no authority to make them do anything, so he has to borrow trust, and the cheapest way to borrow trust is to swear. The oath shows how weak he is, because someone who can force you does not need to convince you that he means well.

فَدَلَّىٰهُمَا بِغُرُورٍ ۚ فَلَمَّا ذَاقَا ٱلشَّجَرَةَ بَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْءَٰتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِن وَرَقِ ٱلْجَنَّةِ ۖ وَنَادَىٰهُمَا رَبُّهُمَآ أَلَمْ أَنْهَكُمَا عَن تِلْكُمَا ٱلشَّجَرَةِ وَأَقُل لَّكُمَآ إِنَّ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنَ لَكُمَا عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

"So he brought them down by delusion. And when they tasted of the tree, their private parts became apparent to them, and they began to fasten together over themselves from the leaves of the Garden. And their Lord called to them: Did I not forbid you from that tree and tell you that Satan is to you a clear enemy?"

Al-Aʿrāf (7:22)

Read the verbs in order and notice who does what. He whispered, he spoke, he swore, and he led them down by deceiving them, and then they tasted the tree. In the whole sequence, the act itself belongs to them. He supplies the words, but they are the ones who reach out.

As they eat, none of what he promised arrives, neither becoming angels nor living forever. Instead they see their own nakedness, feel ashamed, and start covering themselves with leaves from the Garden. The Arabic in 7:20 had already named the whisper's real goal: to expose to them what had been hidden. What he promised and what happened were opposites, and they only saw it afterwards.

The verbs, and the one thing they have in common

Across the Quran, what this enemy does falls into four groups. Look for the pattern rather than memorising the list:

  • Making things look good. The word is tazyīn, beautifying, and what he beautifies is almost always people's own deeds, so that they look fine to them. Close to it are gilded speech and ghurūr, which means delusion or a false appearance. All of these change how a deed looks, and none of them does the deed.
  • Suggesting. This is waswasa, the whisper, casting a thought in, and a thought cast in can still be turned away.
  • Working on feelings. This includes the sudden jab that comes as a flash of anger, stirring people up, and frightening them. It also includes a promise that runs on fear: in one verse, in a passage about giving, he promises you poverty and orders you to do shameful things, while God in the same breath promises forgiveness and abundance. His order there has nothing behind it to enforce it, and the two offers are placed side by side so you can choose.
  • Moving attention. He makes people forget, and he gets in the way. In one verse the Quran states his working goal outright, which is to keep you from remembering God and from prayer, and he does this by distracting you.

Now go through that list again and look for a verb that moves a hand or a foot. You will not find one. Nothing the Quran says he does can force anything to happen, because every one of these verbs works on how things look, how things feel, and what stays in front of your mind.

So he changes how you see things, but he does not push you anywhere. It is like someone changing the lighting in a room, while you are still the one who decides where to walk.

Why you cannot tell his voice from yours

The fact that makes all of this work is a fact about a single word.

The word for his whisper, waswasa, appears five times in the Quran. Four of those times it describes the whisperer at work, and the fifth is this:

وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَـٰنَ وَنَعْلَمُ مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ ۖ وَنَحْنُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ

"And We have already created man and know what his soul whispers to him, and We are closer to him than his jugular vein."

Qāf (50:16)

It is the same verb, but here the one whispering is a person's own self. Al-Muyassar explains it simply as what his self says to him.

So the Quran uses one word both for a suggestion that comes from outside and for the talk that goes on inside, and that is exactly how he works. A thought he casts in and a thought of your own arrive the same way and sound the same, so you cannot sort them by how they feel.

That is practical, and a little freeing. The question "was that thought from him, or from me?" has no reliable answer, and you do not need one, because what you should do with the thought is the same either way.

The asymmetry, and why the cure is not strength

يَـٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ لَا يَفْتِنَنَّكُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ كَمَآ أَخْرَجَ أَبَوَيْكُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ يَنزِعُ عَنْهُمَا لِبَاسَهُمَا لِيُرِيَهُمَا سَوْءَٰتِهِمَآ ۗ إِنَّهُۥ يَرَىٰكُمْ هُوَ وَقَبِيلُهُۥ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا تَرَوْنَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينَ أَوْلِيَآءَ لِلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

"O children of Ādam, let not Satan tempt you as he removed your parents from Paradise, stripping from them their clothing to show them their private parts. Indeed, he sees you, he and his tribe, from where you do not see them. Indeed, We have made the devils allies to those who do not believe."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:27)

He sees you from where you do not see them. That is his whole advantage, and it comes from where he stands rather than from any power. He works outside the edge of your vision, and that is all it gives him.

This settles what the answer has to be. If his advantage were strength, you would need more strength to beat it. His advantage is that he cannot be seen, so the answer is to see. The next module is built on a verse that says this in exactly those words.

The companion is a result, not an attacker

One correction is worth making here, because it changes who is responsible for what.

وَمَن يَعْشُ عَن ذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُۥ شَيْطَـٰنًا فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينٌ

"And whoever is blinded from the remembrance of the Most Merciful — We appoint for him a devil, and he is to him a companion."

Az-Zukhruf (43:36)

Look at the order in this verse. The person turns away first, and only after that is the companion assigned to him. Al-Jalālayn explains the verb as "We cause it." So the companion did not start the problem; it came because of it. Elsewhere in the Quran its method is the one we have already met, which is making things look good, and that still cannot make a person act.

So the true picture is not "a devil attacked me and I fell." It is closer to this: a person let their remembrance of God grow dim, and then a companion came that made the dimness worse.

The ceiling, and it is exact

Everything above describes how he works, and what comes next must never be separated from it.

إِنَّهُۥ لَيْسَ لَهُۥ سُلْطَـٰنٌ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ إِنَّمَا سُلْطَـٰنُهُۥ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يَتَوَلَّوْنَهُۥ وَٱلَّذِينَ هُم بِهِۦ مُشْرِكُونَ

"Indeed, there is for him no authority over those who have believed and rely upon their Lord. His authority is only over those who take him as an ally and those who through him associate others with God."

An-Naḥl (16:99–100)

The word here is sulṭān, which means authority or control. Al-Muyassar explains it as tasalluṭ, having the upper hand. Over believers who rely on their Lord, this verse gives him none of it at all, not a small amount and not a limited amount.

Then the second half of the verse tells you the one way he ever gets that authority, which is when people take him as an ally. Al-Jalālayn explains that as obeying him. In Sūrat al-Ḥijr, God says the same thing to him directly: Indeed, over My servants you have no authority, except those who follow you of the deviators (15:42). When al-Muyassar explains that verse, he names the place this authority would have had to reach, which is their hearts.

So there is a gap in his method, and it comes at the very last step. He can supply suggestions, make things look good, stir up fear, and make you forget. What he can never supply is the moment when you take the suggestion and make it your own. That last step belongs to the human being, and if the human does not take it, nothing happens.

We do not have to take this on anyone else's word, because we have it from his own mouth:

وَقَالَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ لَمَّا قُضِىَ ٱلْأَمْرُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَعَدَكُمْ وَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ وَوَعَدتُّكُمْ فَأَخْلَفْتُكُمْ ۖ وَمَا كَانَ لِىَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن سُلْطَـٰنٍ إِلَّآ أَن دَعَوْتُكُمْ فَٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِى ۖ فَلَا تَلُومُونِى وَلُومُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُم ۖ مَّآ أَنَا۠ بِمُصْرِخِكُمْ وَمَآ أَنتُم بِمُصْرِخِىَّ ۖ إِنِّى كَفَرْتُ بِمَآ أَشْرَكْتُمُونِ مِن قَبْلُ ۗ إِنَّ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

"And Satan will say, when the matter has been concluded: Indeed, God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, but I betrayed you. And I had no authority over you except that I invited you, and you responded to me. So do not blame me, but blame yourselves. I cannot be called to your aid, nor can you be called to my aid. Indeed, I deny your having associated me before. Indeed, the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment."

Ibrāhīm (14:22)

Al-Jalālayn explains what he is saying he never had: quwwa wa-qudra, the force and power to make you follow him. Al-Muyassar adds that he had no power to compel them, and no proof either.

In his own words, the whole exchange came down to this: he called, and they answered.

So that is his whole method, described honestly. It reaches very far, yet it has almost no force. One thing decides whether it works on you at all, and that is the subject of the next module.
