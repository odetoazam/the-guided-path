Module 4 — What the Quran Won't Tell You

This module is about gaps. Some of the things people feel most sure of in this story are not in the Quran at all, and some of what is in the Quran was deliberately left open. Both of these teach us something.

What is not there

There is no serpent in this story. The Quran does mention snakes, and Mūsā's staff turns into one more than once, but no snake appears in the garden in any of the seven tellings. In the Quran the tempter is a voice, not an animal.

The tree is never identified either. Every time, it is simply "the tree," with no kind of tree and no fruit named. Commentators have offered guesses for centuries, and the Quran has never settled the question, which itself tells you how little it matters.

Ādam's wife is never named anywhere in the Quran. The name people use for her comes from outside it.

And there is no physical description of Iblīs at all, no shape and no appearance. Even though he appears so often in the book, the Quran gives you nothing to picture.

When you put these together, a pattern appears. Every missing detail is the kind that would turn this into a story about those particular characters, long ago. Without those details, what remains is a process that belongs to no particular time, which is exactly why it can still be happening now. The things the Quran leaves out are what keep the story about you.

Told seven times, and never the same twice

The refusal is told in seven chapters, and each telling keeps the refusal itself but changes what surrounds it. One gives you God's question that diagnoses him, and another gives you the four directions he says he will come from. One gives you a detail that appears nowhere else, and the rest of this module is about that one.

The scene comes back so often because each retelling answers a different question. Two of the eleven verses that name him stand outside the scene. One is a single line in a description of the Fire, which names his armies, and the other is the verse we will end this module with.

The disagreement that has never been settled

First, though, let us look at one of the nine verses inside the scene. In Sūrat al-Kahf the scene is told again, and this time it includes a phrase that appears nowhere else in the Quran:

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ ٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِـَٔادَمَ فَسَجَدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ كَانَ مِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ فَفَسَقَ عَنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّهِۦٓ ۗ أَفَتَتَّخِذُونَهُۥ وَذُرِّيَّتَهُۥٓ أَوْلِيَآءَ مِن دُونِى وَهُمْ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّۢ ۚ بِئْسَ لِلظَّـٰلِمِينَ بَدَلًا

"And when We said to the angels: Prostrate to Ādam — so they prostrated, except Iblīs. He was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord. Then will you take him and his descendants as allies other than Me, while they are enemies to you? Wretched it is for the wrongdoers as an exchange."

Al-Kahf (18:50)

He was of the jinn. But the command was given to the angels, so was he one of them or not?

The earliest scholars genuinely disagreed about this. The disagreement is recorded openly and has never been resolved. Al-Ṭabarī begins his discussion of the phrase with the words the people of interpretation differed, sets out each position with its chains of narrators, and does not decide between them. Here is what he preserves.

The first position is that Iblīs was an angel. Reports going back to Ibn ʿAbbās say he was among the noblest of the angels, a keeper over the Gardens, with authority over the lowest heaven. On this reading, "of the jinn" describes where he served, the way you would call a man Makkī or Madanī because of the place he belongs to. Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib is reported to have said he was the chief of the angels of the lowest heaven. Reports from Qatāda say that "the jinn" was the name of a tribe among the angels, and that Iblīs belonged to it.

This side has a real argument behind it, not just an opinion. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to have put it in one line: if he had not been one of the angels, he would not have been commanded to bow. The command in the verse is addressed to the angels, and he was held responsible for disobeying it, so he must have been among those being addressed.

The second position is that he was truly one of the jinn. Ibn Kathīr takes the verse at its plain meaning and argues from what Iblīs was made of. He says his original nature betrayed him, because he was created from fire while the angels were created from light. He cites the report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim from ʿĀʾisha that the Messenger ﷺ said the angels were created from light, and the Quran itself says elsewhere that the jinn were created from fire. In Ṣād, Iblīs's own stated reason is You created me from fire. On this reading, the exception in the verse means exactly what it seems to mean: he stood among the angels without being one of them.

Al-Ḥasan is reported to have summed it up by saying that God returned him to his lineage, so that when the test came, what he really was came out.

We are going to leave this question open, because the scholars never settled it among themselves. Individual scholars took positions, but the tradition as a whole did not, and it would be dishonest to invent an agreement that nobody reached. Named scholars held both views for stated reasons, and now you have heard both.

What is worth noticing is that neither answer changes anything this course has taught. Whatever he was made of, he was given the command, he refused it, and his reason stayed the same. The disagreement is about what kind of being he was, while the verse is about how he responded, and the Quran spends its words on how he responded.

The verse that says he never actually knew

Now we come to the second of the two verses outside the scene.

وَلَقَدْ صَدَّقَ عَلَيْهِمْ إِبْلِيسُ ظَنَّهُۥ فَٱتَّبَعُوهُ إِلَّا فَرِيقًا مِّنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

"And Iblīs had already confirmed through them his assumption, so they followed him, except for a group of believers."

Sabaʾ (34:20)

The key word is ẓann, which means an assumption or a guess. Al-Ṭabarī spells out what that means in his own words: God's enemy said it as a guess on his part that he would manage it, not as knowledge, and then it turned out to be true.

That overturns something most people assume.

When he stood there and swore he would lead them all astray, he did not know he could. He had no evidence and had never done it before. All he had was a theory about human beings, that we would choose what is close at hand over what is true, and that most of us would not be grateful. He tested that theory, and it turned out he was right about many people.

So the confidence in his declaration was never certainty; it was a gamble. The painful part is the next words in the verse, so they followed him. His gamble did not succeed because he was powerful. It succeeded because people proved his guess right for him, one at a time, throughout history.

He never knew you. He only had a theory about you, and each time you follow him you hand him the proof, while each time you do not, his theory fails.

Notice too what the verse does not say. It does not say everyone followed him, because except for a group of believers sits in the same sentence as his success. This is the third time in this course that a claim of total victory has come with its own limit attached.

(A small note for anyone curious: the early reciters read the verb here in two ways, one with a doubled letter and one without. That gives either "he made his assumption come true about them" or "his assumption about them proved true." Al-Ṭabarī judges both to be well-known readings that are close in meaning, and the point holds either way.)

What the gaps were protecting

Here is what this module adds up to. The Quran gives us no serpent, no kind of tree, no name for Ādam's wife, and no face for Iblīs. The question of what kind of being he was is left open by the scholars who knew most, and his declaration of war turns out, when you look closely, to have been a guess.

All of these gaps point the same way. The Quran has held back the details that would let you file this away as ancient history about an impressive being, and it has kept the details that describe a process you are living inside right now.

There is one module left, and in it the whole story turns to face you.
