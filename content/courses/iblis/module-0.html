Start Here — The Scene

Every course on this site so far has started with a life. A person is born, is given something, is tested, and comes out the other side changed. This course cannot start that way, and the reason why is the first thing worth knowing.

One scene, told seven times

The Quran names Iblīs in eleven verses, and nine of them tell the same moment again, spread across seven different chapters. In that moment he is told to bow, and he does not. That is the whole scene.

Nothing develops from it. He does not grow, fall further, think again, or change, so he is the same at the end of the Quran as he is at the start. Dāwūd is corrected and turns back to God, and Sulaymān is given everything and asks the right question about it. Those are life stories. This course is more like a doctor studying how an illness works.

In the Quran, Iblīs stands for a method. He says his method out loud once, and after that he carries it out for the rest of history. That method is what we are studying here.

So the aim of this course is unusual, and it helps to know it from the start. You are living inside this method right now without being able to see it. That may sound dramatic, but it is close to what the Quran itself says, and we will reach that verse later in the course.

The scene, straight through

God tells the angels that He is placing a khalīfa, a steward, on the earth. He forms the human being, and once He has shaped him and breathed into him, He commands the angels to bow down. In Sūrat Ṣād, God gives the command in His own words:

إِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى خَـٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن طِينٍ فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَـٰجِدِينَ

"When your Lord said to the angels: I am going to create a human being from clay. So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration."

Ṣād (38:71–72)

Notice that the command is about what God did with the clay: He proportioned it and He breathed into it. The order to bow comes after the breath, and the clay is never given as the reason for anything.

Every one of the angels obeys, and then one figure does not:

فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ ٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ

"So the angels prostrated, all of them together — except Iblīs. He was arrogant, and was of the disbelievers."

Ṣād (38:73–74)

The whole course grows out of that one exception. The Arabic says kulluhum ajmaʿūn, "all of them together", using two words for "all" as if to close every gap, and yet one gap opens anyway.

God asks him a question, and he answers it. Module 1 looks closely at those two sentences, so for now we will simply read them once:

قَالَ يَـٰٓإِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَن تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَىَّ ۖ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلْعَالِينَ

"He said: O Iblīs, what prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Were you arrogant, or were you of the exalted?"

Ṣād (38:75)

قَالَ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ ۖ خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍ

"He said: I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay."

Ṣād (38:76)

After this he is thrown out. Then he asks for something and gets an answer. He asks to be left alive until the Day they are raised, and the answer gives him the extra time but quietly changes the end date to the Day of the known time. If you read the two side by side, you can see the reply is not what he asked for. The date he chose would have carried him past death, and that is exactly the part he does not get. He is given time, but God is the one who measures it.

Then he announces what he will do with that time. In the very next breath, without anyone asking him, he names the people he knows he will never be able to touch.

So the scene runs in this order: he refuses, he gives his reason, he is thrown out, he makes a request, he is given time, he makes his announcement, and then he states his own limit.

The honest frame

There are three things to know about how this course is built.

First, Iblīs is on display here as evidence, never as a hero. The Quran gives him more direct speech than it gives most prophets, and every one of those speeches convicts him. He is allowed to talk because his own words are the case against him. If a course starts finding him interesting, it has already gone wrong, because we are here to understand how the trap works, not to admire the one who set it.

Second, nothing in this course is a technique. We will take his method apart in detail because the Quran does the same. Every stage of it comes with a limit that the Quran itself states, and no stage will be described here without its limit in the same breath. Those limits are the real finding. Without them, you would have a manual for the wrong side. With them, you have the truth, which is that his whole method is far weaker than it feels.

Third, when the scholars disagree, you will be told. At least one real disagreement in this material has gone on for twelve centuries without being settled. We will name it, say who held which view, and leave it open, because honesty requires that and because the disagreement itself teaches something.

The course map

The course has six parts, and it moves in a spiral. You are told that now so you can notice it as it happens.

  • Module 0 · Start Here (you are here). The scene, and why this course studies a method rather than a life.
  • Module 1 · "I Am Better Than Him." The one sentence he says twice, word for word, in two different chapters. We take his argument seriously enough to show why it holds together on its own terms, and then show exactly what it leaves out.
  • Module 2 · The Machine. What he actually does, the verbs the Quran uses for it, what a whisper is and is not, and why he can see you when you cannot see him, along with everything that still does not give him.
  • Module 3 · The Switch. Two verses that say exactly opposite things, the one difference between them, and why the Quran's answer to an enemy you cannot see is not armour.
  • Module 4 · What the Quran Won't Tell You. The things the Quran leaves unsaid, the disagreement that is still open, and the verse that shows he never knew what would happen and was only guessing.
  • Module 5 · Blame Yourselves. His last two statements, both of which deny responsibility, and the one sentence that separates him from the first human being.

Before we begin, here is where the course is heading. By the end you will have been handed a switch, and the surprising thing about it is how small it is.
