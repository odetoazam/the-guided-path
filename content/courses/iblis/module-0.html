Start Here — The Scene

This course is built around one scene, the moment Iblīs was told to bow and would not. The Quran tells that scene in seven different chapters, and each one gives some of the details. Here they are put together in order, so you know what happened before we look at any part of it closely. Iblīs is not the hero of this story. He is shown to us so that we can see how he works, and how little he can really do.

God tells the angels

Before the first human being was made, God told the angels what He was about to do. He was going to create a human being from clay, and when He had shaped him and breathed His spirit into him, they were to bow down to him.

إِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى خَـٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن طِينٍ فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَـٰجِدِينَ

"When your Lord said to the angels: I am going to create a human being from clay. So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration."

Ṣād (38:71–72)

Everyone bows except one

God did as He had said, and the angels bowed, every single one of them. Only Iblīs did not.

فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ ٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ

"So the angels prostrated, all of them together — except Iblīs. He was arrogant, and was of the disbelievers."

Ṣād (38:73–74)

The command was given to the angels, and it reached Iblīs too, because God later asks him why he did not bow when He had commanded him. One verse says that Iblīs was of the jinn, and since the earliest times the scholars have disagreed over whether he was an angel or a jinn, so this page leaves that question open.

God asks him why

God spoke to Iblīs directly and asked him what had kept him from bowing.

قَالَ يَـٰٓإِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَن تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَىَّ ۖ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلْعَالِينَ

"He said: O Iblīs, what prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Were you arrogant, or were you of the exalted?"

Ṣād (38:75)

Iblīs answered by comparing what the two of them were made of.

قَالَ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ ۖ خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍ

"He said: I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay."

Ṣād (38:76)

In another telling he says it even more bluntly, that he would never bow to a human being made out of clay.

He is thrown out

So God sent him out. He told him to go down from where he was, because it was no place for him to be arrogant in, and He told him that he was now among the disgraced. He told him that he was cast out, and that God's curse would be on him until the Day of Judgement.

He asks for time

The Quran does not record Iblīs asking to be forgiven. Instead he asked God to give him more time, and to let him live until the Day people are raised from the dead. God gave him time, and in His answer He set the end Himself, saying it would last until the Day of the known time.

He says what he will do

Then Iblīs said what he would do with that time. He said it was God who had made him go astray, and he swore that he would sit waiting for people on God's straight path. He said he would come at them from in front and from behind, from their right and from their left, and that God would find most of them ungrateful. He also asked God, "Do You see this one You have honoured above me?" If he were given time until the Day of Resurrection, he said, he would bring all of his descendants under his control except a few. He swore by God's might that he would make wrong things look good to them on the earth and lead them all astray.

But in the same breath he made an exception. He said he would not lead astray God's sincere servants.

God answers him

God answered him. He told him that he would have no authority over God's servants, only over those who chose to follow him. He told him to go and try what he could with his voice, his riders and his men on foot, his share in people's wealth and children, and his promises. Straight after, the Quran says that everything Satan promises is only a deception, and that God is enough as a guardian. Then God said that He would surely fill Hell with Iblīs and with every one of them who followed him.

After the scene

The Quran tells a little more about him in other places. In the garden he whispered to Ādam and his wife, and he swore to them that he was only giving them good advice, and they ate from the tree. One verse says that what Iblīs had assumed about a people came true, because they followed him, all except a group of believers. And on the Last Day, when everything has been decided, he will tell those who followed him that he never had any power over them. He only called them, and they answered, so they should blame themselves and not him.
