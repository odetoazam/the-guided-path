Start Here — The Story

This is the whole story of Iblīs, from his creation to his end on the Day of Judgement. It is told in order from the Quran and the authentic sayings of the Prophet ﷺ. Iblīs is shown to us as a warning and never as a hero, so at every step the sources also show how little he can really do.

Made from fire

Long before there were any human beings, God created the jinn. The Quran says He made them before humans, from a fierce and scorching fire. In another place it says He made them from a flame of fire. The Prophet ﷺ said that the angels were created from light and the jinn from a flame of fire, and Ādam from what you have been told (Muslim).

The Quran says plainly that Iblīs "was of the jinn," and he himself later told God that he was made from fire. Yet he was there among the angels when God spoke to them, and the command God gave them reached him too. From the earliest generations the scholars have differed over whether he had ever been counted among the angels. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said he was never an angel for the blink of an eye, and others held that he had been one.

Many popular retellings give him an older name, ʿAzāzīl, and make him the keeper of Paradise or the chief of the angels of the lowest heaven. Ibn Kathīr says that most of these reports come from the People of the Book, and that the Quran is enough without them.

He looks at the hollow shape

Then God began to make Ādam. The Prophet ﷺ said that when God had shaped Ādam in the garden, He left him there for as long as He willed. Iblīs began to walk around him to see what he was. When he saw that Ādam was hollow inside, he knew that this was a creature who would not be able to hold himself back (Muslim).

So the first thing Iblīs learned about human beings was their weakness. He did not see what God would give this weak creature. God would breathe His spirit into him and teach him the names of all things. And after Ādam fell, God would show him the way back.

God tells the angels

Before the first human being was made, God told the angels what He was about to do. He was going to create a human being from clay, and when He had shaped him and breathed His spirit into him, they were to bow down to him.

إِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى خَـٰلِقٌۢ بَشَرًا مِّن طِينٍ فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُۥ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَـٰجِدِينَ

"When your Lord said to the angels: I am going to create a human being from clay. So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration."

Ṣād (38:71–72)

Everyone bows except one

God did as He had said. The angels bowed, every single one of them. Only Iblīs did not.

فَسَجَدَ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ كُلُّهُمْ أَجْمَعُونَ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ ٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ

"So the angels prostrated, all of them together — except Iblīs. He was arrogant, and was of the disbelievers."

Ṣād (38:73–74)

In one telling the Quran says he refused and was arrogant. In another it says he "was of the jinn, so he departed from his Lord's command." The word used there is fasaqa. Ibn Kathīr explains that the Arabs used it for a ripe date splitting out of its skin, and for a mouse coming out of its hole to do damage. So Iblīs broke out of the place where obedience had kept him.

God asks him why

God spoke to Iblīs directly and asked him what had kept him from bowing when He had commanded him.

قَالَ يَـٰٓإِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَن تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَىَّ ۖ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلْعَالِينَ

"He said: O Iblīs, what prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two hands? Were you arrogant, or were you of the exalted?"

Ṣād (38:75)

Iblīs answered by comparing what the two of them were made of.

قَالَ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ ۖ خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍ

"He said: I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay."

Ṣād (38:76)

In another telling he says it more bluntly. He would never bow to a human being made from dry clay of dark mud. In another he asks, "Shall I bow to one You created from clay?" Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that Iblīs was the first to reason by comparison. He weighed fire against clay and put his own reasoning above a direct command from God.

Ibn Kathīr says his comparison was wrong even on its own terms. Clay is steady and patient and makes things grow, while fire is light and hasty and burns. And Ādam had an honour Iblīs left out, because God had made him with His own hands and breathed His spirit into him. Ibn Kathīr adds that the excuse Iblīs gave was worse than the sin itself.

He is thrown out

So God sent him out. He told him to go down, because it was no place for him to be arrogant in, and He told him he was now among the disgraced. He called him rajīm, one who is driven away. He told him that God's curse would be on him until the Day of Judgement.

Ibn Kathīr says Iblīs was brought down from the highest gathering to the earth, small and disgraced. God's decree barred him from ever living there again. Whatever rank he had reached by his worship was taken from him because of his pride and envy.

He asks for time

The Quran never records Iblīs asking to be forgiven. He asked for something else. He asked God to give him time and to let him live until the Day people are raised from the dead. God gave him time, but He set the end Himself. He said it would last until the Day of the known time.

Ibn Kathīr says God granted it to lead him on step by step and to give him rope. It was no honour to him.

He says what he will do

Then Iblīs said what he would do with that time. He blamed God for his own going astray. He swore that he would sit waiting for people on God's straight path. He said he would come at them from in front and from behind, from their right and from their left. And he said God would find most of them ungrateful.

Qatāda, one of the early scholars, noticed what was missing from that list. He said Iblīs comes at a person from every side except from above, because he cannot come between a person and the mercy of God.

Iblīs also pointed at Ādam. "Do You see this one You have honoured above me?" he said. He said that if he were given time until the Day of Resurrection he would take hold of Ādam's children, all except a few. The word he used was la-aḥtanikanna. Al-Ṭabarī explains it as taking hold of them and pulling them up by the roots. He quotes an old Arab poem that used the same word for a year of drought that ate up a people's wealth.

But in the same breath Iblīs made an exception that he could not get around. He swore by God's might that he would lead them all astray, except God's sincere servants among them.

God answers him

God answered him with a limit of His own.

إِنَّ عِبَادِى لَيْسَ لَكَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَـٰنٌ إِلَّا مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَكَ مِنَ ٱلْغَاوِينَ

"Indeed, over My servants you will have no authority, except those who follow you of the deviators."

Al-Ḥijr (15:42)

He told him to go and unsettle whoever he could with his voice. He could gather against them his riders and his men on foot, take a share in their wealth and their children, and make them promises. Straight after, the Quran says that what Satan promises is only a deception. It says again that he has no authority over God's servants, and that God is enough as a guardian. Then God said that He would fill Hell with Iblīs and with every one of them who followed him.

In the garden

God placed Ādam and his wife in the garden, and He warned Ādam about Iblīs. He told him that this was an enemy to him and to his wife, so he should not let him drive them out of the garden into hardship. In the garden they would not go hungry or naked, and they would not be thirsty or burned by the sun.

But Iblīs whispered to them. He said their Lord had only forbidden the tree so that they would not become angels, or live forever. He offered to lead Ādam to the tree of eternity and to a kingdom that never wears away. He swore to them by God that he was only giving them good advice. So he brought them down by deceit, and they ate. As soon as they tasted the tree their nakedness showed to them, and they began to cover themselves with leaves from the garden.

Iblīs had his first success, and it did not last. Ādam and his wife did the one thing Iblīs had never done. They said that they had wronged themselves. They told their Lord that if He did not forgive them and have mercy on them they would be among the losers. Then Ādam received words from his Lord, and God accepted his repentance. God later turned this scene into a warning for all of Ādam's children.

يَـٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ لَا يَفْتِنَنَّكُمُ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ كَمَآ أَخْرَجَ أَبَوَيْكُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ يَنزِعُ عَنْهُمَا لِبَاسَهُمَا لِيُرِيَهُمَا سَوْءَٰتِهِمَآ ۗ إِنَّهُۥ يَرَىٰكُمْ هُوَ وَقَبِيلُهُۥ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا تَرَوْنَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا ٱلشَّيَـٰطِينَ أَوْلِيَآءَ لِلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

"O children of Ādam, do not let Satan tempt you as he removed your parents from Paradise, stripping them of their clothing to show them their private parts. He sees you, he and his tribe, from where you do not see them. We have made the devils allies of those who do not believe."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:27)

On the earth

After that, Ādam and his wife were sent down to the earth, and Iblīs was an enemy to them there. Ibn Kathīr says that Iblīs gave everything he had to leading Ādam's children astray, by every road and from every place of ambush. Yet he never had force on his side. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said that by God, Iblīs never struck them with a stick or forced them into anything. He only called them to false hopes and deceptions, and they answered him.

The Prophet ﷺ said that Iblīs swore to his Lord by His might and majesty. He would not stop leading the children of Ādam astray as long as their souls were in their bodies. God swore by His own might and majesty that He would not stop forgiving them as long as they asked Him for forgiveness (Aḥmad, graded sound by al-Arnaʾūṭ).

The Quran says that Iblīs found his guess about people to be true, because they followed him, all except a group of believers. It also says why he was allowed any room at all. He was given no authority over them. It was only so that God would make known who believes in the next life and who is in doubt about it.

In the stories of the prophets

The Quran says that God gave every prophet enemies, devils from among people and jinn, who whisper fine-sounding lies to one another. It adds that if God had willed they would not have done it. So Iblīs and his helpers appear again and again in the lives of the prophets, and each time the story shows where they stop.

Yaʿqūb told his young son Yūsuf not to tell his dream to his brothers, because Satan is an open enemy to people. Years later Yūsuf stood before his parents in Egypt. He said God had been good to him, after Satan had stirred up trouble between him and his brothers. Satan's work was named in one sentence, and God's kindness filled the rest of the story.

When Mūsā struck a man who was fighting with one of his own people, the man died. Mūsā said at once that this was the work of Satan, an open enemy who leads astray. Then he turned straight to God. "My Lord, I have wronged myself so forgive me," he said. And God forgave him.

When Ayyūb had suffered for a long time, he called out to his Lord that Satan had touched him with hardship and pain. God told him to strike the ground with his foot. There was cool water to wash in and to drink, and God removed the harm that was on him.

When the wife of ʿImrān gave birth to Maryam, she prayed that God would protect her daughter and her daughter's children from Satan the rejected. The Prophet ﷺ said that no child is born without Satan touching it at birth, so it comes into the world crying from that touch. The only ones spared were Maryam and her son (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

In the time of the Prophet ﷺ

On the day of Badr, the Quran says, Satan made the idol-worshippers' plans look good to them. He told them that no one could defeat them that day and that he would stand by them. Ibn ʿAbbās said Iblīs came with his troops and his banner in the shape of Surāqa ibn Mālik, a chief of the tribe of Mudlij. But when the two armies met and he saw the angels, he turned on his heels. "I am free of you," he said. "I see what you do not see. I fear God."

Once the Prophet ﷺ was praying and his Companions heard him seek refuge in God from someone and curse him with God's curse. Afterwards he told them that Iblīs, the enemy of God, had come with a flame of fire to push into his face. That was why he had sought refuge and cursed him. Then he had wanted to take hold of him. He said that if it were not for the prayer of his brother Sulaymān, Iblīs would have been tied up for the children of Madīna to play with (Muslim).

The Prophet ﷺ said that every one of us has a companion from the jinn assigned to him. His Companions asked if that was true of him too. He said that it was, but God had helped him against his companion. So it submitted and only told him to do good (Muslim).

The Prophet ﷺ said that Satan sat in wait for a man on each of his roads. On the road to Islam he said, "Will you leave your religion and the religion of your fathers?" The man disobeyed him and became Muslim. Then Satan waited on the road of emigration and the road of striving, and each time the man disobeyed him and went on. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever does this has a right with God to enter Paradise (Aḥmad, graded strong by al-Arnaʾūṭ).

The Prophet ﷺ said that Satan had given up hope that those who pray in the Arabian Peninsula would ever worship him. What he still hoped for was to set them against one another (Muslim). So the Prophet ﷺ guarded even small things. One night he was walking his wife Ṣafiyya home when two men of the Anṣār passed by. He called them over and told them she was Ṣafiyya. Satan runs through a person the way blood runs, he said, and he feared Satan might put some bad thought in their hearts (al-Bukhārī).

There was even a Companion whom Satan kept away from. The Prophet ﷺ told ʿUmar that whenever Satan met him walking along a road, Satan would take a different road (al-Bukhārī).

His work today

Ibn Kathīr writes that Iblīs is alive today, because the Quran says he was given time. The Prophet ﷺ said that Iblīs sets up his throne on the water and sends out his troops to put people to the test. The one closest to him is the one who causes the most harm. One comes and says he did this and that, and Iblīs tells him he has done nothing. Then another says he did not leave a man until he had separated him from his wife. Iblīs draws that one close and says, "You are the one" (Muslim).

The Quran answers that whole army in one line. It says that the plot of Satan has always been weak. And the Prophet ﷺ showed how easily it breaks. When the call to prayer is made, Satan runs off so that he will not hear it (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). When the month of Ramaḍān comes, the devils are chained (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).

When a person reads a verse of prostration and goes down in prostration, the Prophet ﷺ said, Satan goes off weeping. "Woe to me," he says. "The son of Ādam was told to prostrate and he did, so Paradise is his. I was told to prostrate and I refused, so the Fire is mine" (Muslim). The refusal from the first day still follows him, and every believer who prostrates reminds him of it.

The Prophet ﷺ said that Satan may come to one of you and ask who created this and who created that, until he asks who created your Lord. When it reaches that point, the Prophet ﷺ said, seek refuge in God and stop there (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). The Quran gives the same shield. When you read the Quran, seek refuge in God from Satan the rejected, because he has no authority over those who believe and put their trust in their Lord. His authority is only over those who take him as a friend.

The known time runs out

Iblīs asked to live until the Day people are raised. The Quran says only that his time lasts until the Day of the known time. Al-Ṭabarī explains that if he had been given exactly what he asked for he would never die, because there is no death after the raising. So he understood the known time as the day God has written for all creation to perish, and Iblīs with it. He points to the verse that says every soul will taste death. No reliable source describes how or when Iblīs dies.

The Day of Judgement

On the Day of Judgement a person who was led astray will blame his companion. The Quran says the companion will answer, "Our Lord, I did not make him rebel. He was already far astray." Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Qatāda said this companion is the devil who was assigned to him. God will tell them both not to argue before Him, because He had already sent them the warning.

Then, when everything has been decided, Iblīs will speak to those who followed him. Ibn Kathīr says the plain sense of the verse is that he gives this speech after they have entered the Fire.

وَقَالَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ لَمَّا قُضِىَ ٱلْأَمْرُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَعَدَكُمْ وَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ وَوَعَدتُّكُمْ فَأَخْلَفْتُكُمْ ۖ وَمَا كَانَ لِىَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن سُلْطَـٰنٍ إِلَّآ أَن دَعَوْتُكُمْ فَٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِى ۖ فَلَا تَلُومُونِى وَلُومُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُم ۖ مَّآ أَنَا۠ بِمُصْرِخِكُمْ وَمَآ أَنتُم بِمُصْرِخِىَّ ۖ إِنِّى كَفَرْتُ بِمَآ أَشْرَكْتُمُونِ مِن قَبْلُ ۗ إِنَّ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

"And Satan will say when the matter has been decided: Indeed, God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, then I broke my promise to you. I had no authority over you except that I called you and you answered me. So do not blame me, but blame yourselves. I cannot answer your cry for help, nor can you answer mine. I reject your making me His partner before. Indeed, for the wrongdoers there is a painful punishment."

Ibrāhīm (14:22)

The word he uses for helper is muṣrikh, from ṣarkha, a scream for help. It means the one who answers when someone screams for help. On that day Iblīs admits that he cannot answer anyone's scream, and that no one can answer his.

His last words confirm what God told him on the first day. He had no real power over the people he misled. He could only call them. The Quran says that the idols and those who were led astray will be thrown into Hell, and with them the soldiers of Iblīs, all together. So God keeps the promise He made when Iblīs first refused, to fill Hell with him and with those who followed him.
