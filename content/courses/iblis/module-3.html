Module 3 — The Switch

Everything so far has been setup. This is the module the course was built around.

We have an adversary with one advantage of position, which is that he works where you are not looking, and a set of tools that cannot touch your will. So what decides whether the machine runs at all?

The Quran answers with two verses. They sit in different chapters, they are not usually read together, and they are exact opposites of each other.

The verse where the machine turns on

وَمَن يَعْشُ عَن ذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُۥ شَيْطَـٰنًا فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينٌ

"And whoever is blinded from the remembrance of the Most Merciful — We appoint for him a devil, and he is to him a companion."

Az-Zukhruf (43:36)

The verb translated "is blinded" is yaʿshu, and al-Ṭabarī gives its literal sense: looking without steadiness because of a defect in the eye. That is dimness rather than blindness. The eyes still work, but they have stopped resolving.

What they have stopped resolving is named in the verse, and it is dhikr al-Raḥmān, the remembrance of the Most Merciful. Something goes dim toward that, and the apparatus is assigned. It is assigned after the turning away, and never before it.

The verse where the machine turns off

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّقَوْا۟ إِذَا مَسَّهُمْ طَـٰٓئِفٌ مِّنَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنِ تَذَكَّرُوا۟ فَإِذَا هُم مُّبْصِرُونَ

"Indeed, those who fear God — when an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember, and at once they are seeing."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:201)

Now put the two verses side by side.

In the first, sight goes dim toward remembrance, and a companion is assigned. In the second, they remember, and at once they see.

It is the same variable running in opposite directions. Sight dims away from remembrance and the machine switches on. Remembrance returns, sight comes back, and the machine is over. One switch, two positions.

Look at how fast it is. The Arabic is fa-idhā hum mubṣirūn, where the particle means "and there they are," suddenly, with no interval. No struggle is described, and no wrestling. They remember and they are seeing.

The size of the attack it dissolves is worth noting too. The word is ṭāʾif, which al-Jalālayn explains as something that lightly visited them. It is a passing touch rather than a siege. This is the early stage, before anything has escalated, and that is the moment the switch costs least to throw.

Al-Muyassar describes where it leaves them: on clear sight, disobeying the devil. They are not victorious after a fight. They are able to see again, and at that point there is nothing to fight.

The proof is in his name

If the whole apparatus really is keyed to remembrance, you would expect the Quran to say so somewhere directly. It does, in the last chapter of the book, and it does it by naming him for it.

قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ إِلَـٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ

"Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the Sovereign of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer — who whispers into the breasts of mankind — from among the jinn and mankind."

An-Nās (114:1–6)

The title is al-waswās al-khannās, the whisperer who retreats.

Al-Jalālayn explains the name: he is called this because he withdraws from the heart whenever God is remembered. Al-Muyassar says he whispers at the moment of heedlessness and hides when God is remembered.

See how strange that is. Of all the things you could name an adversary for, his power or his cunning or his hatred, the Quran names him for the thing that makes him leave. His title is his limitation. The escape route is printed on the enemy.

The last verse widens it in a way people often miss: from among the jinn and mankind. Al-Jalālayn reads this as a clarification, that the whisperer can be a jinn or a human being. A human whispers in the way that suits a human, out loud, and it reaches the same destination by the same route. So part of the mechanism is just a conversation, and the same limit holds for it: the words arrive, and the act is still yours.

What "remembrance" actually means here

It would be easy to walk away from this thinking that a phrase repeated enough times acts as a charm. That is not what these verses describe, and treating it that way turns a truth into a superstition.

Look at what remembrance does in 7:201. It does not repel anything. It restores sight. The result is that the person can see the situation accurately again, and once they can, the thing that was working only in the dark is already gone, because retreating is what he does.

So remembrance here is closer to coming back to what is actually true than to a formula. That God is real. That He sees this. That the thing being made to look urgent and fine is neither. A repeated phrase helps because it is a way of doing exactly that, on purpose, at the moment your attention has drifted.

That makes the whole struggle lighter than what most people are carrying. You are being asked to keep the lights on, not to be strong enough to beat something.

The oath that guarantees his defeat

There is one more thing, and it is the strangest sentence in this material.

Go back to the moment of expulsion in Sūrat Ṣād. He has been cast out, he has asked for time, and he has been granted it. Now he makes his declaration of war. What he swears by is the point:

قَالَ فَبِعِزَّتِكَ لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ

"He said: By Your might, I will surely mislead them all."

Ṣād (38:82)

By Your might. Bi-ʿizzatika.

He is declaring war on God, and to make the declaration binding he has to swear by something. The only thing available to swear by is the might of the One he is declaring war on. There is no rival power on offer. He knows it, and he says it out loud in the act of rebelling.

That is the divine name al-ʿAzīz, the Almighty, and the Quran establishes it here by letting the hostile witness testify. The enemy's own oath is the evidence.

Then, without being asked, in the very next breath, he writes the exception himself:

إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ ٱلْمُخْلَصِينَ

"Except, among them, Your chosen servants."

Ṣād (38:83)

It is a declaration of total war with the surrender clause written in by the aggressor, one verse after the declaration. He announces that he will take everyone, and in the same breath he concedes the people he cannot take.

These two verses are one unit and should never be quoted apart. Quote 38:82 alone and you have given him a dramatic line. Quote both and you have what is there, which is a boast carrying its own refutation, sworn by a might that belongs to the other side.

Where that leaves you

Put the module together.

The apparatus runs on your attention being elsewhere. It has no authority it did not receive from you. Its operator is named for retreating, he swore his oath by a power that is not his, and he named the exempt himself.

The Quran's answer to an unseen adversary was never armour. It is remembering, because remembering restores sight, and sight is the one thing the whole operation cannot survive.
