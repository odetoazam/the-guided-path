Module 5 — Blame Yourselves

We have watched the refusal, the reason, the machine, and the switch. There is one thing left: what he says at the end. He gets two closing statements in the Quran, and both of them are the same move.

The first: he quits

You have already met this verse, in Module 2, as evidence of the limit. Read it again now for something else — for its tone. This is a speech made after everything is over:

وَقَالَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ لَمَّا قُضِىَ ٱلْأَمْرُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَعَدَكُمْ وَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ وَوَعَدتُّكُمْ فَأَخْلَفْتُكُمْ ۖ وَمَا كَانَ لِىَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن سُلْطَـٰنٍ إِلَّآ أَن دَعَوْتُكُمْ فَٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِى ۖ فَلَا تَلُومُونِى وَلُومُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُم ۖ مَّآ أَنَا۠ بِمُصْرِخِكُمْ وَمَآ أَنتُم بِمُصْرِخِىَّ ۖ إِنِّى كَفَرْتُ بِمَآ أَشْرَكْتُمُونِ مِن قَبْلُ ۗ إِنَّ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

"And Satan will say, when the matter has been concluded: Indeed, God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, but I betrayed you. And I had no authority over you except that I invited you, and you responded to me. So do not blame me, but blame yourselves. I cannot be called to your aid, nor can you be called to my aid. Indeed, I deny your having associated me before. Indeed, the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment."

Ibrāhīm (14:22)

He does not gloat. He does not claim a victory. He stands in front of the people who followed him for their entire lives and says: God told you the truth, I told you a lie, I never had any power over you, and I am not helping you.

Every word of it is accurate. That is what makes it terrible.

The second: the same move, in miniature

كَمَثَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنِ إِذْ قَالَ لِلْإِنسَـٰنِ ٱكْفُرْ فَلَمَّا كَفَرَ قَالَ إِنِّى بَرِىٓءٌ مِّنكَ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"Like the example of Satan when he says to man: Disbelieve. But when he disbelieves, he says: Indeed, I am disassociated from you. Indeed, I fear God, Lord of the worlds."

Al-Ḥashr (59:16)

The whole arc in one verse. Say it. Then, the moment it is done: I have nothing to do with you. And the last clause is almost unbelievable — I fear God, Lord of the worlds. He ends by taking the correct theological position, out loud, about the God he declared war on.

He is never confused about what is true. That was never his problem.

The sentence, and the other sentence

Now put his closing words beside the words of the first human being.

Iblīs, at the end of everything: fa-lā talūmūnī wa-lūmū anfusakum — do not blame me; blame yourselves.

And Ādam, in the garden, immediately after eating:

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ

"They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:23)

Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā — our Lord, we have wronged ourselves.

They are the same sentence. Both point at the self. Both are true. Both are said by someone who has just been shown to be in the wrong.

One of them is said in the garden, out loud, to God, while there is still time to say it. The other is said at the end of the world, to a crowd, about other people.

He knows the right answer. He says it correctly, in front of everyone, at the last possible moment. He just never once said it about himself.

That is the whole distance between the two. Not information — he had the information. Not intelligence — his argument was well-formed. The difference is a sentence, said in time, and said in the first person.

Which is what Ibn ʿAbbās was pointing at in the line we met in Module 1: if a man's sin is in arrogance, do not hold out hope for him; if his sin is in disobedience, hold out hope. Both of them did a wrong thing in the same garden. Only one of them could turn around, because only one of them was able to say the sentence about himself.

Three questions

Take these slowly. They are the course, applied.

1. What true thing do you use to get out of a thing you know you should do? His whole case was a fact — fire really is above clay. Most of our refusals come with an accurate reason attached, and the accuracy is exactly what makes them feel like reasoning instead of resistance. What is your fire-and-clay sentence?

2. Where did your sight go dim? Not "what sin are you committing" — earlier than that. The machine needs an opening, and the opening is attention drifting away from God, usually somewhere ordinary and unalarming. Where has it drifted? That is the place, and it is upstream of everything else.

3. What are you still blaming? A person, a circumstance, an upbringing, a pressure. Some of it may be genuinely at fault — Iblīs really did invite. And it will change nothing, because the only sentence that ever moved anybody is the one Ādam said, and it has ourselves in it.

Where this leaves you

The course has really only established one thing, and it is smaller and better news than most people expect.

The apparatus you have been taught to fear turns out to have no power at all. It has reach — enormous reach — and no force. Its operator is named, in the last chapter of the Quran, for the fact that he retreats. He swore his one great oath by a might that belongs to God, and named the people he could not touch in the very next breath, without being asked. And what looked like certain knowledge about human nature was, al-Ṭabarī says plainly, a guess.

That is al-ʿAzīz — the Almighty — and the reason this course exists is that the Quran did not simply assert it. It let the hostile witness testify. The enemy's own oath, the enemy's own exception clause, the enemy's own closing confession that he never had authority at all: the case is made by the one making the accusation.

So the practical instruction at the end of an anatomy of the adversary is not a technique, and not armour. It is the switch from Module 3, and it is available to you right now, in whatever room you are in.

They remember, and at once they are seeing.

He is al-khannās. The retreater. Turn the lights on and he is already gone — and the only sentence you need is the one Ādam found, said now rather than at the end: we have wronged ourselves. It is the sentence that reopens everything, and it was given to the first human being on his worst day, which tells you what it was made for.
