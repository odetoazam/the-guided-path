Module 5 — Blame Yourselves

We have looked at the refusal and the reason behind it. We have seen how he works and the switch that stops him. One thing is left, and that is what he says at the very end. The Quran gives him two closing statements. In both of them he does the same thing. He walks away from the people who followed him.

The first: he quits

You already met this verse in Module 2, where it showed the limit of his power. This time read it for its tone, and remember that this speech comes after everything is over.

وَقَالَ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنُ لَمَّا قُضِىَ ٱلْأَمْرُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَعَدَكُمْ وَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ وَوَعَدتُّكُمْ فَأَخْلَفْتُكُمْ ۖ وَمَا كَانَ لِىَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن سُلْطَـٰنٍ إِلَّآ أَن دَعَوْتُكُمْ فَٱسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِى ۖ فَلَا تَلُومُونِى وَلُومُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُم ۖ مَّآ أَنَا۠ بِمُصْرِخِكُمْ وَمَآ أَنتُم بِمُصْرِخِىَّ ۖ إِنِّى كَفَرْتُ بِمَآ أَشْرَكْتُمُونِ مِن قَبْلُ ۗ إِنَّ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

"And Satan will say, when the matter has been concluded: Indeed, God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, but I betrayed you. And I had no authority over you except that I invited you, and you responded to me. So do not blame me, but blame yourselves. I cannot be called to your aid, nor can you be called to my aid. Indeed, I deny your having associated me before. Indeed, the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment."

Ibrāhīm (14:22)

He does not gloat or claim a victory. He stands in front of the people who followed him their whole lives. He tells them that God told them the truth and that he told them a lie. He tells them he never had any power over them, and that he will not help them now.

The terrible thing is that every word he says is true.

The second: the same move, in miniature

كَمَثَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَـٰنِ إِذْ قَالَ لِلْإِنسَـٰنِ ٱكْفُرْ فَلَمَّا كَفَرَ قَالَ إِنِّى بَرِىٓءٌ مِّنكَ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

"Like the example of Satan when he says to man: Disbelieve. But when he disbelieves, he says: Indeed, I am disassociated from you. Indeed, I fear God, Lord of the worlds."

Al-Ḥashr (59:16)

Here the whole story fits into one verse. He tells a man to disbelieve and the man does it. The moment it is done, Satan says I have nothing to do with you. The strangest part is his last line: I fear God, Lord of the worlds. He ends by saying the right thing out loud about the very God he declared war on. Even here all he did was speak, while the man was the one who acted.

So he is never confused about what is true, and confusion was never his problem.

The sentence, and the other sentence

Now put his closing words beside the words of the first human being.

At the end of everything Iblīs says fa-lā talūmūnī wa-lūmū anfusakum. It means "do not blame me, blame yourselves."

Ādam says this in the garden, right after eating from the tree.

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ

"They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."

Al-Aʿrāf (7:23)

Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā, "our Lord, we have wronged ourselves."

In a real sense these are the same sentence. Both point to the self, both are true, and both are spoken by someone who has just been shown to be in the wrong.

The difference is when and how they are said. Ādam says his out loud to God in the garden, while there is still time. Iblīs says his to a crowd at the end of the world, and he says it about other people.

He knows the right answer and even says it correctly in front of everyone, but only at the last possible moment and never once about himself.

That is the whole distance between the two of them. He had all the information, and his argument was well made. So neither knowledge nor intelligence separates them. The difference is one sentence, said in time and said about yourself.

Ibn ʿAbbās was pointing to this in the saying we met in Module 1. If a man's sin comes from arrogance, do not hold out hope for him. If it comes from disobedience, hold out hope. Both of them did wrong in the same garden, but only one of them could turn back. That was because only one of them was able to say that sentence about himself.

Three questions

These three questions take everything in the course and turn it toward your own life.

First, what true fact do you use to get out of something you know you should do? His whole case rested on a fact, since fire really is above clay on the measures he chose. Most of our refusals come with an accurate reason attached, and that accuracy is what makes them feel like careful thinking instead of resistance. What is your own version of his fire-and-clay sentence?

Second, where did your sight grow dim? Look past the sin you are committing to what happened before it. Everything he does needs an opening, and that opening is your attention drifting away from God. It usually happens in some ordinary moment that does not feel dangerous at all. Where has your attention drifted? That is the place to look, because it comes before everything else.

Third, what are you still blaming? It might be a person or a situation. It might be the way you were raised, or some pressure you are under. Some of it may truly be at fault, just as Iblīs really did invite people, though an invitation was all he ever had. But blaming it will still change nothing. The only sentence that has ever turned anyone around is the one Ādam said, and it has the word "ourselves" in it.

Where this leaves you

In the end the course has shown only one thing, and it is smaller and better news than most people expect.

The enemy you have been taught to fear has no real power at all. He reaches very far, but he has no force. In the last chapter of the Quran he is named for the fact that he retreats. He swore his one great oath by a might that belongs to God. Then in the very next breath he named the people he could never touch, without being asked. And al-Ṭabarī says plainly that his seeming knowledge of human nature was only a guess.

That is what it means for God to be al-ʿAzīz, the Almighty. This course exists because the Quran does not simply state it. It lets the enemy himself prove it. The case is made by his own oath and his own exception. It is made by his own final admission that he never had any authority at all. So the proof comes from the mouth of the one who stood against God.

So after studying this enemy closely, the practical advice is the switch from Module 3. You can use it right now, wherever you are.

They remember, and at once they are seeing.

He is al-khannās, the one who retreats. So when you remember God he is already gone. The only sentence you need is the one Ādam found, and you can say it now instead of at the end: we have wronged ourselves. That sentence opens the way back to everything. God gave it to the first human being on the worst day of his life, and that tells you what it was made for.
