# Post-regeneration audit — Al-Kahf two-gardens parable (18:32–44)

Audited 2026-07-30 against the regenerated (untruncated) tafsir reports.
All three files: **MODERATE**. No fabricated attributions, no non-Quranic Arabic,
every ayah verifies verbatim, morphology clean throughout. The defects are all of one
kind: **an ikhtilāf the full report records that the file settles silently.**

---

## `018-al-kahf/ayahs-032-036.md` — MODERATE

**MODERATE — ثَمَر (18:34) presented as settled.** The file glosses it as an
undifferentiated bundle ("fruit, produce, wealth, yield"). al-Ṭabarī opens the phrase
with a *qirāʾāt* survey and then three **named** meaning-camps:

- Mujāhid — «كان له ذهب وفضة» (gold and silver)
- Ibn ʿAbbās / Qatādah — «المال الكثير من صنوف الأموال» (all categories of wealth)
- Ibn Zayd — «الأصل» (the capital / principal)

and issues a *tarjīḥ*: «وأولى القراءات في ذلك عندي بالصواب قراءة من قرأ "وَكَانَ لَهُ
ثُمُرٌ" بضمّ الثاء والميم لإجماع الحجة من القرّاء عليه» — **a reading different from the
one the file prints.** Ibn Kathir adjudicates separately for "fruits" as the more
apparent meaning.

**MINOR — arrogance spread across three commentators.** The file says al-Ṭabarī, Ibn
Kathir and al-Muyassar all read وَهُوَ ظَالِمٌ لِّنَفْسِهِ as arrogance + disbelief +
denial. al-Ṭabarī: «كفره بالبعث، وشكه في قيام الساعة، ونسيانه المعاد إلى الله تعالى».
al-Muyassar: «بالكفر بالبعث، وشكه في قيام الساعة». **Neither contains an arrogance term.**
Only Ibn Kathir has it. The file's entire self-wronging theme rests on ẓulm = arrogance,
where the two Arabic sources define it narrowly as denial of the resurrection.

**MINOR — the dual→singular garden shift** (جنتين → جنته) is read as moral appropriation.
al-Jalalayn explains it as idiom: «ولَمْ يَقُلْ جَنَّتَيْهِ إرادَة لِلرَّوْضَةِ وقِيلَ
اكْتِفاء بِالواحِدِ». Reflection presented as if the text is signalling.

**MINOR — فَجَّرْنَا's doubling** read as force. al-Ṭabarī gives a *distributive* reason:
«فثقل الجيم منه، لأن التفجير في النهر كله» — the cleaving runs through the whole river.
Which would have strengthened the file's own "woven into the structure" point.

**Named occasion-report the file gestures at generically:** al-Ṭabarī gives it in full —
ʿUyaynah and al-Aqraʿ to the Prophet ﷺ: «نحن سادات العرب، وأرباب الأموال، فنحّ عنا
سلمان وخبَّابا وصُهيبا».

**Credit where due:** no source names the two men, calls them brothers, or makes them
Israelites — al-Muyassar has only «رجلين من الأمم السابقة». **The file correctly asserts
no isrāʾīliyyāt.** Its *kiltā/ātat* grammatical note is a faithful, well-sourced summary
of al-Ṭabarī («أصله كلّ»), corroborated by al-Jalalayn.

## `018-al-kahf/ayahs-037-041.md` — MODERATE

*The passage where truncation hurt most.*

**MODERATE — حُسْبَانًا: the file supplies three calamity-forms sourced to no one.** It
attributes "thunderbolt, fire, stones" to "classical tafsir". **None of the four
commentators says any of them.** What the report holds:

- al-Ṭabarī (lexical) — «والحسبان: جمع حُسْبانة، وهي المرامي» (hurled projectiles)
- Ibn Zayd — «الحُسبان: قضاء من الله يقضيه» (a decree/reckoning)
- Ibn Kathir's *tarjīḥ* — "a mighty rain which would disrupt his garden and uproot its
  plants and trees"

The file's section is named for the reckoning sense, which rests on a **single minority
narration**. And Ibn Kathir's rain reading — mercy's own instrument turned against him —
would have served the file's theology better than what it invented.

**MODERATE — مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ sits on a two-camp iʿrāb dispute the file settles silently.**
al-Ṭabarī: مَا accusative, object of شاء, apodosis كان elided → *"whatever Allah willed,
came to be."* Others: nominative with implied هو → *"this is what Allah willed"* —
which al-Muyassar and al-Jalalayn adopt by inserting هذا. **A real meaning fork under the
sentence the file is titled after**, and its clipped English fits neither analysis cleanly.

**MODERATE — لَّـٰكِنَّا۠ loses the mirror.** All three Arabic sources agree the phrase is
*"but I SAY: He is Allah, my Lord"* — a deliberate counter-**speech-act** to the boaster's
speech-act — with هُوَ as *ḍamīr al-shaʾn*, not subject (al-Ṭabarī «ولكن أنا أقول»;
al-Jalalayn «ضَمِير الشَّأْن تُفَسِّرهُ الجُمْلَة بَعْده»; al-Muyassar «وإنما أقول»).
al-Ṭabarī also rules on the recitation: «فليس ذلك بالفصيح من الكلام، والقراءة التي هي
القراءة الصحيحة عندنا ما ذكرنا عن العراقيين». The file reads it as bare predication and
never mentions the disagreement — **whose trace is the orthography لَّـٰكِنَّا۠ itself.**

**MINOR — al-Ṭabarī given Ibn Kathir's restriction.** The file says al-Ṭabarī locates the
"better" in the Hereafter. al-Ṭabarī is unqualified: «فعسى ربي أن يرزقني خيرا من بستانك
هذا». The Hereafter specification is Ibn Kathir's. The file states it correctly later, so
this is also an internal inconsistency.

**Restored material the file teaches without its warrant:** the two hadiths that are the
direct prophetic basis for its adab — Abū Mūsā's «ألا أدلك على كنز من كنوز الجنة؟ لا حول
ولا قوة إلا بالله» and al-Jalalayn's «لَمْ يَرَ فِيهِ مَكْرُوهًا». Plus al-Ṭabarī typing
the poor believer as «مثل سَلْمان وصُهَيب وخباب».

## `018-al-kahf/ayahs-042-044.md` — MODERATE

*18:44 is where the loss was total — al-Ṭabarī's entry is essentially two qirāʾāt
adjudications, both beginning far past the 500-char mark.*

**MODERATE — the file's longest theme is built on the sense al-Ṭabarī explicitly rejects,
using that sense's own proof-text.**

- ٱلْوَلَـٰيَةُ (fatḥ) = *muwālāh* in religion, proof-texted by ﴿اللَّهُ وَلِيُّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا﴾
- ٱلْوِلَايَةُ (kasr) = «الملك والسلطان»

al-Ṭabarī rules for **kasr** and dismisses the other: «أولى من الخبر عن الموالاة التي لم
يجر لها ذكر ولا معنى، لقول من قال: لا يسمَّى سلطان الله ولاية، وإنما يسمى ذلك سلطان
البشر». The file's Theme 4 — its longest — is protective guardianship / loyal care, and it
recruits **2:257 ﴿اللَّهُ وَلِيُّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا﴾ as its proof, which is precisely the
proof-text al-Ṭabarī assigns to the reading he rejects.** Ibn Kathir's fatḥ reading is not
protection of believers either: it is the **disbelievers' forced submission** at the moment
of punishment (40:84; Firʿawn at 10:90-91).

**MODERATE — هُنَالِكَ relocated from the Resurrection to the garden's collapse.** Three of
four place it in the Hereafter: al-Ṭabarī «حين حلّ عذاب الله بصاحب الجنتين في القيامة»;
al-Jalalayn «أيْ يَوْم القِيامَة»; Ibn Kathir "There (on the Day of Resurrection)". Only
al-Muyassar generalises. The file settles for the minority and gives no sign. Ibn Kathir
also records a **waqf** disagreement over whether هُنَالِكَ closes 18:43 or opens 18:44 —
so the file's premise ("The opening *hunalika*") is itself one of two options.

**MODERATE — ٱلْحَقِّ / ٱلْحَقُّ settled silently at the textbook case-variation site.**
al-Jalalayn: «بِالرَّفْعِ صِفَة الوَلايَة وبِالجَرِّ صِفَة الجَلالَة»; Ibn Kathir pairs
each with a proof-text (25:26 for rafʿ, 6:62 for jarr). The file's choice matches
al-Ṭabarī's *tarjīḥ*, so nothing is wrong — but the **rafʿ** reading ("the TRUE walāyah,
not the false one, belongs to Allah") is arguably closer to the file's own argument about
false protectors than the reading it built on.

**MINOR — al-Ṭabarī paraphrased in the file's vocabulary rather than his own**, so the two
operative terms in the summary (*walayah*, "such a moment") are the two things he actually
adjudicates against.

## Cross-cutting

Unlike the contested-verses audit — where every error ran toward **comfort** — these
errors run toward **tidiness**: a contested word flattened into a gloss list, a
two-camp *iʿrāb* dispute resolved into one clean reading, a *qirāʾāt* ruling omitted at
the exact spot where the orthography preserves its trace. Nothing here is invented to
please a reader; it is smoothed to make a cleaner essay.

In three of these cases the *rejected* reading would have served the file's own argument
better than the one it chose — the rain rather than the thunderbolt, the extension rather
than the force, the "true walāyah not the false" rather than the guardianship. **The
honest version was also the stronger one.** That is now the third time tonight that has
been true.
