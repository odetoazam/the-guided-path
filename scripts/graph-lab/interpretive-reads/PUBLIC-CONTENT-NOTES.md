## `/posts/quran-9-5-sword-verse-kill-the-unbelievers` — one additive gap

**Why it surfaced:** the interpretive read of `009-at-tawbah/ayahs-004-006.md`
returned a critical flag, and noted the public 9:5 article rests on that file's
reading. Audited the published article directly.

**What is already right** (checked against the live page, 2026-07-31):
- The tadabbur-vs-fatwā disclaimer renders — it comes from the PostPage
  component, not from `content_html`, so a `content_html` grep alone will
  wrongly report it missing.
- The article names the sword-verse designation rather than hiding it, and
  frames the classical dispute: *"the central scholarly debate was only ever
  about which boundary applied — whether the verse was specified (takhṣīṣ) …
  or whether it abrogated (naskh) certain earlier verses."*
- 9:6 (asylum and safe escort) is present.

**The gap: 9:4 is never mentioned.** The article works from the 9:2 four-month
grace period forward but omits the verse that exempts treaty partners who kept
their terms — *"except those with whom you made a treaty … who have not failed
you in anything nor supported anyone against you: complete their treaty to
their term."*

This is the passage's own strongest limit on the command, and both al-Ṭabarī
(in his tarjīḥ) and Ibn Kathīr stress that unbroken fixed-term treaties were
honoured to term. Including it would strengthen the article's existing thesis
using the Qur'an's own next-door verse, which is more durable against
adversarial quotation than any commentary.

**Suggested fix** (one sentence, additive, no thesis change): after the 9:2
grace-period material, note that 9:4 exempts treaty partners who kept faith and
orders their terms completed — so the command in 9:5 is bounded by the verse
immediately before it as well as the one immediately after.

**One claim worth a second look while in there:** *"Not one major mufassir
removed the field."* An absolute about the whole tafsīr tradition. The intended
sense (nobody read 9:5 stripped of its surrounding limits) is defensible, but
Ibn Kathīr's own edition carries Ad-Daḥḥāk's "it abrogated every agreement of
peace" and reads the command coercively. Either narrow the claim to the named
commentators the article actually cites, or soften "not one" to "the major
commentaries".

## ⚠ `041-fussilat/ayah-053.md` — FALSE THESIS. Needs regeneration, not a patch.

The first UNGROUNDED thesis in the whole pass (53 files read at the time). Every
other flagged file has a sound thesis with defective scaffolding; this one's
central claim does not survive the tafsir.

**The file reads 41:53 as an "evidence program" — signs unfolding across
centuries of human scientific discovery until truth self-clarifies.** That
reading depends on two moves the commentators unanimously reject:

1. **The pronoun in أَنَّهُ ٱلْحَقُّ.** All four editions gloss it as **the
   Qur'an**, with no ikhtilaf — al-Jalalayn: ﴿حتى يتبين لهم أنه﴾ أي القرآن
   ﴿الحق﴾; al-Muyassar: أن القرآن الكريم هو الحق; Ibn Kathir: "until it becomes
   manifest to them that this (the Qur'an) is the truth". The file reads it as
   truth-in-general, which is what lets the verse become a prophecy about
   science rather than a statement about the Qur'an being vindicated.
2. **أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِ is inverted.** The file has Allah turning away from the
   doubter to address the Prophet. The editions read the sufficiency as aimed
   AT the deniers, as proof of the Prophet's truthfulness (al-Muyassar: أولم
   يكفهم دليلًا على أن القرآن حق، ومَن جاء به صادق).

Plus a claim that is simply false, verified twice — once by the reviewer, once
directly: the file says fingers-in-ears is "literally described in this surah".
Surah Fussilat contains أذان only at 41:5 (deafness in the ears) and 41:44.
The fingers-in-ears image is not in this surah at all.

**Why this one is different:** the other 26 flags are additive — supply the
missing ikhtilaf and the file is sound. Here the thesis itself is the defect,
so a paragraph cannot fix it. Per the master validation policy this belongs in
the priority regeneration queue via `/quranic-tadabbur`, not in an editing pass.
It stays `validated: false` until then.

**Method note worth keeping:** this file was caught because the wave-3 brief
warned the reviewer that 41:53 and 86:5-7 are the two most common iʿjāz ʿilmī
anchor verses in circulation and that house style quarantines that move.
Targeting known-risk verses with an explicit warning is what surfaced it —
a generic read might not have pushed on the pronoun.
