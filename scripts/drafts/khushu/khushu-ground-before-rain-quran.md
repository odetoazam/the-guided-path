<article class="prose-blog">
  <p class="text-lg leading-relaxed">The Quran uses a particular word for what a heart does in prayer. It uses the same word for a field lying flat, dry and finished, in the season before the rain comes. Once you have seen the second use, the first stops sounding like an instruction and starts sounding like a description of terrain.</p>

  <p>The word is <em>khushu'</em>, and it turns up seventeen times. It is the second thing said about the successful believers, right after they are named:</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      قَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ فِى صَلَاتِهِمْ خَـٰشِعُونَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">Successful indeed are the believers — those who are humbly submissive in their prayer.</p>
    <cite>Al-Mu'minun (23:1-2)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:23:2:6 root=خشع pos=N -->

  <p>Most people meet the word here, take it to mean concentration, and spend years trying to manufacture more of it. The Quran's other uses suggest the effort is aimed slightly wrong.</p>

  <h2>Where else the word goes</h2>

  <p>Follow it around the Book and look at what kinds of things it gets attached to. Voices, hushed on the Day when nothing carries but a whisper. Eyes, lowered as people come out of their graves. Faces, on a day when they will be worn down. A mountain, which the Quran says would be seen split apart and <em>khashi'</em> if the Quran were sent down onto it.</p>

  <p>These are not descriptions of effort. Nobody's face is straining. In almost every case the word is an active participle — a description of the state a thing is <em>in</em>, rather than a report of something it is doing. Voices and eyes and faces and mountains do not perform this. It happens to them, in the presence of something larger.</p>

  <p>And then there is the one that reorganises the whole word.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      وَمِنْ ءَايَـٰتِهِۦٓ أَنَّكَ تَرَى ٱلْأَرْضَ خَـٰشِعَةً فَإِذَآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَآءَ ٱهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَحْيَاهَا لَمُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰٓ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled — and when We send down water upon it, it stirs and swells. Indeed, He who gave it life is the giver of life to the dead. Indeed, He is over all things competent.</p>
    <cite>Fussilat (41:39)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:41:39:10 root=خشع pos=N -->

  <p>The land is <em>khashi'a</em>. Flat, low, holding nothing, giving nothing back. It has no way to change that condition from inside itself. What changes it arrives from above — and when the water lands, the ground stirs and swells.</p>

  <h2>The state, and what meets it</h2>

  <p>Put the two verses next to each other and a picture forms that is different from the usual one.</p>

  <p>Khushu' in prayer, on this reading, describes the condition of a person who has stopped generating and started receiving. Lowered. Quiet. Making no claim. The English word "concentration" suggests a tightening, a person gripping their attention harder — which is close to the opposite of ground lying open.</p>

  <p>The land verse also settles the question of what such a state is for. Bare ground is not admirable in itself; it is simply what is there before the water. Its whole significance is that it can receive. And the sentence does not end with the field. It ends with the dead being raised, using the ground as the proof.</p>

  <h2>The verse that asks for it directly</h2>

  <p>Only twice does this word appear as a verb rather than a description, and one of those is a question put to believers.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      أَلَمْ يَأْنِ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَن تَخْشَعَ قُلُوبُهُمْ لِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا نَزَلَ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ وَلَا يَكُونُوا۟ كَٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ مِن قَبْلُ فَطَالَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْأَمَدُ فَقَسَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ ۖ وَكَثِيرٌ مِّنْهُمْ فَـٰسِقُونَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">Has the time not come for those who believe that their hearts should be humbled at the remembrance of Allah and what has come down of the truth — and that they should be like those given the Scripture before, upon whom the term was prolonged, so their hearts hardened? And many of them are defiantly disobedient.</p>
    <cite>Al-Hadid (57:16)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:57:16:9 root=خشع pos=V -->

  <p>Two things are worth holding here. The trigger named is remembrance — the hearts are to soften <em>at the remembrance of Allah and what has come down of the truth</em>. Something descends, and the softening is the response to it, exactly as in the field.</p>

  <p>And the opposite condition is named with a word from farming country: hardness. Hearts that went hard because too much time passed. That is the failure mode the verse warns against, and it is a soil problem rather than a knowledge problem. The people described had the Scripture. What they lost was the ability to be moved by it.</p>

  <h2>What comes next in the surah</h2>

  <p>Then the very next verse does something that reads as deliberate once the field is in your mind.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      ٱعْلَمُوٓا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحْىِ ٱلْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا ۚ قَدْ بَيَّنَّا لَكُمُ ٱلْـَٔايَـٰتِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">Know that Allah revives the earth after its death. We have made clear to you the signs, that you might reason.</p>
    <cite>Al-Hadid (57:17)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <p>A verse about hearts going hard, and immediately: God revives the earth after its death. The consolation is placed where a person who has just recognised themselves in the previous verse would reach it a second later.</p>

  <p>The order matters. First the diagnosis, which is unflattering — you have had this long enough, and something in you has set. Then a fact about ground, offered as a sign to reason from. Land that has died is the Quran's standing example of a condition that looks permanent and is temporary.</p>

  <h2>The one place it is called heavy</h2>

  <p>Al-Baqarah adds the practical note, and it is honest about the cost.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      وَٱسْتَعِينُوا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ ۚ وَإِنَّهَا لَكَبِيرَةٌ إِلَّا عَلَى ٱلْخَـٰشِعِينَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">And seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, it is heavy except upon the humbly submissive.</p>
    <cite>Al-Baqarah (2:45)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:2:45:18 root=خشع pos=N -->

  <p>Prayer is heavy, the verse says — and light for one kind of person only. The weight is real and the Quran declines to pretend otherwise. What lifts it is the state, not extra strength.</p>

  <p>Which turns the usual advice around. A person struggling in prayer often concludes they need to try harder, and the verse suggests the heaviness is a symptom of the ground rather than a shortage of effort. Ask what has hardened, and what could soften it.</p>

  <h2>What this asks of someone with a practice</h2>

  <p>The word's own range does the teaching. Voices drop, eyes lower, faces are worn, a mountain splits, and a field lies open — all described with one word, and in every case the change comes from outside the thing itself.</p>

  <p>So the question a settled practice eventually has to face is less about technique than about condition. The hearts in Al-Hadid were not short of information. Time had simply passed over them, and something set. What the verse offers them is remembrance as the water, and the earth as the proof it works.</p>

  <p>Ground that lies open is doing very little. That turns out to be the point.</p>
