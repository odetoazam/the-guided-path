<article class="prose-blog">
  <p class="text-lg leading-relaxed">There is a verse people quote to explain why they stopped trying, and it says the opposite of what they think. It arrives at the end of a list of things to do — and the word for reliance is the last item, placed deliberately after all the others.</p>

  <p>The verse is in Al-'Imran, addressed to the Prophet ﷺ after a battle that went badly. What it asks for is a sequence.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ ٱلْقَلْبِ لَٱنفَضُّوا۟ مِنْ حَوْلِكَ ۖ فَٱعْفُ عَنْهُمْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَهُمْ وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">So by mercy from Allah you were lenient with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you. So pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in the matter. Then when you have resolved, rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely upon Him.</p>
    <cite>Al-'Imran (3:159)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:3:159:44 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->
  <!-- morphology:3:159:51 root=وكل pos=N -->

  <h2>Four instructions in a fixed order</h2>

  <p>Read the second half slowly. Pardon them. Ask forgiveness for them. Consult them in the matter. Then, when you have resolved — rely.</p>

  <p>Every step before the last one is work. Pardoning people who have just cost you dearly is work. Asking God's forgiveness on their behalf is work. Consulting them, after their advice contributed to the outcome, is harder still. And resolving — settling on a course when the last one ended badly — is the hardest of the four.</p>

  <p>Reliance enters only once all of that is behind you. It is attached to the moment of resolve, and resolve is what happens at the end of a process, when a person has gathered the information available and made a decision they will now have to live with.</p>

  <p>Al-Jalalayn glosses the phrase with unusual bluntness. When you have resolved, he writes, on carrying out what you intend <em>after the consultation</em> — rely on God: trust in Him, and not in the consultation. Al-Muyassar reads it the same way: consult them in matters that call for consultation, and when you have resolved on something after consulting, carry it out depending on God alone.</p>

  <p>Both commentators keep the consultation and the reliance. Neither treats them as competitors. The consulting produces the decision; the reliance decides where you place your confidence once the decision is made. And the object of that confidence is the point — a person who consults and then trusts the consultation has simply moved their dependence from God onto a committee.</p>

  <h2>The father who did both in one sentence</h2>

  <p>The clearest demonstration belongs to Ya'qub, sending his remaining sons back to Egypt with a father's anxiety and a father's practicality.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      وَقَالَ يَـٰبَنِىَّ لَا تَدْخُلُوا۟ مِنۢ بَابٍ وَٰحِدٍ وَٱدْخُلُوا۟ مِنْ أَبْوَٰبٍ مُّتَفَرِّقَةٍ ۖ وَمَآ أُغْنِى عَنكُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ مِن شَىْءٍ ۖ إِنِ ٱلْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ ۖ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ ۖ وَعَلَيْهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلُونَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">And he said: O my sons, do not enter by one gate, but enter by different gates. And I cannot avail you against Allah in anything. The decision is only for Allah. Upon Him I have relied, and upon Him let those who would rely, rely.</p>
    <cite>Yusuf (12:67)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:12:67:35 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->
  <!-- morphology:12:67:42 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->

  <p>Watch how much is packed into one speech. He gives a specific tactical instruction — split up, use different entrances. Then he immediately concedes that his instruction protects nobody from anything God has decided. Then he says that judgment belongs to God alone. Then he declares his own reliance. Then he generalises it into a principle for anyone else who would rely.</p>

  <p>He does not choose between the precaution and the trust. He states both inside a single breath, in that order, and the concession sits between them like a hinge. The instruction is worth giving. The instruction guarantees nothing. Both are true, and a person who can hold them together at once is describing what the word actually means.</p>

  <p>It matters that this is Ya'qub speaking. He is the man who has already lost one son to a story he never believed, who has wept until his sight failed, and who is now watching the same road take another. His reliance is not the calm of someone with little at stake. It is what he has left after doing everything a father can do.</p>

  <h2>What the reliance is attached to</h2>

  <p>At-Talaq puts the promise in its plainest form, and the setting is worth noticing: it comes inside the legislation for how a marriage ends, addressed to people in the middle of a legal and domestic mess.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      وَيَرْزُقْهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَحْتَسِبُ ۚ وَمَن يَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَهُوَ حَسْبُهُۥٓ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ بَـٰلِغُ أَمْرِهِۦ ۚ قَدْ جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدْرًا
    </p>
    <p class="translation">And He will provide for him from where he does not expect. And whoever relies upon Allah — He is sufficient for him. Indeed, Allah accomplishes His purpose. Allah has already set for everything a measure.</p>
    <cite>At-Talaq (65:3)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:65:3:10 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->

  <p>The reply to reliance is not a promise that the plan will work. It is a statement about sufficiency: whoever relies on God, God is enough for him. And the provision described arrives from a direction the person had left out of their calculations — from where he does not reckon.</p>

  <p>That phrasing assumes the reckoning happened. Somebody sat down and worked out where help might come from, made a list, and the list turned out to be incomplete. Reliance is what a person does about the gap between their best planning and everything their planning could not see.</p>

  <h2>Reliance inside a working life</h2>

  <p>Shu'ayb says it in the middle of an argument with a town that is cheating its customers, and the sentence around it is full of effort.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      ‎…إِنْ أُرِيدُ إِلَّا ٱلْإِصْلَـٰحَ مَا ٱسْتَطَعْتُ ۚ وَمَا تَوْفِيقِىٓ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ ۚ عَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَإِلَيْهِ أُنِيبُ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">…I only intend reform as much as I am able. And my success is only through Allah. Upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn.</p>
    <cite>Hud (11:88)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:11:88:52 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->

  <p>Reform as much as I am able. That clause sets a person's own capacity as the measure of their obligation, and it sits immediately before the statement of reliance. Al-Jalalayn glosses the success he mentions as his ability to do this and everything else he has been asked to do — which places the reliance underneath the effort rather than in place of it.</p>

  <p>The same pairing shows up in the Quran's short description of the believers, where reliance is the final item after two others: hearts that stir when God is mentioned, faith that increases when the verses are recited, and then — upon their Lord they rely.</p>

  <blockquote class="ayah-quote">
    <p class="arabic" dir="rtl" style="font-family: var(--font-amiri,'Amiri'),serif; font-size: 1.5rem; line-height: 2.2; color: rgba(201,168,76,0.85);">
      إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ ٱللَّهُ وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَإِذَا تُلِيَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَـٰتُهُۥ زَادَتْهُمْ إِيمَـٰنًا وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ
    </p>
    <p class="translation">The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts tremble; and when His verses are recited to them, it increases them in faith; and upon their Lord they rely.</p>
    <cite>Al-Anfal (8:2)</cite>
  </blockquote>

  <!-- morphology:8:2:26 root=وكل pos=V form=V -->

  <p>Al-Jalalayn's gloss on the last clause is three words long: in Him they place their confidence, and in no one else. The word keeps resolving to the same question — where a person's weight is resting.</p>

  <h2>For the one who has already done what they could</h2>

  <p>Put the verses side by side and the shape is consistent. Reliance appears after consultation and resolve. It appears alongside a father's tactical instructions and his admission that they secure nothing. It appears in a man's account of reforming what he is able to reform. It answers with sufficiency rather than with a guarantee of outcomes, and it delivers what arrives from outside the calculation.</p>

  <p>Which makes it a word for a particular moment — the one where the doing runs out. You made the calls. You took the advice. You chose, knowing the choice might be wrong. And then there is a stretch of time you have no way to fill, between the decision and whatever it turns into.</p>

  <p>That stretch is where the Quran puts <em>tawakkul</em>. It asks for it only once your hands are empty, and it answers with a single clause: He is enough for him.</p>
